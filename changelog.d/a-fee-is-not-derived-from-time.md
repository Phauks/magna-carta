The matter's Fees tab is a work-in-progress capture ledger, and the invoice window is where every operation on a bill lives.

Fees was built as a read-only projection of invoice lines: it listed what bills had already charged and called that the fee ledger. That put the effect where the cause belongs. A fee is a flat charge for professional services somebody records by hand — its own dated draft with a timekeeper, a description, an amount and a billing disposition — riding the same chain as a time entry and a cost, and claimed onto a draft invoice exactly as either of those is. It is never computed from time entries, drawn out of them, or consolidated from them. The ledger is now built on the shared work-in-progress surface: the columns are Date, Entry Number, Date Entered, Timekeeper, Description, Amount, Billable, Attachments and Status; the status column is the review chain rather than an invoice's standing; the `Drawn From` column and the vocabulary around it are gone; and a New Fee record window opens in the product's one two-pane window. The agreement is compacted to `Arrangement v3 · in force since <date>` with View and Amend, and the terms live behind View.

**No route serves a fee record yet**, so the ledger stands empty and says so, and every write renders where the design puts it, disabled, naming the store it waits on. That store — capture, update, void and delete over a fee row, plus the claim path — is the next slice of this work.

Billing gains the invoice window the design has asked for since round five. Identity, lines and totals sit on the left, or the claim workbench on a draft; Payments & Credits, History and Delivery are tabs beside them; and one action bar of fixed geometry sits beneath both, so an act a bill refuses is disabled with its reason rather than vanishing and moving everything else. Adjust Invoice takes its place on that bar, marked against the billing-model revision it waits on, rather than being left out. The sidebar is cut to identity, standing, a few figures, whether the bill was delivered, and Open Invoice; the money acts that were scattered across row menus now live in the window, where a reader can see what they are acting on. The register anchors on Created, and the `Total (all matters)` column and the rest of the multi-matter apparatus are gone.

A charge in the wrong ledger can be moved rather than voided and re-recorded. "Move to Another Ledger…" is in the shared row menu, and its dialog states what carries and what does not for the exact pair chosen — because the differences between the pairs are the whole decision — keeps the entry number, and explains why a moved record wears a number whose shape names where it was first written down. The move itself is one cross-table write with refusals a client cannot be trusted to enforce, and it is marked against that route.
