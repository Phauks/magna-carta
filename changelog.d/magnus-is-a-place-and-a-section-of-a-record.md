**Magnus has an interface, in both of the homes it was always going to need.**

The assistant's backend has been complete and unreachable since it landed: the conversation record,
both fail-closed consent gates, the queue-dispatched path to an answer, the audit and the metering.
There was no way in. There are now two, and they are two on purpose.

**The sidebar's Magnus is a place** — the member's own workspace, every conversation they have had
across everything, newest first, each citing the matter its transcript is kept on when it is kept on
one. A conversation started here belongs to no matter.

**A matter's Magnus is a section of that record** — the conversations kept on that matter's file and
no others, where a new one is filed on the matter without the screen asking. It draws no header and
no way back, because the matter's own masthead and section strip are already on screen.

Both are one component read through two routes, on the Tasks precedent: a conversation looks and
behaves the same wherever it is opened, and neither screen knows the other exists. What differs is
what the place IS, not what it can do.

**The one thing the matter section says that the workspace does not** sits above its composer:
_Magnus reads only what you type in this conversation. Keeping the conversation on this matter's file
does not let Magnus read the matter: not its documents, not its calendar, not its parties._ Standing
inside a matter is exactly the context that makes a reader assume the opposite, and the matter link
scopes the record rather than the request. That line goes away when the assistant can genuinely be
given a matter to read, and not a day before.

**A member accepts a disclosure before their first question**, and it says the things a member — not
an administrator — needs told: what leaves the platform, that the assistant sees only what they type,
that it can do nothing but answer, that the transcript is a firm record readable inside the firm and
never by a client, and that it can be wrong, is not legal advice, and is theirs to verify. The
version accepted is recorded, so a change to what the assistant can see or do asks everybody again.

**Every state the record can actually reach is on screen.** The two firm-closed screens name which
consent is missing and who can change it, rather than offering a control the reader cannot use. A
question in flight shows its pending answer. Each of the five ways an answer can fail is said in
words a member can act on — the model was unavailable, the firm switched the assistant off while the
question waited, the day's allowance is spent, the message was too long, it was cancelled — and none
of them reads "something went wrong". An answer built from a shortened conversation says so, because
only the member can tell whether the dropped part mattered.

**The transcript reads as a record, not a chat window.** The new `Conversation` component in the
design system draws a speaker label over a body, hairline-separated, in the register the rest of the
platform reads its records in — no bubbles, no avatars, no coloured alignment, no motion. It holds
no copy of its own.

Magnus stays **off until a firm turns it on**, and turning it on still takes the settings write path:
the firm-administrator enablement screen is the next piece. Until then every firm's Magnus renders
its closed state and names the gate.

## Public

**You can now ask Magnus a question, in two places.**

**Magnus in the sidebar** is yours: every conversation you have had, newest first, with the matter
each one is filed on named underneath. A conversation started there belongs to no matter.

**Magnus on a matter** — the Magnus tab — keeps the conversation on that matter's file. It shows the
conversations belonging to that matter and nothing else, and anything you start there is filed on it
without your having to say so. A conversation started in a matter appears in your sidebar list too:
one conversation, read from two directions.

**Magnus sees only what you type.** Not your files, not your calendar, not your directory, and not
the matter you are standing in. Filing a conversation on a matter says where the transcript is kept;
it does not hand Magnus the matter. If you want Magnus to work with something, put it in your
message.

**Magnus only answers.** It cannot open a matter, book time, file a document, send anything, or
change any record.

**Magnus can be wrong.** Its answers are not legal advice and nobody has checked them. Verify
whatever you rely on.

The first time you open it you are shown a short disclosure and asked to accept it — what leaves the
platform, what Magnus can see and do, that the transcript is kept as a firm record your colleagues
with permission can read and your clients never can, and that it can be wrong. You accept it once,
and again only if what Magnus can see or do changes.

You can name a conversation, and delete one. Deleting removes it and everything in it, which is the
only way to remove what was typed.

Magnus is off until your firm turns it on, and that takes two separate decisions: your firm must
allow its information to reach an AI provider at all, and it must enable Magnus specifically. If
either is missing, the page says which one and who can change it.

Full guide: [Asking Magnus](docs/user/features/asking-magnus.md).
