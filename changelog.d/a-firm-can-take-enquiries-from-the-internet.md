**Public intake forms.** Put a page on the internet that somebody who is not yet your client fills in — who they are, how to reach them, and what happened — then read what arrives in one queue.

Write the questions from a fixed set of eight kinds, publish the form, and hand out its link. Your firm's own custom fields are deliberately not on that list: a custom field can point at a record in your directory, and a public form able to use one would let a stranger aim a value at somebody you know.

A published form freezes. You can keep editing afterwards — your changes sit in a draft nobody outside the firm sees until you publish again — and answers people already sent stay attached to the questions they were actually asked, so you are never guessing which wording somebody was answering.

**Nothing a stranger sends becomes a person, a matter or an intake on its own.** Accepting an enquiry records that a member read it and decided to take it further; putting the person into your directory is still your act. That is what keeps a flooded form a list you clear rather than a corpus you clean.

A link that has been scraped can be replaced — every link already handed out stops working, which is the point — and a form can be closed and reopened. There is no delete: a form holds the enquiries people sent it, and those are your record of who asked you for help.

## Public

Take enquiries straight from your website. Build a form, publish it, and share its link — people who are not yet your clients tell you who they are and what happened, and it all arrives in one queue you work through.

You choose the questions and how they are worded. Change them later and the form on your site updates when you publish again; answers people already sent stay attached to the questions they were actually asked, so nothing is ever ambiguous months on.

Nothing that arrives touches your contacts or your matters until you say so. Read an enquiry, then accept it, decline it, or mark it as spam. A link that has been picked up by spammers can be replaced, and a form can be switched off and back on without losing anything.
