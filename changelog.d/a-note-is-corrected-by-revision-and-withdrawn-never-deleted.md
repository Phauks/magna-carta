Matter notes: the Notes section on a matter is real, and a note is a record rather than a text field.

Until now the platform had no note anywhere. What it had were scalar `note` columns on a dozen individual records, each one nullable free text on one row, attributable to nobody, editable in place, with no history and no reader but that row's own detail view. The matter's Notes section had promised "dated, attributed, and searchable" since the shell was built.

A note is dated and attributed when it is written, and those never move. Correcting one appends a version and keeps the old one, so the history is every body the note has ever had, each with its own author and instant; a colleague correcting a partner's note is recorded as having corrected it. Nothing is edited in place, because what makes a note worth having is that it was written then, by that person, saying that.

Withdrawing is the answer to text that should never have been typed: another client's privileged material, somebody's medical information, a note filed against the wrong matter. The words stop being shown everywhere, including the matter's activity history and search, while the record that the note existed, who wrote it and who withdrew it stays, with a required reason standing where the text was. There is no delete anywhere in the feature: no button, no route, no capability. The one case where bytes must genuinely leave is a legal question that reaches a person through the erasure report.

Reading, writing and withdrawing are three separate permissions, seeded to the seats that work matters and not to Billing or Read-only. A note joins the matter's activity history as one more entry carrying its author and its date and never a word of its text, on the same discipline that keeps an email's subject line off that read. No merge field can resolve to a note, so work product cannot be assembled into a letter under the firm's letterhead, and notes stay outside discovery collection: producing one means exporting it as a document first, which gives it a version, a hash and a custodian of record.

Search finds notes on the matter you are on, and matches the current text only. A note corrected to take out a name stops being findable by that name, which is usually why somebody corrected it.

## Public

**Matters now have Notes.** Write down what happened on a case that nothing else records: the phone call, the standing instruction, the reasoning behind a decision. A note is dated and attributed the moment you write it, and those never change.

**Correcting a note keeps what it said before.** Fix a typo or add what you left out, and the earlier version stays readable in the note's history, with the name of whoever wrote it. Nothing is edited over the top, because what makes a note worth having is that it was written then, by that person, saying that.

**Withdraw anything that should never have been written down.** Another client's privileged material, somebody's medical information, a note filed against the wrong matter. The text stops being shown everywhere, including the matter's activity history and search, while the record that the note existed, who wrote it, and who withdrew it stays with your reason in its place. Nothing is deleted, and your firm can see that a note came down and by whose hand.

**Notes stay inside the firm.** Clients never see them, and there is no setting that makes one visible. They are never merged into an assembled document, so a note cannot end up in a letter under your letterhead, and they are not swept into discovery review sets. Reading, writing and withdrawing are three separate permissions, so your firm decides who does each.

**Find a note again.** Search a matter's notes for what they say now. A note corrected to take out a name stops being findable by that name, which is usually why it was corrected; the earlier wording is still in that note's history.
