Time is entered in hours, in units, or as the span between two clock times.

Three ways of saying the same thing, because firms are three. **Hours** — "1:30", "1.5", "90m" — is what everybody understands and is always available. **Units** counts the firm's own billing increment: a practice that bills in tenths thinks in tenths, and asking that person to convert six units into 0.6 hours is asking them to do arithmetic the product already knows how to do. **A span** is the two clock times the work happened between, which is how somebody who was in a meeting from nine to half past ten actually remembers it.

All three are live at once and each is a view of the same minutes: type in any of them and the others follow.

**A unit is whatever the firm bills in, and is asked rather than assumed.** Six minutes is the prevailing default and a firm on quarter hours means two and a half times as much work by "two units" — so the increment is read from the firm's own setting, scoped to the matter and the member, and never guessed.

**How many of the three are on screen is the reader's own setting**, in the record window's menu. All three show by default, because a capability nobody can see is a capability nobody has; a member who only ever thinks in one of them can put the other two away. The last one standing cannot be turned off — a window with no way to state how long the work took cannot record time — so the control refuses rather than obeys, and says so.

A span that ends earlier than it starts is read as crossing midnight, because that is a real evening.
