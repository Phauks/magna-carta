**Closing a matter asks for the date its paper file may be destroyed after, no earlier than a firm-set minimum, and the date shows on the calendar.** New per-firm table `matter_file_retention` (`20261109T180511_a-matter-file-carries-its-destruction-date.sql`): one row per matter keyed by `matter_id`, `destroy_on` under the shared civil-date shape CHECK, who recorded it and when, and a `(destroy_on, matter_id)` index for the firm-wide window. A table rather than a column because a CHECK on `matter` is a rebuild of the table most of the schema points at. `packages/matters/src/matter-file-retention.ts` composes the write: `isCivilDate` refuses a day that does not exist (`invalid-file-destruction-date`), an unknown matter or the platform's holding matter is `matter-not-found`, null deletes the row, and every write audits `matter.file_destruction_date.set` or `.cleared` with `{ previousDestroyOn, destroyOn }` because the row only ever holds the current date. `POST /api/v1/matters/{id}/close` takes an optional body `{ fileDestroyOn }` (a civil date, or null for none) and writes the date in the close's own batch; `composeFileDestructionDateAtClose` refuses a date earlier than the close plus the firm's `matters.fileRetentionMinimumYears` (`file-destruction-date-before-minimum`, naming the earliest date; 0, the default, is no minimum; a close on 29 February counts to 1 March), and a later date or none is always accepted. Reopening clears the date in the reopen's batch, audited. `PUT /api/v1/matters/{id}/file-destruction-date` (behind `matters:edit`, idempotent) changes it afterwards on a closed matter only (`matter-not-closed` otherwise), under the same floor. `GET`/`PATCH /api/v1/matters/settings` read and write the minimum; the PATCH needs the new `matters:manageRetention` permission, seeded to Owner/Admin in the same migration, and audits `matters.settings.updated`. The web gains **Settings › File Retention**; `MatterDetail` gains `fileDestructionDate`. The `matterFileDestruction` timeline source (kind `fileDestruction`, matter-anchored, no label) puts the date on the federated read, the web calendar's **File destruction dates** layer stops being a Soon row and reads it under the same `layer:fileDestruction` key, and the matter's Activity section names it. The close step's right panel gains a **Paper File** section, **Destroy After** (prefilled at the earliest allowed date) or **No Destruction Date**. On the status window of a closed matter, **Destroy After** is a date field with **Record** and **Clear**: unlike the office it is not saved as the field changes, because a segmented date field holds a complete wrong date at each keystroke of a typed year. The platform derives no date and nothing deletes anything when one arrives.

## Public

**Record when a matter's paper file may be destroyed.** Closing a matter now asks about its paper file: choose **Destroy After** and a date, or **No Destruction Date**. Your firm can set a minimum number of years under **Settings › File Retention** (Owner and Admin only); a close then refuses a date earlier than the close plus that minimum, and opens on the earliest allowed date. A later date, or none, is always allowed. On a closed matter the date can be changed afterwards from the status window, and reopening a matter clears it. Magna Carta never works the date out for you, since retention periods depend on your jurisdiction and your firm's policy, and it never destroys or deletes anything when the date arrives. It is a reminder: the dates appear on the calendar under **Dates, File destruction dates**, and every change is kept in the matter's audit history with the date it replaced.
