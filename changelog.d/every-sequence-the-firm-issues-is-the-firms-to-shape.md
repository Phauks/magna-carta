**Every sequence the platform draws is now shaped by a scheme, and the numbering screen is eight folds instead of one long menu.** The register's `fixed` formulation is retired (`packages/sequence-numbering/src/purposes.ts`): `invoice`, `time_entry` and `communication` join the five already scheme-shaped, so `SchemeNumberingPurpose` is `NumberingPurpose` and a new run is a row rather than a renderer. Three additions made that reach without changing an issued number. `month`, `day` and `matter_number` join `NumberSegment`, its zod union and `renderNumber`, so the day-scoped call number and the matter-prefixed entry number are templates rather than special cases. `scopeSource: "segments" | "owner"` on the register says whether the counter scope is derived from the template's scoping parts (the collision-proof general case) or passed by the domain — a time entry counts within its matter's handle, an invoice within the letterhead chosen at finalize — and `schemeSchemaFor` refuses a scoping mark on an owner-scoped run, since the switch would do nothing. `scopeDistinguishedBy` names the part such a scheme must carry (time entries: `matter_number`); the invoice run's is conditional and `finalizeInvoice` refuses `unknown-number-scope` by name for a bill drawn under a scope its scheme cannot render. Its mirror, `invoice-numbering-needs-letterhead`, refuses a firm-wide bill once the scheme prints the office code, where the bill would otherwise have reached the renderer and failed as a server error. `renderInvoiceNumber`, `renderEntryNumber`, `renderCommunicationNumber` and their start-value constants are gone; `planSchemeDraw` gained `{ scopeKey, count }` and returns a `SchemeDrawPlan` carrying every number of a block, over the new `guardedAdvanceStatement(…, by)`. The call counter's scope keys are re-keyed by `20261002T192145_communication-number-scope-parts.sql` from `2026-09-08` to the `2026:09:08` the scoping parts derive. On the web surface, `settings/numbering/+page.svelte` is an `Accordion.Root type="multiple"` with the current example on each heading; `numbering-scheme-editor.svelte` puts reorder and delete at the row's end, sizes cells to content, states `preview.parts[index]` in a cell the purpose cannot customize, and offers the starters as "Load an example" in the table's last row. `tooling/lint-settings-scroll/` walks all 27 settings routes through their component graph and fails a nested scroll region, with 15 stated allowances. Two defects the static scan cannot see were found by rendering the page: the parts table was still 1038px in an 848px column because each `ChoicePicker` asked for a text input's default width, which a content-sized table takes as a column floor (the picker gains `fillsCell`, Part alone takes the spare width, the long heading wraps, the row controls are `icon-sm`; `apps/web/test/numbering-parts-table.test.ts` pins it), and `NavigationLayout`'s content-box navigation column was `h-full`, so its gutters overshot the row by 48px and scrolled the canvas on every settings page (it is `self-stretch` now, and the layout's Storybook story measures that neither column passes the layout's bottom).

## Public

**Every number your firm issues is now yours to shape.** Invoice numbers, time entry numbers and call and text reference numbers join matters, consultations, intakes, conflicts checks and custody packets under **Settings, Numbering** — eight sequences, each built from the same parts. Nothing you have already issued changes: each one starts out rendering exactly what it rendered before, and only changes when you change it. Two new parts came with them, **Month** and **Day**, and time entry numbers can carry the **Matter Number** they belong to.

**The numbering screen reads as a list again.** Each sequence is now a fold, closed by default, with its current number shown on the heading — so the page opens as a summary of all eight and you open only the one you want. The stray second scrollbar is gone — on this page and on every other settings page, where the whole page could be nudged a short way beside the list's own scrolling — and the table fits beside the settings list on any screen 1220 pixels wide or wider, so the controls at the end of each row are always in view. In the parts table, **Move Up**, **Move Down** and **Delete** sit together at the end of each row, the parts offered are in alphabetical order, and a cell you cannot type into now shows you what it will fill in instead of leaving you guessing. **In Force** now reads **Current**. The ready-made shapes moved under **Load an example**, because they were never the only shapes available.

**One thing to check if you bill on more than one letterhead.** An invoice number counts within the letterhead it is issued under, and that letterhead now has to appear in the scheme. If your firm has opened a second invoice numbering scope, add the **Office Code** part to the invoice scheme before your next bill on it; until you do, that bill is refused rather than given a number your firm-wide run could issue as well. The reverse holds too: once invoice numbers print the office code, every bill needs a letterhead, so leave the part out unless your firm issues under more than one.
