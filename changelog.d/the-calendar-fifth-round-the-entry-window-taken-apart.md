**The calendar's fifth round: the interface taken apart along its own rules.** A modularity review of `apps/web/src/lib/calendar/` found the entry window at 2,150 lines deciding what the module's conventions say a pure module decides, and one host reading what the shared context already held. This round is behaviour-preserving. The window's decisions are pure modules with tests: `entry-verbs.ts` (which verbs a record's state allows, and the tab counts), `entry-updates.ts` (a correction never re-sends participants), `continue-draft.ts` (the fourth draft), `entry-links.ts` (which end of a link is the other one, and what may still be related), `calendar-permission.ts` (the one wording of the calendar-manage refusal, used by the window, the firm screen, the matter section and the three settings catalogs), and `draft-text.ts` (the trim-to-null, wall-clock and civil-date readings the four drafts and three forms each carried a copy of). The Links and Rooms tabs are their own components owning their reads, forms and failures (`entry-links-tab.svelte`, `entry-rooms-tab.svelte`); the window's event, firm-event and continuance panes render `record-timing-fields.svelte` and the new `event-type-select.svelte` instead of six hand-written copies of the same fields, which also ends the drift between "End date" and "End Date". The matter section's List view moves into the module as `matter-calendar-list.svelte`, and the page reads through the shared context, by the owner's ruling. The view's per-scope sentences and addresses move down into `calendar-scope.ts`, `calendar-window.ts` and `calendar-reads.ts`; the untested pure modules gain tests. The window is under 1,300 lines and every question it used to answer inline is asserted without a browser.
