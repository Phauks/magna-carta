**The New Check panel has two panes, and every setting says what it searches.** The panel asked for
parties, a matter, a trigger, four source checkboxes, a band, three hop budgets and two caps in one
column, with the meanings on hover and a footer reading `Firm floor · Party · Balanced · Defaults
depth 1 · closure cap 500 · candidate cap 5000`. Almost none of that could be acted on by somebody
who had not built the engine.

It is now two panes. The left one asks the two things a member always fills in — the parties, and
the matter the check is filed under — and under them says, in sentences, what pressing **Run Check**
will actually do: how far it will walk each kind of relationship, whether it can reach the firm's own
people, how names will be compared, and which trigger the log will file it under. The right pane
holds the search settings, each with its own line saying what it searches rather than a hover title.

**The names changed with the layout.** `Party`, `Graph`, `Imputation` and `Content` are now
**Name or identifier**, **Relationship**, **The firm's own people** and **Documents and messages**.
`Strictness` — a dial, not a thing — is **Name matching**, and its two settings, `Balanced` and
`Over-inclusive`, are **Standard** and **Widest**; both say out loud that they match names that
*sound* alike, which was true before and said nowhere. `Control` and `Family` are **Ownership and
control** and **Family and personal**. `Closure cap` and `Candidate cap` are **Related parties to
reach** and **Candidates to record**. These are the same stored values under better words, so the
firm's log, the settings screen and the printed report all read the new ones.

**A check no longer asks which trigger to file it under.** Preliminary and comprehensive name *when*
a check ran, not how it searched — the pre-engagement screen, or the moment the firm accepts — and
that is a fact the record already holds. A check filed against a matter the firm has taken on is
comprehensive; every other check is preliminary. The panel says which, rather than asking a member to
classify their own act.

**And a matter is optional, in what the field says as well as in what it accepts.** It always was
accepted without one; the field now says that a matter is where a check is *filed*, not what it is
checked *against* — naming one adds that matter's own parties and lets the search reach the firm's
own people, and leaving it empty is a real choice rather than an omission.

**A blank number is the firm's own, and now shows which number that is** — inside the box, as its
placeholder, instead of in a block of shorthand at the bottom of the panel.

## Public

**The New Check panel now has two sides, and every setting says what it searches.** The left side is
what you are asking — the parties, and the matter to file the check under if there is one — and under
them, in plain sentences, what pressing Run Check will actually do: how far it will follow each kind
of relationship, whether it can reach your own people, how names will be compared, and how the check
will be filed. The right side is the search settings, each with a line under it saying what it
searches, and you can leave every one of them alone.

**The settings are named for what they search.** Where a check looks is now **Name or identifier**,
**Relationship**, **The firm's own people** and **Documents and messages**. How widely it matches
names is **Name matching**, set to **Standard** or **Widest** — and both of those say out loud that
they match names that *sound* alike, which they always did. How far it walks is **Ownership and
control**, **Family and personal**, **Representation** and **Corporate family**. Where it stops is
**Related parties to reach** and **Candidates to record**. Your firm's log, the settings screen and
the printed check all read the new words.

**A check does not need a matter, and the field says so.** A matter is where a check is filed, not
what it is checked against — finding the matters that might conflict is the point of running one.
Naming one adds that matter's own parties to the search and lets the check reach your own people.

**You are no longer asked whether a check is preliminary or comprehensive.** Those two words say when
a check ran, not how it searched, and your record already knows: a check filed against a matter your
firm has taken on is comprehensive, and every other check is preliminary. The panel tells you which.

**A number you leave blank shows the number it will take** — your firm's own setting, inside the box.
