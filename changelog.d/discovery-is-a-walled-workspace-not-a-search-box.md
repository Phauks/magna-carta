**The discovery surface has a design.** Slice 5·1 is built end to end and completely invisible; this is where its screens go and what they are made of.

The framing question it answers is whether discovery is a super-powered search engine. It is not, and five built things say so: the split catalog is keyed by corpus rather than by matter so a matter search is structurally unable to name an opposing party's production, the per-review-set access list is fail-closed and its reader is not the matter's reader, the corpus has forward-only states where a search has none, its outputs are documents a court reads, and the reviewer's act is coding a queue rather than searching. Search is one criteria provider among several.

Three addresses, one per reader: the matter section that lists a matter's review sets, the firm-wide register for a member whose day is review rather than a case, and the review workspace, which leaves the matter chrome because the matter's consolidated search and its create control would otherwise sit on screen aimed at a different corpus than the documents underneath them.

The expandability answer is the repository's own, applied four times: declared registries validated at module load, rendered by one screen, every entry carrying a maturity stage so an unserved capability renders disabled with its line instead of being absent. Criteria providers (the one narrowing control), reviewer panels, document renditions, and the deliverables the corpus produces. Under them one property does the load-bearing work — a selection is a criteria set and never a list of identifiers — which is what makes bulk coding over search results compose on the day search is deployed rather than being built then.

Six owner questions, two of which cannot be defaulted: the address, and the access-list dead end, which the surface makes reachable by a person clicking a button.
