**The matter status seal is the control that changes it, and the program answers whether a matter can close.** Pressing the seal on a matter's masthead opens the status window; the lifecycle action left the overflow menu, which held nothing else and is gone with it. The window is short by design: the standing, whether the matter can close, what becomes of the paper file, and the one step the standing allows. One step per standing, never a status picker, because none of the three operations takes a status on the wire.

`GET /api/v1/matters/{id}/close-readiness` is new. The close hook's rules are pure reads with a separate commit guard, so running them read-only is the same question the close asks and none of the write — which means the window can state a verdict instead of making somebody press a button to discover it. A blocked verdict disables the press, per the standing rule that a control does not offer a press whose refusal is predictable; `checking` and `unknown` never do, because nothing has been predicted there and the close is the authority either way. The route reports an already-closed matter as not closable with that as its blocker, so one definition of the word survives. A close refused at the last moment answers in the same shape and lands in the verdict rather than beside it, so the window never carries two accounts of the same fact. Those sentences reach the screen through a new `errors` array on the shared problem envelope, which until now dropped everything a refusal said beyond its one summary line.

**Physical file** and **Destroy after** render and say what they wait on. Neither is modelled: the platform has no register of the firm's OWN paper files, and no retention policy anywhere to derive a destruction date from — the custody register is explicit that it runs no timer at all, destroying an original on a written basis by two people rather than because a date arrived. Custodian is client property left with the firm to guard, which outlives the matter that brought it in, and is not the firm's file for a matter; conflating them would put a client's will where a paper file belongs. The gap is now a ledger node (`matters.physical-file`) rather than an absence.

The masthead also gained the way back, as a bare arrow at the head of the ledger line — the labelled `BackLink` rule earns its label above a masthead, where "back" is ambiguous; on a matter's own ledger line the docket is the only place up.

## Public

**Change a matter's status by clicking its status badge, and be told whether it can close before you try.** The badge at the top of a matter opens a short window: what the matter's standing is, whether it can be closed, and what becomes of the paper file. Magna Carta checks the firm's own rules for you and says yes or no up front. Where the answer is no, it names what is in the way — the amount, the trust account it sits on, and the ways to clear it — and the Close button stays disabled until it is cleared. The window also asks where the firm's physical file sits and when it may be destroyed; neither is recorded yet, and it says so rather than leaving the questions off the screen. There is also a back arrow to the matters list at the top left of every matter.
