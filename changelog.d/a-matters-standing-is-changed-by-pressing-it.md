**The matter status seal is the control that changes it, and closing is a checklist rather than a paragraph.** Pressing the seal on a matter's masthead opens the status window; the lifecycle action left the overflow menu, which held nothing else and is gone with it. One step per standing, never a status picker, because none of the three operations takes a status on the wire.

The close is the only step with a gate under it, so it is the only one that gets room. **Before it can close** renders the two registered rules as a checklist, each pointing at the matter section that settles it — and each row rests unticked, because neither rule is readable from here yet: the matter trust-ledger read takes a trust-account handle no route hands out, and the client-credit read takes a Directory entity id the matter record does not carry. A tick nobody verified is the one state a checklist must not invent. One press then resolves the whole list, because a refused close reports every rule that refused and silence about a rule is that rule passing; the refusing rows carry the subsystems' own sentences, which name the amount, where it sits and the movements that clear it. A refusal citing a rule this build has never heard of becomes its own blocked row rather than disappearing. Those sentences reach the screen through a new `errors` array on the shared problem envelope, which until now dropped everything a refusal said beyond its one summary line.

**Worth settling first** is read live from what the firm actually holds against the matter — unbilled time and costs, the unpaid receivable, custody packets — and is deliberately not part of the gate: an uncollectable receivable's lawful exit is a write-off, and a firm holds a client's original for decades after their last matter closed, so blocking on either would only teach people to write off what they meant to chase or to unlink a packet from its matter. A destination whose section is not built (Trust) is named with the Soon mark instead of dressed as a link, and the way to settle a requirement disappears once the close has ticked it.

The step says no before the server has to where it can (a sample matter, or an account without the permission the endpoint names) and stays offered where it cannot: a session read that failed is unknown, not unprivileged. The masthead also gained the way back, as a bare arrow at the head of the ledger line — the labelled `BackLink` rule earns its label above a masthead, where "back" is ambiguous; on a matter's own ledger line the docket is the only place up, and a second caps label competed with the number for the same register.

## Public

**Change a matter's status by clicking its status badge, and see what closing needs before you close.** The badge at the top of a matter opens a window that says what the change will do. Closing spells out that the file is archived and not sealed, and shows the two things that have to be true first as a checklist, each linking to the part of the matter that fixes it. Press Close and the list answers itself: whatever is in the way is marked with the amount and the ways to clear it, and everything else is ticked. Below it, anything the firm still holds against the matter — unbilled work, money still owed, original documents in safe custody — is listed with a link to where it is settled. None of those stops a close. There is also a back arrow to the matters list at the top left of every matter.
