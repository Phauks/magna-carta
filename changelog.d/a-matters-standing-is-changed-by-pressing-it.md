**The matter status seal is the control that changes it, and the step it offers is spelled out before it is taken.** Pressing the seal on a matter's masthead opens the status window; the lifecycle action left the overflow menu, which held nothing else and is gone with it. The window states what the step does to the record and, for a close, what the firm's own money rules check first, and then runs the real operation over the open/close/reopen routes: one step per standing, never a status picker, because none of the three operations takes a status on the wire.

A refused close is the interesting answer, so it is rendered as one: each rule's own sentence, naming the amount, where it sits, and the movements that clear it. Those sentences reach the screen through a new `errors` array on the shared problem envelope, which until now dropped everything a refusal said beyond its one summary line. The step says no before the server has to where it can (a sample matter, or an account without the permission the endpoint names) and stays offered where it cannot (a session read that failed is unknown, not unprivileged). The words live in `matter-lifecycle.ts` with a test over them rather than inside the markup.

The matter masthead also gained the way back: a return to the docket at the head of the ledger line, ahead of the number, wearing the eyebrow register the number already wears.

## Public

**Change a matter's status by clicking its status badge.** The badge at the top of a matter now opens a window that says exactly what the change will do before you make it. Closing a matter spells out that the file is archived and not sealed (it still takes entries), that today becomes its close date, and that the client's trust ledger has to be clear first. If closing is refused, you are told the amount, the ledger it sits on, and the ways to clear it, rather than a generic failure. There is also a link back to the matters list at the top left of every matter.
