**The surface over slice 5·1, which was built end to end and completely invisible.** Three addresses, one per reader: `matters/[id]/discovery` (the section, entry only), `/discovery` (the register, scoped by the per-review-set access list rather than by matter access), and `/discovery/review-sets/[id]` (the workspace, outside the matter chrome — the matter header mounts a consolidated search on every section by construction, and that search must never reach a corpus another party produced).

Four registries carry the growth, each arriving with the screen that renders it and each validated at module load: `criteria.ts` (five narrowings served, four rendered visible and disabled with what each waits on and which read it will compose into), `deliverables.ts` (two records written, three declared), `review-panels.ts` (three reading panels built, five to come, none of which changes the reviewer's shell), and `renditions.ts`, whose ordering is the reviewer's own order of questions — an excepted document leads with its named reason even when text was salvaged, because the reason is what a person has to act on. Under all of it, **a selection is a criteria set and never a list of identifiers**, which is what makes bulk coding over search results compose the day keyword narrowing lights up rather than being built then.

Four backend additions. `readVisibleReviewSets` is the matter-scoped builder without its matter clause, registered in the visibility fitness pass in the same commit, because the wall is the access list and a register scoped by matter access would hide from a contract reviewer exactly the work they were given. `GET /api/v1/loads/{loadId}/artifacts/{artifact}` presigns the manifest or the exception report in either form, audited as an export — the two records the ingest lane exists to produce were previously reachable only as R2 keys on a row. And the reviewer's two: one document by identifier with its family placing, its digests and every named reason it could not be read (carrying no R2 key — an object key is an internal address a client can only misuse), and its extracted text, served through the discovery routes and **gated by the review set's access list rather than by the file permissions the native bytes sit behind**, which is what makes the pane work for a reviewer brought in for one production and holding no file permission at all.

Recorded rather than assumed: the last-member revoke refusal that the feature ledger and the 5·2 design both list as an open owner question **is built**. The residual hole was on a different verb — the guard counted ROWS, while a deactivated member keeps their access row and is absent to every read, so a list reading "two members" could hold one person who left the firm last year and removing the other would strand the corpus. `selectReviewSetAccessCountStatement` now joins `member` on `status = 'active'`, and `readReviewSetsHeldOnlyBy` answers what a deactivation has to ask first. **Nothing in the platform deactivates a member** — the column exists, every permission read gates on it, and no route sets it — so the precondition has no caller and is named at the seam instead, beside `status` in the members route. A precondition registry with one entry and no route would have been scaffolding. The surface still does not offer the access list.

## Public

**Discovery has screens.** A matter's new **Discovery** section lists the review sets on that case and starts one; the **Discovery** row in the sidebar lists every review set you can open across every case; and opening one gives you its documents and its loads.

What you can do end to end: create a review set, name the Custodians of Record whose material it holds, declare a load — saying what is coming before it arrives, which is what lets the manifest state a finding rather than a claim — put the matter's own files into it, and watch every document reach one of exactly two endings. Nothing is copied: each document points at the version you picked, so your storage does not double and a later version of that file does not silently change what is under review.

**The two records a load produces can now be handed over.** The manifest says what arrived; the exception report names every document that could not be read, with the reason for each. Both download from the load, in a printable form for attaching to a letter and a machine-readable one. Taking a copy is recorded in your firm's audit log — which load, which record, which form, and never a word of the contents.

The documents screen narrows from one control: by Custodian of Record, by load, by whether a document was read, by the named reason it was not, and by family. Whatever you narrow to is in the address bar, so a narrowed list is a link you can send to somebody else on the review set. Searching by keyword, narrowing by date, and narrowing by coding are shown in the same control and unavailable, each saying what it is waiting for.

**Opening a document opens the reviewer**, walking exactly the documents you narrowed to, in that order — brackets move along it without your hands leaving the keyboard. You get the text the engine read out of the document, beside what the document is: its dates, its size, the engine that read it, the hashes recorded for it, what arrived with it in the same family, and every other copy of it the review set holds with the Custodian of Record who held each. A document that could not be read still opens, and leads with the named reason rather than a blank pane. Coding it, and seeing the original rather than its text, are named there and not built yet.

A review set you have not been put on does not appear at all, on any of these screens. That is deliberate: material another party produced is often under a protective order that not everybody on a case is cleared for, so working the matter is not enough, and a review set that showed itself while refusing you would already have said something.
