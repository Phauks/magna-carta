Address autocomplete: the street box of the one address form offers real addresses as a person types, and choosing one fills the rest of the form.

The engine is Smarty, and the reason is its storage terms rather than its coverage. The address a person picks is written into a permanent record, and of the large autocomplete services one lets a customer keep only its own place identifier while another treats every result as temporary. Smarty lets the firm keep the data, including after cancelling. The United States goes to its postal autocomplete, which returns the parts directly — so resolving a US address costs nothing and reaches nobody — and every other country goes to its international service in two steps, a free search and a charged lookup only when somebody chooses.

A building with many units is offered as one line with a count, and choosing it offers the units in turn, because writing the building's line into a record whose client lives in unit 4B is a letter that does not arrive.

The Worker serves it through two read-shaped POSTs rather than GETs. Its invocation log records every request's full URL, query string included, and what is being sent is a client's address a few characters at a time; a body is not in that log. What the Worker counts is which operation ran, which engine served it and how it came out — enough to watch the volume that drives the provider's bill, and nothing that could carry an address.

**Dormant until provisioned.** With no Smarty key pair set, every answer is "unavailable" and the address form behaves exactly as before. Setting `SMARTY_AUTH_ID` and `SMARTY_AUTH_TOKEN` switches suggestions on and starts the provider's meter; removing either switches them off. Neither is in the deploy's required secrets, so no deploy waits on them.
