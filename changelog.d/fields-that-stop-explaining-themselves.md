Several fields on the time and cost windows stop explaining themselves and start working.

- **Every code field carries a `(None)` row, for every firm.** A firm that does not code its time is the commonest configuration in the product, and it was getting two paragraphs apologising that it had no codes turned on, under two fields it could not use. `(None)` is also what the field READS at rest rather than a blank: an empty box says nobody has filled this in, and `(None)` says the entry carries no code. The explanatory line survives for the two states that really are inabilities — this account may not read the catalogue, or the read failed.
- **The internal memo moved under the description and lost its badge.** They are one act of writing that differs only in who reads it; down beside the arithmetic the memo was a stranded field a reader met after they had finished with the entry. The "Internal" chip beside the label said in a second voice what the word Internal in the label had already said.
- **The Billing dropdown lost "No charge, on the bill at nothing".** It is non-billable under a longer name — both mean the client is charged nothing and the entry carries no value, and the only difference anybody could name is whether the line prints, which is a question about a bill rather than about this entry. Two rows for one disposition make a member choose between them, and there is no right answer to that choice.
- **A document can be attached before the entry is saved.** "Save the entry first" is a true statement about the route and a wrong one about the work: the person writing up an afternoon has the document open in front of them. The picks are held and applied the moment the entry is written.
- **The cost window writes the same pair in the same shape** — Description then Internal Memo, stacked and full width — and commits with the same words, "Save & Close". A cost and a time entry are recorded minutes apart by the same person, and the fields they share should not move between them.
- **Voided, never struck.** One word for the act, across the history rail, the sheet, the ledger and the record window.
