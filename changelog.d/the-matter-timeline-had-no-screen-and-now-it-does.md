The matter's Activity section: fourteen subsystems' worth of history, in order, on one screen.

The federated timeline has been built, tested and served since the calendar slice — `GET /api/v1/matters/{matterId}/timeline`, fourteen declared sources read in place under one sort key, one cursor and one visibility contract, with nothing projected or copied. **Nothing in the web app called it.** The matter's story was scattered across the sections that each hold a piece of it, and the read that puts it back together answered to no screen.

**The screen names no source and switches on none.** `packages/calendar/src/sources/all.ts` declares where a source's rows live and whether it has a non-privileged label, and deliberately says nothing about presentation; `apps/web/src/lib/matters/activity-presentation.ts` is the other half — one entry per source key, carrying the sentence, the noun the Kind filter narrows by, its glyph, and what its declared label actually means. Adding a subsystem to the platform's timeline is one entry there, and `activity-presentation.test.ts` scans the calendar package's own declarations and fails until it exists, so a new source cannot reach a person's screen as an unexplained row.

**The rows are thin because the confidentiality design makes them thin.** Most sources declare no label at all — a note's first eight words and an email's subject line are exactly the disclosure a read spanning a whole matter must not make — so an entry states what happened, when and by whom, and the words stay behind the link, one authenticated request into the subsystem that owns them. Where a source does declare a label the screen names the FIELD beside it, because a document's type set beside a document row reads as the file's name unless something says which fact it is.

**It is the product's table, not a list that resembles one.** `DataTable.Grid`, six columns — Date (anchored, because a chronology read in another order is not one), Time, Kind, What, Details, Who — and with them every affordance the shared grid already carries: sorting, per-column filtering, a set filter with counts, show/hide/pin/reorder/resize, and a footer that says how many rows a filter is hiding. There is no filter bar above it, because narrowing to notes is the Kind column, narrowing to last month is the Date column, and narrowing to one person is Who. Kind's set filter declares the platform's whole vocabulary rather than reading it off the loaded rows, so a matter with no costs yet still offers "Cost" instead of telling a reader it can never have one.

**Every row says what the thing is.** The federated read still projects almost nothing, because it spans matters; the screen names its rows by following the deep link eagerly instead — one bounded read of each owning subsystem's own listing, through that subsystem's own access gate, joined on the row identity the timeline already carries. Six sources name their rows today: a document by its file name with its size, version and quarantine state; a note by its opening line; time by its narrative with the hours; a cost by its description with the amount; an event by its title with where it is held; a deadline by its title with whether it is overdue. The remaining sources keep the platform's own sentence, and adding one is a single loader in `activity-detail.svelte.ts`. Reading through each subsystem's own gate is also what keeps a narrower permission honest without the timeline having to know about one: a member who may not read notes gets a refusal there and rows that say a note was written without saying what it said.

**Nothing says a document was "filed".** To a lawyer, filing means filing with a court, and this platform files nothing with a court: a document is added to the matter, an email is saved to it. The source keys stay `documentFiled` and `emailFiled` because they are wire contracts — which is exactly why the words a person reads are decided at the presentation layer and not taken from them. The standing rule is in `docs/conventions/design-corrections.md`; the word remains correct in its own sense, as in a filing fee or a filing deadline, and the test is whether a court is on the other end of it.

Two deep links were repaired on the way. `emailFiled` pointed at a matter section spelled `email` and the three custody sources at `custody`; the app routes `emails` and `custodian`, so every one of those entries would have landed on a screen that does not exist. A conflicts check has no matter section at all, so its entries state what happened and offer nowhere to go — the screen asks the section registry whether a target is built rather than keeping a second list of which links work.

## Public

**See everything that has happened on a case, in one place.** The matter's new Activity section is the whole story in date order: email saved to the matter, documents added and assembled, hearings and deadlines, time and costs booked, client money moved, items in your custody, conflicts checks, the notes your firm wrote, and the day the matter opened. It is the screen to open when you pick up a file you have not touched in months, and the one to read from when you need to account for what was done and when.

**Every row says what the thing is.** A document gives you its file name, size, version and whether the scanner has held it back. A note gives you its opening line. Time gives you the narrative and the hours; a cost its description and amount; a hearing its title and where it is; a deadline its title and whether it is overdue. You read the case down the page without opening anything.

**It is the same table as everywhere else.** Sort by any column, filter each one in its own header, show and hide and reorder the columns, and the footer tells you how many rows a filter is hiding. Narrowing to notes is the Kind filter; narrowing to last month is the Date filter; narrowing to one person is Who.

**Follow anything into the record behind it.** Each entry opens the thing it describes, in the section that holds it, with everything that section holds about it.

**"Added" is not "filed".** A document added to the matter's own record has not been filed anywhere: filing means filing with a court, and Magna Carta files nothing with a court on your behalf. The word appears here only in its own sense, as in a filing fee or a filing deadline.
