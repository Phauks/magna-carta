**An empty table no longer lights up under the pointer, and a table says when there is more to the side.** Two findings from one owner walk over the tables.

The first was reported as "the hover does not extend down the entire table, it stops about halfway", and the table it was reported on was an EMPTY one. The empty, filtered-empty and loading placeholders sit inside the table so the frame holds its height, which makes each of them a `<tr class="mc-table-row">` in the same body as the records — and the row hover found them there. Hovering an empty register painted a grey band exactly as tall as its message (measured at 70% and 78% of two frames on the table-states page, and about half of a taller one) with the rest of the frame left white: a hover that appears to give out partway down the table. The ruling against this was issued on 2026-08-30 and only half carried out — marking the placeholder's CELL does nothing, because the treatment being withheld belongs to the ROW. The row now carries `data-placeholder="true"`, and the shared recipe passes it over for the hover fill and for the `cursor: pointer` that promised a click there was nothing to click. Records are untouched. The story tests hold the cursor half of the treatment on both sides — a record offers itself, a placeholder does not — because the fill answers only to a real pointer, which the browser-mode driver does not supply.

The second is the gilt scroll-edge hint, which existed only inside the sidebar: a thin gold rule that DRAWS in at the edge of a scroll area while more content lies that way, the affordance the rail's hidden scrollbar gave up. It is now `ScrollEdgeHint` in the design system, taking the scrollport as a prop (the consumer keeps their own element, classes and handlers) and watching it through its scroll event plus a ResizeObserver over both the scrollport and its content, so a table gaining rows or a column being widened moves the rule without a scroll event. `Sidebar.Content` moves onto it unchanged, and `DataTable.Grid` now wears its inline pair: a table whose columns run past the frame says so on the side it runs off, and the rule swaps to the leading edge once the reader reaches the far one. The block pair is deliberately not drawn on the grid — its vertical scrollbar is visible and already says that much.
