**The build panel's Database section reports on both databases, not one.** Every request crosses the control plane — the account, the session, the firm's own record — before the firm's database is consulted at all, and the panel timed the second one while giving the first a bare ping: no round trip, no reason when it failed, and nothing about the lineage it was running. A member whose screens were slow could read "Answered in 12 ms" off a healthy firm row and learn nothing about the leg actually failing them. Both legs now answer the same two questions, built from one row builder so they cannot drift apart in what they measure or how they say it: a timed round trip with the database's own words when it does not answer, and its applied migration lineage against the head this build carries — each against its OWN journal, since the two lineages have different heads. The firm's bucket becomes a row of its own rather than a clause on the control-plane row, and it still answers when the control plane's probe does not, because the fact comes from the firm record the request was already resolved through. Six rows in two groups of three, the firm's first and the platform's underneath, so a reader who finds the firm's group healthy has somewhere to look next. On the wire `schema` is now `firmSchema` and `controlPlaneSchema` joins it; the control-plane lineage is read as one aggregate over its own `_migrations` ledger, and a control plane that has never been migrated has no such table, so that read says "not read" rather than guessing "behind" from an absence.

## Public

**The version panel now checks both of the databases behind your session.** Magna Carta's own database holds your account, your sign-in and the record of your firm; your firm's database holds everything your firm does. Every screen uses both, so the panel now reports on both in the same words — whether each answered and how long it took, and whether each has caught up with the version you are running. Your firm's file storage gets a line of its own. If a screen is slow or refusing, the section says which of the two databases it is, instead of showing you a healthy firm and leaving the rest unsaid.
