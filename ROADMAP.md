# Roadmap

The **living** execution tracker — what is in progress, what is next, what just shipped. It is updated in the same PR as the work (a definition-of-done item; see [`docs/conventions/documentation.md`](docs/conventions/documentation.md)).

This is **not** the white paper's _Build roadmap_, which is the stable **phase strategy** (Phase 0–7 and rationale). This file tracks execution against those phases and owns one thing: **narrative scope and ordering** — which stream is live, what it does next, what order the rest comes in, and why.

**It deliberately does not carry per-capability status.** [`docs/now.md`](docs/now.md) — the build register — is the complete inventory: every capability at its real status, the counts across all nine statuses, what is blocked on the owner, what is in build, what is queued, and how the counts have moved. It is generated from the [`roadmap.d/`](roadmap.d/) feature ledger and gated against it, so it cannot drift. Anything that is per-capability status is linked to here, never restated.

The other homes: per-slice stage (research → spec → plan → built → tested) in [`STATUS.md`](STATUS.md) · what shipped and when in [`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md) · decisions in [`docs/Decision-Log.md`](docs/Decision-Log.md) · subsystem design in [`docs/specs/`](docs/specs/) · integration adapters and their state in [`docs/integrations.md`](docs/integrations.md) · owner-side deploy and account actions in [`docs/deploy-queue.md`](docs/deploy-queue.md).

## Next

The remaining build, in the order it comes, tracked against the white paper's _Build roadmap_ (which owns the phase order and rationale — this section does not restate it). Phases 3–7 are **sliced provisionally**: a named slice list with a scope blurb each, **scope not design**. The boundaries are a planning aid, not a commitment, and each slice still gets its own design → spec → plan cycle when it opens, so the slice set may shift as a phase's design cycle begins. The spine is unchanged — documents finished first, the Financial Core immediately after, then litigation, client-facing, integrations.

Italic _gate_ notes are the cross-slice prerequisites: a deploy that must precede another, a review that blocks a build. Those are ordering facts and they bind.

Firm provisioning is the pilot: high value, lower blast radius than client money. The forward-only pipelines (ingest, scan, bundle, inference) stay on Queues and Durable-Object coordinators, because a rollback primitive buys a retry-forward pipeline nothing.
- **GPU password-recovery rungs.** Stage 1, the benchmark harness, is built. _Gate: the owner benchmark run — deploy the registry, rent the first rigs, commit the profiles_ — before Stage 2 wires the rung into the recover flow with its teardown trio. Rung 2 (desktop GPU) belongs to the Tauri phase.

### Phase 6 — Client-facing & scheduling (sliced provisionally)

Slices 6·1 (case calendar and matter timeline) and 6·2 (firm events, resources, links and continuance) are built and tested; their interface is the gap, tracked in _Now_. The Notifications subsystem is the shared dependency the rest route through. Still ahead:

- **Tasks & workflows / matter plans — the record, the plan and the time link are built; the rest of the workflow surface is not.** `@magna-carta/tasks` ships the record end to end: `matter_task` with a **nullable matter**, so a firm's own work (renewing the malpractice policy, interviewing a candidate) lives beside its client work in one list rather than in somebody's spreadsheet. That nullability is the slice's central decision and the opposite of the call `firm_event` made — the divergence turns on where the records are read, and the does-not-apply branch it creates is confined to one composable fragment every read shares rather than answered with a second table. Nothing derives a task's date and no overdue column exists, both held by scans. Cancelling and deleting are both real and distinguished. Four notification topics for two acts, plus a sixth audience, because the design's `designated` is the trust-compliance designation and would have delivered every assignment to nobody, silently. **Matter plans followed**, on the owner's 2026-09-01 answer to **W8**: a plan **offers** its tasks and a member accepts them. Asking for a proposal is a GET that writes nothing — it reads the matter's anchor, adds the offsets, resolves each step's role against the live matter team, and hands back tasks a person edits and drops; acceptance is the POST, and only what survived is written. Nothing reconciles afterwards: the accepted tasks are the firm's account of the work, and the template is only where they came from. The one piece of date arithmetic, `addCalendarDays`, is named and isolated so the calendar's scan can see it — plain calendar addition, no holidays and no rules, which is exactly why the result needs a person's eye before it becomes work owed. **W11** (does the platform ever ship curated plans) and **W12** (may the anchor vocabulary grow past `matter_opened`) stay open and neither blocks: the build took the design's own answer to each — ship none, keep one anchor. **Then the brief's third named requirement, time**: one nullable `task_id` on `time_entry`, answering **T2** as the scope study recommended — ONE task per entry, because an entry split across two tasks needs an allocation of its minutes and an allocation is a second ledger; two entries is the honest encoding, and a join table stays an additive migration if many-to-many is ever wanted. The task must be on the entry's own matter, which SQLite cannot express without a trigger, so one service assertion is the enforcement and a task on NO matter is a refusal rather than a wildcard — the firm's own work is timeless by construction. The minutes read back are minutes and never money, and batched over a page of tasks rather than a read per row. Four screens plus a field: the matter's Tasks section, My Work at `/tasks`, plan authoring under Settings, the proposal sheet, and the task picker in the time record window. **The load profile earned its keep on its first run** — page 20 costs less than page 1, the time read is one statement for a page of fifty, and `tasks.my-work-page-1` scans because the unnarrowed firm-wide read has nothing to seek on, recorded by name with the number rather than fixed in the change that measured it. **And the seam an addon writes through**, because the owner intends to mirror tasks to and from an outside tool and none of what that needs can be retrofitted: an external-identity table generic over an open `system` string (no vendor is named anywhere in the schema), a deletion tombstone written in the delete's own batch because a row that is gone cannot be enumerated afterwards, an `updated_by` stamp so a two-way sync can tell its own writes from a person's, and a change feed of two keyset streams carrying INDEPENDENT positions — one shared cursor drops rows silently at a clock collision, permanently. The platform still ships no adapter, no sync engine, no conflict policy and no field mapping; those are the addon's, on `@magna-carta/integrations`, and guessing at them now would be machinery with no consumer. **Next:** a plan's event and document steps, starting a timer from a task (behind the live-timer run store becoming plural), and the board view behind **T3**, which needs firm-defined statuses. Scope study: [`docs/specs/2026-09-01-tasks-scope-study.md`](docs/specs/2026-09-01-tasks-scope-study.md).

## UI roadmap — the deferred-surface build order

Every backend capability that shipped ahead of its UI, in the order the UI stream builds them (agreed with the owner 2026-07-02). The design system (0·7a) is on `main`; this is the queue it unblocks. **In-commit discipline:** a slice that ships a backend capability without its UI adds its surface to a stage here; the line leaves when the UI lands. Re-sequencing is a deliberate edit by the UI stream, not drift.

**Ordering principle:** the frame first (everything renders inside it), then the session (every API call needs one), then the two archetype screens everything else copies (List, Detail), then maximum "humans can exercise built backends" per unit of work — the project's testing bottleneck. Craft threads (dark design pass, empty/loading/error states, NVDA passes, visual-regression baselines) run through every stage rather than being one.

**Stage 1 — the frame (slice 0·7b core; strictly ordered)**

- **Real App Shell in `apps/web`** — routes, the assembled chrome (Sidebar/TopBar/BrandMark parts from the design system; nav registry plus permission filtering in the app), route-level View Transitions. No backend dependency. Landed.
- **Auth (slice 0·4 backend)** — sign-in, registration and invitation redemption have shipped. What remains on this line: password reset and recovery-code entry as real screens, the 2FA and passkey enrolment surfaces, and the alternative-method row, which stays absent until a second method exists behind it.
- **Appearance settings** — theme/mode/density/motion pickers over the wired axes; proves the Settings archetype.

**Stage 2 — archetype proofs on real data (slice 1·1; strictly ordered after stage 1)**

- **Matters list** — the owner-approved Engraved Ledger list screen on the real matters API; the remaining register details land as components here.
- **Matter workspace** — masthead, tabs and overview; the Detail archetype. List and Detail extraction on rule-of-two happens here.
- **Files: upload + browser** — in flight as the matter document surface (see _Now_). Drag-and-drop upload, the file table, Locations; the reusable upload machinery for every later tool.
- **Command palette wiring** — the Ctrl+K trigger is in the chrome; matters and navigation are the first providers. Early because it compounds with every later surface.

**Stage 3 — tools area (cheapest real utility; most test-unblocking)**

- **Tools shell** — the Tools nav section and the `/tools` listing page have landed. What remains: the `id → component` map, integration `Dispatch` and health branching, and role-seeding of the remaining `tools:` permissions.
- **First tool widgets (backends done and tested):** the network speed test has shipped. Remaining: Metadata Inspector & Scrubber, File Hash & Integrity, File Type Verifier, File Repair & Salvage's attach-to-matter flow, and the firm-usage snapshot view.
- **AI-text detection UI** — band-not-proof contract (the numeric score lives under `provenance`; never a "% AI" headline). _Coordinate: its vendor adapter is still on the backend queue._

**Stage 4 — the money spine (backends on `main`; order flexes with appetite)**

- **Time tracking (slice 3·1)** — the running-timer chip in the chrome plus time entries.
- **Ledger-voice components** (MoneyText/MoneyCell and the double-rule total, on `@magna-carta/money`), then the billing and costs surfaces (3·3, 3·4).
- **Trust UI (slice 3·2)** and the **report surfaces (slice 3·6)** — both inherit the ledger voice; the bar-audit pack has a backend and no screen.

**Stage 5 — documents deepening**

- **Preview and safe delivery (2·3), enhanced bundling, PDF services surfaces (2·5), and the calendar interface (6·1/6·2)** — the calendar is the largest built-but-unreachable surface in the program.

**Stage 6 — the long tail (small, independent; pull forward freely as backends land)**

- **Directory (1·3)** — the entity-graph UI over the built backend, plus the pronoun surface.
- **Custom fields (1·4)** — admin UI and the Convert preview/apply flow. _Coordinate: the Convert re-scaling fix must land first; show pre/post values._

What is deliberately unbuilt behind the same swappable port: an optional **CDR disarmed rendition** (all launch options were eliminated as destructive or retired) and a **commercial engine** run on our own compute. The rule that governs both: borrow outside expertise, never outside custody — a commercial engine on our own compute yes, a data-holding SaaS no. Research: [`docs/research/upload-malware-scanning.md`](docs/research/upload-malware-scanning.md).
- **Version-history delta storage** — store superseded document versions as zstd `--patch-from` deltas against a keyframed base, re-packing the OOXML ZIP with stored entries first (deflate scrambles bytes on a one-word edit, defeating naive deltas), with a roundtrip content-hash verification, as an async job at the existing supersede-to-infrequent-access hook. The seam is reserved (`storage_encoding` on version metadata). _Gate: golden rule 10 — at $0.01/GB-month, full snapshots must reach roughly 100 GB per firm before costing $1/month, so this waits on real version churn and a load-profile number._ (ADR-0077.)
- **Telemetry analytics tier (Iceberg-on-R2)** — register the redacted Logpush-in-R2 logs as R2 Data Catalog Iceberg tables for zero-egress querying, with compaction and snapshot-expiration maintenance. Analytics Engine remains the MVP path and the seam sits on data already written. _Gate: adopt when a trigger binds — retention past ~92 days, exact unsampled counts, rich joins, or cost at scale — and re-verify the open-beta limits at that point._ (ADR-0056, _Proposed_.)
- **Post-quantum signature migration** — passkeys/WebAuthn and the PAdES seal to ML-DSA/SLH-DSA, plus a PQC-capable external-KMS-rooted key hierarchy. Seamed today via algorithm versioning. _Gate: upstream standards (FIPS 204/205/206, COSE, ETSI PAdES, CA support)._ Verify Cloudflare hybrid PQC TLS is on for every hop and log any endpoint that will not negotiate it. (ADR-0046.)
- **Root-key rotation and customer-managed keys (BYOK).** Rotation is cheap by design — the root key wraps only the per-firm keys, never the field ciphertexts, so rotating it re-wraps N keys with zero data re-encryption; the ciphertext frame already reserves the version bytes. _Gate: before the first real client onboards, or sooner on a compromise or compliance trigger._ BYOK wraps a firm's key under the firm's own KMS instead, giving that firm a cryptographic kill-switch without breaking server-side processing — a future enterprise tier, not MVP, because the per-firm key is already the seam. True zero-knowledge encryption is explicitly out of scope; the only viable carve-out is a narrow opt-in sealed vault for fields that never need processing. (ADR-0046.)
- **Branded per-firm subdomains + the safe-delivery host model.** When firms get branded URLs the tenancy boundary becomes the subdomain, which reshapes both the preview delivery host and the session-cookie scope. Decided shape: one neutral, shared, cookieless delivery host embeddable by any firm subdomain via a wildcard `frame-ancestors` — which does not weaken authorization, because the signed firm-scoped token names one object and the embedding origin was never the gate. Session cookies stay host-only. Seamed now (the preview header builder emits the wildcard; a fitness test pins the cookie invariant). _Remaining when branded URLs are built: provision the wildcard host, move the app to per-firm subdomains, point `FILES_ORIGIN` at the real host (it is a placeholder today, so previews are inert until then), and bind the session's firm to the requested subdomain server-side._
- **Edge hardening (in-Worker)** — six request-handling improvements, all app/build work rather than dashboard config (the triage and the decision to skip Cloudflare Snippets are in [`operations.md`](docs/conventions/operations.md)). **(1) A security-header baseline is the one do-now gap** — no CSP, frame-options, content-type-options, referrer-policy or permissions-policy exists in the Worker or SPA today; add a global header middleware, with HSTS set at the zone. **(2) A deliberate CORS policy**, gated on the Tauri desktop client and the custom-hostname portal landing. **(3) Webhook HMAC verification and outbound request signing** in the integrations HttpClient seam, gated on the first signed integration. **(4) A maintenance-mode toggle.** **(5) Mining `request.cf`** for telemetry enrichment and bot-aware handling on sensitive routes. **(6) Honeypot / tarpit** — defense in depth, low priority, and tarpitting costs Worker duration.
