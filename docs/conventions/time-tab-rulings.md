# Matter time tab — the owner's standing rulings

> **Scope, as of round five: this ledger covers all four financial matter tabs — Time, Fees,
> Costs, and Billing.** Round six corrected the MODEL beneath two of them (a fee is a directly
> recorded charge, not a derivative of time; cost classes do not exist) and fixed one record-window
> architecture across all four. **Round seven took that record window out of this artifact's
> scope**: by the owner's ruling it is the product's one component, shared with the Time Sheet, so
> R7's M-rulings bind wherever a record is opened — not only on these four tabs. Round five merged the four surfaces into one review artifact, The Matter
> Ledger ([`docs/mockups/matter-ledger.html`](../mockups/matter-ledger.html)), and the owner's
> rulings now land here for every one of them. Earlier rounds' rulings were issued on the Time
> surface; where a ruling
> generalizes (the one table machinery, the status register, the details-panel pattern, the
> dialog family, the footer grammar) it binds the other three tabs exactly as it binds Time.
> The file keeps its name and home so its history stays put.

Every ruling the owner has issued on the time surface, cumulative, newest round last. **A ruling
here is settled.** It is not re-derived, re-proposed, or re-litigated by a later round, and a round
that silently reverts one is a regression, not a redesign. The review surface is The Timesheet
mockup (The Matter Ledger from round five on); rulings bind the app screen when the design ports.

Read this file before changing the surface, and check the built surface against it after. Do not
rebuild the surface from scratch: edit it. Regeneration is what loses these.

Status values: `settled` (built in the mockup and confirmed) · `regressed` (was settled, a later
round undid it) · `open` (asked for, not yet built) · `superseded` (a later ruling replaced it,
with the pointer).

Rulings from the file manager's ledger that generalize beyond that surface
([`file-manager-rulings.md`](file-manager-rulings.md)) bind here too — Lucide-only icons, no inert
controls, say what a control will do, the person's name never "You", never "Just now", filter
controls inside the column header sharing one hover, active filters unmissable, consistent row
heights, dialogs capped at the viewport, selection presenting details in a panel while the reader
stays in the list (N22c).

## R6 — round six: the corrected fee model, the two-pane record, and the quiet chain

Round six is a MODEL round before it is a design round. Three of the owner's corrections change
what the data IS, not how it looks, and the rest follow one architecture: **every record — time
entry, fee, cost, invoice — opens in the same two-pane window.** Rulings carry F (fees), C
(costs), A (approval), M (the modal architecture) and L (the list and its controls).

**The owner's fee ruling, in substance:** a fee is a directly recorded flat charge for
professional services. It is its own WIP entry — date, description, amount, status, disposition —

progress. It is never computed from, derived from, or drawn out of time entries.

**The owner on cost classes, verbatim:** "there is no hard or soft cost bullshit, that is not a

### Q-MERGE — the owner's reopened question: should Time and Fees be one page?

**Recommendation: NO — keep two tabs, and revisit only if the fee ledger stays this thin in
practice.** The legacy one-ledger-with-a-TYPE-column pattern is the obvious candidate, and now
that both run off one template it is a small change to make. It is still the wrong trade here:

1. **The columns do not agree.** Time's ledger earns Hours, Rate and the arithmetic that ties
   them to Amount; a fee has none of those, and a merged table either shows empty cells on every
   fee row or hides the columns that make the time ledger readable. The TYPE column pattern pays
   for merging with a permanently ragged table.
2. **The reading tasks are different.** Time is read in bulk and scanned by day and timekeeper —
   forty rows on this fixture. Fees are read one at a time and are few. Merging buries the small
   set inside the large one, and the fee arrangement strip (version, effective date, View / Amend)
   has nowhere to live that is not misleading over a mixed list.
3. **The owner already settled the adjacent question.** Round three ruled fees are their own
   surface and "the invoice is where time and fees become one ledger" (TP1). The invoice does that
   job today and does it correctly: the bill consolidates time into a professional-services line
   and prints each fee as its own line.
4. **The template already bought the real win.** What made merging attractive — one screen's worth
   of behavior, not two — is delivered: one chain, one panel, one review list, one record modal.
   Merging the tabs now would spend the ragged-table cost to buy something already owned.

**What would change the answer:** a firm whose practice is mostly flat-fee, where the fee ledger
is the long list and the time ledger the short one. Then the merged ledger with a TYPE column is
right, and because both already run off one template, it is a configuration of the same surface
rather than a rewrite. Recorded so the option stays cheap.

## R10 — round ten: a misfiled entry moves

The owner, on reading that refiling was still listed as a gap: **"you should be able to refile to
another matter."** L4 settled the design in round six — the RECORD carries a matter field so a
misfiled entry moves — and it had stayed a registered API gap ever since, because the route it
needs is a cross-matter write with its own permission rather than a `PATCH` of `matterId`. This
round builds it.

| #   | Ruling                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Status  |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| L4b | **A misfiled entry MOVES; it is not struck and re-typed.** The remedy keeps the record — its number, its memo, its codes, its files and its history. Striking and re-recording loses all five and leaves a struck row on a matter the work was never really on.                                                    | settled |
| L4c | **It is its own route, not a field on the update.** `POST /time-entries/{id}/refile`. Two ledgers change, two matters' totals move, and the rate the entry froze was resolved against the matter it is leaving; a patch field would let anything that can edit a narrative move money between matters by accident. | settled |
| L4d | **The entry number is KEPT** (R3, applied to matters as it was to ledgers). After a move its prefix names the matter the entry was FIRST written on — provenance, not an assertion about where it lives now — and the record says so on its hover.                                                                 | settled |
| L4e | **The rate and the rounding are RE-ASKED against the destination**, on the entry's own work date. Both resolve through the matter, so the answers the old matter gave are answers to a different question. The snapshot invariant is untouched: it forbids a later card edit re-rating a standing entry, not this. | settled |
| L4f | **What it refuses:** anything past draft, anything on a bill, anything a draft invoice has claimed, a matter this firm does not have, and the matter it is already on. The last is refused rather than treated as a no-op — a move that moved nothing still reported success.                                      | settled |
| L4g | **Every move is on the record**, one audit event naming both matters (R5, applied to matters). A move nobody can see is indistinguishable from a misfiling nobody noticed.                                                                                                                                         | settled |
| L4h | **A written entry cannot be taken OFF a matter.** The record has to be somewhere, so the field offers other matters and refuses the empty choice — the one thing a move cannot do.                                                                                                                                 | settled |

**Notes recorded this round:**

- The Matter field is now settable on every DRAFT, on both surfaces — the sheet's cell and the
  record window — and states where the entry is and why it stays on anything past draft.
- Recorded judgement: no undo step is pushed for a move on the Time Sheet. Moving it back is another
  refile, which the reader can simply do; a stack entry that silently re-ran a cross-matter write on
  a record two people may be looking at is a different act from putting a typed cell back.
- Recorded judgement: the entry keeps the rate CARD it resolved on, so a firm with several cards
  keeps the entry on the one it was captured under; the TIER within that card is what the
  destination re-answers.

## API gaps the mockup is honest about

> **Re-asked against the contract, 2026-09-02 (R9).** Eight of the entries below had been CLOSED by
> shipped routes while the built screens went on refusing the fields: the entry number, the entered
> date, the enterer, the resolved rate, both UTBMS codes, the internal memo, the many-attachment
> relation and the per-entry event history. They are struck through in place rather than deleted, so
> the register keeps its history and the shape of the mistake stays visible: **a gap register is
> only honest while somebody re-asks it against the contract.** The app's own list lives in
> `apps/web/src/lib/time/time-gaps.ts` and is now down to eight entries; check it and the served
> summary in `apps/web/src/lib/matters/time-api.ts` before adding to either.

Consolidated across rounds, in rough priority order. The per-matter listing and capture contracts
need:

1. ~~**The two dates:** `createdAt` (the entered date) is stored but not served on the listing (TL8).~~ **CLOSED** — `createdAt`, `createdByUserId` and `createdByName` are all served.
2. **Dual attribution:** delegated capture — the create route refuses a foreign
   `timekeeperUserId` today — plus a served enterer attribution; the audit trail already records
   `actorUserId`, so the capture half exists (TD9).
3. ~~**Entry numbers:** no matter-scoped sequence exists on the record (TL7).~~ **CLOSED** — `entryNumber` is drawn at capture through `@magna-carta/sequence-numbering`, served on the listing and returned by the capture ack. An entry captured before the sequence existed carries null, which the surfaces state rather than backfill.
4. **Invoice linkage:** `time_entry.invoice_id` exists on the row but the listing serves neither
   it nor the invoice number a billed entry should name (TV2).
5. **Un-approve:** kick-back is submitted-only in the API; TS4 wants approved → draft as well.
6. ~~**Status history:** no endpoint serves an entry's audit events.~~ **CLOSED** — `GET /time-entries/{id}/events` serves them oldest first, cursor-paged, each with its instant, its actor and the redacted detail the write recorded.
7. **Unsubmit** (TS6): no route returns a submitted entry to draft at the timekeeper's own hand —
   kick-back is the reviewer's act, not the asserter's.
8. **Delete-draft** (TR1): the API has void only; the two-verb model needs a delete route scoped
   to never-submitted drafts, refused for anything with a review record.
9. **Optional kick-back reason** (TS8): the kick-back route requires a reason today; it must
   accept its absence and record it only when given.
10. **Enterer permission to work a draft** (TK2): the update route must accept the enterer of a
    delegated draft, not the timekeeper alone — the same dual-attribution gap as capture (gap 2).
11. Bulk submit / bulk approve (TL14, TR2) can compose from the per-entry routes client-side; a
    batch route is an optimization, not a blocker.
12. **Round five, cross-tab:** the invoice ADJUSTMENT model (BM1) has no route and CONFLICTS with
    the accepted finalized-is-immutable decision — a pending ADR must revise the billing model
    (finalized-and-unapplied is adjustable with recorded revisions; any application locks) before
    the routes (`PATCH .../invoices/{id}/lines/{n}` post-finalize + a revisions read) exist.
13. **Round five, costs:** an inline vendor-create for the payee path (CP1) — the capture flow
    needs a directory create, not only a typeahead read; the cost twin of the delete-draft gap

    (CR1 renders it honestly).
14. **Round five, fees:** the From Entries modal (FD1) needs the per-line WIP-claim read
    (`invoice_wip_claim` served per line) — the fees spec's consolidation-read gap, now with a

    the claim path: a draft invoice claims fee records exactly as it claims costs.
16. **Round six, cost classes (C1):** any hard/soft class column or enum on the cost record is
    removed from the schema, not merely hidden; `vendorId` stays nullable, which is the whole of
    what the class was standing in for.
17. **Round six, the review gate (A1):** the gate is a FIRM SETTING the listing and capture
    contracts must serve, because with it off the submitted and approved statuses must never be
    reachable — the client cannot be the only thing enforcing that. Gap: no firm-level
    `reviewGateEnabled` is served or settable.
18. **Round six, moving an entry between matters (L4):** **CLOSED FOR TIME** —
    `POST /time-entries/{id}/refile` exists, on exactly the shape this entry asked for: its own
    route rather than a `PATCH` of `matterId`, refusing anything past draft, on a bill or claimed,
    keeping the entry number and re-resolving the rate and rounding against the destination. FEES
    still want the same route.
19. **Round six, attachments (M3):** every WIP record type needs a MANY-attachment relation, an
    attach and a DETACH route (detach must not delete the underlying file), and the counts served
    on the listing — the cost half of this is the existing `receiptFileId` gap (13), generalized
    to time and fees and widened from one file to many. **CLOSED FOR TIME** — the relation, the
    list/attach/detach routes and `attachmentCount` on the listing all exist and the record window
    uses them; an attachment references a document already on the entry's own matter, and detaching
    removes the link and never the file. Fees and costs still want the same shape.
20. ~~**Round seven — REQUIRED WORK, not a gap: the internal memo (M6).**~~ **CLOSED** — `memo` is a
    nullable column on the time entry, served on the listing and settable on capture and PATCH, and
    the built Time Sheet and record window now write and read it. Its confidentiality is asserted
    against a real rendered print sheet (`apps/web/test/time-record.test.ts`), not promised. The
    original specification, kept for the record: a **nullable `memo` TEXT column**
    on the time entry, **served on the listing and the single read**, and **settable on PATCH**.
    It **generalizes to all three record types** — the memo answers "what does the firm need to
    remember about this charge that the client must never see", which is a question a cost and a
    fee ask exactly as a time entry does, and the record window is one component (M3), so a
    per-type memo would be a per-type field list for no reason. Same column, same three tables.
    The one hard requirement beyond storage is **exclusion by construction**: the memo must not be
    reachable from any client-facing projection — the invoice render model, the LEDES export, the
    print sheet, the client portal — which is a decision about which columns those readers select,
    made once, not a filter applied per call site.
21. **Round seven — reclassification (R1–R5)** is a CROSS-TYPE MOVE and needs **its own route**,
    not a `PATCH` of a kind field: it deletes from one table and inserts into another inside one
    transaction, carries the entry number across a table whose sequence did not mint it, writes
    the audit event, and must refuse anything claimed, billed or past draft — refusals the client
    cannot be trusted to enforce. Sketch: `POST /matters/{m}/charges/{id}/reclassify {toKind}`.
    Two schema consequences: the **entry-number column must accept a number the target table's own
    sequence never issued** (so the sequence is per-matter-per-kind for MINTING only, never a
    uniqueness assumption for reading), and each table needs a **nullable provenance record**
    (`reclassified_from`, when, by whom) plus the **`incomplete` flag** that keeps a record out of
    `claimable` until its kind's required fields are filled.
22. **Round seven — attachments by reference (M9):** the attach route must accept an EXISTING
    document id and associate it, distinct from an upload that creates an object. The join needs to
    record which it was, so detaching a referenced file never deletes the matter's document.

    half — denormalize at capture); ~~the resolved rate on the listing~~ **CLOSED** (`rateE4`,
