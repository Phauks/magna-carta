summary: "A guarded write — an UPDATE or DELETE whose WHERE repeats the precondition its caller read — is marked `guarded(statement, label)` in the effect it rides, and a marked statement that affects no rows ABORTS its transaction rather than being reported on after the batch has committed. Before this, the WHERE clause protected the row and nothing else: `batch()` surfaces no per-statement affected-row count, `commitEffectBatch` returned void, and the route answered 200, so a member who lost the race was told their edit landed and found out by reloading — while the audit row riding the same batch, carrying no guard of its own, recorded an edit that never happened. Checking the count after the commit cannot fix the second half, which is why the check is inside the transaction: the DO-side and node-side SQLite adapters both raise `GuardedWriteRefusedError` mid-batch, `transactionSync` and `ROLLBACK` take the audit row and the outbox message with it, and the Durable Object returns it as a typed `unaffectedGuard` outcome alongside the existing `uniqueConstraint` one so nothing depends on an error message surviving RPC. An adapter that cannot enforce guards declares nothing, and `commitEffectBatch` refuses such a pairing outright with `UnenforceableGuardError` rather than committing unguarded — a silently dropped guard is the exact bug being closed. The fee and cost ledgers adopt it first, and their routes map the refusal onto the 409 conflict they already return for the sequential refusal, so no contract moves and a lost race is indistinguishable from a plainly illegal request, which is what it is. `pnpm run lint:guarded-writes` keeps it from eroding: a builder guarded at one call site must be guarded at all of them."

**The read that checks a precondition is advisory; the WHERE clause that repeats it is not — so the WHERE clause has to be able to refuse, and refuse the whole batch.**

**Context:** a domain mutation reads its row's state in one round trip and commits in another, with no transaction across the two. What holds the line when something moves in between is never the read — it is the `AND status = 'draft'` the statement carries, which matches nothing once the row has moved.

That half worked. The other half did not, and the interleaving harness is what made it visible. Two verifiers written against the fee freeze and the cost billing lock showed the invoice protected and the report wrong: `batch()` reports no per-statement affected-row count, `commitEffectBatch` returned `void`, and the route answered `200`, so a member whose edit was refused was told it succeeded. A statement builder's own comment said "meta.changes === 0 and the service refuses" — the service never saw `meta.changes`, because the statement rode a batch.

Worse, and less obvious: the audit statement rides that same batch and carries no precondition of its own. A refused edit left `fee_entry.updated` and `cost_entry.receipt_attached` behind. For a platform whose audit trail is evidence, a trail recording acts that did not happen is the more serious of the two.

The second problem is what decides the mechanism. Reporting the row count out of the batch afterwards fixes the first and cannot touch the second — by then the audit row has committed. The refusal has to happen while the transaction is still open.

**Decision:** `guarded(statement, label)` marks a statement in an `EffectWrite`; a marked statement affecting no rows raises `GuardedWriteRefusedError` from inside the transaction, and everything riding with it rolls back.

**Guards travel by position, not on the statement.** The object handed to an adapter is the port's `SqlStatement` and an adapter is entitled to expect exactly that; a marker property riding on it would be honoured by whichever adapter happened to look and be invisible to the others. `composeEffectBatch` returns the statements and a position-to-label map beside them.

**Every adapter that can commit a batch enforces the same rule, and one that cannot says so by omission.** The Durable Object's SQLite adapter and the node SQLite adapter both check and raise mid-transaction; the worker-side proxy marshals the label over RPC and rethrows what comes back. `commitEffectBatch` refuses to commit a guarded effect through a database that has not declared `enforcesGuardedWrites`, because the alternative is committing it unguarded and the only sign would be the absence of a refusal nobody was watching for.

**The refusal crosses the RPC as a typed outcome, beside the one that already exists.** `commitBatch` has returned `{ ok: false, uniqueConstraint }` rather than throwing since the per-firm database moved into the object, for the reason that an error message may not survive serialization. `unaffectedGuard` is the same shape for the same reason.

**A lost race answers exactly as the sequential refusal does.** The fee and cost routes map `GuardedWriteRefusedError` onto the `conflict` 409 they already return when the pre-commit read sees a frozen entry. No new problem type, no contract change, no snapshot movement — and correctly so: a member who lost a race and a member who asked for something plainly illegal are in the same position, and the answer should not tell them apart.

**Every guard-shaped statement in the repository has been decided about, and the gate is hard.** The first cut marked six builders across the two ledgers and left about a hundred and thirty undecided; that backlog has since been read through one statement at a time. Fifty-one are `guarded()`. Eighty-nine carry `fitness:unguarded-write` with the reason a zero is an outcome somebody wanted, and those reasons fall into seven recurring shapes — at-least-once replay, claim-or-nothing, mirror columns whose real guard is a unique index, set-shaped updates, statements riding a guarded sibling, probes that match nothing in the happy case, and partial progress by design. Nothing is undecided, which is what lets `pnpm run lint:guarded-writes` fail on a new one rather than counting it: the expensive part was the reading, and it does not have to happen twice.

**Two of the exemptions are load-bearing and would have been bugs to mark.** `refuseFinalizeOnFrozenWorkInProgressStatements` aims a CHECK-forbidden status at rows that must not exist, so it matches nothing in the happy case and guarding it would refuse every invoice finalize. `reRateEntryStatement` visits every unbilled entry a rate card produced, and one of them being invoiced inside the window must skip that entry rather than abort the correction to the other ninety-nine. Both read like textbook guarded writes from their SQL alone.
