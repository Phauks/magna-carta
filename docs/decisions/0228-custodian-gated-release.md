**Gated release is a precondition on the one movement write path, captured on the append-only row and enforced by trigger — and the vocabulary of acceptable authority is not invented here.**

**Context:** The register records movements without gating them, deliberately: friction on the movement a firm makes daily is how a register stops being kept. Release is the one exception, because it is the only movement that is irreversible from the firm's side — a packet checked out to the wrong person can be chased, an original handed over is gone — and the two questions asked afterwards are always the same: on what authority did it leave, and how did you know that was the right person. The register's design named the seam and left the gate to this slice.

**Decision:** (1) **The gate rides the existing write path.** There is no release route and no release service; `recordCustodyMovement` with `kind: "released"` is the path, with more required of it. A dedicated release endpoint would be a second place the append-only invariant, the derived status, and the audit row all have to be right, and the register's central API decision is that there is exactly one such place.

(2) **The capture lives on the ledger row, not a side table.** Four columns beside the borrower columns that already answer "who has it": the authority the release is made on, how the collector was identified, the member who made that check, and the second signatory. A side table could be written after the fact, or edited; the point of the capture is that it records what was established *before* the property left, and only the append-only row can carry that claim.

(3) **A trigger, not a CHECK.** Adding a CHECK to `custody_movement` means rebuilding the table and copying every existing row through it. Releases recorded before this gate existed carry no capture and were recorded honestly under the rules that then applied — making them pass would mean writing a basis nobody asserted into a ledger whose entire value is that nobody could do exactly that. A `BEFORE INSERT` trigger binds every row appended from here on and leaves the record of what actually happened alone. Two further triggers make the shape structural: two signatories must be two people (the requester/approver split the destruction request row already carries), and release capture is refused on any movement that is not a release, where it would read as evidence of a check nobody made.

(4) **Identity is captured as an assertion, not a copy.** The evidence column records what was checked, not the document's numbers. The row can never be edited or deleted, so a passport number typed there is permanent, and nothing the register does needs it.

(5) **Two-signatory release is the firm's choice and defaults off.** Whether two people must sign an original out of a safe is a firm's own risk decision — a small office where one person is the only person present cannot operate under a rule the platform imposed. The unconditional half of the gate (that authority and identity are captured at all) does not depend on the setting.

(6) **No authority vocabulary.** A grant of probate, letters of administration, and the fact that a power of attorney dies with its grantor are jurisdiction-specific. The basis is free text the firm writes, on the same footing as the ownership role and the destruction basis, and nothing parses it, matches it, or decides whether it is good enough. Whether a verified jurisdiction catalog of release authority is wanted at all remains an owner decision, alongside the retention and destruction flags this design has never softened.

**Consequences:** A `released` movement now requires a borrower and the capture; the register's other movements are unchanged and the daily check-out stays frictionless. Deposit and release receipts remain unbuilt: document automation exists, but its assembly path is matter-routed and matter-permissioned, and a receipt for a matterless packet would have to route through the system holding matter — a routing and permission decision this slice does not settle. The binding half already ships, since a signed receipt links to its packet as a `custody_scan` of kind `receipt`. Client-portal visibility of "items we hold for you" stays blocked on a portal grant model that does not exist, with the granularity question deliberately still open ahead of it.
