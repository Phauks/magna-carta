**The conflicts engine surfaces and explains; it does not score. Evidence tiers replace the weighted scorer, strictness becomes a recall knob, sources are a typed registry with a firm-set floor, and firm personnel are a source rather than a node type.**

**Context:** ADR-0013 accepted the conflicts requirements — firm-wide scope, configurable strictness and depth, an immutable log — and slice 1·3 built the recall side into the Directory (ADR-0085): scheme-keyed phonetic codes on every name, an FTS5 trigram substrate, blind-index identifiers, the conflict-relevant edge types, and `findDuplicateCandidates`, which already runs the three blocking channels and resolves merged-away aliases to their surviving master. The research doc ([`docs/research/conflicts-matching-engine.md`](../research/conflicts-matching-engine.md) §D) proposed completing this with a Fellegi-Sunter weighted scorer, severity bands, and strictness expressed as a score threshold. ADR-0085 reserved the firm-personnel-as-Directory-entities question for this design cycle. Reviewer adjudication UX is deferred by the owner.

**Decision:** (1) **No scorer.** Every candidate the blocking channels surface is shown; nothing is filtered, ranked, or suppressed by a numeric score. A threshold is a mechanism for converting missed conflicts into invisible ones, which contradicts the recall bias the research doc itself establishes — a missed conflict is a malpractice event, a false positive costs a reviewer seconds. A conflicts check surfaces candidates for an attorney or clerk to adjudicate; it does not decide, so its ordering does not require probability. Nothing accepted is contradicted: ADR-0013 specifies no score, and Fellegi-Sunter appears only in the research doc — which declares itself input, not design — and in the white paper's open _Needs development_ callout. (2) **Evidence tiers.** Each hit ranks by what kind of thing matched: exact identifier (blind-index equality) > exact normalized name > phonetic code > trigram overlap only; orthogonally, each hit records how the entity was reached — directly, or through a graph path with the edges traversed. Tiers order and group the reviewer's queue; they never remove a hit. Volume is handled by grouping and counts, with weak tiers expandable, not by suppression. (3) **Strictness is a recall knob**, not a threshold: *balanced* runs primary Double Metaphone + trigram + identifiers over current names; *over-inclusive* adds the secondary Double Metaphone code, alias/maiden/former-name rows, and a lower trigram bar. It dials recall up, never precision by exclusion — the white paper's "over-inclusive (fuzzy/phonetic/alias) available to enable," read literally. Beider-Morse is **not** in this slice; the over-inclusive band ships without its cross-origin surname handling, and the seam is already paid for by ADR-0085's open `scheme` CHECK (additive rows + a backfill sweep, no migration). (4) **A typed source registry** replaces a flat depth number. **Party** (Directory entities + `matter_party` role-links firm-wide, all Locations) matches by similarity and is always on. **Graph** (`directory_edge`: principal-of-org, spouse/family, corporate parent/subsidiary/successor) traverses by identity to a configured depth, then matches each reached entity by similarity. **Imputation** matches by **identity only, never name similarity**. **Content** (email participants; email and document text) is registered but unavailable. (5) **A firm-set floor.** User-selectable scope otherwise manufactures a clean check — narrow the scope, get a clean result, and the log faithfully records it. Firm policy sets a mandatory minimum; a per-run selection may only *add* sources above the floor, never subtract; the Party class is always in the floor; and the scope in force is written verbatim into every log entry, so an auditor knows exactly what was and was not searched. A source that cannot run records as `unavailable`, never as `searched and clean`. (6) **Firm personnel are a source, not a node type** — resolving the ADR-0085 reservation in favour of the 1·3 position. `member` carries a flat `display_name` with no `directory_name` row, no phonetic code, and no FTS entry, so personnel are structurally invisible to the similarity channels, and that is correct: a partner sharing a surname with a client is not a conflict, and a firm named "Smith Law" would otherwise hit every client named Smith on every check, forever. The real question — is one of our own people a party to, or related to, a party in, some matter — is an identity join: `member.user_id` → `directory_person.member_id` → `matter_party` / `directory_edge`. A lawyer who is genuinely a party is already an ordinary Directory entity with an optional `member_id` link (ADR-0085) and is matched by the Party class like anyone else. Prior-firm lateral history is a **registered source with no table**: with capture UX deferred nothing would populate it, and registering it makes the log record "no data" rather than silently omit a Rule 1.10 dimension. (7) **The immutable log is three append-only per-firm tables.** `conflicts_check` (who, when, trigger, subject, scope/strictness/depth in force, inputs searched — identifiers **by reference**, never plaintext), `conflicts_hit` (resolved entity, evidence tier, matched fields, connecting graph path, surfacing source class), `conflicts_disposition` (`cleared` / `waiver_obtained` / `wall_erected` / `representation_declined`, with reviewer identity and rationale). Dispositions are a **table, not a column on the hit**: a column is updated in place and destroys the prior value, but a reviewer who clears a hit and later reverses must leave both acts visible, so a reversal is a second row and the current disposition is the latest row. No `UPDATE` and no `DELETE` ever touches the three tables — enforced by SQLite triggers and a fitness probe. `wall_erected` is a recordable outcome, not a wall implementation; ethical-wall enforcement stays deferred (white paper). (8) **Scoring runs in TypeScript**, in a new `@magna-carta/conflicts` package following the Directory pattern. Perf is not a reason for Rust: blocking bounds the candidate set to hundreds, and comparison over a few name fields is sub-millisecond. The only genuine Rust candidate is Beider-Morse, which is a *write-time* concern — phonetic codes are precomputed on every `directory_name` insert — and so belongs to a separate decision with its own backfill, not to the scorer's home. (9) **API routes only; `trigger` is a required request parameter.** Nothing auto-fires: auto-firing on party-add would couple the Directory write path to the engine and need a queue consumer, and the periodic re-scan has nobody to notify while reviewer UX is deferred. Both are additive, not retrofits — the log already records which trigger fired.

**Considered & rejected:** a Fellegi-Sunter trained scorer (needs labelled matches no firm has on day one, and a probability is harder to defend to a bar auditor than the evidence behind it); a transparent weighted score with severity bands (still a threshold, and the weights become the thing under cross-examination — "we computed 0.73 against a 0.70 cutoff" invites the question of who chose 0.70); free per-run scope selection with no floor (the log stays honest but a narrowed scope produces a clean check indistinguishable at a glance from a thorough one); modeling firm personnel as Directory entities for imputation (conflates the assigned-to and about-them axes and creates a third identity to reconcile — ADR-0085's original reasoning, which the identity-join source vindicates); name-similarity matching against personnel (guaranteed noise on every run, since personnel are in every check's imputation scope, and a surname collision is not a conflict); a `disposition` column on `conflicts_hit` (in-place update destroys the audit trail the log exists to provide); building the prior-firm lateral table now (ships empty either way with capture deferred); a Rust/WASM scorer core (no measured hot path — golden rule 10 — and it buys nothing toward Beider-Morse, which is write-time); Tantivy for candidate generation (D1-native is shipped and sufficient; Tantivy stays the reserved scale path, measured against the load harness).

**Consequences:** The conflicts engine consumes the shipped Directory corpus without changing its write path. Open research decisions H.3 (scoring model) and H.5 (D1 vs Tantivy) are **closed** — H.3 by deleting the scorer, H.5 by the shipped D1-native `findDuplicateCandidates`. H.4 (graph depth and edge types) is fixed at one hop plus transitive corporate-family closure. H.6 (log schema and append-only enforcement) is fixed by the three tables. H.7 (reviewer UX) remains deferred. The "strictness is a strict superset" invariant becomes a fitness test and is the structural guarantee that no strictness setting can hide a hit. When the Content class lands, email *participants* are structured (exact `directory_email` equality) and distinct from email *bodies* and document text (unstructured mentions, capped below tier 2, never auto-clearing), and the participant channel must exclude the firm's own domains or every internal email matches.

**Diverges from** [`docs/research/conflicts-matching-engine.md`](../research/conflicts-matching-engine.md) §D (the Fellegi-Sunter scorer) and the white paper's _Needs development_ phrasing of "strictness/score thresholds" — recorded here rather than made silently; the research doc's other findings (recall bias, mandatory match explanation, graph-aware matching, two-channel blocking, D1 feasibility, and the log's required contents) are adopted in full. **Serves ADR-0013**; **resolves the firm-personnel reservation in ADR-0085**; builds on **ADR-0084** (protected fields, blind index), **ADR-0086** (merge keeps merged-away aliases in the conflicts corpus).
