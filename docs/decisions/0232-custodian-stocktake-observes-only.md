**A stocktake observes and reports. Corrective movements are proposals a person records, never writes the count makes.**

**Context:** The implementation plan for slice 4·2·4 asks for a scan of a location, a reconciliation of expected against actual contents, and a discrepancy report — with the constraint that "every discrepancy resolution is a movement, never an edit". That sentence rules out one failure (silently editing `current_location_id`) but leaves a second one open: a count that *appends* the movements itself. It would satisfy the letter of the constraint and still be wrong, and it is the shape a stocktake naturally grows into once the reconciliation knows exactly which movement would fix each line.

**Decision:** (1) **Nothing on the stocktake path writes the register.** The two new tables are the complete write surface: `custody_stocktake` and `custody_stocktake_sighting`. No movement is appended, no packet or item status or location changes, and no review date rolls, when a count is opened, recorded against, or closed. Three reasons, in order of weight. A count's entire value is the difference between what the register claims and what is physically on the shelf; a count that wrote the register into agreement with itself would erase that difference at the moment of discovering it. A movement appended by the count would be an assertion in an append-only ledger that no person made, in a table whose only claim is that nobody can put words in a member's mouth. And the resolution of a discrepancy is genuinely a judgement — an original that is not where it should be may have been moved, mis-labelled, taken by a partner who forgot to record it, or lost — which a count cannot make and a person must.

(2) **The reconciliation carries proposals.** Each misplaced line names `moved` to the location it was actually seen at; each unexpected line names `found`. They are fields on a report and there is no route that applies them: acting on one means posting the movement to `POST /custody/packets/{packetId}/movements`, the same path every other state change in the subsystem goes through. A missing line deliberately proposes nothing — the honest answer to an empty shelf is an investigation, and a register that suggested recording a loss the moment a shelf came up empty would be teaching firms to close the record on the exact failure that most needs one open. An unregistered object likewise proposes nothing: what to do with an unidentified item in a safe is a decision about a client's property.

(3) **The boundary is a fitness function, not a convention.** `packages/custodian/test/purity.test.ts` scans the stocktake sources (comments stripped) for a register mutation, a status or location setter, a ledger insert, and any destruction path, and fails on all four. The boundary has to hold against a call site nobody has written yet, which a behavioural test cannot cover.

(4) **Sightings are append-only at the database.** A sighting is a named person's assertion that they physically saw a specific original on a specific shelf on a specific day, which is the whole evidentiary value of a count; one that could be edited afterwards would say whatever the last writer wanted. It therefore carries the same three triggers `custody_movement` and `trust_movement` carry — `RAISE(ABORT)` on update and delete, plus the `BEFORE INSERT` guard closing the INSERT-OR-REPLACE bypass that SQLite leaves open with `recursive_triggers` off — and a fourth refusing an observation recorded against a count that has already closed, since that would change a reconciliation somebody may already have acted on. A mistake is corrected by recording the later, truer observation, and the reconciliation takes the last one; both stay on the record.

(5) **Findings are frozen on close; an unfinished count is abandoned rather than closed.** The reconciliation's lines are recomputed on every read against a register that keeps moving, so they answer "what is different now". The counts frozen at close answer "what did the count find", which is the number a firm reports and must not have drift under it — the two disagreeing later is not a fault, it is the register having been put right. A count begun and never finished freezes nothing: the reconciliation of a half-done walk reads as a safe full of missing originals, which would be the most alarming and most wrong thing this subsystem could tell a firm, so `abandoned` is a first-class outcome rather than a tidy-up.

(6) **No new movement kind, and no new permission.** Adding a `stocktake` movement kind would mean rebuilding `custody_movement` to widen its CHECK constraint and re-proving three triggers against the rebuilt table — for a row the count has no business writing anyway. `custody:read` and `custody:manage` already express the two things a stocktake needs, and the design's recommendation against minting further custody permissions stands.

(7) **Concurrent counts are allowed.** A two-office firm counting two safes on the same Saturday is the ordinary case, and every observation names its stocktake explicitly, so there is no ambiguity a uniqueness constraint would resolve. Two counts whose scopes overlap each report honestly on what their own counters saw.

**Consequences:** The register is never modified by a count, so a stocktake is safe to run against live data and safe to abandon. A firm that wants the reconciliation applied does so one movement at a time, with a person's name on each — which is more work, and is the point. Destruction and retention remain entirely untouched and owner-gated (flags C4 to C11): a shelf coming up empty is an investigation, not a reason to end the firm's custody of a client's irreplaceable original. The remaining 4·2·4 work — the will-on-death statutory deadline engine and its jurisdiction catalog, and the succession/transfer manifest — is unaffected and stays gated on owner decision C8 and on a catalog verified against primary text.
