**Conversion of a firm's data into the platform is consensual and guided: detection and reporting are always on, the rewrite itself is opt-in, and the product supplies input, guidance, and feedback at the decision.**

**Context:** _Owner decision, 2026-08-04._ Every subsystem that takes a firm's existing material and makes it usable here faces the same question, and each was about to answer it separately. Template registration meets Word `MERGEFIELD` constructs. A migration from another practice-management system meets a foreign matter-numbering scheme, a foreign trust ledger, a contact model that does not decompose the way the Directory does. File conversion meets formats that lose fidelity in one direction. Encoding normalization meets money stored as a decimal string and dates stored in a local format. In each, the tempting default is to do the sensible thing silently at import time, because it is fewer clicks and the outcome is usually right. That default is wrong here for a specific reason: this is a law firm's own record, most of it privileged, some of it the evidentiary version of something. A silent rewrite means the stored bytes stop matching what the firm supplied with no moment at which anyone agreed, and when the transformation is subtly wrong the firm discovers it downstream — in an assembled document, a trust reconciliation, or a production — with no record of what was changed or why.

**Decision:** Wherever a conversion of user data takes place or may take place, three rules hold.

**Detection and reporting are always on.** The platform inspects what it was given and tells the firm what it found — what constructs, formats, or structures are present, where, how many, and what each would become — independent of whether anything is done about it. A firm always learns what is in its own material. Reporting is not gated on choosing to convert, because the report is often the reason to choose.

**Conversion is opt-in, never silent.** A rewrite of the firm's data runs on an explicit request. Uploading, importing, or registering is not itself consent to be rewritten. Where a conversion genuinely cannot be declined — a storage encoding the platform requires to hold the value at all — it is stated plainly at the point of import rather than performed quietly, and what it does is reversible or recorded.

**The product offers input, guidance, and feedback.** At the decision the firm sees what will change, what it will become, what cannot be carried across, and what the platform is unsure about; after the run it sees what actually happened. Where the platform has a recommendation it makes it, as a recommendation.

**Confirmations accumulate.** Where a conversion asks the same question repeatedly across a set — the same field name in forty templates, the same practice-area code in eleven thousand matters — the firm's answers are recorded as reusable per-firm mappings, so a bulk migration converges on one pass of human confirmation rather than one per item. A stored mapping is visible and editable, never an invisible cache: a confirmed mapping that was wrong stays wrong until someone can find and correct it.

This binds the product, not one importer. The document-automation template model instantiates it first — `MERGEFIELD` constructs are always detected and reported, conversion to the brace form runs only on request, and confirmed name-to-key mappings accumulate in a per-firm field-mapping library — and the practice-management migration path, the file-conversion utility, and any future importer inherit the same three rules rather than re-deciding them.

**Considered & rejected:** **Convert silently and well** — fewer steps, and the outcome is usually right; rejected because "usually" is doing the work, the failure is discovered downstream rather than at the decision, and the firm has no record of what was changed. **Convert silently but log it** — an audit row is a record for an investigation, not a decision by the firm; it answers "what happened" long after the moment when "should this happen" could have been asked. **Ask every time, with no accumulation** — honest and unusable at migration scale, where the same question recurs thousands of times; the confirmation-library rule exists to keep consent meaningful without making it a tax proportional to the size of the firm's history. **Leave the posture to each subsystem** — the state we were in, and it produces a product where importing a template asks and importing ten thousand matters does not, for no reason a firm could predict. **A blanket "never transform user data"** — overshoots: the platform genuinely cannot store a foreign format unchanged in every case, and refusing all conversion would make migration a manual re-entry exercise. The rule is consent and visibility, not abstinence.

**Consequences:** Every importer now carries a detection-and-report stage that is real work even when nothing is converted, and a decision surface with guidance rather than a progress bar. Bulk migrations gain a confirmation pass that a silent importer would not have, offset by the mapping library, which is the mechanism that keeps that pass bounded. Data held in a partially-converted state becomes normal rather than exceptional, so downstream consumers must handle material that was reported-but-not-converted — a real cost, and the price of not rewriting a firm's records without asking. In exchange, no firm discovers at a filing deadline that the platform quietly changed something it supplied, and every conversion has a moment, a record, and a person attached to it.
