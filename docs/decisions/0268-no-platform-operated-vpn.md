**No platform-operated VPN — the confidentiality concentration is disqualifying, and TLS already solves the stated problem.**

**Context:** The owner asked how we could implement VPN services for our users, and then named the goal directly: _"I want to assist firms in accessing their files securely over insecure wifi."_ The phrase covers at least three unrelated products — a network control gating access to us, a general-purpose VPN we operate for firms, and a posture of coexisting with the VPNs firms already buy — and the owner confirmed he meant the first two. This decision settles the second. The first is settled separately in [`network-access-controls-firm-supplied`](network-access-controls-firm-supplied.md); the third falls out of it as hygiene.

**Decision:** (1) **The stated goal is already met, and this must be said before anything is costed.** A connection to `app.magnacarta.esq` from a hotel lobby is TLS 1.3 to Cloudflare's edge, and `.esq` is on the HSTS preload list **as an entire zone** — Google Registry preloads its top-level domains, so every second-level domain inherits forced HTTPS in every major browser without registering individually, verified live against the `hstspreload.org` API during the assessment. There is no plaintext first request to strip, downgrade attacks of the `sslstrip` class do not apply, and a captive portal cannot serve a plaintext impostor of our login page at our own hostname. The FTC's current consumer guidance states the general case in as many words — _"things have changed... Because of the widespread use of encryption, connecting through a public Wi-Fi network is usually safe"_ — and does not mention VPNs at all. A tunnel would encrypt an encrypted channel.

(2) **What a tunnel genuinely adds is narrow, and each piece is either already solved or better solved elsewhere.** Hostname metadata is largely closed by default-on DNS-over-HTTPS and by Encrypted Client Hello (RFC 9849, March 2026) across Cloudflare-fronted sites, and a VPN's remaining contribution is to substitute our identity as the observed destination for Cloudflare's, which is not an improvement. A captive portal that has persuaded the user to install a rogue root CA is the one attack that defeats TLS, and a VPN does not reliably stop it either — the device is compromised, and the same administrative access that installed the CA can inspect, block, or race the tunnel; the controls that survive are certificate pinning (impossible in the browser since HPKP's removal, available in the Tauri shell) and origin-bound passkeys. Protecting a device's stray non-TLS traffic is a real benefit from the firm's own VPN and has nothing to do with reaching us. A stable egress IP for allowlisting is real and is the firm's to buy, on Cloudflare's Enterprise-only dedicated egress IPs.

(3) **The confidentiality objection is the reason, and it is not a cost-benefit argument that a motivated owner can overturn.** Gateway's logging reference is the specification of the exposure: DNS logs carry query name, type, resolved IPs and the identity block (email, user ID, device name, device ID, last authenticated); network logs carry source and destination IP and port, SNI and the same identity; HTTP logs carry host, **full URL**, method, status, user agent and the same identity. That is a per-lawyer, per-device, timestamped record of every hostname and URL a firm's staff reach. It is categorically unlike the matter files we hold, which are the documents a firm chose to give its vendor about representations already undertaken. Whole-internet metadata reveals **matters we were never told about** — an unfiled complaint inferable from repeated visits to a court's e-filing system, an undisclosed representation inferable from sustained traffic to a company's investor pages, a merger visible in due-diligence lookups weeks before anything is public — the firm's relationship to adversaries, and the personal life conducted on a work laptop. It is inferential in a way document custody is not: holding a file tells you what the firm wrote, holding six months of browsing tells you what the firm is thinking about and when it started.

(4) **The legal exposure cuts in the direction that specifically harms the client, which is the sharpest form of the argument.** Operating a VPN makes us a provider of an electronic communication service under ECPA, and the Stored Communications Act draws a hard line between content and non-content. Connection logs, timestamps, IP addresses, session records and subscriber information are **non-content**, obtainable on a **subpoena** rather than the warrant content requires. Today a firm's browsing metadata sits with the firm: an adversary must go to the firm, and the firm — the party who owns the privilege, knows which matters are implicated, and has both standing and a professional obligation to fight — moves to quash. If we operate the VPN, the same material sits with a third party who holds no privilege in it and can be reached in litigation the firm may not be party to. We would become the cheapest available route to a law firm's confidences, and we would have built that route deliberately. Whether the firm learns in time to object would come to rest on our diligence rather than on their standing.

(5) **It is the one dataset our confidentiality architecture cannot cover.** Every rule we hold ourselves to — redact by default, never log privileged content, no third-party error SaaS, application-level encryption narrowly scoped to an audited role-gated chokepoint, per-firm crypto-shred — exists to **minimise what we can see**. A VPN maximises it, in a form the machinery does not reach: `protected()` encrypts declared fields written through the firm-scoped data-access layer, but routing telemetry is generated continuously by the act of forwarding traffic, not written as domain rows, and destroying a firm's wrapped key would not render it unreadable. We would be creating the only corpus in the company outside the encryption chokepoint. The breach surface is correspondingly worse than a matter-data breach, because it discloses unfiled matters, undisclosed clients and third parties who never had any relationship with us — and the firm cannot scope the damage, having never had an inventory of what they handed over.

(6) **Reselling is separately and independently barred.** Cloudflare's Zero Trust service-specific terms state that a customer shall not resell Cloudflare Zero Trust to third parties in an ASP, managed-security, outsourcing, time-sharing or service-bureau relationship absent written permission, with violation a material breach subject to immediate account termination — of the account this entire platform runs in. The MSP path exists (PowerUP: Resell, Manage, Distribute, Consult; Cloudflare's own commentary describes MSPs embedding rather than reselling, on not-for-resale licensing with consumption tooling it is _"still building"_), but it is a partnership negotiation, a contract, a billing integration and a compliance obligation undertaken to deliver a benefit measured at close to zero. Building our own is worse: Workers are HTTP-inbound only and cannot terminate a tunnel, so a concentrator means infrastructure outside Cloudflare, abandoning the single-provider simplicity that is a stated platform advantage — and then bandwidth for every byte of a firm's internet use, abuse handling for a shared egress IP, IP-reputation management (the day our egress lands on a blocklist is the day a lawyer cannot reach a court's filing system), and 24/7 network on-call, because a VPN that is down is not a degraded feature but a firm that cannot work.

(7) **A desktop-only VPN client is refused on independent grounds.** ADR-0023 permits the Tauri shell only optional local accelerations with a complete web fallback; a VPN available on desktop and not on web is a platform-exclusive feature. Memory safety is **not** among the reasons to refuse — Cloudflare's WARP datapath is BoringTun and MASQUE, both Rust, and `boringtun` is a published crate, so golden rule 14 would be satisfiable. The reasons are (3) through (6), and they are sufficient without it.

**Considered & rejected:** **reselling Cloudflare Zero Trust** (forbidden by the service-specific terms, with the account at risk); **the MSP partner path** (a corporate workstream for near-zero security benefit); **building our own concentrator** (a network product, an on-call rotation, an abuse desk and an IP-reputation problem); **a Tauri-only VPN** (fails ADR-0023 parity independently); **a "tunnel to the platform"** (a category error — we have no private network; Cloudflare's own documentation is explicit that protecting a public hostname needs neither a tunnel nor a client install); **requiring the WARP client for staff access** (forces an agent rollout on every firm for a benefit TLS already provides, and breaks the bring-your-own-device reality of small firms); **operating a VPN only for portal recipients** (incoherent — you cannot ask an opposing party's expert to install a client to read one shared folder; the per-grant assurance model is the designed answer); and **marketing any of this as "bank-level security"** (selling a lawyer a tunnel against a threat TLS already handles, while the real risk is the phishing email in their inbox, consumes the attention and budget that passkeys and patched laptops need and leaves the firm's actual posture worse). Reinforces **ADR-0003** (per-firm isolation), **ADR-0017**/**ADR-0038** (redact by default, never log privileged content, no third-party error SaaS), **ADR-0046** (encryption scoped narrowly to an audited chokepoint, per-firm crypto-shred) and **ADR-0023** (web/desktop parity).
