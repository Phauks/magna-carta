**A one-time sign-in link proves control of a mailbox at one instant, is stamped at that assurance, and can never be more than that.**

**Context:** The one-time link is the method most often shipped as a convenience and most often ending up as the account's real credential, because it is the path everything else falls back to. It has three genuine jobs here — activating an invited member, letting an occasional portal visitor in without a standing credential, and rescuing somebody who has mislaid a password — and one job it must never acquire, which is being the thing that authorises an irreversible act. Separately, the library's defaults for this plugin are written for a self-serve consumer product and two of them are actively wrong for an invitation-only platform.

**Decision:** A session established by a link or an emailed code **alone** is stamped `mailbox`, the lowest level of the assurance lattice, and therefore cannot satisfy any `stepUp` gate — enforced by the ordering, not by a maintained list of endpoints. Two library defaults are inverted and **pinned by test**. **`disableSignUp: true`** on both `magicLink` and `emailOTP`: the default auto-registers an unknown address, which would be a second unguarded door into the `user` table beside a signup route that always refuses. And the plugin's documented behaviour on an existing **unverified** account — removing its password and revoking its sessions, on the reasoning that mailbox ownership is the source of truth — lands on precisely the state an invited member occupies between redeeming an invitation and setting a password, so a Miniflare test asserts a verify against an unverified account leaves the `account` row untouched. **Account linking is set explicit** for the same class of reason: `disableImplicitLinking: true`, `trustedProviders` empty, `allowDifferentEmails` off, because the library links accounts sharing an address by default and an identity provider asserting an address it does not control would otherwise inherit a password account. Requesting a link **answers the same body in the same time** for an unknown address, a known address, and an address on a federated domain; the federated case sends mail to the **address** saying the firm signs in through its provider, and tells the caller nothing. Verifying a spent, expired or unknown token is one refusal. A firm may disable links for its own members (owner flag **F3**, narrowing only).

**Considered & rejected:** _letting a link satisfy step-up when it is very recent_ (recency is not strength; the whole point of the lattice is that the two are different axes); _a longer-lived link so people do not have to re-request_ (a link is a bearer credential sitting in a mailbox, and its life is its exposure); _keeping the library defaults and documenting the risk_ (both defaults produce a state change an anonymous caller can trigger, and a documented risk on an auth surface is an unfixed defect); _hashing left at the default_ (`storeToken: "hashed"` and `storeOTP: "hashed"` mean a control-plane read hands nobody a working link); _refusing links entirely and shipping only codes_ (the code is the better rescue path and the link is the better activation path — the invitation flow already points at a mailbox, and a typeable code closes the shipped `reset-code` gap where a link cannot be opened on the device asking).

**Consequences:** Three entries leave `unbuilt-capabilities.ts` — `one-time-link`, `reset-code`, and (with the `captcha` plugin over the same surface, plus our own verification middleware on the `/api/v1` joining routes) `human-verification`. `emailOTP` also becomes the emailed second-factor channel `code-channels.ts` already describes, which retires `second-factor-choice` once more than one channel is enrollable. Whether a link may establish a session on its own at all is owner flag **F2**, recommended yes at `mailbox` assurance only. Builds on **ADR-0073** (the integration shape), **ADR-0280** (the neutral joining posture this inherits), **ADR-0074** (the transactional email path these ride).
