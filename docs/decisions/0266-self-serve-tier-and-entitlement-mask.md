**Self-serve tenancy, with the free tier as a mask over the one permission registry — not a second authorization system beside it.**

**Context:** Firm creation is a secret-gated platform endpoint on a fleet of about one, and the code says so in three places — the `firm.name` column, the provisioner's resume-identity note, and the absence of any bot control on the creation path all carry the same comment: platform-admin-controlled input, revisit if self-serve signup ever exists. The owner's direction opens that door: registration through the sign-in surface, a small free tier to showcase the product with limited file usage and certain functionality locked, and escalation to a full tier after an account exists. Three questions had to be answered before anything could be built: whether Cloudflare's account-level limits permit an unbounded population of tenants at all, how a free tier's limits get enforced without standing up a parallel "may this firm do X" check that drifts from the permission registry enforced across six inventories, and what happens to an abandoned trial workspace that may hold privileged material.

**Decision:** (1) **Cloudflare does not bound this; we do.** The per-firm database is the firmId-addressed `FirmDatabase` Durable Object, so D1's databases-per-account limit is irrelevant — only the control plane is D1. R2 permits a million buckets per account, raisable. Durable Object instances are unlimited. The three real numbers are the R2 REST budget of 1,200 requests per five minutes account-wide (which bounds provisioning *burst*, and is why provisioning is a rate-limited queue consumer), the AI Search cap of 5,000 instances per account against an allocation of one per firm (which is a reason to withhold that one feature, recorded as a capacity reservation rather than a cost preference), and — the one that actually binds — the platform's own serial fleet-wide per-firm cron loop, which is correct for a curated fleet and becomes the scaling constraint the moment the fleet is a population of strangers.

(2) **Registration is two stages, and the split is the primary anti-abuse control.** Sign-up writes one `firm` row in `pending_verification` and one unverified owner — no bucket, no key, no Durable Object touch, no notification configuration. Only the owner's verification click enqueues provisioning, and the queue consumer calls the existing `provisionFirm` orchestration unchanged: it is already step-guarded and resumable, which is exactly the contract at-least-once delivery needs. A spam run therefore costs two control-plane rows per attempt rather than an account resource, and `status = 'active'` keeps meaning "a firm somebody confirmed", which is what the cron enumerates.

(3) **The resume identity moves from the firm's name to its id.** A globally-unique name column was the right call for administrator-supplied input; on a public endpoint it is three separate defects — many real firms share a name, probing for a name is a tenant-enumeration oracle of exactly the kind the trusted-insider 403/404 split exists to prevent everywhere else, and registering a well-known firm's name costs one email address. The human-facing name moves to an additive `display_name` column and the `UNIQUE` constraint stays satisfied rather than dropped, because dropping it in SQLite is a destructive table rebuild against a table holding data.

(4) **The free tier is a mask, and this is the load-bearing decision.** The entitlement catalog is code beside the permission registry and it names *the same permission strings routes already declare* — no parallel vocabulary. The withholding happens inside the chokepoint's existing `loadPermissions` port, which returns the member's effective set intersected with what the firm's tier permits; no new step, no new port, no route metadata, no handler discipline. The single `permissions.has(required)` check stays exactly where it is and gains one branch: a key withheld by entitlement renders 402 `upgrade-required` rather than 403 `permission-denied`, because telling a firm Owner "your role does not grant this" when they hold every role is both a support ticket and a lie. A fitness test asserts every registry key is explicitly classified in every tier, so the catalog cannot drift behind the registry. It is not a second authorization system because it has no vocabulary of its own, no enforcement point of its own, and no route declaration of its own — it is an intersection applied to the input of the one check that already exists. The reserved `RouteMeta.planTier` field is the wrong seam to fill and should be retired: it would put a tier declaration on hundreds of routes in a second vocabulary maintained by per-route discipline, where two routes reaching one capability could classify it differently. Quotas are the second axis and are deliberately not forced into the mask — they are numbers, checked at the point of consumption against the storage rollup that already exists, and an over-quota firm is refused new writes and never deleted down to fit.

(5) **Two reasons to withhold, unlocking differently.** Withheld *for integrity* (an unverified firm should not do this, and verification rather than payment unlocks it): trust accounting in full, invoice finalization/application/crediting, the platform e-signature seal, the client portal, platform-branded mail capture, postage. Withheld *for cost* (payment unlocks it): the discovery load act, Magnus, AI Search, the Container-backed tools. Trust is the sharp case and the answer is yes, withhold it — not merely because a self-serve firm is unverified, but because the jurisdiction corpus already fails closed on unreviewed rules, because a trust ledger is not a demo anyone benefits from, and because "never reap a firm holding client money" is only a cheap absolute rule if a free tenant cannot hold any. Everything else stays: matters, directory, conflicts, time, costs, documents, automation, custodian, calendar, notifications, upload within quota, keyword search. The lock list is short on purpose.

(6) **Reaping follows the custodian precedent rather than inventing one.** Dormancy is measured in human activity, never in emptiness. The ladder warns at 60 and 90 days, drops the firm out of the fleet-wide cron at 120 — recovering the recurring cost six months before anything is destroyed, so the platform is never under cost pressure to shorten the clock — warns again at 150, suspends to export-only at 180, builds an export bundle at 210, and only then reaches destruction. Destruction is crypto-shred plus bucket deletion plus Durable Object deletion, with the control-plane row and its whole audit chain retained forever. A never-activated tenant (no session, no byte, empty audit log) is destroyed automatically because the absence of client data is structural; anything ever used goes through a destruction request a named platform operator approves. Any Custodian or legal hold, and any non-zero trust balance, refuses auto-proposal outright. An undelivered final warning stops the clock rather than counting as notice.

(7) **A lapsed paid firm is not a free firm.** Thirty days of full-function grace — a failed card must not lock a lawyer out of a hearing-date calendar — then read-only with export, indefinitely, enforced by the same mask and no second mechanism. Reads of trust and billing survive: a firm is never locked out of *seeing* its own client-money records whatever it owes. Downgrading to free is refused, because applying free quotas to a firm holding forty gigabytes means either destroying data to fit or leaving it permanently over-quota; over-quota is tolerated indefinitely on a lapsed firm. Upgrading and restoring both write exactly one column and move no data — the free tier is a mask over a fully-formed firm, not a lesser kind of tenant, and recording that is what prevents a future "shared workspace for free tenants" proposal.

(8) **Verification is a second axis, not part of the tier.** Paying requires nothing but a working payment method; the integrity-withheld capabilities require a check. So `verification_status` sits beside `entitlement_tier` and the mask is the intersection, making both *paid but unverified* and *free but verified* expressible — the second being exactly the firm worth wanting. The honest first version is domain control plus an attested bar number and jurisdiction, recorded, spot-checked by a human and revocable, and never described in a user-facing string as automatically verified: there is no national registry to call, and claiming a check the platform did not perform is worse than performing none.

**Considered & rejected:** **populating `RouteMeta.planTier`** (a reserved seam, and the wrong one — per-route tier declarations in a second vocabulary are the drift disaster this decision exists to avoid); **a per-firm `setting` row for the tier** (entitlement is a platform fact about a firm, and a firm setting its own tier is the whole problem); **deriving entitlement by replaying payment events** (one lost message yields a wrong answer that persists — the integration must supply an authoritative current answer plus a paid-through instant and a reconciliation read); **downgrading a lapsed paid firm to free** (forces a choice between destroying data and permanent silent over-quota); **blocking disposable email domains, SMS verification, VPN-range blocking, and a card requirement on the free tier** (the first three are theatre against stale lists and real users; the fourth would work and would also end the showcase, so it is a product decision and not recommended here); **automatic destruction on a timer alone** (refused by the same reasoning the custodian design refused retention periods — the seams exist and the numbers do not). Extends **ADR-0003** (tenancy), **ADR-0067** (the one chokepoint) and **ADR-0068** (trusted-insider access control — the mask reuses its denial vocabulary and adds one branch); reuses **ADR-0084** (crypto-shred as the destruction mechanism), **ADR-0049** (destruction is policy-driven, hold-blocked and audited) and **ADR-0078** (the exact per-firm storage rollup is the quota measurement, already built). Depends on Turnstile actually being stood up, which is currently an unchecked item in the operations runbook rather than shipped code.
