**Money & currency representation — integer minor units at two fixed scales (amounts 2dp / rates 4dp), ISO-4217 currency-tagged USD-only (no FX), arithmetic via Dinero.js v2 (`Dinero<bigint>`) behind a thin in-house `money` seam, rounding named per boundary (half-up tax/invoice, banker's GL, largest-remainder splits); floats forbidden.** **Context:** money binds the ledger, trust, time/rates, billing, and payments and is the most painful convention to migrate after rows exist; a §9 member queued alongside the time model (ADR-0055). The crown-jewel requirements are exactness (trust three-way reconciliation must reconcile to the cent), sub-cent precision where calculations need it (rate×quantity, tax, contingency splits, mileage at $0.655/mi), and a representation that survives a future move of ledger math to Rust→WASM (ADR-0024/0039). **Decision:** (1) **Two storage scales, both integer minor units in D1:** amounts (balances, ledger postings, invoice-line totals, payments, trust entries) at **2dp/cents**; rates and unit prices at **4dp**, the extra precision kept so products don't lose money before rounding — the established billing-system pattern (more precision in calculation, round late, at the transaction boundary). (2) **ISO-4217 currency on every value** (always USD now; ledger accounts single-currency, postings inherit), the field present so multi-currency is a later evolution but **no FX machinery is built** ("design for the superset, ship the subset"). (3) **One arithmetic engine, not two systems** — **Dinero.js v2** (now stable, v2.0.2 Mar 2026; widely adopted; `Dinero<bigint>` gives float-free, ceiling-free amounts, `transformScale` across the 2dp/4dp split, and largest-remainder `allocate`) **wrapped by a thin in-house `money` module** owning the branded `Money`/`Rate`/ratio types, the scale convention, currency tagging, the formatting layer (`Intl.NumberFormat`, explicit currency — mirroring the ADR-0055 time formatter), and DAL enforcement. The wrapper is the swappable seam (same ports posture as ADR-0032/ADR-0047), so the library can later yield to an in-house or Rust→WASM core without touching a stored row — because the **representation** (integer minor units), not the library, is the expensive-to-migrate part. (4) **Percentages/ratios are a dimensionless decimal, never `Money`.** (5) **Rounding named per boundary, never one global default** — `money` exposes intent-named helpers: **tax → round-half-up** (Streamlined Sales & Use Tax §324 statutory rule; Nevada is an SSUTA member), **invoice-line totals → half-up** (client expectation), **GL/financial-statement aggregation → banker's/half-even** (GAAP-accepted, removes half-up's cumulative upward bias), **splits/allocations → largest-remainder** (sums to the whole exactly — contingency client/fee/cost/lien, payment application). Raw rounding requires an explicit mode; rounding only at boundaries, never mid-calculation. **Enforced, not trusted:** `Money`/`Rate` are the only types the DAL accepts for money columns; a coherence fitness function (ADR-0051) bans float math on money and `REAL` money columns in D1, and flags rounding outside the named helpers; fixtures (ADR-0034) mint money only through these types. **Considered & rejected:** **float/decimal-string money** (precision loss — disqualified); **one scale everywhere (cents only)** (rounding rates to whole cents loses real money on volume and drifts reconciliation); **single 4dp scale everywhere** (stored balances at sub-cent disagree with penny-exact bank/trust statements); **build the arithmetic in-house from scratch** (reuse-first, ADR's golden rule #1 — and Dinero now meets every requirement incl. bigint + multi-scale + allocate, so the build isn't earned; the wrapper keeps the irreversible storage decision ours regardless); **full multi-currency now** (no current need — a Nevada firm bills USD; FX revaluation is real complexity for zero benefit); **one global rounding default** (the correct mode is context-specific and tax is legally fixed). **Consequences:** money is exact where it settles and precise where it calculates; the ledger sum-to-zero (ADR-0008) and no-negative-trust (ADR-0009) invariants stay penny-exact and reconciliation is correct by construction; cost is the Dinero dependency (behind a seam) and the discipline of the two-scale convention + named rounding. Reuses **ADR-0034** (fixtures), **ADR-0051** (fitness function), the ADR-0055 formatter pattern; binds **ADR-0008**/**ADR-0009**/**ADR-0032**/**ADR-0033** (ledger, trust, payments, time-rates) and LEDES export (ADR-0033). Implementation detail in the _Foundational data conventions_ spec. **Amendment (2026-06-08):** the `@magna-carta/money` implementation uses Dinero.js v2's **default number calculator** (`Dinero<number>`, JS safe-integer amounts), **not `Dinero<bigint>`**. Two findings drove this: (a) amounts never approach 2^53 ($90T at cents / $900B at a 4-dp rate) in legal practice management, so JS-number integer arithmetic is exact across our range; (b) Dinero 2.0.2's bigint build is broken for `multiply`, `transformScale` (all rounding), `allocate`, and `toDecimal` — it mixes `number` and `bigint` inside its own compiled `multiplyFn`. The number calculator runs the entire surface correctly. The branded-seam discipline, the two storage scales, and the named rounding helpers are unchanged; storage columns (`*_minor`/`*_rate_e4` `INTEGER`) are unchanged. The documented escape hatch — swap `src/dinero.ts` to a `decimal.js`-backed custom calculator via `createDinero({ calculator })` — is a one-file change behind the seam if amounts ever exceed 2^53 (verified working end-to-end on 2026-06-08).
