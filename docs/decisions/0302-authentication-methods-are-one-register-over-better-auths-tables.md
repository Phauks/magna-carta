**Every way a principal may prove who they are is one row in one register, and the register is a projection over the library's tables rather than a second store of credentials.**

**Context:** Better Auth spreads enrolment across four tables — `account` (passwords and federated links), `passkey`, `twoFactor` (the seed and the backup codes), and `ssoProvider` — with no single place to ask what a principal holds. That was tolerable with one method. With passkeys, one-time links, emailed codes, TOTP, recovery codes and firm identity providers it stops being tolerable, because three of the four questions the platform must answer are asked across the tables rather than inside one: **may** this account use a password (a policy question about a principal, not a fact in `account`); is this the **last** live method (a count over four tables, and the answer decides whether a removal is allowed); and what was **enrolled** in the ninety minutes after that sign-in (the takeover investigation, which needs one ordered list and gets four unordered ones).

**Decision:** A control-plane `authentication_method(id, principal_id, kind, status, label, assurance_ceiling, provenance, enrolled_at, last_used_at, revoked_at)` register, `kind` one of `password | passkey | totp | emailCode | oneTimeLink | recoveryCode | identityProvider`. It is a **projection**: credential material stays in the library's own tables, the register is maintained by `databaseHooks` on those models plus our writes, and a Miniflare reconciliation test asserts exactly one live register row per library credential and no orphans in either direction. `provenance` records what created the row — a self-service enrolment, an administered recovery, a domain-verified provider — because that is the first question asked after a takeover and the one `account` cannot answer. Which methods an account may use resolves **most-restrictive-wins** over three layers: a **platform floor** in code beside the permission registry (a portal client never holds a firm identity provider; the last live method cannot be removed; a recovery method exists before a second factor is the only thing past a password); **firm narrowing** through the existing declarative settings registry and its scoped resolver, which may require more and never less; and **enrolment**. The effective set is never published for an address that has not authenticated.

**Considered & rejected:** _querying the four library tables at each call site_ (the last-method count and the enrolment timeline are the reasons this fails, and every call site would carry the same four-way union); _making the register the source of truth and storing credential material in it_ (a second credential store beside the library's, diverging on the first upgrade, and it would put a TOTP seed outside the encryption arrangement already settled for it); _forking or patching Better Auth to add the column_ (an upgrade tax on every release for a fact the library has no reason to hold); _a firm-writable floor_ (a multi-tenant platform holding privileged material cannot let one tenant's configuration become the platform's weakest link and then defend the result); _deriving "may use" from enrolment alone_ (then a firm cannot require phishing-resistant sign-in, and turning a method off means deleting somebody's credential rather than declining to accept it).

**Consequences:** Adding a method becomes a register kind, a floor entry and a mounting, rather than a branch in every gate. The reconciliation test is load-bearing and is a Miniflare test rather than a source scan, because the property is about rows and not about text. Builds on **ADR-0073** (the Better Auth integration shape), **ADR-0084** (credential material the library owns stays under its own at-rest encryption), **ADR-0040** and **ADR-0132** (the settings registry and its scoped resolver), **ADR-0068** (the permission registry this floor sits beside).
