**A bank statement parsed from a PDF is evidence, not truth: it is refused unless its own arithmetic proves the reading, it may only ever be matched against a ledger entry and never cause one, and what is kept has to be enough to re-derive it.**

**Context:** Three-way reconciliation shipped under ADR-0246 with two ways for the bank's figures to arrive — a member types them, or a member imports a delimited file the firm exported from its bank. The file firms actually have is a PDF. Online banking hands them one, the delimited export is not where a member starts, and at the small local banks a solo practice uses for its trust account it frequently does not exist. So the practical state today is that a firm with a PDF statement types it, line by line, monthly, per trust account, into the one record a disciplinary body may later read.

The temptation in a feature like this is a parser that is right most of the time, and "most of the time" is doing all the work. A misparsed amount that lands in `trust_bank_statement_line` is, from that moment on, indistinguishable from the bank having said it: the firm hunts for a trust discrepancy that does not exist, or — if the misparse happens to be compensated elsewhere — the proof balances and certifies an agreement that was never proved.

**Decision — the confidence model is the statement's own arithmetic.** A bank statement is not an unconstrained document. It states an opening balance, a closing balance, and the items between them, and those figures are related by arithmetic the bank performed. If the parsed rows foot to the parsed closing balance, every amount and direction is corroborated by two figures the parser read independently, from a different part of the page, under a different layout rule; a single misread digit breaks the footing by a power of ten, and a misidentified column breaks it comprehensively. Where the statement prints a running balance the check also localizes, turning "this statement is off by $180" into "line 34 was read wrong."

That gate is arithmetic rather than a model output, and it is explicable to a bar auditor in one sentence. So the outcomes are assigned by failure class and none of them is "import it with a doubt attached": a layout that cannot be resolved refuses the preview naming what was found; rows that do not foot refuse the import reporting the difference; one unreadable row is a rejected row with a reason and a page-and-line locator, to be typed in; and a statement that reads cleanly and foots is **reported and nothing more** — storing still takes the separate explicit act quoting the preview fingerprint back, because ADR-0214's opt-in rule is not conditional on the platform's own confidence. The recognizer's per-line confidence is retained on the stored row as provenance, so a member can tell a line transcribed exactly from a born-digital PDF from a line read off a photocopy at 0.83, and it is used only to order a review and to explain a failure.

**Decision — the import proposes and a person disposes, structurally.** An imported line has no client and no matter: `DEPOSIT 5,000.00 REF 41221` says nothing about whose money it is, and `ensureMatterTrustLeaf` derives the client from the matter's own client party and refuses rather than take a caller's word, because a caller naming a client beside a matter was once enough to file one client's money onto another client's ledger. The bank leg is the only independent input in the three-way proof, so a line that could post an entry would begin feeding the book leg and the proof would compare the bank statement with itself — balancing more often, and losing the one signal that means something reached the ledger without going through the composition guard. A movement caused by a parse has no authorizer, and the platform would have to attribute it either to a member who authorized no payment or to itself. And it is a strictly larger authority than automatic matching, which the shipped matcher already refuses by having no composer that accepts a suggestion.

The important part is that nothing needs building to enforce it. `trust_bank_statement_line` has no relation to the ledger; the only join between a line and an entry is `trust_reconciliation_match`, and that row exists only because a member named both ends. The PDF import contributes rows to a table that structurally cannot post, and it stays that way: no ledger identifier on the line, no suggested-entry column, no consumer that reads lines and composes anything. The one case that tempts an exception — the monthly service charge, the commonest unmatched withdrawal — is answered with something smaller than an exception and is flagged rather than assumed: the surface may pre-fill the ordinary bank-charge composer, which the member submits under their own identity, in the firm-funds category that structurally cannot describe somebody else's money.

**Decision — provenance is retained rather than recomputed.** Recognition is server-canonical and not cross-architecture deterministic, and model pins move, so re-running extraction a year from now is a fresh reading rather than a reconstruction. What is kept is therefore the chain itself: the original PDF verbatim in the firm's bucket, firm-scoped and content-addressed, arriving through the ordinary ingest chokepoint so it is malware-scanned like every inbound file; its digest on the statement row; the extraction artifact with per-line geometry, read source, and the engine's own provenance triple; the parser version and resolved layout profile as values; the parsed rows with a locator back into the artifact; and the preview fingerprint a named person confirmed. Page images are deliberately not kept. This also repairs an existing defect rather than only serving the new path: the delimited importer takes its file in a request body and drops it after parsing, so a statement already recorded as `imported` cannot be re-derived from anything the platform kept, and `imported` currently means two different things.

**Decision — parsing runs where the platform already parses, and nothing new is adopted.** The native text layer is read in pure Rust as WASM in the Worker over `crates/pdf-core`, behind the decompression guard and the page ceiling that already sit there; pages a shared gate marks as scans go to the OCR Container that is already deployed and network-isolated, per page rather than per document so a scan stapled to a born-digital statement is not half lost; and table reconstruction runs in TypeScript in `@magna-carta/trust`, beside the money rules it must obey. The one Rust addition is an exposure rather than an engine: `textpos.rs` already computes glyph boxes and `textextract.rs` discards them for a single-column reading order that interleaves columns — fine for a keyword index and fatal for a table, where the whole question is which column a number sat in.

**Considered and rejected:** **A PDF table-extraction library** — the mature ones are Python, or sit on a large C++ PDF parser reading untrusted, privileged bytes, which is exactly the exposure golden rule 14 exists to prevent, and taking one would put a second PDF parser in a platform that deliberately runs one. **A confidence threshold as the gate** — it cannot see structural uncertainty at all, which is where the damage lives: a column misidentified once is a whole statement of amounts read from the wrong place, every one of them a well-formed number. **Importing an unfooted statement with the discrepancy recorded** — faster, and it puts a difference into the bank leg that nobody can attribute, so the firm cannot tell a trust problem from a parse problem, and by the time it matters the file may be gone. **Reading the statement with a model** — it would read a messy statement better than geometry will, and it fails on three independent grounds: the output is not reproducible so the audit chain cannot close; there is no arithmetic corroboration of a generated number, because the footing check works only when the parser read the closing balance off the page rather than producing it; and a client's bank statement through a model is a confidentiality decision with a consent model attached. The narrow form that survives all three is a model suggesting a layout profile a person confirms and that is thereafter applied deterministically, and even that is flagged. **A per-bank profile library shipped by the platform** — a list of supported banks is a maintenance burden that is silently wrong for the bank a firm actually uses; instead columns are inferred geometrically and the firm's own confirmations accumulate into a per-firm profile keyed by a structural fingerprint of the layout, which is ADR-0214's accumulate rule and the same mechanism as the document-automation field-mapping library. **A bank API or aggregator** — already refused by ADR-0246 and not reopened.

**Consequences:** A firm stops retyping its trust bank statement, and the record it produces is stronger than the one it produced by typing, because the source document and the reading of it are both kept and the arithmetic that proves the reading is recorded. Reconciliation itself is untouched: the matcher, the proof, the close, and the meaning of a match are all exactly what they were, which is why the design is small. The costs are real and named: a statement that does not foot cannot be imported at all, so a firm with a genuinely irregular statement is back to typing; recognition of a scanned statement is a Container round trip that must ride a job rather than a request; the retained statement and its extraction artifact are new privileged bytes in every firm's bucket with a retention question attached; and the one-line-to-one-entry rule bites harder here than on a delimited export, because a bank that summarizes the day's deposits summarizes on the statement it prints. Nine questions (S1-S9) go to the owner, four of which decide what gets built rather than how — whether a footing failure may be overridden, whether a scanned statement may be imported at all, how long a client's bank statement is retained and what may erase it, and whether the retention repair is applied to the delimited path.
