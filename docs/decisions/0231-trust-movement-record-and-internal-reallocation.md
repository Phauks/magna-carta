**A trust movement carries an append-only client-ledger record beside its ledger entry, and a matter-to-matter reallocation posts no bank line.**

**Context:** The trust ledger and its structural guards are built; what was missing was the movements themselves. Two shapes had to be settled before the first deposit could be recorded. The trust record-keeping rules ask a firm to keep a per-client ledger showing the source, amount, purpose, payee, and running balance of every movement, and — in several jurisdictions — a written memorandum authorizing each disbursement and identifying the responsible attorney. The double-entry ledger holds amounts, direction, dates, and balances; it holds none of the rest, and it must not learn to, because the moment it carries a payee it stops being an account-agnostic primitive. Separately, a firm that receives funds against the wrong matter of a client needs to move them to the right one, and the shape of that movement was undecided.

**Decision:** (1) **One `trust_movement` row per trust entry this subsystem composes**, carrying the source or payee, the purpose, the method, the reference, the recording member, and the authorizing member. It is append-only by trigger, like everything else hung off the ledger: a correction is a reversing entry with its own record, never an edit, because a payee that can be rewritten later is a client-ledger record that says whatever the last writer wanted it to say.

(2) **The payment method is a modelled value, not prose.** Several jurisdictions prohibit specific disbursement methods outright — a cash withdrawal, ATM or debit access, a check payable to Cash — and a rule engine cannot refuse a method it cannot see. Recording it from the first movement also means binding a real jurisdiction policy later gates future disbursements without anyone having to reconstruct how past ones were paid. The refusal itself stays behind a port whose default permits every method, and that default is a statement about this platform's knowledge rather than about any jurisdiction's rules.

(3) **The record's column combinations are a CHECK, not composer discipline.** Which columns each kind may carry — an external payee only on a bank movement, an internal method only on a reallocation, an authorizing member required whenever money leaves a client ledger — is enforced by the database, so a record that misdescribes its movement aborts the batch that carries the entry it describes. The composer's job is then only to produce refusals a person can act on, never to be the guarantee.

(4) **A reallocation between two matters of one client posts no bank line.** The entry debits the source leaf and credits the destination leaf, and touches nothing else. No bank movement happened, so the trust-bank leaf's balance is unchanged; both leaves are inside the client-side total, so that is unchanged too — which means the book side and the client side still agree by construction, the property the whole tree exists to give. The alternative shape, a disbursement and a receipt posting offsetting bank lines, would put two items on the account that can never clear a bank statement and would make every subsequent three-way reconciliation of that account wrong by the amount moved. The composition guard is widened to admit exactly this one bank-free shape: every line a matter leaf, of one client, across at least two distinct leaves. Debiting and crediting a single leaf is refused by name, because it moves nothing.

(5) **The ledger gains a `pendingAccounts` declaration on the posting side.** Lazy leaf creation was already the intended shape — a matter's trust leaf is built at its first deposit, and the leaf, its zero floor, and the first posting belong in one batch — but the posting path read its account snapshots from the database and could not see a row its own batch was creating. The declaration mirrors the one account creation already takes for a pending parent, and a stored row always wins over a declared one, so a caller cannot talk the validator out of what the database says.

**Consequences:** A movement now refuses outright when the trust account's office recorded no jurisdiction, distinctly from a jurisdiction whose rules are not encoded — the rules that govern a movement cannot be resolved without one, and neither refusal ever falls back to a baseline. The `trust_movement` row is the read contract the client ledger, the trust journal, and the dormant-funds aging list will all render from. Whether an earned-fee transfer out of trust hard-requires a finalized invoice remains an open owner decision and stays behind its existing refusing port; whether a specific jurisdiction forbids a specific disbursement method remains unencoded and stays behind the new one.
