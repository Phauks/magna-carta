**The PAdES platform seal is a dedicated sign-worker behind a typed `SealPort` service binding; the signing key is a single self-signed platform certificate held as a Worker secret on that worker.** **Context:** the e-signature core (the pure-Rust seal primitive, the pkijs CMS backend at PAdES-B-B baseline, `sealDocument`, and the ESIGN/UETA model + Certificate of Completion) is built and proven end to end. Making it a product feature needs a home in the runtime and a provisioned signing identity. Two homes were weighed against the owner's stated goals — modularity, ease of development, and reuse. **Decision:** (1) **A dedicated `apps/sign-worker`, not in-process in the app worker.** Every heavy/native engine here already lives in its own worker reached via a service-binding port (scan, convert, edit, ocr, recovery), and the worker doctrine names the PDF engine for that split; the seal follows it. The decisive reason is not compute weight (the seal's prepare/embed are lightweight WASM, and the app worker already runs the decryption/email WASM engines in-process) but the **signing key**: centralizing it in one minimal-surface worker keeps it out of the large app worker's blast radius and gives the platform a **single authoritative signing service** reusable — through one `SealPort` — by every future consumer (matter documents, the client portal's signing, document-automation auto-seal, discovery production) without replicating the key. (2) **`SealPort` is a typed contract in `@magna-carta/signing`** (request: document + seal metadata + signer records + signing time → response: sealed PDF + document hash + Certificate of Completion), with shared wire encode/decode so both sides serialize identically — the reuse seam. The app worker reaches it via a `SIGN_SERVICE` binding and a `resolveSealPort` adapter that falls back to a "seal unavailable" port when the binding is absent (the `resolveScanPort`/`resolveEnginePort` deferral pattern), so the route is testable and deployable before the sign-worker is. The sign-worker imports `@magna-carta/pdf`'s WASM as a `WebAssembly.Module` (the established email/decryption route mechanic) plus `@magna-carta/signing`. (3) **A single self-signed platform certificate.** ESIGN/UETA validity and the seal's tamper-evidence do not need a CA-rooted certificate — the platform seal exists to make the record tamper-evident, not to assert PKI trust, so one self-signed Magna Carta identity is the designed default; the private key (PKCS#8) and certificate (DER) are **Worker secrets on the sign-worker**, imported at runtime as a non-extractable `CryptoKey`, deferred-activated (the worker returns "seal not configured" until the secrets are set). The external-KMS-rooted key and per-signer PKI stay the deferred seams (the encryption-and-keys PQC posture and the plugin e-signature port). (4) **Scope: the server-applied platform seal** — a document in, a sealed document + Certificate of Completion out, synchronously (sealing is milliseconds, not a background job); the signer-facing workflow (envelopes, external signers, portal signing, attribution capture) is a later, larger subsystem that this seal and the Certificate-of-Completion renderer already anticipate. PAdES level is **B-B** now; B-T (RFC 3161 timestamp) is deferred pending a timestamp-authority choice. **Considered & rejected:** **in-process in the app worker** — simplest and technically fine (wasm-in-workerd is routine here), but it spreads the signing key across the largest-surface worker and buys no reuse the packages don't already give, losing the single-authority and least-privilege properties that serve the modularity/reuse goals; **a CA-issued certificate now** — unnecessary for tamper-evidence and ESIGN/UETA, and the notarial/PKI long tail is already a named port; **per-firm signing keys** — the seal is the platform's, not the firm's (the firm's assurance comes from the audit trail + attribution), so one identity is simpler and correct; **async job** — needless for a millisecond operation. **Consequences:** the platform gains one reusable, least-privilege signing service behind a typed port, deployable and testable ahead of activation; the cost is a new worker + service binding + the one-time platform-certificate generation, and the discipline of carrying the deferred-adapter fallback. Builds on the seal primitive, the CMS backend, and `sealDocument`; extends the dedicated-worker/service-binding pattern (scan, convert, edit); the platform seal + ESIGN/UETA workflow it serves are the _E-signature_ design.
