**A client of a firm and somebody who works at one are different kinds of principal in the schema, not the same user row told apart by reading an invitation.**

**Context:** The white paper already specifies portal accounts as "a distinct external principal type (never staff RBAC)". The shipped invitation route does not implement that: it writes the same `INSERT INTO "user"` for both invitation kinds and records the difference only on the `invitation` row. The first surface that needed the distinction — the community board, which must refuse portal principals because a post signed with a real name from a client-of-one-firm account discloses that firm-client relationship to every reader — could only reconstruct it with a correlated subquery counting `invitation` rows of kind `clientOfFirm`, and its own comment says the intended shape is that a portal principal be structurally incapable of holding a board identity. Three things are wrong with that subquery and only the first is usually noticed: it is a check every future surface must remember; it reads **history** rather than state, so a portal client the firm later hires is marked forever and a firm member who was once a client of a different firm is marked wrongly; and it depends on a row nothing promises to keep, so the day an invitation is expired-swept a portal client silently becomes a firm member to every reader.

**Decision:** Four changes, each doing a different job. (1) **`user.principal_kind`** — text, NOT NULL, `firmMember | portalClient`, written only by the two account-creating paths, protected by a `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger that raises on any change, so the handler is structurally incapable of the mistake rather than merely careful. (2) **`CHECK ((principal_kind = 'portalClient') = (firm_id IS NULL))`** — the load-bearing one. `user.firm_id` is what every firm-scoped surface reads to place a caller; populated for a portal client, a surface that forgets the kind places them **inside** the inviting firm, open by default. Null, the same surface resolves no firm and answers the reserved cross-firm 404 — closed by default. The difference between those two failure modes is the entire value of the change. (3) **The kind rides the session**, `RouteMeta` gains `principals` defaulting to `["firmMember"]`, and the chokepoint gains a **surface-admission** gate ahead of tenancy; an unlisted kind is refused as absent, never with a 403 confirming the principal exists, and a route registered without the field fails at startup. (4) **A portal principal has no `member` row in any per-firm database** — RBAC resolves from `member`, so no row means no permissions in any firm under any bug, and the portal's own authority is `PortalGrant` resolved by a resolver that cannot read `member`. Multi-firm membership is a purpose-built `portal_membership(principal_id, firm_id, directory_entity_id, status)`, every row naming a Directory entity so there is no shadow user list. A person who is staff at one firm and a client of another holds **two accounts**, because the kind is immutable and the whole design rests on that; the offer to switch appears only **after** authentication.

**Considered & rejected:** _keeping the invitation-history check and documenting it_ (it is derived from a mutable, sweepable row and is wrong for the two ordinary fact patterns above); _a boolean `is_portal` beside a still-populated `firm_id`_ (keeps the fail-open default, which is the actual defect); _the Better Auth `organization` plugin as the membership model_ (a second membership model in the control plane beside the per-firm `member` mirror, contradicting the one-organization-per-user line, and it brings an active-organization-on-session concept the portal must specifically not have — a stale active firm plus a grant lookup that trusts it is exactly how a cross-firm leak happens, and the bug is invisible to anyone testing with one membership); _a separate `portal_user` table entirely_ (duplicates sessions, credentials, the method register and every recovery path, for a distinction two columns and a trigger already make structural); _making the kind mutable so a hired client can be converted_ (mutability is what the trigger exists to refuse; conversion is a new account and a data-migration conversation, not a column write).

**Consequences:** `firmId` stops being `required: true` on the Better Auth user model and becomes nullable, and the compiler forces every reader to say what it does with null — a one-time cost paid by a compiler rather than an ongoing cost paid by reviewers, and this is the cheapest moment to pay it, since both account-creating paths landed this week. The community board's subquery becomes `session.principalKind === "portalClient"` and its comment's stated intent is satisfied. Builds on **ADR-0003** (tenancy), **ADR-0036** (the portal grant model), **ADR-0068** (trusted-insider RBAC and the reserved cross-firm 404), **ADR-0067** (route metadata at the chokepoint), **ADR-0280** (the joining paths this changes).
