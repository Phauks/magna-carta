**A session records how strongly it was established and when, and step-up becomes a property of the operation rather than a decision a handler remembers.**

**Context:** With one credential a session is a boolean and that is honest. With four methods it stops being honest: a session established by clicking an emailed link and a session established by a user-verifying passkey are not the same thing, and a platform that treats them alike has made its strongest method worth exactly as much as its weakest. The platform already needs the distinction in one shipped place — the malware-flagged download sets `stepUpRequired` on a 409 and the firm scan-policy stores a preference for it — and `problem.ts` calls the field a forward seam because there is nothing behind it. Better Auth supplies a freshness primitive (`session.freshAge`, default one day) but no notion of strength, and its clock is `createdAt`, which does not move when somebody re-proves themselves.

**Decision:** The session carries **`assurance`** on a four-level total order — `mailbox` (a one-time link or emailed code alone, proving control of a mailbox at one instant and nothing more) < `credential` (a password against a verified address, or a federated assertion carrying no multi-factor claim) < `multiFactor` < `phishingResistant` (a user-verifying passkey, or a federated assertion whose authentication-method claims are phishing-resistant) — and **`authenticated_at`**, which a step-up re-verification moves without minting a new session and which activity extension does not move. `freshAge` stays configured as the coarse backstop; the fine clock is ours, because a re-verification that forced a new session would rotate a cookie mid-operation. **Step-up becomes route metadata**: `stepUp: { assurance, maxAge } | null` on `RouteMeta`, enforced at the chokepoint in an ordered gate after RBAC and before idempotency, answering **403** carrying the existing `stepUpRequired` field. The counter-rule ships with it: **step-up is for acts that are irreversible, or that create a new way in.** Everything else is RBAC's job. Writing a time entry on a sensitive matter is not a step-up act however sensitive the matter, because the trusted-insider model already answered that question. The platform does **not** claim NIST AAL conformance — the lattice is shaped by the same thinking, and naming an AAL in a UI or a contract would assert verifier-impersonation resistance and re-authentication evidence nothing here produces.

**Considered & rejected:** _a boolean "recently authenticated"_ (cannot express that a mailbox link should never satisfy a trust-disbursement gate no matter how recent it is — strength and recency are two axes and collapsing them loses the one that matters); _`freshAge` alone_ (compares against `createdAt`, so a re-verification either does nothing or must mint a new session); _numeric AAL levels_ (a conformance claim, made in a UI, defensible only with evidence the platform does not generate); _step-up decided per handler_ (the flagged-download path is the existing example and the reason the field is called a seam — one handler setting it by hand is exactly what does not generalise to ten acts); _a 401 for an unmet step-up_ (the caller **is** authenticated; a 401 tells a client to sign in again, which is the wrong remedy and loses the session).

**Consequences:** Ten named acts (address of record, method enrolment and removal, disabling a second factor, narrowing a firm's method policy, issuing a firm-member invitation, registering an identity provider, widening a portal grant, trust disbursement and trust configuration, bulk decryption of protected identifiers, crypto-shred and whole-firm export, the flagged download, privileged support-grant redemption) become declared rather than remembered; the list and its `maxAge` are owner flag **F12**. The flagged-download path stops setting the field by hand. Every method added later declares its assurance ceiling once, in the register, instead of being reasoned about at each gate. Builds on **ADR-0067** (route metadata at the chokepoint), **ADR-0068** (RBAC, and where its remit ends), **ADR-0158** (the firm scan policy that already stores a step-up preference).
