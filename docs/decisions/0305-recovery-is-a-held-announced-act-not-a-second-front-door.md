**Recovery is an authentication method with the ceremony removed, and is therefore designed, announced, delayed and audited as one.**

**Context:** Any method can be added; the question is what happens when a person loses it, and that is where account takeover lives. A law firm's account holds privileged client material, so the loss cases are not hypothetical inconveniences: a passkey on a stolen laptop, a magic link to a mailbox somebody else now controls, an identity-provider tenant that revoked a person or that no longer exists. The trap is that every one of these has an easy answer that quietly reduces the account to its weakest link — most often "we email you a link" — and a passkey defeated by an emailed link was never a passkey.

**Decision:** Recovery paths live in the same register as every other method and spend the same shared attempt ladder. The mechanism is a **hold**: any recovery that would admit somebody at an assurance the account's enrolled methods would not have granted is (1) recorded as a **pending recovery** with a stated effective instant; (2) **announced immediately to every channel of record** — the address of record, every enrolled second-factor channel, and the firm's Owners and Admins — never one channel, because the attacker holds one; (3) **delayed** by a window (owner flag **F7**: twenty-four hours for a firm member, seventy-two for a portal client); (4) **cancellable by any valid existing session or one click from any notified channel**, which locks the account into administered-recovery-only, so the person who still holds the account can always stop the person who claims to have lost it; and (5) on effect **revokes every session and every method enrolled since the request was made**, so a recovered account does not silently keep the attacker's newly enrolled passkey. Its converse is cheap and closes the commonest takeover shape: **enrolling a method is itself a notified event.** The **primary path is firm-administered** — an Owner or Admin re-aims the shipped three-fact invitation machinery (code, surname, date of birth, one neutral refusal) at an existing account — and its hold is **zero**, because an attributable human at the firm who knows the person is a stronger control than a delay and delaying them punishes the good path. Changing the **address of record** is a step-up act at the account's **highest enrolled** assurance plus the hold plus notification to both addresses, and the endpoint structurally refuses any session whose assurance is `mailbox` whatever its age: a link may start a recovery, never complete one. Spending a **recovery code** drops the session to `credential` and blocks every step-up-gated act until a replacement factor is enrolled (owner flag **F9**). **Platform-administered recovery** (owner flag **F8**) is permitted only where the firm has no other Owner or Admin, is a two-person act by named platform-staff principals with the full hold, an audit row naming both, and a notification to the firm's billing contact of record.

**Considered & rejected:** _an emailed recovery link that mints a full session_ (a link is mailbox assurance whatever it is called, and this is the single change that would make every stronger method pointless); _knowledge-based answers_ (a firm's own data is discoverable and much of it sits in public court records — "your first matter number" is a question an opposing party can answer); _support confirming identity by voice or by a scan of a document_ (the platform has no identity-proofing capability, and pretending otherwise produces a path attackers optimise against and staff feel obliged to satisfy); _routing platform recovery through the support-grant framework_ (that framework is consent-anchored — the member's request **is** the consent — and a person who cannot authenticate cannot consent, so bending it would turn a consent model into a claim model); _removing the old methods before the new one is proven_ (the order is always prove, then replace); _a hold with no cancel_ (the delay is only protective if the losing party can act inside it); _notifying only the address of record_ (the mailbox is the channel most likely to be the one that was compromised).

**Consequences:** An `account_recovery` record, a cron sweep that effects a pending recovery whose window has passed, and outbox-driven notification fan-out to every channel. Recovery becomes a page in `docs/user/` that people read on their worst day, which is a product requirement rather than a documentation chore. The hold windows and the platform-recovery bound are configurable with the recommended defaults above, and none is hard-coded. Builds on **ADR-0280** (the three-fact invitation machinery this reuses), **ADR-0157** (the support-access framework, and the reason it does not stretch here), **ADR-0064** (the transactional outbox the notifications ride), **ADR-0059** (the notifications registry).
