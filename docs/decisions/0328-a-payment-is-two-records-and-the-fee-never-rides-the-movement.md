**The processor's fee never rides the movement it paid for, and the record that says the two were one event lives outside the ledger.**

**Context:** ADR-0032 settled that fees never touch trust and that the enforcement is separate deposit routing. `assertTrustComposition` already makes it structural for every caller: once a line touches a trust account, every other line on the entry must belong to the same trust account, so a fee expense has nowhere to sit on a trust entry at all. What ADR-0032 did not say is where the fee _does_ sit, and there are only three candidate homes: netted out of the deposit, borne by the firm's own cushion inside the trust account, or an unrelated operating entry.

The first is refused structurally. The second reads plausible and is not available: `trust_firm_funds_movement` has exactly two kinds, and the rule text every jurisdiction in the corpus states in materially the same words authorizes the lawyer's own funds in trust for the **institution's** service charges. A card processor's discount rate is not that, and only four researched jurisdictions extend the permission to merchant fees — Arizona expressly (ER 1.15(b) with Rule 43(a), which also permits offsetting chargebacks), plus one further state, with Texas and Maine instead permitting a transit account outside trust. Nevada, the one jurisdiction this platform ships, records `processorFees` as `GAP`: NRPC 1.15, SCR 78, SCR 216-221 and the State Bar handbook were searched for `credit card`, `processing fee`, `merchant`, `surcharge` and `processor` and nothing addresses it, and the ethics-opinions index was never worked through. The field does not resolve and is not permitted to.

**Decision:**

(1) **Two entries, and no third option.** The money is `Dr Trust Bank / Cr the client's matter leaf` for the **gross** amount, composed through `composeTrustDeposit` and therefore through `composeTrustEntry`. The fee is `Dr Merchant Fees / Cr Operating Bank`, an ordinary entry touching no trust account. The fee is the firm's expense, borne by the firm, on every trust-destined payment, under every possible answer to the unresearched jurisdiction question.

(2) **The join is a record, not an entry, and that is the whole design.** A `payment` row names the trust movement and the fee entry. An entry joining them would be one entry with a trust leaf and a fee expense on it, which is the commingling. So the relationship is stored where relationships belong and not where money is.

(3) **The firm's own money may not cover a fee inside a client's ledger, and the refusal is by name because the shape is expressible.** `composeTrustDeposit` takes a free-text `source`, so a firm could deposit its own funds onto a client's matter leaf and describe them as anything. The composition guard cannot catch it: the other leg is the trust bank, so no line is outside the trust account and nothing looks wrong. It has to be refused deliberately, at the payments boundary, rather than assumed unreachable.

(4) **What the operating account must do to make this lawful is a processor configuration, and the capability descriptor is where the platform records it.** The merchant account serving the trust rail settles gross, settles per transaction, and has every fee, adjustment and chargeback debited to the operating account. An adapter that cannot declare `grossTrustSettlement` and `separateFeeAccount` returns `capability-unsupported` for the trust rail — a first-class dispatch outcome the caller branches on — while the operating rail is unaffected.

(5) **A processor that debits the trust account anyway is surfaced, never absorbed.** The fee arrives as a bank statement line no ledger entry can clear. It stays in `readUnmatchedTrustBankStatementLines`, the three-way proof reports a `bank-to-book` difference of exactly that amount, and a close freezes the unbalanced answer. That is the existing "an unbalanced proof is recorded, never forced" posture applied to a condition reconciliation exists to catch, and the honest reading of it is that the firm has a misconfigured merchant account and a real shortfall in a client trust account.

**Consequences:** A trust-destined card or ACH payment is expressible today without one change to `@magna-carta/trust`, because `card` and `electronic_transfer` are already movement methods and the deposit is an ordinary gross deposit. What payments adds is the fee's home and the record that ties the two halves together. The arrangements this refuses — netting, a merchant-fee cushion in trust, a transit account with a transfer window — are each lawful somewhere in the corpus and none is buildable without primary-source research and an attorney review that has not happened. Open questions for the owner are in `docs/specs/2026-08-15-payments-design.md`, flags K2, K3 and K12. Builds on **ADR-0032**; constrained by **ADR-0009** and the composition guard.
