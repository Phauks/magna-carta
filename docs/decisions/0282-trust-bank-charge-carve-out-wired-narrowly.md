**The bank-charge carve-out is wired, and 'narrowly' is a property of the schema and the type system rather than a note in the manual.**

**Context:** A trust bank account is charged by its institution — monthly maintenance, per-item, wire fees. That money leaves the account with no client-attributable authorization behind it, and the trust ledger had no path for it at all, so a firm's real bank statement could not be reconciled: the charge appeared as a statement line that would sit unmatched forever. The recorded rule is NRPC 1.15(b) in the primary-source research (`docs/research/2026-08-07-nevada-trust-accounting-rules.md`), quoted verbatim: "A lawyer may deposit the lawyer's own funds in a client trust account for the sole purpose of paying bank service charges on that account, but only in an amount necessary for that purpose." Every jurisdiction in the narrative corpus permits materially the same thing. The research recorded the rule and deliberately did NOT wire it, listing four obstacles; the owner has now directed that it be wired, "narrowly".

The hazard is specific and it is not that a firm will misread a manual. A permitted movement of money out of a trust account with no client-side authorization is, at the entry level, indistinguishable from the thing the whole subsystem exists to prevent. If the carve-out is a flag on the composition guard, then every future caller of that guard — billing's apply-funds path, the payments card rail, whatever comes next — is one boolean away from moving client money on a firm-funds entry.

**Decision:**

(1) **A separate table, and the narrowness is its shape.** `trust_firm_funds_movement` has no `node_id`, no `matter_id`, no `client_entity_id`, and no payee column. The one node its entries can touch is the account's single `firm_funds` node, derived from `trust_account_id` and never stated by a caller. A row of this table cannot describe a movement of anybody else's money, which is a stronger statement than any check could make. `trust_movement` keeps meaning exactly what it meant before — client money moved — so no client-ledger read had to learn an exclusion, and none of them can forget one. A test asserts the absent columns by `pragma_table_info`, because "there is nowhere to put a client" is the load-bearing fact and it would otherwise be invisible.

(2) **Two kinds, and neither of them pays a person.** `firm_funds_deposit` puts the cushion in; `bank_charge` records the institution taking a service charge out. There is deliberately no kind that pays anybody: a payment out of a trust account to a person is a disbursement and goes through the disbursement queue with its authorization. The `bank_debit` method is refused on a deposit and required on a charge, so the two directions cannot wear each other's face.

(3) **A second composition door, narrower than the first — not a relaxed first door.** `assertTrustFirmFundsComposition` admits exactly two lines: the named firm-funds node, and that same trust account's bank leaf. A matter leaf, a client node, the liability root, a second trust account, or anything outside trust is refused. `assertTrustComposition` — the door client money goes through, and the one billing and payments inherit no-commingling from — still refuses a firm-funds line absolutely; its refusal code was renamed to say why (`firm-funds-entry-touches-client-money`) but its behaviour is unchanged. The three rules the general door spends most of its length on (one client per entry, postings on matter leaves, the bank-or-reallocation shape) are absent from the second door because the shapes they police cannot occur there, not because they were waived.

(4) **The zero floor is how "not borne by client funds" becomes a database guarantee.** The firm-funds node carries `min_balance_minor = 0`, written in the same batch that creates it, exactly as a matter leaf's is. A charge larger than the firm's own money in the account would drive that node negative and the `account_balance` bounds CHECK aborts the whole batch. A named pre-check refuses first and states the remedy — deposit firm funds to cover it, then record the charge — but the pre-check is not the guarantee, and a test proves that by committing two charges each composed against the same reading of the cushion. Refusing rather than absorbing is deliberate: a firm whose cushion is short has a real shortfall, the statement line stays unmatched, and the reconciliation reports the difference. That is the honest state, and it is the condition reconciliation exists to surface.

(5) **A capability of its own, seeded narrowest.** `trust:firmFunds` gates opening the carve-out, funding it, and recording a charge. It is a third trust key rather than a corner of `trust:manage` on the same reasoning that split `trust:authorize` off: "may keep the trust books" and "may put the firm's money into a client trust account" are different acts with different exposure. It is seeded to Owner/Admin **only** — narrower than `trust:authorize`, which reaches Attorney — and a firm that wants it wider reaches for a direct grant, which leaves a record.

(6) **Opening it is refused on a pooled interest-bearing account.** The corpus records the pooled account (SCR 217(3) in Nevada) as exempt from service charges by rule. Where an account bears no charges there is no amount necessary to pay them, so there is no permitted deposit — and firm money in a pooled client account with no permitted purpose is bare commingling. The `trust_account.iolta` discriminator the research believed absent has in fact existed since the account model was built, so this refusal costs nothing to enforce. It errs toward refusal in every case the platform cannot resolve, which is the only direction it is safe to err in here.

(7) **The three-way proof gained a fourth figure.** The client leg is now restricted to matter leaves and the firm's cushion is summed separately, with the book-to-client comparison reading `book = client + firm funds`. Both halves were necessary and each fails differently: the client-leg total previously summed **every** node under the trust account, so a cushion would have been silently counted as client money and the proof would have tied out while asserting it; and leaving the cushion out of the comparison would report a `book-to-client` disagreement on every account that enabled the carve-out, poisoning the one signal that means something reached the ledger without going through the composition guard. `frozen_firm_funds_minor` was added to `trust_reconciliation` as a plain nullable column deliberately outside the existing shape CHECK — widening that constraint would rewrite a table whose append-only triggers are the guarantee that a closed attestation never moves, and a `COALESCE(..., 0)` gives the right answer for the rows that predate the column.

(8) **Three fitness queries re-prove it over stored rows.** No entry pairs the firm-funds node with anything but its own account's bank leaf; every firm-funds record matches a ledger line on the firm-funds node for its amount; and the zero-floor query now covers firm-funds nodes as well as matter leaves. A structural invariant that cannot be re-proved against stored data is one that is being trusted rather than held.

(9) **No withdrawal of surplus firm funds was built.** The rule text authorizes the deposit and is silent on taking it back out. A cushion is consumed by the charges it exists to bear, so it is not trapped in ordinary operation; the shapes where it would matter — over-funding, and closing an account with a cushion still in it — are real and are recorded as open questions for the owner rather than answered by inventing a movement a permissive rule does not mention.

**Consequences:** A firm on a non-pooled trust account can now record what its bank actually did, and the statement reconciles. The carve-out cannot be used to move client money by any path this repository contains, and the property is asserted by the schema, by a second narrower composition door, by the balance floor, and by three fitness queries — four independent mechanisms, none of which is a comment. Three questions stay open for the owner and are listed in `docs/specs/2026-08-12-trust-bank-charges-and-unclaimed-funds.md`: whether the cushion should carry a maximum bound (Nevada states no number, and choosing one would be the platform legislating); whether a firm may withdraw surplus own funds, and on what authority; and whether the pooled-account refusal is too strict for a firm whose pooled account does in fact bear charges. Route and interface layers are not in this change.
