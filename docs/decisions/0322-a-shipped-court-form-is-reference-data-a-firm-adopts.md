**A shipped court form is reference data a firm adopts, authored by the platform from prescribed text — never a court's document redistributed, and never a row provisioning writes.**

**Context:** The owner directed a small, provenance-tracked Nevada starter court-form library. Establishing what Nevada actually publishes turned that into a design question rather than a content-entry one.

Six publishers were surveyed and each one's own words recorded (`docs/research/2026-08-14-nevada-court-form-sources-and-terms.md`). The statewide, Supreme-Court-approved corpus — the forms "acceptable to file in any court in Nevada" — is published under an express "All content © Supreme Court of Nevada 2026". The Clark County family and civil corpora, which a Las Vegas practice uses daily, are published by Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada, Inc., a private nonprofit operating under a county contract, under an express "All content © Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada 2026". Washoe County's terms could not be established. The appellate corpus asserts nothing but is the corpus a firm files least often. The Nevada Revised Statutes assert nothing at all, and several sections prescribe a form's operative text verbatim — NRS 155.020(3) and (4) set out the probate hearing notice and the notice to creditors word for word, and NRS 162A.620 does the same for a statutory power of attorney.

So "may we ship a Nevada form" splits into two very different questions depending on whether the thing shipped is a court's *document* or the *text a statute prescribes*.

**Decision:**

(1) **The platform authors its own template from prescribed text and cites the authority.** It does not redistribute a court's published file. This narrows the copyright question to material whose publisher claims nothing, it produces templates in the one authoring form the engine renders, and it is what makes a starter set possible at all rather than blocked behind permission letters. An entry whose basis is a court's own publication carries a recorded written permission as a required field of its type, so an entry without one does not compile.

(2) **The library is a pure package, not per-firm rows.** `@magna-carta/court-forms`, on the `@magna-carta/trust-jurisdictions` model: no database handle, no clock, no network, holding what one jurisdiction publishes plus the provenance that proves it. The sentence that package already lives by governs here too — a correction is a package release, never a migration against every tenant — and it binds harder for forms, because a statute amendment is wrong for every firm at once.

(3) **Nothing is seeded at provisioning.** Provisioning is deliberately five control-plane rows and a per-firm database that migrates lazily on first access, kept that cheap so a rate-limited queue absorbs signups. Adding a template registration, an R2 write and a normalization pass per shipped form would undo the property it was built for, and would write content into firms that never open the Documents area. A starter library is an offer, not an endowment.

(4) **Adoption is an ordinary template registration.** `POST /api/v1/court-forms/{formKey}/adopt` under `templates:manage` runs `prepareTemplateVersion` and files through the same ingest chokepoint every other byte uses. There is no second template store and no privileged path to `clean` — the moment there are two ways for a version to reach that state, the one that is wrong is the second one.

(5) **After adoption the copy is the firm's, and the platform never edits it.** `source_form_key` and `source_form_revision` on `document_template` are provenance and nothing else; no behaviour reads them. A firm may edit, version, rename and archive its copy freely. When the package later ships a corrected revision the firm's copy is **superseded, not replaced**: the firm is told what changed and offered a one-click adopt-as-a-new-version through the existing version route. Automatic rewriting is refused for the reason the assembly design already refused re-merging onto an existing document — the firm may have edited it, and the honest resolution to that is not free.

**Considered and rejected.** *Seeding every firm's catalogue at provisioning* — makes a correction a fan-out write across all tenants and loads the one path built to stay cheap. *A control-plane content store with a distribution mechanism* — real machinery for content that changes a few times a year and fits in source control, where it also gets code review, a diff, and a test. *Redistributing the courts' published files* — cheaper-looking, takes on somebody else's asserted rights in every file, and would still be unusable because those files are fillable PDFs and legacy-form-field `.docx` that the engine cannot bind (recorded separately). *Automatic supersession of an adopted template* — defensible for a firm that has not edited its copy and indefensible for one that has, and telling the two apart reliably is not free.

**Consequences:** The Nevada library can start with material whose reuse position is clean, without waiting on a permission letter, and it grows by permission rather than by hope. A firm holds only what it chose to hold. The largest and most useful Nevada corpora — everything family-law — stay out until both a permission and a fillable-PDF template kind exist, which is a real gap and is named as one rather than filled with something weaker. The provenance columns make "where did this template come from" answerable for a template a firm has since rewritten beyond recognition.
