**Beider-Morse ships as the over-inclusive band's third phonetic scheme, and the flip condition it was deferred behind is met by argument, on the record, rather than by a measurement that cannot honestly be taken.**

**Context:** ADR-0192 registered Beider-Morse as a seam and named its flip condition: "a measured recall gap on cross-origin surnames." The research it drew on found Beider-Morse materially better than Double Metaphone for **personal surnames** specifically, because it was built to reduce false positives across name origins and is roughly fifteen times more granular than Soundex; the seam was left open deliberately, with `directory_name_phonetic_code.scheme` carrying a `length > 0` CHECK rather than an enum, precisely so adding a scheme would be additive rows plus a backfill. The owner has now scoped search depth as work to be done, and the deferral's flip condition turns out to be unreachable in good faith.

**Decision:** (1) **Adopt Beider-Morse now**, as a third scheme computed on every `directory_name` write and enabled at `over_inclusive` strictness only, so the strict-superset invariant that guarantees raising strictness can never hide a hit stays true by construction. (2) **Record that the flip condition is met by argument rather than measurement, and why.** The gap is structural: Double Metaphone encodes English pronunciation, and the population a Nevada firm's Directory actually holds is cross-origin and full of transliterations. A "measured recall gap" would have to be measured against known-correct matches — which is the very answer the engine exists to supply — so the only honest way to observe one is for a firm to miss a conflict and find out later. A gate that cannot be reached without the harm it guards against is a deferral wearing a gate's clothes, and saying so is cheaper than quietly ignoring it. (3) **The cost is already paid.** No schema change, no migration on the name table: additive `directory_name_phonetic_code` rows plus a resumable, budgeted backfill sweep sharing the machinery the match-key backfill needs anyway. (4) **The Rust question is answered by where the cost sits.** Beider-Morse is a **write-time** encoder — codes are precomputed on every name insert and once over the whole corpus — so its Rust/WASM flip condition is a measured **write-path or backfill** regression, not a matching one. Matching remains sub-millisecond TypeScript over a blocking-bounded candidate set. (5) **Golden rule 14 binds the encoder choice explicitly.** A maintained pure-TypeScript implementation is preferred; if the only viable one pulls a C/C++ dependency, including compiled-to-WASM, the work **stops and asks the owner** and records the trade rather than adopting it silently.

**Considered & rejected:** _holding the deferral until a firm reports a miss_ (the measurement is the harm); _replacing Double Metaphone rather than adding to it_ (would change what `balanced` searches and break the superset relationship the whole strictness model rests on); _enabling Beider-Morse at `balanced`_ (same objection — the band's meaning is a shipped default and changing it is an owner decision, not a side effect of adding an encoder); _porting the encoder to Rust up front_ (no measured write-path regression exists, and golden rule 10 governs — the seam is the scheme column, which does not care what language filled it).

**Consequences:** Cross-origin surname recall stops being a structural blind spot at over-inclusive strictness, and the over-inclusive band finally delivers what ADR-0192 said it shipped without. The corpus needs one backfill pass. A second deferral in the same family stays deferred and honest: nickname equivalence (`Bob`/`Robert`) is not a phonetic relationship, needs a curated corpus, and ships **registered and empty** so every check records it `unavailable`. Deepens and partially discharges **ADR-0192**; builds on **ADR-0085** (the open `scheme` CHECK, the write-time derivation discipline); governed by **golden rule 14** on the dependency choice.
