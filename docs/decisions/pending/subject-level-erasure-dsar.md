**Subject-level erasure is designed as the deletion path GDPR Art. 17 / CCPA require of a processor, distinct from the existing firm-level crypto-shred: the recommended shape is (1) a data-subject attribution model so every personal-data row and R2 object is resolvable to the subject it concerns, (2) an erasure orchestrator that hard-deletes or irreversibly anonymizes that subject's live data across the firm's D1 and R2, (3) reliance on the per-firm key + retention-window expiry for backups (backed-up `protected()` ciphertext is already crypto-inaccessible after shred; native-encrypted bulk ages out on the GFS window rather than being surgically edited in immutable snapshots), (4) a legal-hold override that blocks erasure of data under hold, and (5) instruction to sub-processors to erase — all invoked by the controller (the firm) on behalf of a data subject and written to the immutable audit log.** **Context:** firm-level erasure exists and is strong — `shredFirmKey` (ADR-0084) makes a whole firm's protected corpus permanently unreadable — but _subject-level_ (one person, within a still-active firm) erasure is undecided; ADR-0078 explicitly lists per-matter/per-subject destruction (refcounting) as an open reason cross-matter dedup is not settled. As a processor we must give controllers the plumbing to honor a data subject's erasure request. Complications already in the codebase: files soft-delete only with immutable versions (invariants.md); infected/held files are quarantined-never-deleted and destruction is blocked by legal holds (security.md); the bulk of firm data is D1/R2 native-encrypted, which crypto-shred does not touch; backups are immutable bucket-locked snapshots. **Options weighed:** (a) **per-subject crypto-shred** — a unique key per data subject, destroyed on erasure; clean for backups, but adds significant key-management and, critically, does not cover the native-encrypted bulk data that is not routed through `protected()` (most personal data), so it cannot be the sole mechanism; (b) **deletion cascade** — delete/anonymize every row and object keyed to the subject across live D1 + R2 and instruct sub-processors; covers everything live, but backups cannot be surgically edited (WORM snapshots) so they rely on retention-window expiry + crypto-inaccessibility, and legal holds must override; (c) **tombstone + retention-window expiry** — mark and let backups age out; simplest, weakest (live data lingers). **Recommendation: a hybrid of (b) with (a)'s backup property** — cascade hard-delete/anonymize for live data, per-firm-key crypto-inaccessibility + GFS expiry for backups, legal-hold override, sub-processor instruction. The load-bearing prerequisite is the **data-subject attribution model** (every PII-bearing row/object resolvable to a subject) — that is the expensive-to-retrofit piece, so it should be seamed into the directory/matters PII schema before it hardens. **Considered & rejected:** doing nothing (leaves the product unable to support controller DSAR erasure — not GDPR-tenable for EU/UK firms); per-subject keys as the _sole_ mechanism (does not reach native-encrypted bulk data). **Open (owner decision):** the final mechanism per data class; the exact attribution model; erasure-vs-legal-hold precedence rules; how anonymization interacts with the append-only audit log (audit rows are retained, pseudonymized, not erased). **Consequences:** imposes a subject-attribution requirement on new PII-bearing schema (directory, matters, custom fields); pairs with **ADR-data-residency-firm-region-seam** as the two halves of the GDPR/PIPEDA data-lifecycle gap. Builds on **ADR-0084** (crypto-shred, the firm-level analog) and **ADR-0078** (which flagged this open); mapped in [`docs/conventions/compliance.md`](../conventions/compliance.md).
