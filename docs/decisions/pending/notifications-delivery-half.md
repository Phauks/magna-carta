**The Notifications subsystem's delivery half subscribes to the shipped transactional outbox instead of building a second emission path; the notification row carries no string a person typed; an email digest carries category counts and no contents; and matter visibility binds at delivery time through the one predicate, composed in SQL, shared by the feed and the digest.**

**Context:** The Notifications registry was decided (the decision that separates the decision from the delivery) but never specified past its type model, while emission points accumulated across the platform wired to nothing. An inventory found forty-four of them: thirty-one transactional outbox topics that already commit in the same atomic batch as the write they describe and already publish onto a per-firm queue with a topic dispatcher; six calendar emission points declared as a frozen constant precisely so delivery would subscribe to the names rather than invent its own; one query-shaped custody collection point whose own decision record promised that delivery would subscribe to that function rather than grow a second definition of what is due; one placed no-op raise site in email filing; and five job coordinators already broadcasting a terminal state. The honest reading is that the emission half is built and undeclared, which makes the remaining work a policy question rather than a plumbing one.

**Decision:**

1. **Subscription, not a second write.** For every topic already on the transactional outbox, the outbox row IS the emission — a second row beside it would be a weaker copy of a guarantee that already holds and would double the write amplification of every money movement in the platform. The notification translator becomes a **second, non-exclusive pass** in the per-firm queue dispatcher: the owning handler runs first if there is one, then the message is offered to the registry. An unknown topic stays acked and ignored (the bus is open by design); a topic may have both a business consumer and a notification; a translator failure is caught and counted while an owning handler's failure still propagates, because a missed notification is an annoyance and a re-driven cost posting is a double charge.

2. **Comparisons get a bucketed sweep, not a scheduler in the domain package.** Nothing in the calendar writes "missed" and nothing in the custody register schedules anything — both by design and both for good reasons that this decision does not disturb. Their emission points are materialized by the existing per-firm cron pass, calling the shipped functions, with `dedupe_key = "<kind>:<subjectId>:<civilDate>"` computed in the firm's zone. Without the civil-date bucket an overdue deadline raises every five minutes forever. The calendar's three *write*-shaped points (`event.scheduled`, `.rescheduled`, `.cancelled`) instead join the outbox topics, spelled from the package's own frozen constant so two spellings of one emission point cannot exist.

3. **The record carries identifiers, integers, instants, booleans, and declared enum labels — and no string a person typed.** No matter title, client name, filename, email subject line, deadline description, custody item description, or free-text note, enforced by a closed value grammar validated over every registered type rather than by review. Four reasons, in the order they bind: the platform already made this call twice on harder cases (a timeline entry carries only a source's declared non-privileged label; a custody reminder carries a handle, a number, a date, and a coarse bucket and explicitly no owner, borrower, description, or location); the row outlives the access check that justified it, and a row holding a title holds it after the reader stops being entitled to it; a stored title is a stale title, wrong invisibly because the row still looks authoritative; and resolving at read costs nothing, because the read already joins `matter` to enforce visibility. The reader sees "a deadline you are responsible for is due today — matter 8181-3", assembled at read time, never a stored sentence naming the motion and the parties.

4. **Three tables, and the in-app channel gets no delivery row.** `notification_event` (what happened, once), `notification_recipient` (read/dismissed state, the feed row), `notification_delivery` (one row per **outbound** channel, with attempts and a `suppressed` state whose reason is a closed label). The in-app feed is not delivered, it is read: the recipient row's existence is the delivery, and there is no attempt, no transport failure, no retry, and no address to fail against. Read state lives on the recipient row rather than on a channel, so marking an item read settles it for every channel — the question "has this person dealt with it" must not depend on which channel you ask. The `channel` CHECK is seeded with the full set including the deferred `push`, `sms`, and `desktop` values, because widening a SQLite CHECK later rebuilds a growing table and an unused value costs a byte.

5. **An email states the shape of the queue and never its contents.** A fixed, matter-free subject carrying a count; a body of per-category counts and one deep link; no matter numbers, titles, dates, amounts, names, or filenames. In-app and email are not one problem wearing two hats: in-app, every read is behind the session, the chokepoint, and the visibility filter, and a row rendered to the wrong person is a bug with a fix; an email is a copy handed to infrastructure the platform does not control, retained by a provider the firm did not choose, forwarded, backed up, and rendered on lock screens — and, decisively, **it cannot be re-filtered when access changes**. The only content safe against a future access change is content that never depended on access, which is exactly what a category count is. A matter title is client-identifying by construction and a matter number is client-identifying to anyone holding a prior email or invoice. Account and security kinds, which are about the recipient rather than a client and have no matter anchor, keep full content through the same port. Text rather than HTML: nothing HTML buys here, and it keeps the send port's signature unchanged and the body free of remote fetches. **Considered and deferred to the owner:** per-item lines carrying the matter number but never the title, as a firm-tier ceiling with `counts-only` shipped as the default.

6. **Preferences reuse the shipped scope chain; the firm floor binds categories, never a channel that leaves the platform.** One `scoped_setting` key holding the whole per-category matrix (one key, not ten — the resolver reads one key per call and the fan-out path resolves once per recipient), on the existing `user → firm → default` chain where a missing row IS the default. Category grain, not per-kind: thirty switches get toggled by nobody. Email is **digest-only** — an email per event is a mail-bomb, and it inverts the content argument by disclosing the timing and volume of activity per matter even when each line says nothing — with a registry-declared `immediate` escalation reserved for trust-compliance and security kinds. `security` and `trust` are non-silenceable **in-app**; a firm may raise the in-app floor and may **never** force a member's email cadence upward, because forcing email is forcing privileged-adjacent metadata into a mailbox that may be personal, may be outside the firm's administration, and is beyond both the audit log and the wall.

7. **A firm may designate trust-compliance recipients, and the designation is not a grant of access.** Designated members join the recipient set of every trust-category notification for every matter, but where a designated member cannot see the matter the notification is suppressed with a closed reason and **no placeholder appears** — a "1 item you cannot see" row is an existence oracle, the exact failure the platform's 404-not-403 posture exists to prevent. A firm wanting a partner to learn of trust events on a screened matter grants the access; the platform does not leak around its own wall.

8. **The wall binds at delivery time, through one predicate, shared by the feed and the digest.** Access changes after emission: a member removed from a matter must stop seeing yesterday's notifications about it today, and enforcing at emission produces a set frozen at the event plus a revocation sweep that has to be perfect forever. So the feed page, the unread count, and the digest all join the event's matter and compose the existing visible-matter predicate **in SQL, before the `LIMIT`** — a post-filter under-fills the page, makes the cursor mean something different from page to page, and turns the page size into an existence oracle for the rows it removed. The digest is the dangerous read because it runs server-side with no request context, so it resolves through the **same shared function** as the feed, making "the digest respects the wall" one implementation rather than two that can drift. Rows are never deleted or rewritten when access changes; they are filtered, and become visible again if access returns.

9. **Idempotency is a UNIQUE constraint, and delivery publishes before it marks.** `dedupe_key` is unique on the event (`<topic>:<outboxRowId>` for the event-driven path, `<kind>:<subjectId>:<civilDate>` for the comparison path), and the recipient row is unique per `(event, recipient)`, so a redelivery tops up a partial fan-out without duplicating it. A digest sends first and marks second, the ordering the outbox relay already argues for: a crash between the two re-sends a digest, where marking first risks losing one — and a duplicate digest against a missed deadline is not a close call.

10. **Retention: ninety days from occurrence, read or unread, swept nightly.** A notification is a pointer, never a record — the source row, the audit log, and the outbox row are the record, each with its own retention posture — so deleting one destroys no evidence. That is what makes a platform default defensible here where the custody register refuses one outright: the custodian rule is about a client's irreplaceable property, and this is about a badge.

**Considered and rejected:** *a notification topic of its own on every emitting write path* (a second, weaker copy of the transactional guarantee, and double the write amplification on every money movement); *one table with nullable channel columns* (three lifetimes that do not coincide, and a null-heavy row meaning something different in each state); *read state on the channel row* (makes "have they dealt with it" depend on which channel you ask); *storing a rendered title on the row* (stale invisibly, and outlives the access check that justified it); *per-item lines in the digest email* (deferred to the owner, not adopted); *immediate per-event email* (a mail-bomb whose volume is itself a disclosure); *one settings key per category* (one round trip per category on the fan-out path); *filtering notifications after the page is cut* (under-fills the page and turns the page size into an existence oracle); *deleting notifications when access is revoked* (a sweep that has to be perfect forever, where a read filter is exact by construction).

**Consequences:** the forty-four emission points gain one home and become registry entries; no existing emitting write path changes for the thirty-one that already ride the outbox; the calendar and custody packages keep their "no cron, no queue, no delivery here" properties intact; the parked notification-centre stub gains a read API to consume when its slice comes; and the cost is one package, four per-firm tables, one non-exclusive dispatcher pass, one cron step, and the registry discipline. Extends the Notifications subsystem decision (the registry, the audience model, the freshness stance) rather than amending it; honours the custody-reminder decision's promise that delivery subscribes to the one collection point; reuses the transactional outbox, the settings scope chain, the visible-matter predicate, the cursor contract, the enforcement chokepoint, and the redaction discipline. Web push, SMS, desktop-native presentation, external calendar subscription, outbound webhooks, per-kind preference grain, and a per-matter team audience remain named seams with nothing built.
