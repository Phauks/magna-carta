summary: To hold PCI DSS SAQ A, card capture uses Helcim's hosted payment modal (the HelcimPay.js iframe) returning a cardToken — not the in-DOM Helcim.js tokenizer (SAQ A-EP) and not the raw card API (SAQ D); store only the cardToken (never PAN/CVV) and keep the payment surface free of third-party scripts per PCI v4.0.1. Refines ADR-0032.
---

**PCI scope is held at SAQ A by capturing card data exclusively through the processor's hosted payment modal — Helcim's HelcimPay.js iframe, into which the PAN is entered and which returns an opaque `cardToken` — so no cardholder data ever enters our DOM, page, or server; the in-DOM `Helcim.js` tokenizer is rejected because Helcim's own scoping places it at the larger SAQ A-EP, and the raw-card Payment API at SAQ D; we store only the `cardToken` (plus last-four/brand for display), never a PAN and never a CVV (not even encrypted), and the document hosting the modal carries no third-party or analytics scripts so PCI v4.0.1 requirements 6.4.3 (payment-page script inventory) and 11.6.1 (script-integrity monitoring), which now reach even SAQ A, stay trivially satisfiable.** **Context:** ADR-0032 chose Helcim as the launch payment adapter behind a swappable port and set "hosted-fields tokenization → SAQ A" as the posture; the white paper _Payments_ names "Helcim.js" as the mechanism. That naming is imprecise: per Helcim's developer documentation, `Helcim.js` renders card fields **in the merchant's own DOM** and lands the merchant at **SAQ A-EP**, whereas **HelcimPay.js** renders the card fields **inside a Helcim-hosted iframe/modal** the merchant page never reads, landing at **SAQ A**. SAQ A is ~20–30 controls; SAQ A-EP adds the full payment-page-integrity and quarterly ASV-scan surface for no user-experience gain over the modal. As a legal SaaS handling privileged data, minimum card scope is strongly preferred. **Decision:** the card-capture path is the hosted modal (HelcimPay.js or the equivalent hosted-fields mode of whatever processor the port is bound to); the app persists only the `cardToken` and non-sensitive display fields; the payment route's document is script-clean; ACH — the default rail (ADR-0032) — and the trust/escrow rails carry no card data and stay out of PCI scope; the trust invariant _fees never touch trust_ (ADR-0032, invariants.md) is unchanged. A schema-scan fitness function (no column may hold a PAN/CVV) and a payment-surface script check enforce the posture once payments are built. **Considered & rejected:** in-DOM `Helcim.js` tokenization (SAQ A-EP — strictly more audit burden and a larger client-side attack surface for the same tokenized outcome); the raw-card Payment API (SAQ D + third-party QSA involvement); storing the PAN or CVV in any form (defeats the entire scope-minimization and is never necessary with tokenization). **Consequences:** PCI SAQ A becomes a _tested invariant_, not a hope; any future processor swap through the payments port (Stripe/Adyen/etc., per ADR-0032) must preserve the hosted-fields/hosted-modal property or the scope decision is void; corrects the white paper _Payments_ wording and the [`security.md`](../conventions/security.md) "hosted-fields → SAQ A" line to specify the hosted **modal**. Refines **ADR-0032**; mapped in [`docs/conventions/compliance.md`](../conventions/compliance.md).
