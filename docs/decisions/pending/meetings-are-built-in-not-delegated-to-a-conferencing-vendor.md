**A meeting is a room the firm owns, held on the record it belongs to — not a link to somewhere else that the record can only mention.**

**Context:** Every practice-management platform in this market reaches this question and answers it the same way: put a vendor's join link on the calendar entry and let the meeting happen somewhere the product cannot see. It is the cheap answer, and it costs the firm twice. The firm pays a second subscription for the thing it thought it was buying, and the meeting itself — who was actually on the call with the client, for how long, and whether it happened at all — lives in the vendor's account rather than in the matter. A firm asked two years later to show what contact it had with a client can produce every document, every email, every logged call and no trace of the four video conferences that did most of the work.

The owner ruled on 2026-09-18 that meetings are part of the product. The argument is the same one that decided file conversion, optical character recognition and full-text search: borrow expertise, never custody. A video meeting between a lawyer and a client is privileged in the ordinary way, and the record of it belongs in the matter beside everything else.

What made this practical is that the expensive half is a Cloudflare product the platform is already on. A selective forwarding unit — the piece that receives each participant's audio and video and forwards it to the others, so a room of twelve is not twelve times twelve connections — is the part nobody should write. Cloudflare Realtime provides one as a service reached over HTTPS, and the browser's own WebRTC stack talks to it directly. That leaves only the part that is genuinely ours: deciding who may be in the room, brokering the handshake, and writing down what happened.

**Decision:** Media goes from the browser to the Cloudflare Realtime selective forwarding unit and back, and never passes through a Worker. The Worker's whole part is signaling.

A Durable Object per meeting, keyed by firm and meeting, holds the signaling session. Browsers connect to it over hibernating WebSockets; it creates each participant's session with the selective forwarding unit, relays offers and answers, fans the roster out to everyone in the room, and writes attendance through the firm-scoped data-access layer. It holds a roster while the room is live and deletes it when the last socket closes. The application secret that authenticates against the unit lives only in the Worker and in that object; a browser never sees it, so a participant cannot mint a session of their own or reach a room they were not admitted to. There is no container, no media server, no transcoding and no C or C++ dependency anywhere in the path — the browser encodes and decodes, the unit forwards — so the memory-safe-languages rule is satisfied without an exception rather than in spite of one.

**A meeting belongs to exactly one calendar event, rather than being a free-standing object with a link.** This is the load-bearing shape decision and it was not obvious: a free-standing meeting is what every vendor sells, and it would have been less work. It is also a second place to answer every question the calendar already answers. A meeting object of its own needs a title, a time, a participant list, an owner and a visibility rule — all of which the event has, and all of which would then exist twice and drift. Worse, it needs its own answer to the walled-matter question, and a second authorization path into matter-confidential material is exactly the kind of seam this platform refuses elsewhere. So the meeting row carries a status, who started it, who ended it and the instants, and nothing else: the title, the time and the people are read from the event through a join. One meeting per event, enforced by a unique index, created on demand by the first member who presses Start — so an event with no meeting row has simply not been started, which needs no state to represent. Authorization is the event's own: a matter event is reachable when the member may see the matter, a firm event is firm-visible, and the two permission keys govern joining and hosting rather than seeing.

**Attendance is recorded and content never is.** An attendance row per join, with the instant the member joined and the instant they left, closed for everyone at one instant when the host ends the meeting. Audit rows on create, start, join, leave and end, carrying identifiers and instants only. The meeting stores nothing anyone said: no recording, no transcript, no chat, no frames. That is not a gap to be filled later by default — it is what makes the feature safe to ship before the consent machinery exists, because there is nothing captured that any jurisdiction's recording law has an opinion about. Who was in the room is part of what the record says happened, and is registered as retain-under-matter; what was said in it is the firm's to remember, exactly as a meeting in a conference room is.

**Considered and rejected:**

**A vendor integration — putting a Zoom or Teams link on the event.** It is cheaper to build than anything else here and it was rejected on the two grounds the owner's ruling names. The firm carries a second subscription, which is the cost this product exists to remove; and the meeting happens outside the record, so attendance, duration and the fact that it occurred at all are in the vendor's account rather than in the matter. This is not rejected forever: a conferencing port under the integrations package, whose adapter places a vendor's link on an event, is a reasonable thing for a firm that has already standardized on a vendor. It is an option a firm turns on, never the default and never the only path, and it is sequenced well after slice 1.

**RealtimeKit's prebuilt interface toolkit.** Cloudflare ships a higher-level product over the same infrastructure that hands you a working meeting interface in an afternoon. It is in beta, which on its own would argue for waiting. The disqualifying problem is the part that makes it attractive: it brings its own interface. All user interface in this product is Tailwind plus the in-house design system over headless primitives, with no raw styles and no off-token values, held by fitness functions that scan the whole repository. A vendor toolkit rendering its own controls inside our screen is a second design system in the application, and the meeting screen would be the one surface in the product that does not look like the product. Building the state machine against the raw signaling interface is a few hundred lines we own, against a component library we could never bring into compliance.

**A self-hosted selective forwarding unit in a Container.** Running one ourselves would put the media path entirely under our control, which has an honest appeal for privileged audio. Every production-grade implementation is C, C++ or Go: the first two are exactly what the memory-safe-languages rule refuses for code parsing untrusted bytes, and privileged audio and video from a participant's own device is untrusted bytes by the mile. A media server is also an operational commitment of a different order from anything the platform runs today — it is stateful, it is latency-critical, it must be near the participants, and it is the opposite of scale-to-zero. The Cloudflare unit keeps the media on Cloudflare's network, which is the same custody posture as everything else the platform stores.

**Consequences:** Two tables in the per-firm lineage, two identifier types, two permission keys and one Durable Object binding. The routes answer 503 and the calendar shows no Start control when the application identifier and secret are not configured, so the feature is dormant rather than broken on an account that has not provisioned Realtime — the same posture the tools take toward credentials they have not been given. The cost line is metered traffic through the unit rather than a subscription, recorded in the operating-cost register.

Four things are deliberately not in slice 1, each for a reason of its own rather than for time. **External guests** need a way for somebody with no session to enter one room and nothing else; the portal grant model has not started, so slice 2 mints a signed one-time join link on the precedent already set by the tokenized electronic-signature link. **Recording** needs server-side media processing, privileged recordings written through the untrusted-file pipeline, and a consent gate on the jurisdiction profile — about a dozen states require every participant to agree, and the machinery that knows which rule applies where is the same machinery call recording waits on. **Dial-in by telephone** has nothing to bridge to until the telephony port has an adapter. **Rooms larger than twelve** are capped by a constant rather than by the unit, which scales: what is missing is the active-speaker layout, the simulcast layer selection and the bandwidth adaptation that make a large room usable, and those are a slice rather than a setting. Each is a node in the feature ledger, so the sequencing is recorded where the rest of the program's is.
