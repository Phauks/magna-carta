**A deployment names its tier, refuses to serve production through a development adapter, and verifies every precondition of a firm's upload path from the server side — repairing what is idempotent and reporting what is not, before a member is offered Upload.**

Production behaviour has been selected by which optional secrets happen to be set — provisioner, presigner, scan engine, human verification, flags, the object-plane credential — and nothing computed, reported or refused the combination. Each selection was principled on its own; together they allowed a deployment to run the development provisioner against real firms, recording bucket names for buckets that were never created, with no signal. Separately, the one hop of an upload that bypasses every Worker (the browser's presigned PUT, which must bypass them: the request-body cap forbids proxying) is unobservable from the platform, and five different faults — no bucket, no CORS policy, a stale origin list, an S3 key scoped to the wrong bucket, a URL outliving its credential — produce one identical symptom. A week was spent diagnosing one of them from outside, three times.

Resolution: an explicit `DEPLOYMENT_TIER`; one `deploymentMode(env)` that every port's wiring reads from, reported on the status surface and refused at startup where a production tier would serve through a development adapter; and `verifyUploadReadiness(firm)`, a server-side sequence of checks over the bucket's existence, its three settings and the key's ability to sign for it, run as the provisioner's last step before activation, as a daily repairing sweep over every active firm, as a diagnostic route, and as the refusal the file manager's Upload item carries when a firm is not ready. Design: `docs/specs/2026-09-10-upload-readiness-and-deployment-modes-design.md`.

Rejected: proxying uploads through the Worker to observe them (impossible past the request-body cap); a sweep that creates missing buckets (a firm without one was provisioned under the wrong adapter, and the answer is to say so).
