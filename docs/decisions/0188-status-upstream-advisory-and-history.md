**Upstream advisory, and a sample-counted uptime record.** The service-status ADR made `overall` "the worst active component/provider status combined with any active event impact", carving out only `status_unavailable`. In practice that meant a Cloudflare incident published our page as degraded while every probe of ours was green. This amends that rule and adds the history store the renderer was already built with a seam for.

**(1) A provider's status is context, never a verdict.** `overall` is now `worst(our components, our active event impacts)` and takes no provider input at all. A provider whose own feed reports trouble raises an `upstreamAdvisory` on the published snapshot — the troubled providers plus the worst of their statuses — which the page renders as its own surface between the verdict band and the component groups: a quiet card on the sunken surface, never a colored band, carrying only the provider's own severity dot and a link to the status page that is authoritative for it. The reasoning is that the page answers exactly one question — "is Magna Carta out of service?" — and only our own checks can answer it. If a provider's incident does take us down, our probes are what will say so, and the verdict moves for that reason rather than by inheritance. A provider whose feed is unreachable (`status_unavailable`) remains honest uncertainty about them and raises no advisory whatsoever.

**(2) One effective status, shared by the row and the bar.** An operator could declare a major event naming `search` without also setting a component override, leaving that row — and its uptime tick — green beneath a banner announcing an outage. `effectiveComponentStatus(derived, override, activeEventsAffecting)` takes the worst of the three and is used by both `mergeSnapshot` (painting the row) and the cron's history append (painting the tick). The consequence is intended and visible: declaring an event now colors the components it names, where before it moved only the overall verdict.

**(3) Sample-counting, not day-summarizing.** The ten-minute cron increments a per-component, per-day tally of observed statuses in a third KV document, `status:history` — cron-owned, like `status:derived`, and versioned independently of the two live snapshots so a history-shape change can never fail-parse them. Uptime is `operational ÷ (samples − maintenance)` over the window; a day's tick takes the worst status with a nonzero count. This buys three properties a day-summarized store cannot have: a ten-minute blip costs ten minutes of the ratio rather than a whole day, a cron that never ran contributes no sample rather than a green one, and a component with nothing countable reads "No data" rather than a fabricated 100%. An imperfect record is floored at 99.99% rather than rounded up to 100% — a page that rounds away its only outage is worse than one showing no number.

**(4) Maintenance is excluded from uptime, and still visible.** Planned work leaves both sides of the ratio — charging it as downtime punishes doing it well — while its day still wears the calm informational blue, so the record shows that it happened.

**(5) The store adds no binding.** `status:history` lives in the existing `STATUS_KV` namespace, written only by the cron in `apps/worker` and read by `apps/status-worker` alongside the other two keys. The status worker's single-binding fate-isolation invariant is unchanged, as is its never-`5xx` rule: a corrupt or absent history document parses to an empty document and the bars render as no data.

**Considered & rejected.** **Keeping providers in the rollup but relabeling the verdict copy** — the verdict's color and lamp are the one-second read; no wording rescues a red band that is factually about someone else. **Day worst-status storage** (one status per component per day) — smaller, but a ten-minute blip paints a whole day and the percentage is too coarse to be worth publishing. **Deriving uptime from declared service events** — matches what an operator acknowledged, but an outage the probes caught and nobody declared would show as 100%. **A `StatusHistory` Durable Object** — stronger consistency under concurrent writers, but the cron is the sole writer at a ten-minute cadence, and the status worker would need a DO binding its own invariant forbids. **Rendering every day of the archive window** — the genre's literal convention, but eighty-eight rows reading "No incidents reported" is scrolling, not information; one line accounting for the quiet days says the same thing.

**Consequences.** The page can now show that the platform is normally reliable, not merely that it is up right now, and it can no longer be made to announce someone else's incident as its own. Amends the rollup rule of the service-status ADR. Deferred: an uptime figure on the overall verdict, per-tick drill-down into the day's events, and a history window longer than 90 days (the document grows linearly and would need a rollup tier).
