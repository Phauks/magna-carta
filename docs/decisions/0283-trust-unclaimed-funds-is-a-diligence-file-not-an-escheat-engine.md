**Unclaimed client funds are a diligence file and a read. Nothing computes a deadline, nothing sweeps, and no composer here moves money.**

**Context:** The recommendation on the table (owner flag T16) was "aging visibility now, escheat workflow later". Primary-source research (`docs/research/2026-08-07-nevada-unclaimed-client-funds-escheat.md`) then found that the escheat half of that split does not exist in Nevada: NRS 120A.500(6)(f) exempts "a trust account established on behalf of a client", clearly designated as such, from the presumption of abandonment, so the Uniform Unclaimed Property Act's dormancy machinery never engages against one and the State Treasurer's own manual confirms it declines such funds. What Nevada supplies instead is NRPC 1.15(f): a **duty** to take reasonable efforts to locate and distribute, floored at regular *and* certified mail, telephone, *and* email; and, after five years **from receipt of the property**, a **permission** — "may remit" — to send the funds to the State Bar's Clients Security Fund with a certification and supporting documentary evidence. The duty is the searching. The remitting is optional and terminal.

The owner's direction is a workflow with manual confirmation on any money movement.

**Decision:**

(1) **This is not an escheat feature and nothing in it counts down.** There is no eligibility column, no deadline field, and no surface that says a firm must act by a date. Framing a permissive five-year gate as a deadline would assert an obligation no rule imposes, on a record that a disciplinary body may one day read.

(2) **Dormancy is a read-time comparison, and the window is an argument.** `readDormantTrustBalances` lists matter ledgers under one trust account that still hold money and whose most recent movement is on or before a date **the caller states**. There is no default period. Three reasons, and the third decides it: the periods differ by jurisdiction and several gate a permission rather than an obligation, so there is no number the platform is entitled to pick; a stored flag would need a per-firm sweep to maintain and would be stale between runs; and a platform that writes "dormant" onto a client's ledger has asserted something about the firm's conduct it cannot know — the firm may have spoken to that client this morning in a way this platform never saw. This is the same posture and the same reasoning `packages/calendar/src/overdue.ts` holds for an overdue deadline.

(3) **Two dates, because the clocks differ.** The read returns the effective date of the **earliest receipt still sitting on the ledger** alongside the last activity date. The rules' clock runs from receipt of the property, not from the last thing that happened on the matter, so a ledger topped up last year may still hold money received five years ago — and a view that reported only one of the two would answer a different question from the one the duty asks. The window filters on activity; the receipt date is reported and never filtered on, because filtering on it alone would surface active matters.

(4) **The diligence file is the artifact, and it is append-only.** `trust_unclaimed_funds_contact_attempt` records one locate attempt when it was made: the channel (the four the rule enumerates, plus a review of the firm's own records, plus an escape for whatever a board adds later), the date, **the address or number actually used**, the outcome, and who recorded it. "We wrote to the client" is not evidence; "we wrote to 14 Elm Street on 3 March, returned undelivered" is, and it is impossible to reconstruct two years later. A record review carries no contact detail and every other channel must, enforced by a shape CHECK.

(5) **Closure is a separate row, and open is derived.** A review is open when no closure names it — the derivation the hold release and the disbursement decision already use — so no status column exists anywhere that can disagree with the rows, and `UNIQUE` on `review_id` makes two concurrent closures produce one. Four outcomes, and `search_continues` is a first-class one: it is the honest end of most rounds of diligence, and an evidence trail needs it to exist rather than forcing a firm to record a conclusion it did not reach.

(6) **THE MANUAL-CONFIRMATION GATE IS THE ABSENCE OF THE INPUTS.** Closing a review as a distribution names a `trust_movement` that already exists — a disbursement somebody composed, authorized under the firm's policy, and committed through the ordinary path. `composeTrustUnclaimedFundsReviewClosure` receives no amount, no payee, no effective date and no method: there is nothing it could assemble into a ledger entry even if a future author wanted it to. It writes exactly one row and emits nothing. The movement must be a disbursement **from the ledger under review**, checked in the composer and independently by a trigger — a closure naming somebody else's payment would record a client as paid out of another client's money and would read, on the certification a firm eventually signs, as a search that succeeded.

(7) **One open review per client ledger.** Two concurrent diligence files over one balance split the evidence trail in half, and the certification is about the whole search. Because open is derived rather than stored the rule cannot be a UNIQUE index, so it is a read in the composer plus a fitness query over stored rows — the same arrangement the hold model and the one-line-one-entry match rule use.

**Consequences:** A firm can see which client ledgers have gone quiet, on a window it chooses, and can build the contemporaneous evidence record the rule demands. Nothing on this path can move money, and the property is structural rather than reviewed. Deferred, and written into `docs/specs/2026-08-12-trust-bank-charges-and-unclaimed-funds.md` rather than half-built: the Clients Security Fund remittance packet and its certification (which is a document-assembly problem over this evidence, and terminal in a way that wants its own gate); the jurisdiction profile's `UnclaimedFunds` shape, which models neither a permissive remittance, nor a from-receipt clock, nor a destination that will not hold funds for future recovery; the entirely separate NRS 120A holder regime for the firm's **non-trust** property (November-1 reports, 60-to-120-day notices, negative reports, seven-year retention) which must not share logic or copy with this path; and the route and interface layers.
