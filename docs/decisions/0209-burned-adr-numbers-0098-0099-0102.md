**The three vacant register numbers are burned, not missing: reserved, vacated, never written.**

**Context:** The register runs contiguously from ADR-0001 except for three holes — 0098, 0099, and 0102 — and one live document still points into one of them: the slice 2·1 inbound-upload design reserves and then cites ADR-0102 for its async bulk-download build job. A hole in a register that is meant to be contiguous reads as a lost file, and a lost decision is worth hunting for; this entry is the result of the hunt, so the next reader does not repeat it.

Reserving a block of numbers in a design document, before the decisions in it are written, is what produced all three. A reservation is a claim on a number made by prose, and nothing enforces it: two lines of work reserving from the same counter cannot see each other, and the loser's reservation is silently vacated.

**What the history shows.** Reconstructed from `git log`; no file named `docs/decisions/0098-*`, `0099-*`, or `0102-*` has ever existed on any branch, and none of the three numbers has ever appeared in the register.

- **0098 and 0099** were the tail of an ADR-0097–0099 block reserved by the email-filing draft design. That line and `main` had independently reserved 0096 and 0097; the collision was cleared by renumbering the email-filing reservation wholesale to ADR-0104–0106, and 0098/0099 went with it. Nothing was ever written against them, and the email-filing block later moved again — 0104–0106 are now held by unrelated decisions.
- **0102** was the tail of an ADR-0100–0102 block reserved by the slice 2·1 inbound-upload design. One day later, the collision-clearing renumber above independently assigned 0100 to the company-email decision and 0101 to the Tools area, taking two of the three numbers 2·1 had reserved out from under it. Its 0100 and 0101 subjects (the scan engine, and untrusted-file hardening) were later recorded under other numbers; its 0102 subject, the async bulk-download build job, was **built without an ADR of its own** — the shape it was reserved for is decided across ADR-0027 (bulk download streams store-mode ZIP64 with an async-job seam), ADR-0082 (the `client-zip` implementation and that seam's terms), and ADR-0146 (the two-lane bundle build). The design document's citation of ADR-0102 is a citation of a reservation, not of a decision.

**Decision:** 0098, 0099, and 0102 stay permanently vacant. They are never assigned to a future decision, and the register is never renumbered to close the gap — an ADR number, once printed in a commit message, a spec, or a code review, is an address, and reusing one makes every past reference ambiguous forever. This entry is the register's answer to "where are they?", so the absence is documented rather than mysterious.

**Considered & rejected:** **reissuing the three numbers to the next pending fragments** (cheapest, and wrong — the numbers appear in merged history and in a live design document, so a reissue would make those references point at unrelated decisions); **renumbering the register to be contiguous** (breaks every existing citation to every later ADR, for cosmetic tidiness); **back-writing an ADR-0102 for the async bulk-download build job** (the decision is genuinely recorded, across ADR-0027/0082/0146 — a fourth entry restating them would create the duplicate home this repository's documentation rule exists to prevent); **leaving the gap undocumented** (the state we were in; it costs every future reader the same investigation).

**Consequences:** the assembler assigns numbers from the end of the sequence and never backfills, so no future fragment can land on a burned number by accident. The slice 2·1 design keeps its ADR-0102 citation as the point-in-time process record it is; a reader who follows it arrives here. And the underlying hazard is now named: **a design document reserving a block of ADR numbers is a claim nothing enforces**, which is precisely why numbers are assigned at merge from committed fragments rather than reserved in prose — reserve nothing, write the fragment, let the assembler number it.
