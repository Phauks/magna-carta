**The trust sub-ledger is a three-level tree (trust-account liability root, client node, matter leaf) with postings on matter leaves only, and trust is built before the pre-alpha per-firm baseline collapse.**

**Context:** The trust-accounting analysis of 2026-08-04 put seventeen decisions to the owner and named two of them as gating everything else: the sub-ledger granularity, and whether trust builds before or after the pre-alpha per-firm schema baseline collapse. Both were answered on 2026-08-05. The remaining fifteen flags stay open and are deliberately not defaulted by this decision or by the code it authorizes.

**Decision:** (1) **Three levels, not two.** A trust account carries one client-liability root; each trust client gets a node under it; each of that client's matters gets a leaf under the node. The rules speak of a "client ledger" while the firm operates on matters and the matter-close hook is a matter-level event, so a two-level tree makes one of those two readings a derivation — either a matter-close zero-balance check has to ask about a portion of a shared balance the ledger does not know, or every client-ledger report has to roll matters up. The third tier costs one account per trust client and makes both direct.

(2) **Postings land on matter leaves.** The upper two tiers are roll-ups, and a roll-up's stored balance stays at zero forever because a posting batch only writes balances for the accounts its lines touch and lines touch leaves. That is a trap as much as a design: any report reading a parent's stored balance silently reads zero rather than the total it wanted. The roll-up is a `SUM` over descendant leaves, and the other half of the rule — that no non-leaf account carries a non-zero stored balance — ships as a ledger fitness query in the same change that creates the first parent.

(3) **The floor lives on the leaves and is written in the creation batch.** `min_balance_minor = 0` on every matter leaf, set in the same batch that creates the account and its balance row, never as a follow-up write; a leaf that exists for one batch without its floor is a leaf that can be overdrawn in that batch. The client-level no-negative rule then follows from the leaves: a sum of accounts that cannot go negative cannot go negative.

(4) **This is the least reversible decision in the subsystem.** `createAccount` refuses a parent that already carries postings — the creation-side half of the leaf-only invariant, and correct — so a client tier cannot be inserted above matter leaves that have already posted. Getting the tier order wrong is not an expand-contract migration; it is a rebuild of the account dimension.

(5) **Trust builds now, before the baseline collapse and before the first alpha firm.** Once a real firm's database exists, the tree shape is live client-money data and every correction is an expand-contract migration against it; before the collapse, a wrong shape is absorbed into the baseline. That makes the window the reason to build now rather than later. The same gate cuts the other way — the collapse waits on all in-flight slice branches merging — so the schema lands in the fewest, largest migrations the work allows, early in the slice rather than one per sub-slice, and the branches merge rather than parking.

**Consequences:** The sub-ledger registry, the composition guard, holds, and the transfer link land as one migration pair against the current per-firm lineage. The firm-funds carve-out, the `cost_entry` trust link, the earned-fee transfer precondition, protected bank identifiers, the matter-close gate, reconciliation, retention, unclaimed funds, and the jurisdiction rollout all remain open owner decisions; the code ships refusals rather than defaults wherever one of them would otherwise have to be guessed.
