**Traversal depth becomes a typed per-edge-class budget with a recorded closure cap, and every stop the search makes is written on the record.**

**Context:** The shipped `expandGraph` is a correct 0-1 breadth-first search: ordinary edges cost one hop against a `depth` budget defaulting to 1, and corporate-family edges cost zero and are therefore traversed transitively **without bound**. The zero-cost treatment is right — a rename, merger, or successor is the same legal person continuing, and stopping a corporate chain at a hop limit would miss exactly what the search exists to find. "Without bound" is not right: a Directory holding a real corporate group can put thousands of entities in one closure, and the shipped run-check issues one or two subject reads plus four matching queries **per expanded entity**, so a large closure is thousands of queries in one invocation. Separately, one integer cannot express the two things a firm actually wants to say — follow corporate renames as far as they go, but do not walk from an opposing party's spouse to her employer's other directors.

**Decision:** (1) **A budget per edge class**, firm-configurable and recorded in scope: corporate family (`corporate_parent`/`corporate_subsidiary`/`corporate_successor`) stays transitive at zero cost; control (`principal_of_org`/`affiliation`), family (`spouse`/`family_relationship`), and representation (`representation`/`role_assignment`) each default to one hop. Budgets are defaults, not floors — a caller may go deeper, and going shallower is honoured and recorded, exactly as the shipped `depth` already is. The shipped `depth` field keeps working as the non-corporate budget when no per-class budget is supplied, so no caller breaks. (2) **A per-run closure cap.** On reaching it, expansion stops and the check records `expansion_truncated` with the entities reached, the caps in force, and the size of the unexplored frontier; the posture read and the report surface it. **A truncated expansion is never reported as a completed one** — this is the depth analogue of the shipped rule that `unavailable` is never `searched and clean`. (3) **Expired edges are traversed**, with the expiry carried on the path step. Rule 1.9 is about *former* clients and former relationships, so a design that stopped at currency would drop the dimension the rules care most about; the reviewer sees the expiry and weighs it. (4) **Degree-based hub suppression is refused.** Skipping any node with more than N edges is the obvious optimization and it silently narrows the search — a clearing bank that appears on five hundred matters is precisely where a positional conflict lives. Instead the **firm** may mark an entity non-expandable, an attributable act recorded verbatim in every affected check's scope; the entity is still **matched** as a party, and only traversal **through** it stops. (5) **The check row records the resolved channel set verbatim** — schemes run, name roles included, match keys compared, identifier types probed and skipped, per-class budgets, the cap, and any marked entities encountered — so `strictness` becomes the human-facing preset name rather than the only evidence of what happened. A log that records a word means something different in March and September; a log that records the resolved set does not. (6) **The N+1 is removed in the same slice**, not after a measurement: one subject-facts query per node table over the whole expanded set, and each blocking channel run **once** over the set rather than once per entity, making the query count O(channels) rather than O(entities x channels). An equivalence test proving the batched matcher returns exactly the per-entity matcher's hit set over the same corpus is the refactor's correctness proof and gates removal of the old path.

**Considered & rejected:** _keeping one integer and raising the default_ (says nothing about which edges, and doubles the worst case for every class at once); _capping the corporate closure by hop count_ (a merger chain of any length is one legal person; a hop cap would drop the successor entity the search is for); _skipping high-degree nodes automatically_ (a silent narrowing, and the nodes it skips are the interesting ones); _refusing to traverse expired edges_ (drops the former-client dimension); _treating a truncated run as a completed one with a smaller result_ (the failure this subsystem exists to prevent, arriving as a performance decision); _deferring the batching until the load profile shows a regression_ (the regression only appears once the caps are raised, and the batching is a foundation the widening depends on — golden rule 10 governs optimization, not foundations).

**Consequences:** "Configurable search depth" finally names something precise, and every bound the platform applies is legible on the record it produces. The load-profile harness gains a deep corporate graph (chain and fan, sized past the default cap) and the Tantivy and Rust flip conditions become numbers read off it. **F6** (the budget numbers and the cap), **F7** (expired-edge traversal), and **F9** (whether a firm may mark an entity non-expandable) are owner decisions; all three ship as settings with the design's recommended defaults. Deepens **ADR-0192** (which fixed depth at one hop plus transitive corporate closure); builds on **ADR-0085** (the edge types), **ADR-0039** (off the hot path), **ADR-0083** (the load-profile harness).
