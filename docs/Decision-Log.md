# Magna Carta — Decision Log

**Status:** Living record · **Last updated:** 2026-06-08 · **Owner:** Trevor Goldsmith (chief worker)

The authoritative register of architectural decisions (ADRs) for Magna Carta. The [white paper](./Magna-Carta-White-Paper.md) holds the narrative design and full rationale; this log is the durable list of _what was decided_ and its _status_. Each entry is the seed of an ADR — terse by default, expandable to the full template when a decision is contested, revisited, or superseded.

---

## Standard

**Identity.** Each decision has a stable ID `ADR-NNNN`, assigned once in sequence and **never renumbered or reused**. IDs are independent of white-paper section numbers, so reorganizing the paper never disturbs a decision's identity. The `· <area>` label on each entry names the white-paper area that carries its detail — **by name, not section number**, because section numbers shift whenever the paper is reorganized (see the cross-reference policy in the root [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md)).

**Status lifecycle.** `Proposed → Accepted → (Superseded | Deprecated | Rejected)`. Accepted entries are **immutable** — to change a decision, add a _new_ ADR that supersedes it (mark `Supersedes ADR-NNNN` on the new one and `Superseded by ADR-MMMM` on the old), never edit the original. Rejected alternatives are kept inline; they record roads not taken so they are not relitigated.

**Adding a decision.** Append the next sequential ID in the **same commit** as the change that settles it (the docs-in-commit rule). One line suffices for a clear-cut call; promote to the full template when the decision is weighty or contested.

**Entry template (lightweight — the default):**

> `**ADR-NNNN** · <Status> · <white-paper area> — <decision, naming the key rejected alternatives>.`

**Full template (contested / superseding decisions):** Status · Date · Context · Decision · Considered options (pros/cons) · Consequences (positive / negative / risks) · Related ADRs. Keep it under two pages; link back to the white-paper area for detail rather than duplicating it.

> **Numbering history.** The original seed list mislabeled three consecutive decisions as "26" (Encryption, Archive handling, Design system). They are now assigned distinct stable IDs **ADR-0026/0027/0028**, shifting the later entries; this is a one-time correction. IDs are frozen from here on.

- **ADR-0035** · Accepted · Document versioning — **Document versioning & storage tiering:** revision history is **app-managed version objects**, not R2 native versioning (native lifecycle has **no noncurrent-version action** and gives no per-version storage-class control). Decouple a churning **`current`** key (overwritten on every save, Standard) from **immutable `vN` snapshots** cut deliberately — driven by Collabora's **`X-COOL-WOPI-IsAutosave`** header: version on **explicit Save + session-close**, **not per-autosave** (optional debounced checkpoint). Superseded versions transition **Standard → Infrequent Access** in place via S3 **`CopyObject`** (`x-amz-storage-class: STANDARD_IA` — server-side, no retrieval fee, ~33% storage cut on the coldest segment). **Tiering, never deletion** — every version kept forever, IA equally durable/available, safe under legal hold and append-only. **No compression** (corpus already compressed; would break the inline-preview range-stream path). WOPI `current`-overwrite is timestamp-guarded (`X-COOL-WOPI-Timestamp` → **409 / `COOLStatusCode 1010`** on mismatch). Native R2 versioning rejected.

- **ADR-0046** · Accepted · 2026-06-04 · Encryption & keys — **Post-quantum cryptographic posture — agility-first; the symmetric corpus is already quantum-safe; signatures deferred behind seams.** **Context:** the encrypted secret set (PII, plugin credentials, TOTP seeds) and the decades-long confidentiality lifetime of legal data (estate/sealed matters) raise the **harvest-now-decrypt-later (HNDL)** question. **Decision:** treat post-quantum readiness as primarily an _agility_ property, not a cipher swap. (1) **Keep AES-256 as the bulk cipher** — symmetric/hash crypto (AES-256-GCM, AES-KW, HKDF, HMAC) is not broken by Shor and only halved by Grover (AES-256 → 128-bit, infeasible; CNSA 2.0 approves AES-256), so the at-rest corpus needs **no migration** and there is no "more quantum-resistant" bulk cipher to adopt. (2) **HNDL is a transport threat**, met by Cloudflare's hybrid **X25519MLKEM768** TLS — on for inbound and, by default, Worker→origin egress (Phase-2 "PQC-only" downgrade protection targeted mid-2026); third-party plugin endpoints that won't yet negotiate PQC TLS are an inherited gap that is **logged**, not silently accepted. (3) **Public-key signatures** (passkeys/WebAuthn, the PAdES e-signature seal) are forge-able only once a quantum computer exists — a _migrate-before-then_, not retroactive, risk — so they are **seamed but deferred**, riding upstream standards (FIPS 204 ML-DSA / 205 SLH-DSA / 206 FN-DSA; COSE algorithm IDs for the WebAuthn path; ETSI/CA support for PAdES ML-DSA, est. 2026+). (4) **Crypto-agility is the mechanism**: every ciphertext, wrapped key, and signature carries an explicit **algorithm-version identifier**, making a future hybrid **ML-KEM** key-wrap or **ML-DSA/SLH-DSA** signature a versioned rotation behind the existing `protected()` DAL chokepoint, the envelope-rotation machinery (ADR-0026), and the e-signature port (ADR-0045) — never a rewrite. (5) **Prefer symmetric where there is a choice** — session signing stays HMAC (already quantum-safe); AES stays **256-bit everywhere** and KDF/HMAC hashing moves to **SHA-384** for CNSA-2.0 margin where free; the deferred external-KMS-rooted KEK (ADR-0026 _Honest limits_) gains "supports PQC-wrapped keys" as a selection criterion. **Considered & rejected:** swapping the bulk cipher to **ChaCha20-Poly1305** or **AES-256-GCM-SIV** (equally quantum-safe — a performance / nonce-misuse-robustness choice, not a quantum one, and not natively in Workers Web Crypto); adopting app-layer **ML-KEM now** (the app KEK is a symmetric Worker secret wrapped by AES-KW — there is no asymmetric key-establishment to harden until an asymmetric KEK is introduced); **doing nothing** until standards settle (forfeits the cheap agility seam and leaves transport HNDL unaddressed). **Consequences:** at-rest data is quantum-safe today; transport HNDL is mitigated by the platform; the only real migration work (signatures, an asymmetric KEK) is bounded, standards-gated, and reduced to adapter / field-version swaps; the cost is the discipline of carrying algorithm identifiers and the AES-256 / SHA-384 mandate. Extends **ADR-0026** (encryption & keys); touches **ADR-0004** (auth/passkeys) and **ADR-0045** (e-signature seal).

- **ADR-0070** · Accepted · 2026-06-04 · Architectural integrity — **Module-reference policy — no convenience barrel files; reference modules by their specific source path within a package, and import other packages only through their `package.json` `exports` map (a curated, side-effect-free public surface + explicit subpaths, `"sideEffects": false`); enforced structurally by the Oxlint no-barrel rule + dependency-cruiser boundary/no-cycle rules, not discipline.** **Context:** the import convention starts at the first package (slice 0·1) and is expensive to retrofit across a large monorepo. Barrel files (`index.ts` re-export hubs) are a verified perf/correctness sink on this exact stack: Vite's dev server crawls/transforms every module a barrel re-exports for a single import (Vite's own perf guide says avoid them), mixed inline+re-export barrels break tree-shaking (Rolldown can pull a whole barrel + its heavy deps into a shared chunk), and barrels breed circular deps and slow `tsc`/IDE. Rolldown 1.0's lazy-barrel optimization helps at _build_ time but does not fix the dev-server crawl or every tree-shaking edge case, so the guidance remains "avoid." A specific case of the _Architectural integrity & coherence_ boundary-rules category (ADR-0051), not new machinery. **Decision:** (1) **No convenience barrels in source** — intra-package imports reference the specific module (`./trust/rounding.ts`), never an intermediate `index.ts`. (2) **A package's public API = its `package.json` `exports` map** — one small, **curated, side-effect-free** entry and/or explicit subpaths (`@magna-carta/money`, `@magna-carta/money/rounding`), with **`"sideEffects": false`** so tree-shaking still drops unused re-exports; this curated boundary file is the _only_ acceptable barrel-like module, kept flat (no nested re-export chains). (3) **Cross-package imports touch only another package's declared `exports`**, never its internals. (4) **Enforced, not trusted** — the **Oxlint no-barrel rule** on source (already in `vp check`) + **dependency-cruiser / ts-arch** boundary + `no-cycle` rules in CI (the ADR-0051 fitness-function tier), so the wrong import fails CI rather than relying on memory. **Considered & rejected:** **convenience barrels relying on Rolldown's lazy-barrel optimization** (keeps the dev-server crawl, tree-shaking edge cases, and circular-dep risk — optimizes a problem better avoided); **zero barrels anywhere incl. package entries** (maximally safe but loses the curated, discoverable package surface and makes consumer imports more verbose for no real tree-shaking gain once `sideEffects:false` + `exports` are in place); **deep cross-package imports into internals** (defeats encapsulation and the boundary fitness functions). **Consequences:** clean package boundaries with no barrel perf/tree-shaking tax, circular deps caught structurally, and a fast dev server as the module graph grows; cost is slightly more explicit import paths and the per-package `exports`/`sideEffects` discipline (a scaffold makes it uniform). Practitioner detail in [`docs/conventions/typescript.md`](conventions/typescript.md); a one-line nod in the white-paper _Architectural integrity & coherence_ boundary-rules text; first applied in the _Phase 0 · walking skeleton_ spec. Reuses **ADR-0051** (boundary rules / fitness functions / coherence ordering) and the Vite+/Oxc toolchain; binds every future `packages/*` and `plugins/*` module.

- **ADR-0072** · Accepted · 2026-06-09 · Financial Core — **Append-only ledger core — posting model: direction + always-positive amount lines, a structural account model (no CoA product), materialized per-account balances with bounds-CHECK as the D1 batch-abort mechanism, dual entry dates (civil `effective_date` + UTC `recorded_at`), first-class linked reversals; realized as `@magna-carta/ledger` (first domain package) with the `@magna-carta/fixtures` harness calling the domain write paths directly.** **Context:** ADR-0008 fixed the append-only double-entry ledger as the Financial Core's foundation but left the posting model, line encoding, balance mechanism, and correction semantics undesigned; slice 0·3 builds the ledger as the first real consumer of the slice 0·2 primitives, under D1's no-interactive-transactions reality (ADR-0063/0064 amendments: every write is one atomic `batch()`), and must leave the no-negative-trust invariant (ADR-0009, Phase 3) enforceable with zero write-path retrofit. The line encoding is permanent-storage representation — the expensive-to-migrate part (the ADR-0061 logic). **Decision:** (1) **Line encoding = direction + always-positive amount** (`direction TEXT 'debit'|'credit'` + `amount_minor INTEGER CHECK (> 0)` — the Modern Treasury shape): structurally eliminates the silent sign-flip (a flipped sign still sums to zero; direction-as-data must be _stated_, negative/zero lines are impossible at the schema), keeps permanent rows journal-legible for bar audits/exports, and keeps amounts in the domain `allocate()` (ADR-0061) operates in; the balance aggregate is one CASE expression written once in the domain layer. (2) **Structural account model, no CoA product:** `ledger_account` carries type (asset/liability/equity/income/expense; normal balance derived in code, never a column), single currency (lines inherit — no currency column on lines), a parent/subledger seam (a Phase-3 client trust ledger is just a child account row), and soft-close `active` (accounts are never deleted); chart-of-accounts management UI/numbering is Phase 3 on this unchanged schema. (3) **Materialized `account_balance` now, bounds seam included:** one row per account in **normal-balance terms** (positive = normal, so trust bounds read as the rules are written), updated in the same batch as every posting, with nullable `min`/`max` bound columns and a CHECK — **a failed CHECK is D1's only mechanism for aborting a batch on a business condition**, so when Phase 3 sets a trust child's min to 0 an overdraw rolls back the entire posting atomically with zero retrofit; a fitness query proves SUM(lines) = balance. (4) **Dual dates by kind (ADR-0055):** backdatable civil `effective_date` (`YYYY-MM-DD` — what statements/reconciliation key off; no zone column, zone resolution is a boundary concern) + immutable system-set UTC `recorded_at` (audit truth; the gap between them is audit signal); no future-dating beyond the injected clock's today. (5) **First-class reversals:** `reverses_entry_id` UNIQUE FK + `reverseEntry` composing the exact mirror; reversed status derived from the link, never a flag; double-reversal structurally impossible under race (the loser's batch rolls back); the Phase-3 adjustment workflow composes on these primitives additively. (6) **Validation = ledger invariants only:** balanced ≥ 2-line single-currency entries, active leaf accounts only (parents are roll-ups), non-empty description; consumer rules (trust bounds values, period close, type-posting warnings) defer to their consumers atop existing seams. **two-column debit/credit** (journal-familiar but a per-row XOR CHECK, two-column aggregates everywhere, and a column-swap is still silent); **derive balances by SUM until trust needs them** (forces Phase 3 to retrofit materialization into every posting batch right when stakes are highest); **convention-only corrections** (lineage in prose, double-reversals possible, Phase 3 builds the linked version anyway); **full adjustment workflow now** (Phase-3 product surface with no consumer; composes later on these primitives); **ledger inside `data-access`** (mixes domain rules into the generic access layer — the exact seam ADR-0071 split on); **a full timestamp for `effective_date`** (drags in a zone; a check "dated June 27" renders June 26/28 by viewer — the precise bug ADR-0055 forbids). **Consequences:** the Financial Core's foundation is posting-correct by construction — sign-flips impossible, balance enforcement atomic, corrections linked, audit dates honest — and Phase 3 trust/billing/GL land on seams (bounds, effective-date, reversal links, subledger accounts) that already exist;
