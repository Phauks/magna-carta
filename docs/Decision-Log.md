# Magna Carta — Decision Log

**Status:** Living record · **Last updated:** 2026-06-17 · **Owner:** Trevor Goldsmith (chief worker)

The authoritative register of architectural decisions (ADRs) for Magna Carta. The [white paper](./Magna-Carta-White-Paper.md) holds the narrative design and full rationale; this log is the durable list of _what was decided_ and its _status_. Each entry is the seed of an ADR — terse by default, expandable to the full template when a decision is contested, revisited, or superseded.

---

## Standard

**Identity.** Each decision has a stable ID `ADR-NNNN`, assigned once in sequence and **never renumbered or reused**. IDs are independent of white-paper section numbers, so reorganizing the paper never disturbs a decision's identity. The `· <area>` label on each entry names the white-paper area that carries its detail — **by name, not section number**, because section numbers shift whenever the paper is reorganized (see the cross-reference policy in the root [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md)).

**Status lifecycle.** `Proposed → Accepted → (Superseded | Deprecated | Rejected)`. Accepted entries are **immutable** — to change a decision, add a _new_ ADR that supersedes it (mark `Supersedes ADR-NNNN` on the new one and `Superseded by ADR-MMMM` on the old), never edit the original. Rejected alternatives are kept inline; they record roads not taken so they are not relitigated.

**Adding a decision.** On a branch, add `docs/decisions/pending/<slug>.md` (no number yet; reference it as `ADR-<slug>` until merged). The number is assigned at merge by `vp run decisions-build` — once and never changed. Per-file decisions in `docs/decisions/` are the canonical home; the register in the section below is generated by that command and must not be edited by hand. One line suffices for a clear-cut call; promote to the full template when the decision is weighty or contested.

**Entry template (lightweight — the default):**

> `**ADR-NNNN** · <Status> · <white-paper area> — <decision, naming the key rejected alternatives>.`

**Full template (contested / superseding decisions):** Status · Date · Context · Decision · Considered options (pros/cons) · Consequences (positive / negative / risks) · Related ADRs. Keep it under two pages; link back to the white-paper area for detail rather than duplicating it.

> **Numbering history.** The original seed list mislabeled three consecutive decisions as "26" (Encryption, Archive handling, Design system). They are now assigned distinct stable IDs **ADR-0026/0027/0028**, shifting the later entries; this is a one-time correction. IDs are frozen from here on.

- **ADR-0046** · Accepted · 2026-06-04 · Encryption & keys — **Post-quantum cryptographic posture — agility-first; the symmetric corpus is already quantum-safe; signatures deferred behind seams.** **Context:** the encrypted secret set (PII, plugin credentials, TOTP seeds) and the decades-long confidentiality lifetime of legal data (estate/sealed matters) raise the **harvest-now-decrypt-later (HNDL)** question. **Decision:** treat post-quantum readiness as primarily an _agility_ property, not a cipher swap. (1) **Keep AES-256 as the bulk cipher** — symmetric/hash crypto (AES-256-GCM, AES-KW, HKDF, HMAC) is not broken by Shor and only halved by Grover (AES-256 → 128-bit, infeasible; CNSA 2.0 approves AES-256), so the at-rest corpus needs **no migration** and there is no "more quantum-resistant" bulk cipher to adopt. (2) **HNDL is a transport threat**, met by Cloudflare's hybrid **X25519MLKEM768** TLS — on for inbound and, by default, Worker→origin egress (Phase-2 "PQC-only" downgrade protection targeted mid-2026); third-party plugin endpoints that won't yet negotiate PQC TLS are an inherited gap that is **logged**, not silently accepted. (3) **Public-key signatures** (passkeys/WebAuthn, the PAdES e-signature seal) are forge-able only once a quantum computer exists — a _migrate-before-then_, not retroactive, risk — so they are **seamed but deferred**, riding upstream standards (FIPS 204 ML-DSA / 205 SLH-DSA / 206 FN-DSA; COSE algorithm IDs for the WebAuthn path; ETSI/CA support for PAdES ML-DSA, est. 2026+). (4) **Crypto-agility is the mechanism**: every ciphertext, wrapped key, and signature carries an explicit **algorithm-version identifier**, making a future hybrid **ML-KEM** key-wrap or **ML-DSA/SLH-DSA** signature a versioned rotation behind the existing `protected()` DAL chokepoint, the envelope-rotation machinery (ADR-0026), and the e-signature port (ADR-0045) — never a rewrite. (5) **Prefer symmetric where there is a choice** — session signing stays HMAC (already quantum-safe); AES stays **256-bit everywhere** and KDF/HMAC hashing moves to **SHA-384** for CNSA-2.0 margin where free; the deferred external-KMS-rooted KEK (ADR-0026 _Honest limits_) gains "supports PQC-wrapped keys" as a selection criterion. **Considered & rejected:** swapping the bulk cipher to **ChaCha20-Poly1305** or **AES-256-GCM-SIV** (equally quantum-safe — a performance / nonce-misuse-robustness choice, not a quantum one, and not natively in Workers Web Crypto); adopting app-layer **ML-KEM now** (the app KEK is a symmetric Worker secret wrapped by AES-KW — there is no asymmetric key-establishment to harden until an asymmetric KEK is introduced); **doing nothing** until standards settle (forfeits the cheap agility seam and leaves transport HNDL unaddressed). **Consequences:** at-rest data is quantum-safe today; transport HNDL is mitigated by the platform; the only real migration work (signatures, an asymmetric KEK) is bounded, standards-gated, and reduced to adapter / field-version swaps; the cost is the discipline of carrying algorithm identifiers and the AES-256 / SHA-384 mandate. Extends **ADR-0026** (encryption & keys); touches **ADR-0004** (auth/passkeys) and **ADR-0045** (e-signature seal).

This is largely descriptive of where value already lives — the trust/ledger engine (ADR-0008/0009), conflicts (ADR-0013), billing/costs, and document-automation workflows are database-bound through the firm-scoped data-access layer (ADR-0003) and were never client candidates; it sharpens, not reverses, the ADR-0088 client-first-by-default placement (privileged bytes stay on the user's device, but the engine logic shipped there is the _generic_ PDFium engine, not proprietary product logic). **(2) Client hardening = minification only.** No heavy obfuscation / anti-tamper as a primary defense — rejected: it buys little against a determined actor while costing debuggability, performance, and clean observability/CSP (ADR-0017). **No production source maps are shipped to clients** (binds now — cheap, expensive to walk back; also trims shipped bytes). Seam: per-hotspot obfuscation may be revisited at distribution time _only_ if a concrete sensitive client-side routine appears; the default is no. **(3) Legal perimeter (named; owned outside engineering).** Proprietary closed-source SaaS license (already the platform's stance — ADR-0088); EULA / Terms of Service prohibiting reverse-engineering, decompilation, and scraping; trade-secret hygiene — the white paper, specs, ADRs, and research are internal trade secrets and are **not** published; trademark the brand (`magnacarta.esq`); register copyright; patents case-by-case and disfavored (a patent publishes the method). This is a business/legal owner track — engineering names it as a dependency and does not design it; its only real cost is legal/one-time (registration + drafting). **(4) The open/closed line is explicit** (reconciles ADR-0088 §J). Generic engine primitives are _deliberately_ open — upstreamed under MIT (the PDFium sanitization/signing contributions) — which is intentional, not a leak. What is protected is the **proprietary product layer** (trust/ledger correctness, the conflicts engine, document-automation workflows, the RBAC + ethical-walls model, the redaction verification gate), the **firm data**, and the **brand**. Rule: open the generic primitive; protect the product layer + data + brand. **(5) License/entitlement circumvention → server-side enforcement only (deferred seam).** When distribution arrives, entitlements/licensing are checked server-side in a Worker; the client is never trusted to enforce its own license, and the Tauri desktop app is a thin client to the same server entitlements — no offline crack surface for paid features (the ADR-0023 parity model: desktop adds optional local accelerations with a server fallback, never platform-exclusive entitlement logic). Nothing is built now; named as a distribution-time slice. **(6) Tenant-data exfiltration is already governed** by the tenancy/security posture — the firm-scoped data-access layer (ADR-0003), ADR-0052 absence-not-existence responses, sandbox-hardened Containers, and the separate cookieless preview origin (security conventions). This ADR adds nothing there; it references those as the answer to that threat. **(7) Honest non-goal.** Clean-room reimplementation of the product _concept and UX_ is not technically preventable and is explicitly out of scope for technical defense — it is countered only by the legal perimeter (point 3) and the operational moat (trust-accounting/regulatory correctness, data gravity and switching cost, integrations, reputation, feature velocity). **Considered & rejected:** a hardened/obfuscated-client posture (cost > value; the server is the real lever); building license/entitlement enforcement now (YAGNI — distribution is far off; recorded as a seam); treating _all_ internals as secret (contradicts the deliberate ADR-0088 §J openness — the open/closed line is drawn instead). **Consequences.** _Binds now:_ no production source maps; "does this value belong server-side?" becomes a design check on new client-shipped logic; internal process docs are treated as trade secrets. The now-binding rules are recorded in [`docs/conventions/security.md`](conventions/security.md) (_Distribution & IP-protection posture_); everything else is a named future seam surfaced in [`../ROADMAP.md`](../ROADMAP.md) at distribution time. _Cost:_ no material infrastructure cost — value placement is mostly descriptive of existing server-side logic (DB-bound, low-CPU; Workers bill CPU time, not I/O wait), and the ADR-0088 client-first default is preserved; "protected" never means relocating heavy _client-feasible_ compute onto an inline Worker — heavy + protected work goes to a Container/Queue (golden rule 10, ADR-0039, ADR-0083). Real costs are legal/one-time (trademark, copyright, EULA/ToS); the deferred entitlement check is a session-cached per-request lookup. **Status Accepted** (posture only — no implementation). Builds on **ADR-0003** (tenancy isolation), **ADR-0023** (web/desktop parity), **ADR-0052** (absence-not-existence), **ADR-0088** (proprietary-SaaS stance + §J open/closed line); composes with golden rules 3 and 10.

- **ADR-0126** · Proposed · 2026-06-18 · Documents / Editing — **Document editing concurrency & reconciliation — optimistic, never-block, human-reviewed.** **Decision:** the per-document Durable Object is the single authority over three access intents, under three invariants — **reading is never blocked**, **no work is ever silently lost or merged** (every merge is human-reviewed), and **one isolated container per document**. **Online** editing is real-time co-editing: connected editors share one Collabora session and **one** WOPI session lock (first editor locks, co-editors join, last leaver unlocks); a second editor **joins**, never blocked. **Read** is always available — a viewer joins read-only (write hard-rejected server-side) or gets the last committed version. **Offline** editing is optimistic and never blocked: checkout hands back a short-TTL **presigned GET** (key server-derived, ADR-0081/0082); check-in returns through the **ADR-0081 quarantine ingest path** (untrusted bytes — scanned before any container opens them); on a cleared check-in, divergence (by base-version-id + hash) either fast-forwards or is kept as a **conflicting copy** + a `needs reconciliation` flag, resolved by a human `documents:reconcile` decision (**no automatic `.docx` merge**; the conflicting copy is retained forever).
