Read “Partial” carefully

It is the single most misleading number here, and it is the third largest. 125 units of work are partial — delivered in part, and not finished. Counting those as progress is honest; counting them as done is not. A further 113 roll-up parents are also partial and are deliberately not counted: a parent is partial by definition once its children disagree, so it reports its subtree rather than any work of its own.

Shipped plus partial is 458 of 716 (64%). Shipped alone is 47%. Which of the two a reader wants is usually really a question about reach, which is the band below.

  • Client-consent model — Not built. Whether client consent for AI is taken once in the engagement letter or per matter. No AI feature reaches a client's data until this is settled.

Whether to ratify a named exception to the firm-scoped data rule, since a shared board is cross-firm by definition; and how a signed-in member's session reaches the community host, which is a different origin from the application.
  • A supplied asset-schedule template — Not built. Two questions. Whether shipped starter content rides the court-form adoption path designed in docs/specs/2026-08-14-nevada-court-form-library-design.md once its N1-N5 flags clear, or wants a distribution path that does not wait on them — an asset schedule is not a court form and carries none of that design's copyright or attorney-review weight. And what an estate asset schedule should actually say: heading, grouping, columns, whether unvalued assets are listed or counted, and what the document calls itself. Every firm provisioned after it ships inherits the answer, and neither the design nor ADR-0255's A-flags specify any of it.
  • Semantic search — Not built. Whether the semantic half is built for review sets at all, and if so on what trigger. It is DEFERRED rather than undecided-by-neglect — the cost side is now priced (a queried Vectorize index bills the resident corpus once a month at twenty times the storage rate, so a 250,000-document review set is roughly 490 times the keyword half's marginal cost, plus a one-time embedding charge of $261 to $522 per review set), and the recall side has never been measured on legal prose with relevance judgements, which is the only thing that would justify it. The experiment that settles it is specified in full in docs/research/2026-08-14-semantic-half-convergence-and-cost.md and is blocked on acquiring a judged corpus, not on building anything. Until it is answered the per-review-set flag stays a column nothing reads: turning it on would embed a corpus on a guess, and the embedding model pin is unchangeable once one vector exists.
  • Numbering schemes — Not built. Which letter form follows Z — doubled (AA, BB, CC) or bijective (AA, AB, AC). The surveyed trial-practice guidance says double letters and that is the recommendation, but a label goes into filed documents and is frozen onto the member row at issue, so changing the form after the first set has gone out is a migration over records nobody may rewrite. Beside it and unsettled: which starter presets ship, if any — the two candidates read from primary text are numbers-for-plaintiff with letters-for-defendant, and plaintiff-from-1 with defendant-from-500, and both are current practice in the same courthouse, so shipping both means shipping two conflicting readings of one court. Exhibits design flags E1 and E4.
  • In-text exhibit references — Not built. Whether the platform offers to detect the plain-text "Exhibit C" references already sitting in a firm's existing documents and propose bindings for them. It is genuinely useful on a firm's back catalogue and it is a scan over privileged prose, so the recommendation is yes, proposal-only, never applied without a person accepting it one at a time, and only inside a document a member already has open. A no means the feature works only for references authored through the picker, and a firm's existing prose stays unbound forever. Exhibits design flag E8.
  • Issued-set freeze — Not built. Two questions about how strict an issued set is, and both decide a build. Whether issuing is truly irreversible, with a superseding set as the only remedy — the recommendation — or whether an issued-in-error reversal exists, which is a second way for a frozen label to become unfrozen and therefore a second way the whole invariant can fail. And whether a member may correct an issued member's recorded title or underlying file at all: recommended yes, narrowly, audited and never renumbering, because the alternative leaves a firm unable to fix its own record of what it filed. Exhibits design flags E2 and E6.
  • Compiled exhibit package — Not built. Whether a compiled package paginates itself by default, and in which corner. The Nevada rule read for this design requires exhibits over ten pages to be numbered in the lower right — which is exactly where a page produced in discovery already carries its Bates stamp — and no reliable detector for an existing stamp exists, so a false negative overprints a number that was already served and a false positive drops pagination a rule requires. Defaulting on makes the common filing correct and risks overprinting; defaulting off makes every package a member has to remember to configure. Exhibits design flag E5.
  • Chart of accounts — Not built. Does a starter chart of accounts ship? A seeded chart makes a firm usable on day one and also makes the platform's opinion most firms' chart by inertia; the alternative is that a firm cannot bill until its accountant authors one. The recommendation is yes for the accounts and no for the mapping — a small chart of system accounts plus common law-firm expense accounts, marked as a starting point, with no role mapping the firm did not confirm, because the mapping is where the money goes. General ledger design, flag G1.

Ephemeral is the intuitive answer and it means privileged text passes through a place nothing accounts for. Recording it costs a run row and an object per paste. Translation design flag T4.
  • Document translation — Not built. Whether a translated document is a new file beside its source or a new version of it. Recommended a derived sibling linked to the source version: a version chain means successive states of one document, and a Spanish original and its English rendering are two documents that both stay current, so folding them into one chain means a member downloading the latest version of a Spanish contract silently gets English. Choosing the version chain instead removes the link table and breaks the "show me the original" read. Translation design flag T5.
