## Nevada (HOME JURISDICTION — re-verified 2026-08-07 against the primary rule text)

**Overall confidence: HIGH.** Re-verified 2026-08-07 against the Nevada Legislature's official court-rules
text (leg.state.nv.us, publisher stamp `[Rev. 4/15/2026 --2025]`) for RPC 1.15 and Nevada Supreme Court
Rules (SCR) 78, 216, 217, 219, 220, 221 — the full record, with verbatim quotes and the searches that
establish the negatives, is
[`../2026-08-07-nevada-trust-accounting-rules.md`](../2026-08-07-nevada-trust-accounting-rules.md). The
original 2026-05-30 compilation of this entry cited **SCR 78.5 throughout — a rule repealed on
22 August 2019** — matching the State Bar's stale 2019 handbook rather than the live rules; every SCR 78.5
citation below has been corrected to SCR 78.

1. **Governing rule citation.** Nevada Rules of Professional Conduct (RPC) **Rule 1.15 "Safekeeping Property."**
   Trust-account/IOLTA and recordkeeping mechanics are in the **Nevada Supreme Court Rules (SCR)**:
   **SCR 78** (maintenance of trust funds in approved institutions; overdraft notification; recordkeeping —
   **SCR 78.5 was repealed 22 August 2019 and its content moved into SCR 78** the same date; material still
   citing 78.5, including the State Bar's own handbook and the dangling cross-references inside SCR 217(1)
   and NRPC 7.5A, predates the repeal),
   **SCR 216** (creation of the bar foundation / IOLTA disbursement), **SCR 217** (creation and maintenance of
   interest-bearing trust accounts — the core IOLTA rule), **SCR 219** (earnings to client), **SCR 220**
   (earnings to attorney prohibited), **SCR 221** (nominal/short-term determination).

2. **Commingling prohibition specifics.** RPC 1.15(a): a lawyer "shall hold funds or other property of clients
   or third persons … separate from the lawyer's own property." All funds received or held for clients —
   **including advances for costs and expenses** — must be deposited in identifiable trust accounts maintained
   in the state where the lawyer's office is situated (or elsewhere with client/third-person consent). RPC
   1.15(e): disputed funds (where two or more persons, possibly including the lawyer, claim an interest) must
   be **kept separate until the dispute is resolved**, with prompt distribution of undisputed portions.

3. **IOLTA program (mandatory/opt-out/voluntary; name; interest remittance).** **MANDATORY.** SCR 217: "A member
   of the state bar or the member's law firm **shall** create or maintain an interest-bearing trust account for
   clients' funds that are nominal in amount or to be held for a short period of time." Program is administered
   through the designated tax-exempt **bar foundation** under SCR 216 — **the rule names no foundation**; it
   creates a designation power, and "**Nevada Bar Foundation**" comes from the Foundation's and the State
   Bar's own IOLTA pages (corroborated, not primary — flagged for the reviewing attorney). Interest/dividends
   are remitted **at least monthly** to the designated foundation. **96%** of IOLTA funds (after
   reserves/expenses) go to Access-to-Justice-approved civil legal service providers; remaining 4% as the
   foundation deems appropriate (SCR 216(1)(a)). Non-compliance →
   written notice, then $200 assessment after 30 days plus possible suspension (SCR 217(7)).

4. **Firm funds allowed in trust for bank charges? Cap?** Yes. RPC 1.15(b): a lawyer "may deposit the lawyer's
   own funds in a client trust account for the sole purpose of paying bank service charges on that account,
   **but only in an amount necessary for that purpose**." No fixed dollar cap; the cap is functional ("only
   necessary"). Note: SCR 217(3) makes IOLTA accounts **exempt from service charges and fees**, so for the
   pooled IOLTA itself this should rarely be needed.

5. **Advance/flat fee treatment.** RPC 1.15(c): "A lawyer shall deposit into a client trust account legal fees
   and expenses that have been paid in advance, to be withdrawn by the lawyer only as fees are earned or
   expenses incurred." Nevada thus requires advance fees in trust until earned; RPC 1.15(a) reinforces it by
   expressly counting "advances for costs and expenses" among the funds that must be deposited in trust — so
   Nevada has **no earned-on-receipt category by rule**. The flat-fee question is corroborated the same way:
   _In re Discipline of Sull_ (Nev.) holds that labelling a fee "flat" or "fixed" does not make it earned when
   paid — but the opinion's date and citation could not be verified against the official reporter, so the
   finding rests on the rule text and the case is corroboration only (see the 2026-08-07 research record,
   section 7).

6. **Records retention period.** **Five (5) years** after termination of the representation (RPC 1.15(a):
   "preserved for a period of five years after termination of the representation"; SCR 78(1)(b): "maintain
   and preserve for a period of **at least five years**, after final disposition of the underlying matter").
   **Two clocks, one number** — termination of the representation and final disposition of the underlying
   matter are different events, and neither citation is redundant. The State Bar's 2019 handbook still prints
   **seven** years; seven is the pre-2021 text of RPC 1.15(a), superseded effective 19 April 2021.

7. **Required records.** SCR 78(1)(b): checkbooks, canceled checks, check stubs, vouchers, **ledgers,
   journals**, closing statements, accountings/statements of disbursements rendered to clients — or equivalent
   records "clearly and expressly reflecting the date, amount, source, and explanation for all receipts,
   withdrawals, deliveries, and disbursements" of client funds/property; available to the state bar on request.

8. **Three-way reconciliation required? Frequency?** **No — Nevada mandates NO reconciliation, at any
   cadence.** The string "reconcil" appears **zero times** in the complete Nevada Rules of Professional
   Conduct and the complete Nevada Supreme Court Rules (both searched in full, 2026-08-07). The State Bar's
   handbook does teach monthly reconciliation and attributes it to **SCR 217(4)(a) — a miscitation**: that
   subsection directs the **depository institution** to remit interest and transmit statements, and imposes no
   duty on the lawyer. Reconciliation remains sound practice (and the platform's reconciliation feature stays
   worth using); what is refused is the claim that Nevada compels it.

9. **Trust overdraft notification program (banks report to bar?).** **Yes — mandatory.** SCR 78(2): a
   financial institution is approved as a trust depository only if it agrees to **report to state bar counsel
   whenever any properly payable instrument is presented against a lawyer trust account containing insufficient
   funds, whether or not honored.** No trust account may be held at a non-reporting institution. Reports for
   dishonored instruments are made simultaneously with the notice of dishonor; for honored-against-NSF items,
   within **five banking days** (SCR 78(3)–(4)). The reporting duty is the **bank's**, not the firm's.

10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust (fee handling).** Not addressed in NRPC 1.15, SCR 78,
    SCR 216–221, or the State Bar's trust-accounting handbook (all searched 2026-08-07 for `credit card`,
    `processing fee`, `merchant`, `surcharge`, `processor`). The State Bar ethics-opinions index has **not**
    been worked through, so this is **unresearched beyond those sources rather than a settled silence** —
    still **needs verification**. (The platform enforces fees-never-touch-trust structurally for every firm
    regardless.)

11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds.** SCR 219: on client request, **when economically
    feasible**, earnings shall be made available to the client on deposited trust funds that are **neither
    nominal in amount nor to be held for a short period** (i.e., non-IOLTA funds go to a separate
    client-specific interest-bearing account). SCR 221: the determination of "nominal in amount or to be held
    for a short period" rests **exclusively in the sound judgment of the lawyer** (good-faith judgment is
    protected from discipline). No fixed dollar threshold in the rule.

12. **Notable state-specific quirks.**
    - **IOLTA minimum interest/comparability rate is codified at 0.70%** (SCR 217(2)(a)), reviewed/set **twice
      annually** by the Nevada Supreme Court Access to Justice Commission; higher rates permitted if no
      impairment of immediate withdrawal.
    - **Unclaimed/unidentified trust funds go to the Clients' Security Fund, not the state.** RPC 1.15(f) —
      after reasonable efforts (**no less than** regular **and** certified mail, telephone, **and** email,
      certified with supporting documentary evidence), a firm that cannot locate the owner **within five years
      of receipt of the property** (a from-receipt clock, not a dormancy clock) **may** — permissive, never a
      duty — remit the funds to the **Clients' Security Fund**; good-faith remittance is not misconduct, and
      the Fund "shall not hold funds for future recovery" (the remittance is terminal).
    - **Designated client trust accounts do not escheat under Nevada's Uniform Unclaimed Property Act.**
      NRS 120A.500(6)(f) exempts "a trust account established on behalf of a client," clearly designated as
      such, from the presumption of abandonment — so the three-year dormancy machinery never engages, and the
      State Treasurer's Holder Reporting Manual confirms IOLTA funds "can no longer be reported to Unclaimed
      Property." The firm remains an ordinary NRS 120A holder for **non-trust** property (uncashed payroll or
      vendor checks). Full record:
      [`../2026-08-07-nevada-unclaimed-client-funds-escheat.md`](../2026-08-07-nevada-unclaimed-client-funds-escheat.md).
    - **20-mile exception** (SCR 217(5)): if the firm has no office within 20 miles of a complying institution,
      the minimum-rate and some reporting requirements are waived (with annual notice).
    - **Annual IOLTA verification** required on the annual membership-fee statement (SCR 217(4)(b)).
    - Trust accounts must be in **state-bar-approved** institutions only (SCR 78(1)(a)).

13. **Source URLs.**
    - RPC 1.15: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/courtrules/RPC.html
    - SCR 78, 216, 217, 219, 220, 221: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/courtrules/SCR.html
    - State Bar of Nevada IOLTA (program admin / approved institutions): https://nvbar.org/ (search "IOLTA")
    - Nevada Bar Foundation / Access to Justice Commission (interest rate setting): verify via nvbar.org / nevadabarfoundation.org
    - Primary-source research records in this repository:
      [`../2026-08-07-nevada-trust-accounting-rules.md`](../2026-08-07-nevada-trust-accounting-rules.md) and
      [`../2026-08-07-nevada-unclaimed-client-funds-escheat.md`](../2026-08-07-nevada-unclaimed-client-funds-escheat.md)

## Iowa (verified in full)

**Overall confidence: HIGH.** Verified against Iowa Court Rules **Chapter 45 (Client Trust Account Rules)** and
**Iowa R. Prof. Conduct 32:1.15**, official PDFs from legis.iowa.gov (October 2025 compilation).

1. **Governing rule citation.** **Iowa R. Prof. Conduct 32:1.15** (Safekeeping Property) + **Iowa Court Rules
   Chapter 45 — Client Trust Account Rules** (rules 45.1–45.11), which carry the operative detail. Key:
   45.1 (requirement), 45.2 (recordkeeping), 45.3 (account types/institutions), 45.4 (pooled interest-bearing
   / IOLTA + overdraft reporting), 45.5 (allowable monthly service charges), 45.8 (general retainer), 45.9
   (special retainer), 45.10 (flat fee), 45.11 (successor signatories).

2. **Commingling prohibition specifics.** Rule 45.1: client/third-person funds from Iowa-practice matters must
   be deposited in identifiable interest-bearing trust accounts at an institution with an Iowa branch;
   designated "Trust Account." **No** lawyer/firm funds in the account **except** (1) funds reasonably
   sufficient to pay/avoid bank fees that are the lawyer's responsibility, and (2) funds belonging in part to
   client and in part to lawyer (mixed) — the lawyer's portion withdrawn when due unless disputed.

3. **IOLTA program.** **MANDATORY.** Rule 45.4(1): a lawyer "must maintain a pooled interest-bearing trust
   account" for nominal/short-term funds; interest net of allowable monthly service charges is paid to the
   **Lawyer Trust Account Commission** (established by the Iowa Supreme Court). Remittance **at least quarterly**
   (Rule 45.4(4)(a)). This is Iowa's IOLTA program (administered via the Lawyer Trust Account Commission / IOLTA).

4. **Firm funds for bank charges? Cap?** Yes — Rule 45.1(1) allows firm funds "reasonably sufficient to pay or
   avoid imposition of fees and charges that are a lawyer's or law firm's responsibility, **including fees and
   charges that are not 'allowable monthly service charges.'**" Functional cap ("reasonably sufficient"); no
   fixed dollar amount. Rule 45.5 defines "allowable monthly service charges" (only the base monthly
   maintenance fee may be netted from IOLTA interest; transaction fees, wire fees, stop-payment, check printing
   are the lawyer's responsibility).

5. **Advance/flat fee treatment.** Iowa distinguishes three retainer types:
   - **General retainer** (Rule 45.8) = fee for availability over a period; **earned when paid; should NOT be
     deposited in trust.** (Recognizes the true/classic retainer doctrine.)
   - **Special retainer** (Rule 45.9) = fee for contemplated services, paid in advance; **a lawyer may not
     charge a nonrefundable special retainer or withdraw unearned fees** (i.e., held in trust until earned).
   - **Flat fee** (Rule 45.10): if paid in advance, **must be deposited into trust**; may be withdrawn per a
     client agreement on timing/proportion, but the agreement must protect the client's refund right, and
     **in no event may the lawyer withdraw unearned fees.**

6. **Records retention period.** **Six (6) years** after termination of the representation (Rule 45.2(3)(a)).

7. **Required records.** Rule 45.2(3)(a)(1)–(10): a **receipt-and-disbursement journal** with running balance;
   a **ledger record** with running balance for each client/beneficiary; retainer/compensation agreements;
   accountings; bills; disbursement records; bank statements, deposit records, prenumbered canceled/substitute
   checks; **record of all electronic transfers**; **monthly lists of individual client ledger balances and
   monthly triple reconciliations**; portions of client files related to trust transactions. Withdrawals only
   by check to a named payee (not "cash") or authorized bank transfer (45.2(3)(b)(3)).

8. **Three-way reconciliation required? Frequency?** **YES — monthly.** Rule 45.2(3)(a)(9) expressly requires
   "**monthly triple reconciliations** of bank statement balance to receipt and disbursement journal balance to
   sum of individual client ledger balances," plus monthly lists of individual client ledger balances.

9. **Trust overdraft notification program.** **Yes.** Rule 45.4(4)(c): the depository must report to the
   **Client Security Commission** any properly payable instrument presented against insufficient funds (whether
   honored or not), in the prescribed format; honored-against-NSF reports within **five banking days**.

10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust.** Rule 45.2(3)(a)(8) requires detailed records of all
    electronic transfers (date, amount, client, sender, recipient, purpose, authorizer). Specific credit-card
    surcharge/fee allocation: transaction fees are the lawyer's responsibility (Rule 45.5). Confidence MEDIUM
    on credit-card specifics — **verify** against Iowa Lawyer Trust Account Commission guidance.

11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds.** Rule 45.4(2)–(3): funds that **could generate positive
    net earnings for the client** must go in a separate (or sub-accounted) interest-bearing account paying the
    client; otherwise the pooled IOLTA. Factors (45.4(3)): amount, expected duration, interest rate, cost of
    administration, institution capability. No fixed dollar threshold — a net-earnings test.

12. **Notable state-specific quirks.**
    - **Lawyer certification** (Rule 45.10 series / 45.2(3)) and annual trust-account certification obligations.
    - Trust account must be at an institution **with an Iowa branch** (Rule 45.1).
    - Credit-union trust funds only to the extent each client's funds are individually insured (Rule 45.3).
    - **Successor signatory** designation permitted for sole signatories (Rule 45.11).
    - Distinctive, explicit **three retainer categories** (general/special/flat) with different trust treatment.

13. **Source URLs.**
    - Iowa Court Rules Ch. 45 (Client Trust Account Rules): https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/ACO/CourtRulesChapter/45.pdf
    - Iowa R. Prof. Conduct Ch. 32 (incl. 32:1.15): https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/ACO/CourtRulesChapter/32.pdf
    - Iowa Lawyer Trust Account Commission / IOLTA: https://www.iowacourts.gov/ (search "Lawyer Trust Account Commission")

## Minnesota (verified in full)

**Overall confidence: HIGH.** Verified against **Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 1.15** (full text), official from
revisor.mn.gov.

1. **Governing rule citation.** **Minn. R. Prof. Conduct (MRPC) 1.15** "Safekeeping Property" — a long
   self-contained rule (paragraphs (a)–(o)) that incorporates trust-account, IOLTA, recordkeeping, overdraft,
   and definition provisions. Administered by the **Office of Lawyers Professional Responsibility (OLPR)** and
   the **Lawyers Professional Responsibility Board (LPRB)**.

2. **Commingling prohibition specifics.** Rule 1.15(a): all client/third-person funds "shall be deposited in
   one or more identifiable trust accounts"; no firm funds therein except (1) funds reasonably sufficient to
   pay service charges, and (2) mixed funds belonging partly to lawyer. Rule 1.15(b): the lawyer **must withdraw
   earned fees within a reasonable time** and provide written notice (time, amount, purpose) plus an
   accounting; disputed portions stay in (or are restored to) the account until the dispute resolves.

3. **IOLTA program.** **MANDATORY.** Rule 1.15(e): "A lawyer who receives client or third person funds **shall**
   maintain a pooled trust account ('IOLTA account')" for nominal/short-term funds. Trust accounts (incl. IOLTA)
   must be at **eligible financial institutions approved by the OLPR** (Rule 1.15(j)). Program: Minnesota IOLTA
   Program / **Lawyers Trust Account Board (LTAB)** remits to civil legal aid. (Interest remittance mechanics:
   verify exact remittance frequency at lawyerstrust account board page; **needs verification** for exact %.)

4. **Firm funds for bank charges? Cap?** Yes — Rule 1.15(a)(1): "funds of the lawyer or law firm **reasonably
   sufficient** to pay service charges may be deposited therein." Functional cap; no fixed dollar amount.

5. **Advance/flat fee treatment.** Rule 1.15(c)(5): **except as specified in Rule 1.5(b)(1) and (2)**, the
   lawyer must "deposit all fees received in advance of the legal services being performed into a trust account
   and withdraw the fees as earned." Minnesota's **Rule 1.5(b)** permits a written agreement to treat a flat
   fee as the lawyer's property on receipt under specified conditions (availability/flat-fee carve-out) —
   **earned-on-receipt is allowed only under Rule 1.5(b) conditions** (written agreement, prescribed
   disclosures, refundability protections). Verify the precise 1.5(b) conditions against the rule text;
   confidence MEDIUM-HIGH on the cross-reference, **verify 1.5(b) details**.

6. **Records retention period.** **Six (6) years.** Rule 1.15(h): books and records "shall be preserved for at
   least six years … as to books and records relating to funds or property of clients or third persons, for at
   least six years after completion of the employment to which they relate."

7. **Required records.** Rule 1.15(c)(3) + (h): complete records of all client funds/securities/property,
   appropriate accountings; books and records "sufficient to demonstrate income and expenses" of the practice
   and to establish compliance with (a)–(f). Rule 1.15(i): lawyer must **certify annually** (with registration
   renewal) that required books and records are maintained. (Detailed ledger/journal specifics are set out in
   the LPRB's recordkeeping requirements / Appendix 1 to the rules — **verify** for the itemized list.)

8. **Three-way reconciliation required? Frequency?** Rule 1.15 text uses "books and records sufficient to
   demonstrate … compliance." **An express three-way reconciliation mandate is NOT in the rule body itself.**
   Minnesota's **Appendix 1 to MRPC (Trust Account Books and Records)** sets the detailed bookkeeping/
   reconciliation requirements and is the place to confirm three-way/monthly reconciliation. **Needs
   verification** against Appendix 1.

9. **Trust overdraft notification program.** **Yes — mandatory.** Rule 1.15(j)–(n): a financial institution
   must file an agreement to **report to the OLPR** any properly payable instrument presented against
   insufficient funds (honored or not); no trust account may be held at a non-reporting institution; honored-
   against-NSF reports within **five banking days**; lawyers are **conclusively deemed to consent** to the
   reporting/production requirements (Rule 1.15(m)).

10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust.** Rule 1.15(j): every check/draft/**electronic transfer**/
    withdrawal must be signed or directed by an authorized lawyer. Credit-card fee allocation specifics not in
    rule text — **verify** against OLPR guidance.

11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds.** Rule 1.15(f)–(g): only funds that **could not accrue
    earnings for the client (net of costs)** may be placed/retained in the IOLTA; otherwise a separate or
    sub-accounted interest-bearing account paying the client. Factors (g): amount of earnings, cost of
    establishing/administering (incl. lawyer's services), institution capability. Net-earnings test; no fixed
    dollar threshold.

12. **Notable state-specific quirks.**
    - **Definition of "trust account"** is restrictive (Rule 1.15(o)): interest-bearing checking; money-market
      with check-writing; sweep accounts in/collateralized by U.S. Government Securities; or open-end money
      market funds in U.S. Government Securities only.
    - **Annual certification** of books/records at registration renewal (Rule 1.15(i)).
    - Overdraft-agreement cancellation requires only **three days' written notice** to OLPR (Rule 1.15(k)).
    - **Appendix 1** carries the granular bookkeeping rules (cross-check there for reconciliation cadence).

13. **Source URLs.**
    - MRPC 1.15 (full text): https://www.revisor.mn.gov/court_rules/rule/prcond-1.15/
    - MRPC index: https://www.revisor.mn.gov/court_rules/rule/prcond-toh/
    - Rule 1.5 (flat-fee conditions) + Appendix 1 (trust books/records): https://www.revisor.mn.gov/court_rules/rule/prcond-toh/ (navigate to 1.5 and Appendix 1)
    - Office of Lawyers Professional Responsibility (overdraft program, approved institutions, guidance): https://lprb.mncourts.gov/

## Michigan (IOLTA + flat-fee verified; rule text NOT retrieved)

**Overall confidence: MEDIUM.** IOLTA program and flat-fee treatment verified against the **State Bar of
Michigan official IOLTA FAQ**. Rule text (MRPC 1.15 / 1.15A / 1.15B) could not be fetched (courts.michigan.gov

   Confidence on citation numbers: HIGH (well-established). Field details below: **verify against rule text.**

   structure prohibits commingling and requires separate identifiable trust accounts. Verify at the rule URL.

3. **IOLTA program.** **MANDATORY** (verified via SBM IOLTA FAQ). Interest on Michigan IOLTAs is **remitted to
   the Michigan State Bar Foundation** ("If an attorney accepts funds which must be held pursuant to MRPC 1.15
   on behalf of a Michigan client, those funds must be deposited into a Michigan IOLTA. The interest earned on
   Michigan IOLTAs are then remitted to the Michigan State Bar Foundation."). Program: **Michigan IOLTA Program
   (Michigan State Bar Foundation).**

4. **Firm funds for bank charges? Cap?** **Unknown / needs verification** (MRPC 1.15A). General MRPC permits
   firm funds only to pay bank charges in necessary amount. Verify at rule URL.

5. **Advance/flat fee treatment.** **Verified (SBM IOLTA FAQ / Ethics Op. RI-069):** unearned fixed/flat fees
   **must be held in the client trust account until earned.** Fixed fees paid in advance may be withdrawn per a
   **written agreement** providing portions are earned by passage of time or completion of tasks; **absent such
   an agreement, the lawyer must complete all contracted services before withdrawing any advanced fixed fee.**
   A flat/fixed fee charged in advance with no refundability agreement is **unearned** and must be placed in
   trust (RI-069). (True non-refundable "earned on receipt" is constrained.)

6. **Records retention period.** **Unknown / needs verification** — commonly **five (5) years** after the
   representation ends under MRPC 1.15(d)/(e) family (verify exact figure at rule URL).

7. **Required records.** **Unknown / needs verification** (MRPC 1.15A/1.15B: client ledgers, journals, etc.).

8. **Three-way reconciliation required? Frequency?** **Unknown / needs verification** (check MRPC 1.15B).

9. **Trust overdraft notification program.** **Unknown / needs verification.** Michigan has an overdraft
   notification program tied to approved financial institutions — verify at MRPC 1.15A/1.15B and SBM.

10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust.** **Unknown / needs verification** (SBM guidance; processor
    fees must not reduce client funds).

11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds.** Net-earnings test (SBM IOLTA FAQ describes calculating
    expected net return; if zero net return → IOLTA; if net return → non-IOLTA client account). Verify exact
    factors at MRPC 1.15B.

12. **Notable state-specific quirks.** Three-rule structure (1.15 / 1.15A / 1.15B). SBM IOLTA FAQ provides
    worked per-diem-interest examples and confirms national banks can maintain separate per-state IOLTAs.

## Kansas (citation only — rule text NOT retrieved; bot-gated)

**Overall confidence: LOW (field details).** kscourts.org and ksbar.org are JS-/bot-gated; only citation is

## Massachusetts (verified in full)

**Overall confidence: HIGH.** Verified against the full text of **Mass. R. Prof. C. 1.15** (Mass.gov / SJC Trial
Court Law Libraries; effective 09/01/2024).

1. **Governing rule citation.** **Massachusetts Rules of Professional Conduct Rule 1.15** "Safekeeping Property"
   (adopted under SJC Rule 3:07; current version effective Sept. 1, 2024). One of the most prescriptive trust-
   accounting rules in the U.S. IOLTA administered by the **Massachusetts IOLTA Committee.**

2. **Commingling prohibition specifics.** Rule 1.15(b): "A lawyer shall hold trust property separate from the
   lawyer's own property." Trust funds held in a trust account; **no** lawyer funds except (i) funds reasonably
   sufficient to pay bank charges and (ii) mixed funds — the lawyer's portion **withdrawn at the earliest
   reasonable time** after the interest becomes fixed; disputed portions not withdrawn (or restored) until the
   dispute is resolved. Rule 1.15(e)(4): no withdrawals to "cash"/"bearer," no ATM, only prenumbered checks
   identifying the recipient.

3. **IOLTA program.** **MANDATORY** ("comprehensive IOLTA program," Rule 1.15(g)). Pooled **IOLTA account** for
   nominal/short-term funds; interest net of charges remitted **at least quarterly to the IOLTA Committee**
   (1.15(g)(2)). Distribution: **67% to the Massachusetts Legal Assistance Corporation (MLAC), 33% to other
   designated charitable entities** as the SJC orders (1.15(g)(4)(i)). All IOLTA funds must be maintained **in
   the Commonwealth** (1.15(e)(1)).

4. **Firm funds for bank charges? Cap?** Yes — Rule 1.15(b)(2)(i): "Funds reasonably sufficient to pay bank
   charges may be deposited." Functional cap; a separate **bank-fees ledger** must track these firm funds
   (1.15(f)(1)D), and bank fees/charges are treated as an individual client record for reconciliation purposes.

5. **Advance/flat fee treatment.** Rule 1.15(b)(3): "A lawyer shall deposit into a trust account legal fees and
   expenses that have been paid in advance, to be withdrawn by the lawyer only as fees are earned or as
   expenses incurred." Massachusetts thus requires advance fees in trust until earned. (True availability/
   classic retainers and the "advance payment retainer" line are addressed in Mass. case law/ethics guidance —
   **verify** edge cases; the rule text itself mandates trust deposit of advance fees.)

6. **Records retention period.** **Six (6) years** after termination of the representation and after
   distribution of the property (Rule 1.15(f)).

7. **Required records.** Rule 1.15(f)(1): **A. account documentation; B. check register** (chronological, all
   deposits/disbursements with payee, transaction number, client matter, running balance); **C. individual
   client records / subsidiary ledgers** for each client matter (no negative individual-client balance ever);
   **D. bank-fees ledger; E. reconciliation reports; F. bank statements, canceled checks, electronic-
   transaction records, itemized deposit records; G. electronic-record retention** (reproducible hard copy +
   backups). Plus a **separate business account** (1.15(f)(2)) and records for non-fund trust property
   (1.15(f)(3)).

8. **Three-way reconciliation required? Frequency?** **YES — at least every 60 days.** Rule 1.15(f)(1)E
   requires a reconciliation report "on a regular and periodic basis but in any event **no less frequently than
   every sixty days**," verifying that three balances are **identical**: (i) check-register balance; (ii)
   adjusted bank-statement balance; (iii) total of all individual client-matter balances (with bank fees
   treated as a client record). This is an express three-way reconciliation.

9. **Trust overdraft notification program.** **Yes — and more.** Rule 1.15(h): trust accounts may be held only
   in institutions that filed an agreement with the **Board of Bar Overseers** to (i) **report dishonored NSF
   instruments**, and (ii) **report IOLTA account inactivity** (a distinctive Massachusetts feature). Agreement
   covers all branches; cancelable only on 30 days' written notice.

10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust.** Rule 1.15(e)(4) bars ATM/cash/"bearer" withdrawals; 1.15(f)
    requires electronic-transaction records. Credit-card processor-fee allocation not specified in rule —
    **verify** against BBO/IOLTA Committee guidance (general rule: processor fees not charged to client funds).

11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds.** Rule 1.15(e)(6): lawyer's good-faith judgment — IOLTA for
    funds "nominal in amount, or to be held for a short period," otherwise an individual interest-bearing
    account with interest to the client. **IOLTA funds over $100,000 may be temporarily reinvested** in
    repurchase agreements fully collateralized by U.S. Government obligations (1.15(g)(1)). No fixed minimum
    threshold for IOLTA vs. individual; lawyer's judgment governs.

12. **Notable state-specific quirks.**
    - **IOLTA-account-inactivity reporting** by banks (in addition to NSF reporting) — unusual (1.15(h)).
    - **No negative individual-client balance ever** is expressly codified (1.15(f)(1)C).
    - **60-day three-way reconciliation** codified in the rule itself.
    - **Custodian-for-minor-family-member** exemption from operational/records requirements (1.15(e)(7)).
    - **$100,000 repo reinvestment** option for IOLTA balances.
    - Unidentified/unclaimed IOLTA funds have a disposition procedure (Rule 1.15(i)).

13. **Source URLs.**
    - Mass. R. Prof. C. 1.15 (full text, verified): https://www.mass.gov/supreme-judicial-court-rules/rules-of-professional-conduct-rule-115-safekeeping-property
    - Board of Bar Overseers (overdraft/IOLTA-inactivity program): https://www.massbbo.org/
    - Massachusetts IOLTA Committee: https://www.maiolta.org/
