## Texas

1. **Governing rule citation** — Texas Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct (TDRPC) **Rule 1.14, "Safekeeping Property"** (Texas uses 1.14, not 1.15). IOLTA is governed by the **Texas Rules Governing the Operation of the Texas Interest on Lawyers' Trust Account (IOLTA) Program** and Article XI of the State Bar Rules, administered by the Texas Access to Justice Foundation (TAJF). Texas has **no separate detailed trust-accounting/recordkeeping rule** comparable to the ABA Model Financial Recordkeeping Rule.
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Rule 1.14(a): a lawyer "shall hold funds … belonging in whole or in part to clients or third persons … separate from the lawyer's own property," kept in a separate account designated "trust" or "escrow," maintained in the state of the lawyer's office (or elsewhere with consent). Rule 1.14(c): when lawyer and another claim interests, property "shall be kept separate … until there is an accounting and severance"; disputed portions held separate until resolved.
3. **IOLTA program** — **Mandatory.** Participation became mandatory for Texas attorneys; only client funds that are nominal in amount or held for a short period go into IOLTA. Program: **Texas Access to Justice Foundation (TAJF)**. Interest is **remitted to TAJF**, which grants it for civil legal aid. (Comment 2 to Rule 1.14 confirms the rule "does not prohibit participation in an IOLTA or similar program.")
4. **Firm funds in trust** — The rule text does not contain an express ABA-style "funds to cover bank charges" provision; Comment 2 contemplates that the lawyer may withdraw the lawyer's own earned portion. Whether a de minimis deposit of firm funds to cover bank charges is permitted is **best treated as permissible by custom/opinion but not expressly stated in Rule 1.14 — verify against TAJF guidance and Texas ethics opinions.** **(LOW CONFIDENCE on any cap.)**
5. **Advance fee / flat fee treatment** — Comment 2 to Rule 1.14: prepaid fees that "belong to the client until the services are rendered" should be handled under paragraph (c) (kept separate); the lawyer may withdraw after the service is rendered and the fee earned. Texas recognizes **"true/non-refundable retainers"** (a fee paid to secure availability) as the lawyer's property — see **Texas Ethics Opinion 611 (2011)** and **Cluck v. Commission for Lawyer Discipline**, distinguishing a true retainer from an advance fee for services (which must be held until earned). Verify Op. 611 citation.
6. **Records retention period** — **Five (5) years** after termination of the representation (Rule 1.14(a): "preserved for a period of five years after termination of the representation").
7. **Required records** — Rule 1.14(a) requires "complete records of such account funds and other property." Texas does **not** enumerate specific journals/ledgers in the rule; TAJF and State Bar guidance recommend a check register, individual client ledgers, and a general trust ledger.
8. **Reconciliation** — **Not required by rule.** State Bar of Texas guidance recommends **monthly** reconciliation of bank statements to the check register (and recommends a general trust ledger for reconciliation), but three-way reconciliation is **not mandated** by Rule 1.14.
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — **No mandatory bank overdraft-reporting rule.** Texas is one of the jurisdictions that historically has **not** adopted a trust-account overdraft notification rule. **(Verify — confirm no rule currently in force.)**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — Not addressed in Rule 1.14; governed by general principles and TAJF/State Bar guidance. **Unknown / needs verification** on where processing fees may be deducted.
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — Only "nominal or short-term" funds go into pooled IOLTA; funds that could earn net interest for the client should be placed in a separate client account. The lawyer makes a good-faith determination.
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — (a) Uses **Rule 1.14**, not 1.15; (b) **no detailed recordkeeping rule and no overdraft-reporting rule** — unusually light black-letter recordkeeping regime; (c) IOLTA mandatory and administered by TAJF; (d) recognizes true non-refundable retainers (Op. 611).
13. **Source URLs** —
    - https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/safekeeping-property/ (Rule 1.14 text + comments)
    - https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Trust_Accounts (State Bar trust-account guidance; monthly reconciliation recommendation)
    - https://teajf.org/attorneys/what_is_iolta.aspx (mandatory IOLTA; nominal/short-term funds; interest remitted to Foundation)
    - https://teajf.org/financial_institutions/remittance.aspx (remittance to TAJF)
    - Texas Ethics Opinion 611 (true retainer) — verify on https://www.legalethicstexas.com/

## Utah

1. **Governing rule citation** — Utah Rules of Professional Conduct **Rule 1.15, "Safekeeping Property."** IOLTA administered by the **Utah Bar Foundation**. The rule comment references the **ABA Model Financial Recordkeeping Rule**.
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Rule 1.15(a): "A lawyer shall hold property of clients or third persons … separate from the lawyer's own property. Funds shall be kept in a separate account …" The account may only be maintained at a financial institution that **agrees to report insufficient-funds instruments to the Office of Professional Conduct (OPC)**. Comment [2]: commingling is normally impermissible except the (b) bank-charge exception.
3. **IOLTA program** — **Mandatory** (administered by the **Utah Bar Foundation**; nominal/short-term client funds pooled in IOLTA; interest remitted to the Foundation). **(Mandatory status: HIGH confidence from rule structure; confirm current administrative details on the Utah Bar Foundation site.)**
4. **Firm funds in trust** — Rule 1.15(b): "A lawyer may deposit the lawyer's own funds in a client trust account for the sole purpose of paying bank service charges on that account, **but only in an amount necessary for that purpose.**" (No fixed dollar cap; "necessary" standard.)
5. **Advance fee / flat fee treatment** — Rule 1.15(c): "A lawyer shall deposit into a client trust account legal fees and expenses that have been paid in advance, to be withdrawn by the lawyer only as fees are earned or expenses incurred." Earned-on-receipt / flat-fee-as-property treatment is governed by **Rule 1.5** and Utah ethics opinions — **verify Utah's treatment of nonrefundable/flat fees (Utah Rule 1.5 and Ethics Advisory Opinions).** **(LOW CONFIDENCE on flat-fee specifics.)**
6. **Records retention period** — **Five (5) years** after termination of the representation (Rule 1.15(a)).
7. **Required records** — Rule 1.15(a): "Complete records of such account funds and other property." Comment [1]: maintain books and records per generally accepted accounting practice and any recordkeeping rules established by law/court order; cites ABA Model Financial Recordkeeping Rule.
8. **Reconciliation** — **Not expressly stated in Rule 1.15.** Follows GAAP / ABA Model Financial Recordkeeping Rule by reference. **Three-way reconciliation requirement: Unknown / needs verification** (check OPC guidance).
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — **Yes.** The account "may only be maintained in a financial institution that agrees to report to the Office of Professional Conduct in the event any instrument in properly payable form is presented against an attorney trust account containing insufficient funds, irrespective of whether or not the instrument is honored." (Rule 1.15(a).)
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — **Unknown / needs verification** (not addressed in Rule 1.15 text reviewed).
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — Nominal/short-term funds → pooled IOLTA; funds that can earn net interest for the client → separate client account. **(Standard IOLTA framework; confirm exact wording in Utah IOLTA rule.)**
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — Overdraft reporting runs to the **Office of Professional Conduct (OPC)**; comment expressly cross-references the ABA Model Financial Recordkeeping Rule. The attorney rule mirrors the Licensed Paralegal Practitioner (LPP) Rule 1.15.
13. **Source URLs** —
    - https://legacy.utcourts.gov/rules/view.php?type=scrp&rule=3-1.15 (Utah attorney Rule 1.15 text + comments)
    - Utah Bar Foundation IOLTA: https://www.utahbarfoundation.org/iolta (program admin — confirm details; page is JS-rendered)

## Vermont

1. **Governing rule citation** — Vermont Rules of Professional Conduct (V.R.Pr.C.) **Rule 1.15 (Safekeeping Property)**, **Rule 1.15A (Trust Accounting System)**, and **Rule 1.15B (IOLTA / pooled interest-bearing trust accounts)**. Rules 1.15A–C were adopted (no ABA Model equivalent) specifically "to include mandatory IOLTA, random auditing, the financial support for random auditing, and reporting of overdrafts."
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Rule 1.15(a)(1): hold client/third-person property separate; funds kept per Rules 1.15A/B. Rule 1.15(f): no disbursement unless funds are "collected funds"; a lawyer "shall not use, endanger, or encumber money held in trust for a client … for the business of another client" without permission. Disciplinary case law treats even technical commingling as sanctionable (e.g., _In re Farrar_, 2008 VT 31).
3. **IOLTA program** — **Mandatory** (Rule 1.15B). Pooled interest-bearing trust accounts; interest distributed to the **Vermont Bar Foundation** (Reporter's Notes, 2016 amendment). Only funds "not reasonably expected to earn net interest or dividends" go in IOLTA.
4. **Firm funds in trust** — Rule 1.15(b): a lawyer may deposit own funds "for the sole purpose of paying service charges or fees on that account, **but only in an amount reasonably necessary for that purpose**" (2016 amendment added "reasonably"; case law notes there is no fixed dollar amount — _In re PRB Docket No. 2014.133_, 2015 VT 63).
5. **Advance fee / flat fee treatment** — Rule 1.15(c): unless the lawyer has a **nonrefundable fee agreement complying with Rule 1.5(f)**, advance fees/expenses must be deposited in trust and withdrawn only as earned/incurred. **"True/pure retainer" doctrine recognized:** Reporter's Notes (2009) — the rule "does not require deposit … of a 'pure' retainer, a flat fee paid to assure the lawyer's availability and not for performing services"; if services are also to be performed, the advance for services must go in trust.
6. **Records retention period** — **Six (6) years** after termination of the representation (Rule 1.15(a)(1)).
7. **Required records** — Rule 1.15A(a): accounting system with (1) receipts/disbursements journal identifying source and nature; (2) **a record for each client/person showing all receipts and disbursements with a running balance** (client ledgers); (3) records documenting timely notice; (4) records documenting **timely reconciliation** plus a single source identifying all accounts.
8. **Reconciliation** — **Required; at least monthly.** Rule 1.15A(a)(4): "'Timely reconciliation' means, at a minimum, monthly reconciliation of such accounts." (The rule does not use the literal phrase "three-way," but reconciliation of all accounts plus per-client running balances is required.) Vermont also has **random/compliance audits** by Disciplinary Counsel (Rule 1.15A(c)) and Supreme Court-ordered audits (Rule 1.15A(d)).
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — **Yes** — overdraft reporting is one of the stated purposes of the Rule 1.15A–C scheme (formerly Rule 1.15C). **(Confirm current operative overdraft-reporting provision/location.)**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — Rule 1.15A(b)(3): withdrawals/disbursements from a pooled trust account only by (i) check payable to a named payee (not "cash") or (ii) **authorized electronic transfer**; cash withdrawals via debit card are not approved (Comment [3]). Specific credit-card-processing-fee placement: **Unknown / needs verification.**
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — Funds that are "reasonably expected to earn net interest or dividends" (Rule 1.15B(a)) must be held outside the pooled IOLTA (i.e., in a separate client account); nominal/short-term funds go in IOLTA.
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — (a) Detailed **"collected funds" disbursement rule** with five safe harbors (Rule 1.15(g)), including a **$1,000 personal-check** de minimis safe harbor; (b) **mandatory random auditing/compliance review**; (c) only Vermont-admitted lawyers (or supervised persons) may be authorized signatories on a pooled trust account (Rule 1.15A(b)(1)); (d) 6-year (not 5-year) retention.
13. **Source URLs** —
    - https://www.vtcourts.gov/sites/default/files/documents/VermontRulesofProfessionalConduct.pdf (official consolidated rules)
    - Rule 1.15 (unofficial bar-counsel copy): https://vtbarcounsel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/v.r.pr_.c.-1.15-safekeeping-property.pdf
    - Rule 1.15A (unofficial bar-counsel copy): https://vtbarcounsel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/v.r.pr_.c.-1.15a-trust-accounting-system.pdf

## Virginia

1. **Governing rule citation** — Virginia Rules of Professional Conduct **Rule 1.15, "Safekeeping Property"** (Part 6, §II of the Rules of the Supreme Court of Virginia). Rule 1.15 contains a **detailed recordkeeping subsection (d)** and IOLTA provisions; IOLTA is administered in conjunction with the **Virginia Law Foundation / Legal Services Corporation of Virginia** under the Virginia State Bar (VSB).
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Rule 1.15(a)–(b): client/third-party funds (including advances for costs/expenses) must be deposited in one or more identifiable trust accounts; lawyer's funds kept separate. Disputed funds held separate until resolved.
3. **IOLTA program** — **Mandatory** (Virginia IOLTA). Nominal/short-term funds pooled in IOLTA; interest remitted to the IOLTA program for legal aid. **(Confirm exact remittee entity and current administration on the VSB/Virginia IOLTA page.)**
4. **Firm funds in trust** — Permitted to cover bank service charges (standard ABA-style provision). **(Confirm exact paragraph and any cap in Virginia Rule 1.15.)**
5. **Advance fee / flat fee treatment** — Advance fees held in trust until earned; Virginia addresses earned-on-receipt/flat fees via **Rule 1.5** and LEOs (e.g., advance-fee deposits and "nonrefundable fee" treatment). **(LOW CONFIDENCE on flat-fee specifics — verify Rule 1.5 and Virginia Legal Ethics Opinions.)**
6. **Records retention period** — **Five (5) years** after termination of the representation. **(Confirm against current Rule 1.15(d)/(e) text.)**
7. **Required records** — Rule 1.15(d) is unusually detailed: **cash receipts journal, cash disbursements journal, and a subsidiary ledger (individual client ledger) for each client/matter**, plus reconciliations. (The 2020 amendment refined reconciliation terminology and removed the term "subsidiary ledger" in places to clarify usage.)
8. **Reconciliation** — **Yes — effectively three-way.** Rule 1.15(d)(3): periodic reconciliation that reconciles (i) the cash balance from the receipts/disbursements journals to (ii) the subsidiary (client) ledger balances; "reconciliations must be approved by a lawyer in the law firm"; Comment [5] requires that **discrepancies be explained**. **Required frequency: verify (the rule requires periodic reconciliations; confirm monthly vs. quarterly cadence in current (d)(3)).**
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — **Yes.** Virginia requires trust accounts be held at "approved financial institutions" that have agreed to report dishonored trust-account instruments to the VSB. **(Confirm citation — Rule 1.15(g)/approved-institution agreement.)**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — Addressed via VSB guidance/LEOs on credit-card processing and trust accounts; **fees generally must not be deducted from client principal.** **Unknown / needs verification on exact provision.**
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — Nominal/short-term funds → pooled IOLTA; funds capable of net earnings for the client → separate interest-bearing client account.
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — Virginia has one of the **most prescriptive black-letter recordkeeping rules** (explicit receipts journal, disbursements journal, client subsidiary ledgers, lawyer-approved reconciliations with explained discrepancies) of any U.S. jurisdiction.
13. **Source URLs** —
    - Rule 1.15 full text (VSB): https://vsb.org/Site/Site/about/RulesRegulations/rpc-part6-sec2.aspx
    - 2020 Rule 1.15 amendment (recordkeeping/reconciliation redline): https://vsb.org/Site/Site/news/rules-news/20200109-rpc-1.15.aspx
    - Virginia IOLTA / VSB: https://vsb.org/ (confirm IOLTA program page)

## Washington

1. **Governing rule citation** — Washington Rules of Professional Conduct **RPC 1.15A "Safeguarding Property"** and **RPC 1.15B "Required Trust Account Records."** Trust-account/IOLTA administration is tied to the **Rules for Enforcement of Lawyer Conduct (ELC) 15.4 and 15.7** and the **Legal Foundation of Washington (LFW)**.
2. **Commingling prohibition** — RPC 1.15A(b): no use/conversion/borrowing/pledging of client property; (c): hold client/third-person property separate; (h)(8): disbursements for a person may not exceed that person's funds on deposit, and one person's funds must not be used for another.
3. **IOLTA program** — **Mandatory** (pooled IOLTA via the **Legal Foundation of Washington**). RPC 1.15A(i)(1): nominal/short-term funds that won't yield a positive net return go in **IOLTA**, with interest paid to and the program administered by the **Legal Foundation of Washington** (per ELC 15.4 and 15.7).
4. **Firm funds in trust** — RPC 1.15A(h)(1): no lawyer funds in trust **except** (i) funds to pay bank charges "in an amount reasonably sufficient for that purpose," (ii) funds belonging in part to lawyer/in part to client (lawyer's portion withdrawn at earliest reasonable time), and (iii) funds necessary to restore appropriate balances. (No fixed dollar cap; "reasonably sufficient.")
5. **Advance fee / flat fee treatment** — RPC 1.15A(c)(2): except as provided in **Rule 1.5(f)**, advance fees/expenses go in trust, withdrawn only as earned/incurred. Comment [2]: client funds include advance fees "other than retainers and flat fees complying with the requirements of Rule 1.5(f)" — so **flat fees/retainers meeting Rule 1.5(f) may be treated as the lawyer's property** and need not be held in trust.
6. **Records retention period** — **Seven (7) years.** RPC 1.15A(c)(3) (non-fund property records preserved seven years after return); RPC 1.15B sets the seven-year retention for trust account records generally.
7. **Required records** — RPC 1.15B requires: a **check register**, **individual client ledger records** (RPC 1.15B(a)(2)), deposit/disbursement records, checkbook registers, reconciliation reports, etc. RPC 1.15A(h)(5): withdrawals only to a named payee, never to "cash."
8. **Reconciliation** — **Yes — three-way, at least quarterly.** RPC 1.15A(h)(6): reconcile "as often as bank statements are generated or at least quarterly," reconciling the **check register balance to the bank statement balance** AND the **check register balance to the combined total of all client ledger records** (RPC 1.15B(a)(2)).
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — **Yes.** Comment [19]–[20]: only LFW-authorized institutions (which must have a current **Overdraft Notification Agreement under ELC 15.4**) may hold trust accounts; on receiving an overdraft notice, the lawyer must **promptly notify the WSBA Office of Disciplinary Counsel with a full explanation (ELC 15.4(d)).**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — Comment [8]: an **advance-fee credit-card payment must be deposited directly into the trust account** (not run through a general account first); conversely, credit-card payments of earned fees, of Rule 1.5(f)(1) retainers, and of Rule 1.5(f)(2) flat fees must **not** be deposited into the trust account and transferred out. Comment [15]: a lawyer may not receive earnings credits/benefits based on the trust balance.
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — RPC 1.15A(i)(2): funds that will produce a **positive net return** to the client must go in a **non-IOLTA account** — either a separate interest-bearing account for that client or a pooled sub-accounted account paying interest to each client — unless the client requests IOLTA. Determination uses interest, administration cost, and the institution's calculation capability (i(3)).
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — (a) **7-year** retention (longer than most); (b) detailed real-estate-closing duties (RPC 1.15A(j)); (c) "deposit intact"/no split deposits (Comment [10]); (d) LLLT (limited license legal technician) signatory provisions; (e) optional written bank guaranty allowing disbursement before collection (RPC 1.15A(h)(7), Comment [11]).
13. **Source URLs** —
    - RPC 1.15A (official): https://www.courts.wa.gov/court_rules/pdf/RPC/GA_RPC_01_15A_00.pdf
    - RPC 1.15B (required records): https://www.courts.wa.gov/court_rules/ (search "RPC 1.15B")
    - Legal Foundation of Washington / ELC 15.4 & 15.7: https://www.courts.wa.gov/court_rules/ (ELC)

## West Virginia

1. **Governing rule citation** — West Virginia Rules of Professional Conduct **Rule 1.15 (Safekeeping Property)** plus **West Virginia State Bar Administrative Rule 10 (Client Trust Accounts; IOLTA Program)** (approved by Supreme Court of Appeals Administrative Order, Sept. 29, 2014; detailed IOLTA/administration provisions were moved out of RPC 1.15 into Admin Rule 10).
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Admin Rule 10.01 (per RPC 1.15): a lawyer/firm receiving client funds "must keep those funds in a separate account" conforming to RPC 1.15, at an eligible financial institution.
3. **IOLTA program** — **Mandatory for lawyers** (Rule 10.02, per RPC 1.15(f)): nominal/short-term client funds must be held in a pooled interest/dividend-bearing **IOLTA** account, "administered by the West Virginia State Bar." **Bank participation is voluntary** (Rule 10.05), but lawyer participation is mandatory. Interest is **remitted to the WV State Bar** at least quarterly (Rule 10.06) and distributed to Legal Aid of WV, Mountain State Justice, and others (Rule 10.11). **Annual IOLTA reporting required; $200 penalty for noncompliance** (Rule 10.02). Exemptions in Rule 10.07 (judges, government/military, inactive, no-client-funds practices, hardship petitions).
4. **Firm funds in trust** — Governed by RPC 1.15 (standard bank-charge provision). **(Confirm exact RPC 1.15 paragraph/cap — Admin Rule 10 assigns all non-"allowable reasonable fees," overdraft fees, and NSF fees to the lawyer, not the IOLTA principal/interest.)**
5. **Advance fee / flat fee treatment** — Governed by RPC 1.15 and Rule 1.5. **(LOW CONFIDENCE — verify WV's treatment of advance/flat/nonrefundable fees in RPC 1.15 and 1.5.)**
6. **Records retention period** — **Five (5) years** after termination of the representation (per RPC 1.15). **(Confirm against current RPC 1.15 text.)**
7. **Required records** — Per RPC 1.15 ("complete records") and the **WV Client Trust Account Handbook** (OLR/State Bar guidance): client ledgers, journals, reconciliations.
8. **Reconciliation** — **Per RPC 1.15 / Client Trust Account Handbook.** Three-way reconciliation requirement and frequency: **Unknown / needs verification** (check current RPC 1.15 and the Handbook).
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — **Yes.** Admin Rule 10.08: any properly payable instrument presented against a Client Trust or IOLTA account with insufficient funds (whether or not honored) **must be reported by the eligible financial institution to the WV State Bar**; every lawyer is deemed to consent. Institutions must file an overdraft notification agreement (cancelable only on 30-day notice). Rule 10.04(c) makes overdraft-notification agreement a condition of institutional eligibility.
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — WV State Bar publishes **"IOLTA Credit & Debit Card Policies."** Admin Rule 10.05(d): only "allowable reasonable fees" (per-check, per-deposit, fee in lieu of minimum balance, sweep fees, FDIC fees, IOLTA admin fee) may be deducted from interest/dividends; **no fees from principal**; overdraft/NSF and other fees are the lawyer's responsibility. **(Verify card-specific fee handling in the credit/debit card policy PDF.)**
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — Rule 10.03: only funds "so nominal in amount or … held for such a brief period" that they cannot earn income net of costs go in IOLTA; the lawyer weighs amount, duration, rates, administration cost, and institutional capability. Larger/longer-held funds → separate (client-benefit) account.
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — (a) IOLTA mechanics are in a **separate State Bar Administrative Rule (Rule 10)**, not the RPC; (b) explicit **benchmark rate option** (65% of Federal Funds Target Rate) for participating banks (Rule 10.05(e)); (c) detailed **unclaimed-IOLTA-funds escheat-to-Bar** procedure (Rule 10.09); (d) $200 annual-reporting penalty.
13. **Source URLs** —
    - WV State Bar Administrative Rule 10 (full text): https://wvbar.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Rule-10-Client-trust-accounts.pdf
    - WV Bar IOLTA hub: https://wvbar.org/members/iolta/
    - WV Client Trust Account Handbook: https://wvbar.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Client-Trust-Handbook.pdf
    - IOLTA Credit & Debit Card Policies: https://wvbar.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/IOLTA-Credit-Card-Policies.pdf

## Wisconsin

1. **Governing rule citation** — Wisconsin Supreme Court Rule **SCR 20:1.15, "Safekeeping property; trust accounts and fiduciary accounts"** (substantially amended effective **July 1, 2023** to liberalize electronic transactions). IOLTA via **SCR Chapter 13** and the **Wisconsin Trust Account Foundation, Inc. (WisTAF).** Overdraft program run by the **Office of Lawyer Regulation (OLR).**
2. **Commingling prohibition** — SCR 20:1.15(b)(1): hold client/3rd-party property separate; deposit all such funds in identifiable trust accounts; a lawyer "shall not hold any funds in a trust account that are unrelated to a representation." (b)(2): account must be clearly designated "Client Account"/"Trust Account" (an acronym like "IOLTA" alone does not suffice).
3. **IOLTA program** — **Mandatory** (SCR 20:1.15(c)(1), (d)). Nominal/short-term funds → pooled IOLTA at a **WisTAF IOLTA-participating institution**; interest/dividends (less allowable reasonable fees) are **paid to WisTAF**, the beneficial owner (d)(4). Remittance and reporting at least quarterly (d)(6). Bank participation voluntary (a)(9).
4. **Firm funds in trust** — SCR 20:1.15(b)(3): no lawyer/firm funds in trust **except funds reasonably sufficient to pay monthly account service charges.** Each firm must also maintain a separate business/operating account.
5. **Advance fee / flat fee treatment** — SCR 20:1.15(b)(6): advance legal fees/expenses go in a client trust account, withdrawn only as earned/incurred, **except** (a) where the lawyer complies with **SCR 20:1.5(g)** (the "advanced fee placed in business account" alternative with required written notices/disclosures) and (b) credit-card/EFT advance payments may be **temporarily** received in a non-trust account if transferred to trust **within two business days** of receipt.
6. **Records retention period** — **Six (6) years** after termination of the representation (SCR 20:1.15(g)(1)); fiduciary-account records: six most recent years plus a summary for prior years (k)(7).
7. **Required records** — SCR 20:1.15(g)(1): complete records of trust funds, all deposits/disbursements, and other trust property. OLR publishes **"Guidelines for trust account records"** and forms (specifying transaction register, individual client ledgers, reconciliation reports). (f)(2): no cash withdrawals, no checks to "Cash," no ATM/cash-machine withdrawals.
8. **Reconciliation** — **Not stated as a frequency in the black-letter rule;** the **OLR Trust Account Recordkeeping Guidelines require periodic (monthly) three-way reconciliation** (transaction register ↔ bank statement ↔ sum of client ledgers). **Verify cadence in the current OLR guidelines** (rule text itself omits the reconciliation interval). **(LOW CONFIDENCE on exact mandated frequency in rule vs. guidelines.)**
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — **Yes.** SCR 20:1.15(h): all draft trust accounts (and draft fiduciary accounts without alternative protection) must be at an institution that **reports overdrafts to the OLR**; reporting required whether or not the instrument/electronic transaction is honored; report made simultaneously with the depositor notice; OLR holds reports 10 business days for inadvertent-error withdrawal.
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — Extensively addressed (2023 amendments): (b)(6)b. credit-card/EFT advance payments may be received in a non-trust account and transferred to trust within two business days; (f)(1) lawyer must **reimburse the trust account for any chargeback/surcharge/ACH-reversal shortfall within three business days** and before any disbursement; Comment: before charging a client for processing fees, the lawyer should disclose and obtain consent (in writing if Rule 1.5(b) requires) and not violate the processor's terms.
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — SCR 20:1.15(c)(2): funds capable of earning net income for the client/3rd party must be in a **non-IOLTA interest-bearing account** — a separate per-client account, a pooled sub-accounted account, a client-directed investment vehicle, a bankruptcy-estate vehicle, or a designated non-interest account per written client instructions. Selection factors in (c)(3).

## District of Columbia

1. **Governing rule citation** — D.C. Rules of Professional Conduct **Rule 1.15, "Safekeeping Property."** Approved depositories and the DC IOLTA program are governed by **Rule XI, §20, of the Rules Governing the District of Columbia Bar**; program administered with the **D.C. Bar Foundation.**
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Rule 1.15(a): hold client/third-person property separate; trust funds in trust accounts per (b); records preserved five years. Comment [1] states "the longstanding prohibitions of the misappropriation of entrusted funds and the commingling of entrusted funds with the lawyer's property." DC is well known for **strict misappropriation discipline** (near-automatic disbarment even for _negligent_ misappropriation — _In re Addams_, _In re Anderson_).
3. **IOLTA program** — **Mandatory (DC IOLTA), with a multijurisdictional opt-out.** Rule 1.15(b): nominal/short-term trust funds must be held at an "approved depository" in compliance with the **DC IOLTA** program. The requirement **does not apply** if the lawyer is compliant with the trust-accounting/IOLTA rules of the jurisdiction where the lawyer is licensed and **principally practices** (Comment [4]). A lawyer who opted out of, or whose principal jurisdiction lacks, an IOLTA program is **not** excused from DC IOLTA. Interest remitted to the **D.C. Bar Foundation.**
4. **Firm funds in trust** — Rule 1.15(f): "Nothing in this rule shall prohibit a lawyer from placing a **small amount of the lawyer's funds** into a trust account for the **sole purpose of defraying bank charges** that may be made against that account." (No fixed cap; "small amount.")
5. **Advance fee / flat fee treatment** — Rule 1.15(e): "Advances of unearned fees and unincurred costs shall be treated as **property of the client** pursuant to paragraph (a) until earned or incurred **unless the client gives informed consent to a different arrangement.**" Comment [9]: the default is client property, but informed consent can make advances the lawyer's property; regardless, **Rule 1.16(d) requires return of any unearned advances at termination.** So DC permits "earned on receipt"/flat-fee-as-property **only with the client's informed consent.**
6. **Records retention period** — **Five (5) years** after termination of the representation (Rule 1.15(a)).
7. **Required records** — Rule 1.15(a): "Complete records." Comment [2] (citing _In re Clower_, 831 A.2d 1030 (D.C. 2003)): records must be complete enough that "the documentary record itself tells the full story" and an audit can be completed without the lawyer's or client's assistance; lawyers "may wish to look for guidance … [from] the 2010 ABA Model Rules for Client Trust Account Records." DC does **not** enumerate specific journals/ledgers in the black-letter rule.
8. **Reconciliation** — **Not specified in the black-letter rule.** DC points lawyers to the ABA Model Rules for Client Trust Account Records for record/reconciliation practices. **Three-way reconciliation / frequency: Unknown / needs verification** (no mandated cadence in Rule 1.15 text).
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — **Yes (via approved-depository requirement).** Trust funds must be at an "approved depository" as defined in Rule XI of the Rules Governing the DC Bar; approved-depository status entails an overdraft/dishonored-instrument reporting agreement with Disciplinary Counsel. **(Confirm exact Rule XI §20 overdraft-reporting language.)**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — Not addressed in Rule 1.15 text; governed by DC Bar Legal Ethics Committee opinions. **Unknown / needs verification.**
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — Rule 1.15(b)/Comment [6]: only funds "not expected to earn income in excess of costs" go in pooled DC IOLTA; the lawyer reviews at reasonable intervals; larger/longer-held funds belong in a separate (client-benefit) account.
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — (a) **Negligent misappropriation = presumptive disbarment** (_In re Addams_) — DC's signature feature; (b) advances are **client property by default** unless informed consent (stricter default than many states); (c) **multijurisdictional IOLTA opt-out** tied to principal-practice jurisdiction; (d) no enumerated ledgers/reconciliation cadence in the rule (relies on _Clower_ "complete records" standard and ABA Model recordkeeping rules).
13. **Source URLs** —

    - Approved IOLTA depositories: https://www.dcbar.org/attorney-discipline/board-on-professional-responsibility/approved-iolta-depositories
