## Alabama

1. **Governing rule citation** ✅ — Alabama Rule of Professional Conduct **1.15** ("Safekeeping Property"), as amended by Supreme Court of Alabama order Dec. 20, 2024, **effective February 15, 2025**. Recordkeeping and overdraft provisions are integrated into Rule 1.15(e)–(i). IOLTA provisions at Rule 1.15(j)–(k).
2. **Commingling prohibition** ✅ — Rule 1.15(a): property of clients/third persons must be kept separate from the lawyer's own property in a separate account. "No funds of a lawyer shall be deposited in such a trust account, except (1) unearned attorney fees that are being held until earned, and (2) funds sufficient to pay bank service charges … or to obtain a waiver thereof." Funds the lawyer is entitled to as fees/costs may not be used for personal/business expenses until removed from trust.
3. **IOLTA program** ✅ — **Mandatory** (Rule 1.15(j)–(k)). Lawyers must annually certify to the Alabama State Bar that all IOLTA-eligible funds are in an IOLTA account, or claim a listed exemption (no Alabama office; holds no client funds; not in active practice; certain government/in-house/teaching roles). Program beneficiary changed effective Feb. 15, 2025: IOLTA interest now paid to the **Alabama Law Foundation (ALF)** (formerly Alabama Civil Justice Foundation); Supreme Court of Alabama exercises oversight and approves distributions. Lawyer has no claim to interest.
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges?** ✅ — Yes: "funds sufficient to pay bank service charges on that account or to obtain a waiver thereof." No fixed dollar cap stated; limited to the amount sufficient for charges.
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** ⚠️ — Rule 1.15(a) expressly contemplates "unearned attorney fees that are being held until earned" inside the trust account (i.e., unearned fees belong in trust until earned). The rule text does not set out an "earned on receipt" / nonrefundable retainer safe harbor; consult Alabama ethics opinions (e.g., RO-2003-01 and related) for flat/nonrefundable fee treatment. **[VERIFY flat-fee / true-retainer doctrine via Office of General Counsel ethics opinions.]**
6. **Records retention** ✅ — **6 years** after termination of the representation (Rule 1.15(a) and 1.15(e)).
7. **Required records** ✅ — Rule 1.15(e): (1) receipt & disbursement journals; (2) client ledger records (per client/third person); (3) copies of retainer/compensation agreements; (4) accountings to clients/third persons; (5) bills for fees/expenses; (6) records of disbursements on behalf of clients; (7) checkbook registers, bank statements, deposit records, prenumbered canceled checks; (8) electronic-transfer records; (9) monthly trial balances and quarterly reconciliations; (10) portions of client files related to trust transactions. Signatories must be Alabama-admitted lawyers or persons under direct supervision; withdrawals only by check to a named payee (not cash) or authorized electronic transfer.
8. **Three-way reconciliation?** ⚠️ — Rule 1.15(e)(9) requires **monthly trial balances and quarterly reconciliations**. (This is a trial-balance + reconciliation regime; whether it is a strict "three-way" tie-out is implied by the trial-balance + reconciliation pairing. **Confirm whether OGC treats this as three-way.**)
9. **Trust overdraft notification** ✅ — Rule 1.15(i): lawyer must have an agreement with the financial institution requiring the bank to report to the **Office of General Counsel, Alabama State Bar**, whenever a properly payable item is presented against insufficient funds (whether returned, or honored but the overdraft not cured within **3 business days**). Filing of a report is a proper basis for OGC investigation.
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments** ⚠️ — Rule 1.15(e)(8) requires detailed records of electronic transfers. Rule does not contain a specific credit-card-merchant-fee provision in the captured text. **[VERIFY credit-card fee handling via OGC guidance.]**
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** ✅ — IOLTA holds funds "nominal in amount or … held for a short period and from which no income could be earned … in excess of the costs incurred to secure such income." Larger/longer-held funds go in a separate client-specific interest-bearing account for the client's benefit. Multi-factor test in Rule 1.15(k). No fixed dollar threshold in rule.
12. **State-specific quirks** ✅ — (a) Trust accounts must be designated "Attorney Trust Account," "Attorney Escrow Account," or "Attorney Fiduciary Account"; business accounts must carry specific business designations. (b) Disbursement against uncollected funds permitted only at lawyer's own risk with prompt-collection belief, with replacement within **5 working days** of non-collection notice (Rule 1.15(d)). (c) 2025 amendment moved IOLTA beneficiary to ALF under direct Supreme Court oversight.
13. **Source URLs** —
    - Rule 1.15 (amended, eff. 2/15/2025) PDF: https://judicial.alabama.gov/docs/rules/Rule1_15AlaRProfCon.pdf
    - Alabama rules index: https://judicial.alabama.gov/rules/index
    - Alabama Law Foundation (IOLTA): https://alabamalawfoundation.org/

## Alaska

9. **Trust overdraft notification** ❓ **[LOW CONFIDENCE]** — Not in Rule 1.15 captured text. Verify whether Alaska has a separate dishonored-instrument/overdraft notification rule. **Verify via Alaska Bar Rules.**

12. **State-specific quirks** ✅ — Explicit **$100 interest** ceiling for IOLTA eligibility; annual bar-dues-notice election mechanism; remittance to Alaska Bar Foundation.

## California

1. **Governing rule citation** ✅ — California Rule of Professional Conduct **1.15** ("Safekeeping Funds and Property of Clients and Other Persons"), approved by the Supreme Court eff. **Nov. 1, 2018** (a superseding/clarifying version was noted eff. Jan. 1, 2023; verify current text). Related: **Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 6211–6213** (IOLTA), and the **Client Trust Account Protection Program (CTAPP)** rules (State Bar Rules 2.5 et seq.), eff. 2023.
2. **Commingling prohibition** ✅ — Rule 1.15(a): all funds for the benefit of a client/other person, **including advances for fees, costs and expenses**, must be deposited in a labeled "Trust Account" (maintained in California, or elsewhere with written consent + substantial relationship). Rule 1.15(c): lawyer/firm funds may not be commingled except (1) funds reasonably sufficient to pay bank charges and (2) mixed funds, with the lawyer's portion withdrawn at the earliest reasonable time once fixed; disputed portions stay in trust until resolved.
3. **IOLTA program** ✅/⚠️ — **Mandatory** under Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 6211–6212. Nominal/short-term client funds go in an IOLTA account; interest remitted to the **State Bar of California** for legal-services-to-the-poor grants. **[VERIFY current statutory remittance mechanics and rate-comparability rules.]**
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges?** ✅ — Rule 1.15(c)(1): "funds reasonably sufficient to pay bank charges." No fixed dollar cap.
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** ✅ — **Strongest, most explicit in this group.** Rule 1.15(a) requires advance fees in trust. Rule 1.15(b): a **flat fee paid in advance MAY go in the operating account** only if (1) the lawyer discloses in writing that the client has a right to require trust deposit until earned and a right to a refund of any unearned portion; and (2) **if the flat fee exceeds $1,000**, the client's agreement and the (b)(1) disclosures are in a **writing signed by the client**. Comment [2]/[3] distinguish a **true retainer** (a fee paid solely to secure availability, earned on receipt, and **not** placed in trust — see Rule 1.5(d)) from a flat/advance fee. Comment confirms an advance fee is the client's property until earned.
6. **Records retention** ✅ — **No less than 5 years** after final appropriate distribution of funds/property (Rule 1.15(d)(3) / safekeeping records provisions).
7. **Required records** ✅ — Rule 1.15 (and the State Bar's record-keeping standards incorporated by reference): (a) a **written ledger** for each client/person; (b) a **written journal** for each bank account; (c) all bank statements and canceled checks; (d) **each monthly reconciliation (balancing)** of the ledger, journal and account. Separate written journal required for securities/other property held.
8. **Three-way reconciliation?** ✅ — Yes (in substance): Rule 1.15(d) requires **monthly reconciliation** balancing the client ledgers (a), the bank-account journal (b), and the bank records (c). (Monthly.)
9. **Trust overdraft notification** ✅/⚠️ — Yes. California's **CTAPP** (operative 2023) plus the requirement that trust accounts be at financial institutions that have agreed to report dishonored instruments to the State Bar; CTAPP also requires **annual registration and self-reporting/certification** of client trust accounts. **[VERIFY current CTAPP reporting specifics.]**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments** ❓ — Not detailed in Rule 1.15 text; State Bar guidance addresses credit-card processing (merchant fees should not be charged to the trust account / should be drawn from operating). **[VERIFY via State Bar trust accounting guidance.]**
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** ⚠️ — Nominal/short-term funds → IOLTA; funds that can earn net income for the client → separate client-benefit interest-bearing account. No fixed dollar threshold; multi-factor judgment. **[VERIFY.]**
12. **State-specific quirks** ✅ — (a) **$1,000 flat-fee signed-writing** trigger; explicit **true-retainer doctrine** via Rule 1.5(d). (b) **CTAPP** annual registration/self-certification regime (2023) is distinctive. (c) Trust account must generally be maintained **in California** absent written consent.
13. **Source URLs** —
    - Rule 1.15 (text + exec summary): https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/rules/Rule_1.15-Exec_Summary-Redline.pdf and current rules list: https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Attorneys/Conduct-Discipline/Rules/Rules-of-Professional-Conduct/Current-Rules

## Connecticut

1. **Governing rule citation** ✅ — Connecticut **Rules of Professional Conduct Rule 1.15** ("Safekeeping Property") (in the Connecticut Practice Book; current text amended June 10, 2022, eff. Jan. 1, 2023). IOLTA defined within Rule 1.15(a)–(i).
2. **Commingling prohibition** ✅ — Rule 1.15(b): lawyer must hold client/third-person property separate; funds in a separate account in the state (or elsewhere with consent). Rule 1.15(c): lawyer may deposit own funds **only** to pay bank service charges or obtain a fee waiver, **and only in an amount necessary** for those purposes.
3. **IOLTA program** ✅ — **Mandatory** (Rule 1.15(h)): "lawyers and law firms shall participate in the statutory program for the use of interest earned on lawyers' clients' funds accounts." Good-faith multi-factor determination of whether funds can earn net income; nominal/short-term funds go in an IOLTA account. Program administered by the organization designated by the judges of the Superior Court (**Connecticut Bar Foundation / IOLTA program**).
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges?** ✅ — Yes; Rule 1.15(c), "only in an amount necessary for those purposes." No fixed cap.
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** ✅ — Rule 1.15(d): "**Absent a written agreement with the client otherwise**, a lawyer shall deposit into a client trust account legal fees and expenses that have been paid in advance, to be withdrawn … only as fees are earned or expenses incurred." (Note the "absent a written agreement otherwise" carve-out — Connecticut permits an alternative arrangement by written agreement.) **[VERIFY scope of the written-agreement exception via CT ethics opinions.]**
6. **Records retention** ✅ — **7 years** after termination of the representation (Rule 1.15(b) and the records subsection).
7. **Required records** ✅ — Rule 1.15 records subsection: (1) receipt/disbursement **journals**; (2) **ledger records** per client/beneficiary; (3) copies of retainer/compensation agreements (per Rule 1.5); (4) accountings to clients/third persons; (5) bills for fees/expenses; (6) records of disbursements on behalf of clients; (7) checkbook registers, bank statements, deposit records, prenumbered canceled & substitute checks; (8) electronic-transfer records (authorizer, date, recipient, confirmation); (9) **monthly trial balances and at least quarterly reconciliations**; (10) portions of client files related to trust transactions. Withdrawals only by check to a named payee or authorized electronic transfer (**not to cash**).
8. **Three-way reconciliation?** ✅ — Rule requires **monthly trial balances and at least quarterly reconciliations**. The official commentary states "Quarterly reconciliation is recommended only as a minimum requirement; **monthly reconciliation is the preferred**" practice. (Tie-out of trial balance to journal to statement.)
9. **Trust overdraft notification** ❓ — Not in the Rule 1.15 text captured; Connecticut has a separate overdraft notification mechanism (eligible-institution reporting). **[VERIFY via Practice Book / CT Judicial Branch.]**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments** ⚠️ — Rule 1.15 requires detailed electronic-transfer records; commentary addresses not remitting funds the lawyer believes represent earned fees. Credit-card merchant-fee handling not specified in captured text. **[VERIFY.]**
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** ✅ — Good-faith determination based on amount, expected duration, applicable interest rates, and account costs (Rule 1.15(h) factors). No fixed dollar threshold.
12. **State-specific quirks** ✅ — (a) The "**absent a written agreement with the client otherwise**" advance-fee carve-out (1.15(d)) is distinctive. (b) Rules contained in the **Connecticut Practice Book** (court-promulgated, not bar-promulgated). (c) Dissolution/sale-of-practice record-maintenance duties (1.15(m)–(n)).

## Delaware

1. **Governing rule citation** ✅ — Delaware Lawyers' Rules of Professional Conduct **Rule 1.15** ("Safekeeping property") with extensive books-and-records provisions at Rule 1.15(d), and **Rule 1.15A** ("Trust account overdraft notification"). Annual compliance via **Supreme Court Rule 69** (Certificate of Compliance). Advance-fee handling in **Rule 1.5(f)**.
2. **Commingling prohibition** ✅ — Rule 1.15(a): client/third-person property kept separate; funds in a separate account "designated solely for funds held in connection with the practice of law in Delaware," in the lawyer's office state (or elsewhere with consent), except IOLTA-eligible funds per (g). Lawyer's own funds for bank charges allowed **but capped** (see #4). Rule 1.15(d)(12)(G): no lawyer funds in the account except as in (a); (H) earned fees must be transferred out promptly.
3. **IOLTA program** ✅ — **Mandatory** (Rule 1.15(g)): a lawyer who determines under (f) that funds are IOLTA-eligible (nominal/short-term) "must establish and maintain one or more pooled … (IOLTA Account)" in a Delaware financial institution. Exemptions for inactive status (SCR 69(d)) or Certificate of Retirement (SCR 69(f)). Administered by the **Delaware Bar Foundation**; comparable-interest-rate and "Safe Harbor Rate" requirements for eligible institutions. Lawyer has no claim to interest.
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges?** ✅ — Yes, but **explicitly capped at $2,000**: "Funds of the lawyer that are reasonably sufficient to pay financial institution charges may be deposited … however, such amount **may not exceed $2,000** and must be separately stated and accounted for" (Rule 1.15(a)). (Notable — a hard dollar cap, unlike most states.)
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** ✅ — Rule 1.5(f): a lawyer may require advance payment provided (1) written statement that the fee is refundable if not earned; (2) written statement of the basis on which fees are earned; (3) **"All unearned fees shall be retained in the lawyer's trust account,"** with a statement of fees earned provided when funds are withdrawn. Comment [9]: "All fees paid in advance are refundable until earned." (Delaware does **not** permit treating unearned advance fees as earned-on-receipt; they stay in trust until earned. True/availability retainers should be analyzed under Rule 1.5.)

8. **Three-way reconciliation?** ✅ — **Yes, monthly.** Rule 1.15(d)(6)–(8) & (12)(D): manual systems total/balance journals monthly and perform a monthly reconciliation matching journal totals to the check-register balance; the general-ledger balance must be **reconciled monthly** to the bank reconciliation; and **"The monthly bank reconciliation must agree with the total of the client or third party balance listing and the general ledger balance"** — a true three-way tie-out, monthly, with "no unidentified client or third party funds."

## Florida

1. **Governing rule citation** ✅ — Trust-account rules in **Chapter 5, Rules Regulating The Florida Bar** ("Rules Regulating Trust Accounts," Rules **5-1.1** and **5-1.2**), plus **Rule 4-1.15** (Safekeeping Property) in Chapter 4. (Captured text: Chapter 5 RRTFB, current as of Feb. 16, 2026.)

## Cross-cutting notes (for the implementing team)

- **ABA Model Rule 1.15 baseline.** Every jurisdiction here tracks ABA Model Rule 1.15 (separation, no commingling, prompt notice/delivery, dispute-segregation, lawyer's funds allowed only to cover bank charges). Differences are in (a) advance/flat-fee deposit rules, (b) retention period (5 vs 6 vs 7 years), (c) reconciliation cadence, and (d) where the detailed recordkeeping/IOLTA mechanics live (in-rule vs. a separate trust-accounting rule/bar-commission rule).
- **Records retention varies:** 5 years (Alaska, California, Delaware, Idaho; likely Arizona/Arkansas/Indiana — verify); 6 years (Alabama, Florida, Hawaii); 7 years (Connecticut; likely Colorado, Illinois — verify).
- **Where the detail lives:** Several states put the prescriptive trust-accounting rules **outside** RPC 1.15 — Florida (Chapter 5), Idaho (IBCR Section XIII), Hawaii (HRGTA + RSCH Rule 11), Colorado (1.15A–E), Illinois (1.15A/B/C, new 2023), Arizona (Sup. Ct. Rule 43), Indiana (Adm. & Disc. Rule 23). Delaware, by contrast, packs a full books-and-records + three-way-reconciliation regime directly into Rule 1.15(d). Implementers must pull from all relevant sources per state.
- **Three-way reconciliation expressly required (confirmed):** California (monthly), Connecticut (monthly trial balance + ≥quarterly reconciliation), Delaware (monthly), Florida (monthly). Alabama requires monthly trial balances + quarterly reconciliations.
- **Bank-charge cushion cap:** Most states allow only an amount "reasonably sufficient" with no dollar figure; **Delaware imposes a hard $2,000 cap** — the only fixed cap confirmed in this group.
- **Overdraft notification recipient differs:** Alabama & Georgia → State Bar Office of General Counsel; Florida → Bar Staff Counsel; Delaware → Office of Disciplinary Counsel (institutions approved by the LFCP); Illinois → ARDC; Indiana → Disciplinary Commission (verify); Colorado → OARC (verify). Confirmed programs: AL, DE, FL, GA, IL (structure). Others need verification.
