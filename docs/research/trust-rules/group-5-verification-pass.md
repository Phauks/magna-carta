## Kentucky — overall confidence: HIGH (rule text verified)

Verified against full text of **SCR 3.130(1.15)** (eff. 4/1/2022) and **SCR 3.830** (Kentucky IOLTA Fund, eff. 3/1/2020) via the govt.westlaw.com Kentucky Court Rules mirror.

1. **Governing rule citation** — **Kentucky Supreme Court Rule (SCR) 3.130(1.15)** "Safekeeping property" (the KY RPC are codified within SCR 3.130). IOLTA program: **SCR 3.830** "Kentucky IOLTA fund." Advance-fee cross-reference: SCR 3.130(1.5)(f).
2. **Commingling prohibition** — SCR 3.130(1.15)(a): lawyer "shall hold property of clients or third persons … separate from the lawyer's own property"; funds in a separate account in the state of the lawyer's office (or elsewhere with consent). (c): disputed funds kept separate until the dispute is resolved; undisputed portions promptly distributed.
3. **IOLTA program** — **MANDATORY.** SCR 3.830: a lawyer/firm "**shall** create and maintain … an interest-bearing trust account for clients' funds which are nominal in amount or to be held for a short period." Administered by the **Kentucky Bar Foundation, Inc.** as the **Kentucky IOLTA Fund**. No earnings to the lawyer/firm. Comparability: institutions pay the highest rate available to non-IOLTA customers, OR may elect a safe-harbor of **70% of the federal funds targeted rate** (when that rate is 1.0%–4.0%). Interest net of fees **remitted to IOLTA**; depository submits reports.
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges? Cap?** — Yes. SCR 3.130(1.15)(d): a lawyer "may deposit the lawyer's own funds in a client trust account for the sole purpose of paying bank service charges on that account, **but only in an amount necessary for that purpose**." No fixed dollar cap.
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** — SCR 3.130(1.15)(e): "**Except for advance fees as provided in 1.5(f)**, a lawyer shall deposit into a client trust account legal fees and expenses that have been paid in advance, to be withdrawn … only as fees are earned or expenses incurred." So advance fees go in trust **unless** the fee qualifies under SCR 3.130(1.5)(f). **[VERIFY exact 1.5(f) conditions for the earned-on-receipt/flat-fee carve-out — not pulled this pass.]**
6. **Records retention period** — **Five (5) years** after termination of the representation (SCR 3.130(1.15)(a): "preserved for a period of five years after termination of the representation").
7. **Required records** — SCR 3.130(1.15)(a) requires "**complete records** of such account funds and other property." The rule does **not** enumerate specific journals/client ledgers in the black-letter text. **[VERIFY whether KBA recordkeeping guidance / a separate rule itemizes ledgers and journals.]**
8. **Three-way reconciliation required? Frequency?** — **Not required by the rule text.** SCR 3.130(1.15) contains no reconciliation mandate ("reconcil-" does not appear). MEDIUM-HIGH confidence it is not codified; best practice only. **[VERIFY no KBA regulation imposes it.]**
9. **Trust overdraft notification program** — **YES — built into the rule.** SCR 3.130(1.15)(a): the trust account "shall be maintained in a bank which has agreed to **notify the Kentucky Bar Association in the event that any overdraft occurs** in the account." Banks report overdrafts to the KBA.
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — Not addressed in SCR 3.130(1.15) or SCR 3.830 text. **Unknown / needs verification** (check KBA ethics/trust-account guidance; general principle: processor fees not charged to client funds).
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — SCR 3.830(1): no funds in an IOLTA account where the amount/duration would earn the client interest above the costs to generate it; such funds go in a separate client-benefit account. No fixed dollar threshold (net-earnings test; lawyer's judgment).
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — (a) Distinct citation scheme **SCR 3.130(1.15)** with IOLTA in **SCR 3.830**. (b) Overdraft-notification requirement is **embedded directly in Rule 1.15(a)** (notice runs to the KBA). (c) **70%-of-fed-funds** IOLTA comparability safe harbor. (d) Subsections relettered: bank-charge funds are paragraph **(d)** and advance fees are **(e)** (not the ABA (b)/(c) lettering).
13. **Source URLs** —

## Maryland — overall confidence: HIGH (trust-account rules verified; RPC 19-301.15/1.5 advance-fee detail partial)

Verified against full text of **Maryland Rules Title 19, Chapter 400 (Attorney Trust Accounts)** — Rules 19-407 (record-keeping), 19-408 (commingling), 19-409 (interest/IOLTA), 19-411 (approval of institutions/overdraft) — via the govt.westlaw.com Maryland Code & Court Rules mirror.

1. **Governing rule citation** — **MARPC Rule 19-301.15** (= "1.15," Safekeeping Property) in Title 19, Chapter 300 (Maryland Attorneys'' Rules of Professional Conduct). Operative trust-accounting detail is in **Title 19, Chapter 400 (Attorney Trust Accounts)**: **19-402** (definitions), **19-403** (duty to maintain account), **19-404** (required deposits), **19-405** (duty to notify institution), **19-406** (name/designation), **19-407** (record-keeping), **19-408** (commingling), **19-409** (interest on funds / IOLTA reporting), **19-410** (prohibited transactions), **19-411** (approval of institutions / overdraft), **19-414** (funds presumed abandoned).
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Rule **19-408(a)**: an attorney may deposit in a trust account **only** funds required by Rule 19-404 or permitted by 19-408(b). 19-408(b): exceptions are (1) funds to pay fees/service charges/minimum balance (or an agreement to deduct from an operating account), plus expected client-cost advances; (2) mixed funds (lawyer''s portion withdrawn promptly when entitled; disputed portion stays until resolved); (3) pooling of multiple clients'' funds permitted. Rule 19-410(c) prohibits any disbursement that would create a negative balance for any client matter or in the aggregate.
3. **IOLTA program** — **MANDATORY.** Rule **19-409(b)**: interest (net of service charges/fees) belongs to the client/third person, **except to the extent paid to the Maryland Legal Services Corporation (MLSC) Fund as authorized by law** (IOLTA); attorney has no claim to the interest. **19-409(c)**: as a **condition precedent to the practice of law**, every active attorney must file an annual **IOLTA Compliance Report** (AOC sends notice by July 10; report due Sept. 10 via AIS) listing each IOLTA account; default leads to a **Decertification Order** barring practice. IOLTA administered in conjunction with **MLSC**.
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges? Cap?** — Yes. Rule **19-408(b)(1)**: the lawyer may deposit funds to pay any fees, service charges, or **minimum balance** required to open/maintain the account (or instead agree to have charges deducted from an operating account). **No fixed dollar cap.** Rule 19-407(a)(4) requires a record identifying the attorney''s own funds held in the account.
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** — Trust-account chapter permits depositing "funds expected to be advanced on behalf of a client and expected to be reimbursed" (19-408(b)(1)). The advance-legal-fee deposit rule lives in **MARPC 19-301.15** (= ABA 1.15(c)) and **19-301.5 (1.5)**. **[VERIFY exact 19-301.15(c)/1.5 advance-fee and any earned-on-receipt/flat-fee treatment — RPC 19-301.15 black-letter text not pulled this pass; Chapter 300 is nested under sub-headings.]** MEDIUM confidence: Maryland follows the ABA "deposit advance fees, withdraw as earned" model.
6. **Records retention period** — **Five (5) years** after the date the record was created (Rule **19-407(d)**).
7. **Required records** — Rule **19-407(a)**: (1) identification of all trust accounts (institution, account number/name, open/close dates, the interest-bearing agreement); (2) a per-account chronological **deposits-and-disbursements record** (date, amount, client/third person, purpose; for each disbursement incl. electronic transfer, date/amount/payee/client/purpose; plus a written memo authorizing each electronic transfer and the responsible attorney); (3) **per-client-matter records** (each transaction''s date/amount/purpose/payee/check-number and running matter balance; identity of person to receive any returned unused fee/expense); (4) a record identifying the attorney''s own funds in the account. Electronic records allowed if printable (19-407(c)).
8. **Three-way reconciliation required? Frequency?** — **YES — monthly (effectively three-way).** Rule **19-407(b)**: the attorney must create a **monthly reconciliation** of (i) all trust-account records, (ii) client-matter records, (iii) records of the attorney''s own funds held in the account, and (iv) the **adjusted month-end financial-institution statement balance** (bank balance adjusted for subsequent deposits/disbursements). Ties bank balance to the sum of client-matter balances.
9. **Trust overdraft notification program** — **YES.** Rule **19-411**: a financial institution is "approved" only upon filing a written agreement (with MLSC, via the Commission) requiring it, as a condition of accepting trust deposits, to (A) promptly notify the attorney of any overdraft/dishonor and (B) **report the overdraft or dishonor to Bar Counsel**. Dishonored-instrument reports sent within the time for notice of dishonor; honored-against-insufficient-funds items also reported.
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — Rule 19-407(a)(2)(B)-(C) expressly covers **disbursements by electronic transfer** (full transaction record + written authorization memo naming the responsible attorney). Credit-card processor-fee allocation not specified. **Unknown / needs verification** (check Maryland Attorney Trust Account Manual / Bar Counsel guidance).
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — Rule 19-409(b): interest belongs to the client/third person except where paid to the MLSC Fund (IOLTA) for nominal/short-term funds. No fixed dollar threshold (net-earnings/nominal-or-short-term test). **[VERIFY exact nominal/short-term factors in 19-404/19-409 if a precise threshold is needed.]**
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — (a) **Renumbered RPC** to the **19-30x.xx** scheme ("MARPC"; RPC 1.15 = **19-301.15**); operative mechanics are in **Chapter 400** (19-401 to 19-414), not the RPC. (b) IOLTA interest runs to the **Maryland Legal Services Corporation Fund**. (c) **Mandatory annual IOLTA Compliance Report is a condition precedent to practicing law**, enforced by decertification. (d) Overdraft reports run to **Bar Counsel**; approval agreements filed with **MLSC/the Commission**. (e) **19-414** addresses trust funds presumed abandoned. (Rules 19-409/19-411 show "Effective April 1, 2023"; 19-409 version effective **through June 30, 2026** — confirm any 7/1/2026 successor.)

## Nebraska — overall confidence: HIGH (rule text verified)

Verified against full text of **Neb. Ct. R. of Prof. Cond. § 3-501.15** (Safekeeping property) and **Article 9 — Trust Fund Requirements for Lawyers** (§§ 3-901 to 3-907), especially § 3-903 (interest-bearing trust accounts/IOLTA) and § 3-904 (overdraft notification), via the official Nebraska Judicial Branch site (nebraskajudicial.gov; the rules portal migrated from supremecourt.nebraska.gov).

1. **Governing rule citation** — **Neb. Ct. R. of Prof. Cond. § 3-501.15** "Safekeeping property" (Nebraska RPC are numbered § 3-501.x within Chapter 3, Article 5). Trust-fund/IOLTA/overdraft/certification/audit mechanics are in **Chapter 3, Article 9 (Trust Fund Requirements for Lawyers)**: § 3-901 (definitions), § 3-902 (general provisions), **§ 3-903 (interest-bearing trust accounts / IOLTA)**, **§ 3-904 (overdraft notification)**, § 3-905 (trust account certification), § 3-906 (trust account audit), § 3-907 (purpose).
2. **Commingling prohibition** — § 3-501.15(a): a lawyer "shall hold property of clients or third persons … separate from the lawyer''s own property"; funds in a separate account **maintained in the state where the lawyer''s office is situated**. (e): when two or more persons (possibly including the lawyer) claim interests, property is kept separate until the dispute is resolved; undisputed portions promptly distributed.
3. **IOLTA program** — **OPT-OUT (mandatory unless a written Notice of Declination is filed).** § 3-903(B): "**Unless an election not to do so is submitted** in accordance with § 3-903(C), a lawyer or law firm shall maintain an interest-bearing insured trust account for clients'' funds which are nominal in amount or … held for a short time." Election to decline is filed in writing with the Chief Justice **by February 15** of the applicable year (§ 3-903(C)). Interest (net of service charges/fees/intangible taxes) belongs to clients; for IOLTA-deposited nominal/short-term funds it is **remitted at least quarterly to the Nebraska Lawyers Trust Account Foundation**, used **solely for the support of Legal Aid of Nebraska** (§ 3-903(B)(5)-(6)). No earnings to the lawyer/firm.
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges? Cap?** — Yes. § 3-501.15(b): a lawyer "may deposit the lawyer''s own funds in a client trust account for the sole purpose of paying bank service charges on that account, **but only in an amount necessary for that purpose**." No fixed dollar cap.
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** — § 3-501.15(c): "A lawyer shall deposit into a client trust account legal fees and expenses that have been paid in advance, to be withdrawn by the lawyer only as fees are earned or expenses incurred." Nebraska tracks ABA Model Rule 1.15(c) verbatim — advance fees go in trust until earned. The rule text contains **no earned-on-receipt / flat-fee carve-out**; true-retainer/flat-fee treatment, if any, would come from § 3-501.5 and Nebraska ethics opinions. **[VERIFY § 3-501.5 / NSBA ethics opinions for any flat-fee exception.]**
6. **Records retention period** — **Five (5) years** after termination of the representation (§ 3-501.15(a): "preserved for a period of 5 years after termination of the representation").
7. **Required records** — § 3-501.15(a) requires "**complete records** of such account funds and other property." Comment [1] directs the lawyer to "maintain on a current basis books and records in accordance with generally accepted accounting practice and comply with any recordkeeping rules established by law or court order" (i.e., ABA Model Financial Recordkeeping pattern). The rule does **not** itemize client ledgers/journals in black-letter. **[VERIFY whether § 3-905 certification rule or NSBA guidance enumerates specific ledgers/journals.]**
8. **Three-way reconciliation required? Frequency?** — **Not expressly required by § 3-501.15.** No reconciliation cadence is stated in the rule text. Nebraska does have a **trust account certification rule (§ 3-905)** and an **audit rule (§ 3-906)** — **[VERIFY whether § 3-905/§ 3-906 impose a reconciliation requirement/frequency.]** MEDIUM confidence that no formal three-way reconciliation is codified in the RPC itself.
9. **Trust overdraft notification program** — **YES — mandatory.** § 3-904: a financial institution is approved as a trust depository only if it files an agreement with the **Counsel for Discipline of the Nebraska Supreme Court** to **report to the Counsel for Discipline** any properly payable instrument presented against a trust account with insufficient funds, whether or not honored. No trust account may be held at a non-reporting institution; agreement covers all branches, cancelable only on 30 days'' notice. Dishonored-instrument and honored-against-NSF reports delivered within **5 banking days**; the Counsel for Discipline publishes an annual list of approved institutions.
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — Not addressed in § 3-501.15 or Article 9 text reviewed. **Unknown / needs verification** (check NSBA / Counsel for Discipline guidance; general principle: processor fees not charged to client funds).
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — § 3-903(B)(2): the IOLTA account includes **only** clients'' funds nominal in amount or to be held for a short period; larger/longer-held funds earning net interest for the client go in a separate client-benefit interest-bearing account. No fixed dollar threshold (net-earnings test; lawyer''s judgment).
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — (a) Unusual citation scheme **§ 3-501.15** (Article 5 RPC) with the operative trust-fund regime in **Article 9** (§§ 3-901 to 3-907). (b) IOLTA is **opt-out via a February 15 Notice of Declination**, not strictly mandatory. (c) IOLTA interest funds **Legal Aid of Nebraska** through the **Nebraska Lawyers Trust Account Foundation**. (d) Overdraft reports run to the **Counsel for Discipline**. (e) Separate **trust account certification (§ 3-905)** and **audit (§ 3-906)** rules.

## Missouri — overall confidence: HIGH (rule text verified)

Verified against full text of **Mo. Sup. Ct. Rule 4-1.15** "Trust Accounts and Property of Others" and **Rule 4-1.155** "IOLTA Accounts" via the official courts.mo.gov rules pages (rendered with Playwright). (Rule 4-1.145 defines IOLTA/approved-institution terms; not fully pulled.)

1. **Governing rule citation** — **Mo. Sup. Ct. Rule 4-1.15** "Trust Accounts and Property of Others" (the Missouri RPC are Rule 4; RPC 1.15 = **Rule 4-1.15**). Companion rules: **Rule 4-1.145** (definitions / IOLTA framework), **Rule 4-1.155** "IOLTA Accounts," and **Rule 4-1.22** "File Retention." Overdraft reporting is implemented via advisory-committee regulations referenced in Rule 4-1.15(a)(2).
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Rule 4-1.15(a): a lawyer "shall hold property of clients or third persons … separate from the lawyer''s own property"; client/third-party funds in a separate account **designated "Client Trust Account"** (or similar), in the state of the lawyer''s office (or elsewhere with consent). (a)(1): every client trust account is an IOLTA, non-IOLTA, or exempt account; **no earnings to any lawyer/firm**. (e): disputed-interest property kept separate until resolved; undisputed portions promptly distributed. (a)(4): **receipts deposited intact**; (a)(5): withdrawals **only by check to a named payee (not cash) or authorized electronic transfer**; (a)(6): no disbursement against uncollected funds.
3. **IOLTA program** — **MANDATORY** (unless exempt or all accounts are non-IOLTA). Rule 4-1.15(g): a lawyer/firm "shall establish and maintain one or more IOLTA accounts into which shall be deposited all funds of clients or third persons" per Rules 4-1.145 to 4-1.155. Administered by the **Missouri Lawyer Trust Account Foundation (MoLTAF)** (defined in Rule 4-1.145). Rule 4-1.155(a): IOLTA holds funds from which no net income could be earned for the client; no earnings to the lawyer. **[VERIFY exact remittance frequency/percentages in Rule 4-1.145.]**
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges? Cap?** — Yes. Rule 4-1.15(b): a lawyer "may deposit the lawyer''s own funds in a client trust account for the sole purpose of paying financial institution service charges on that account, **but only in an amount necessary for that purpose**." No fixed dollar cap.
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** — Rule 4-1.15(c): advance legal fees/expenses go in a client trust account, withdrawn only as earned/incurred — **EXCEPT** "an **advanced flat fee which does not exceed $2,000** is exempted from this requirement and may be deposited into another account." **Notable Missouri quirk: a $2,000 advanced-flat-fee safe harbor** (flat fee ≤ $2,000 need not be held in trust). Flat fees above $2,000, and other advance fees, go in trust until earned.
6. **Records retention period** — **Six (6) years** after the **later of** (1) termination of the representation or (2) the date of the last disbursement of funds (Rule 4-1.15(f)). (Note: this differs from the often-assumed 5 years.)
7. **Required records** — Rule 4-1.15(f)(1)-(11): (1) **receipt and disbursement journals** (date, source, description of deposits; date, payee, purpose of disbursements); (2) **ledger records for all client trust accounts** showing per client/beneficiary the source/amount of funds, persons for whom held, charges/withdrawals, and disbursement payees; (3) fee/engagement/retainer/compensation agreements; (4) accountings to clients/third persons; (5) bills for fees/expenses; (6) disbursement records; (7) checkbook registers, bank statements, deposit records, prenumbered canceled/substitute checks; (8) **electronic-transfer records** (authorizer, date, recipient, confirming trust account number, completion date/time); (9) **reconciliations**; (10) trust-related portions of client files; (11) **records of credit-card transactions** with clients (to the extent permitted by law and the PCI-DSS).
8. **Three-way reconciliation required? Frequency?** — **Reconciliation required each statement cycle; the rule does not use the literal phrase "three-way."** Rule 4-1.15(a)(7): "A reconciliation of the account shall be performed reasonably promptly each time an official statement from the financial institution is provided or available" (i.e., **monthly** in practice). Rule 4-1.15(f)(9) requires reconciliations be kept. Given the mandated per-client ledger records (f)(2) plus journals (f)(1), this functions as a three-way regime, but the rule text mandates "a reconciliation," not an explicit bank=journal=client-ledger tie-out. MEDIUM-HIGH confidence. **[VERIFY whether OCDC/Court Operating Rule 4 (Uniform Record Keeping) specifies a formal three-way reconciliation.]**
9. **Trust overdraft notification program** — **YES.** Rule 4-1.15(a)(2): client trust accounts must be in an **approved institution**, and every Missouri lawyer "shall be **conclusively deemed to have consented to the overdraft reporting and production requirements** mandated by the regulations adopted by the advisory committee." Banks report overdrafts under those regulations. **[VERIFY exact reporting recipient/timing in the advisory-committee regulations referenced.]**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — Addressed: Rule 4-1.15(a)(5) permits authorized electronic transfers; (f)(8) requires detailed electronic-transfer records; (f)(11) **expressly requires records of credit-card transactions with clients** to the extent permitted by law and the **payment card industry data security standard (PCI-DSS)**. Processor-fee allocation specifics not stated; general principle is fees not charged to client principal. **[VERIFY fee-allocation guidance.]**
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — Rule 4-1.155(a)(2)-(5): IOLTA holds funds from which no income could be earned for the client in excess of the costs to secure it; other funds go in a **non-IOLTA trust account**. Multi-factor test (amount/expected interest, cost of a separate account incl. lawyer''s services and tax reporting, institution capability) resting in the lawyer''s **good-faith judgment**; reviewed at reasonable intervals. No fixed dollar threshold.
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — (a) RPC numbered **Rule 4-x.xx** (RPC 1.15 = **4-1.15**); trust-account regime split across **4-1.15 / 4-1.145 / 4-1.155 / 4-1.22**. (b) **$2,000 advanced-flat-fee exemption** from the trust-deposit requirement — distinctive. (c) **6-year retention measured from the later of termination or last disbursement.** (d) Three account types: **IOLTA / non-IOLTA / exempt**. (e) Annual **trust-account certification on the enrollment statement** (4-1.15(h)). (f) **Deemed consent** to overdraft reporting. (g) Express **credit-card / PCI-DSS** recordkeeping requirement (f)(11).

## Louisiana — overall confidence: HIGH (RPC text verified; overdraft program outside the RPC PDF)

## North Dakota — overall confidence: HIGH (rule text verified)

Verified against full text of **N.D. R. Prof. Conduct 1.15** "Safekeeping Property and Professional Liability Insurance Disclosure" (**effective 4/1/2026**) via the official ndcourts.gov rules viewer.

1. **Governing rule citation** — **N.D. R. Prof. Conduct 1.15** "Safekeeping Property and Professional Liability Insurance Disclosure." IOLTA administered by the **North Dakota Bar Foundation** under **Administrative Rule 24** of the North Dakota Supreme Court; overdraft program and approved institutions administered by the **Disciplinary Board**.
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Rule 1.15(a): a lawyer "shall hold property of clients or third persons … separate from the lawyer''s own property"; funds **deposited in one or more identifiable interest-bearing trust accounts** per (f). (e): property in which two or more persons (possibly the lawyer) claim interests kept separate until the dispute is resolved; undisputed portions promptly distributed.
3. **IOLTA program** — **MANDATORY.** Rule 1.15(f)(1): "A lawyer who receives funds of clients or third persons **shall maintain a pooled interest-bearing trust account** for … funds … nominal in amount or expected to be held for a short period." Interest (net of transaction costs) is **paid to and administered by the North Dakota Bar Foundation** per Administrative Rule 24; the **Foundation holds the entire beneficial interest** in the interest. (All ND trust accounts must be interest-bearing.)
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges? Cap?** — Yes. Rule 1.15(b): a lawyer may deposit own funds "only for the purpose of **paying bank service charges and fees associated with credit card payments or electronic funds transfer payments** related to that account, but only in an amount necessary for that purpose." No fixed dollar cap.
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** — Rule 1.15(c): "**Except as provided in Rule 1.5(f)**, a lawyer shall deposit into a client trust account legal fees and expenses that have been paid in advance, to be withdrawn … only as fees are earned or expenses incurred." Advance fees in trust until earned **unless** the fee qualifies under **Rule 1.5(f)**. **[VERIFY Rule 1.5(f) conditions for the carve-out — not pulled this pass.]**
6. **Records retention period** — **Six (6) years** after termination of the representation (Rule 1.15(h): records "shall be preserved for at least **six years** after termination of the representation").
7. **Required records** — Rule 1.15(h): the lawyer must "maintain on a current basis **records sufficient to demonstrate compliance** with the provisions of this Rule." The rule does **not** enumerate specific client ledgers/journals in the black-letter text. **[VERIFY whether Disciplinary Board / SBAND guidance itemizes ledgers/journals.]**
8. **Three-way reconciliation required? Frequency?** — **NOT codified.** Rule 1.15 contains no reconciliation/three-way mandate ("reconcil-" does not appear). HIGH confidence it is not required by rule; best practice only.
9. **Trust overdraft notification program** — **YES.** Rule 1.15(l): a financial institution is approved as a trust depository only if it files an agreement with the **Disciplinary Board** to **report to the Board** any properly payable instrument presented against a lawyer trust account with insufficient funds, whether or not honored. No trust account may be held at a non-reporting institution; agreement covers all branches, cancelable only on **three days'' written notice**; the Board annually publishes an approved-institution list.
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments to trust** — **Expressly addressed.** Rule 1.15(b): a lawyer may accept **credit-card or EFT payments for unearned fees as temporary deposits into the general operating account** if the client/third-party funds are **promptly transferred to the client trust account**; and the lawyer may deposit firm funds to pay **bank service charges and credit-card/EFT processing fees** (amount necessary only). Distinctive — ND explicitly permits a temporary operating-account landing for card/EFT payments with prompt transfer to trust.
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — Rule 1.15(f)(2): all client/third-person funds go in the pooled IOLTA **unless** deposited in (i) a separate interest-bearing account for the particular client (interest, net of transaction costs, to the client) or (ii) a pooled sub-accounted interest-bearing account crediting each client. Net-earnings/nominal-or-short-term test; no fixed dollar threshold.
12. **Notable state-specific quirks** — (a) Rule title bundles **professional-liability-insurance disclosure** with safekeeping. (b) **All trust accounts must be interest-bearing**; IOLTA → **North Dakota Bar Foundation** under **Admin. Rule 24** (Foundation holds the entire beneficial interest in the interest). (c) **Express credit-card/EFT temporary-operating-account provision** with prompt transfer to trust (1.15(b)). (d) **Annual compliance certification at license renewal** to the Supreme Court clerk (1.15(i)). (e) Overdraft reports → **Disciplinary Board**, with an unusually short **3-day** cancellation-notice requirement. (f) **6-year retention; no codified three-way reconciliation.** (g) Effective date **4/1/2026** — a current/recent version.
