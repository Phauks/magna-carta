## New Hampshire

1. **Governing rule citation** — NH Rules of Professional Conduct **Rule 1.15 (Safekeeping Property)**; IOLTA program established by **NH Supreme Court Rule 50** **[VERIFIED — Rule 50, via nhbar.org/NH Bar Foundation IOLTA page]**. Trust-account recordkeeping detail also in Rule 1.15 and Sup. Ct. Rule 50. **[KNOWLEDGE]**
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Lawyer must hold client/third-person property separate from the lawyer's own; no commingling except permitted firm funds for bank charges (Rule 1.15(a),(b)). **[KNOWLEDGE]**
3. **IOLTA program** — **Mandatory.** Program administered by the **New Hampshire Bar Foundation**; under Sup. Ct. Rule 50, nominal/short-term client funds go in an interest-bearing account and interest is remitted to the NH Bar Foundation for charitable purposes. **[VERIFIED — NH Bar Foundation IOLTA page]**
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges?** — Yes, in an amount reasonably necessary to cover service charges / minimum balance (Rule 1.15(b)). **[KNOWLEDGE]**
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** — Advance fees and unearned retainers must be held in trust until earned; a "true retainer" (payment to secure availability) is earned on receipt and need not be deposited in trust. Flat fees treated as advance fees absent compliant agreement. **[KNOWLEDGE — confirm against NH Rule 1.15 and Ethics Committee guidance]**
6. **Records retention** — Commonly 6 years after termination of representation. **[LOW-CONFIDENCE — verify exact period in Rule 1.15 / Rule 50]**
7. **Required records** — Client ledgers, receipts/disbursements journal, bank statements, cancelled checks, reconciliations. **[KNOWLEDGE]**
8. **Three-way reconciliation** — Reconciliation required; frequency (monthly vs. periodic) **[LOW-CONFIDENCE — verify]**.
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — NH has an approved-institution / overdraft-reporting requirement tied to Rule 50 / Rule 1.15. **[LOW-CONFIDENCE — verify mechanics]**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments** — Permitted; processing fees must not be charged against client principal; lawyer must ensure chargebacks/fees don't invade other clients' funds. **[KNOWLEDGE]**
11. **Separate interest-bearing account thresholds** — Funds too large or long-held to be nominal/short-term must go in a separate account earning interest for the client (standard IOLTA logic). **[KNOWLEDGE]**
12. **Notable quirks** — IOLTA authority is **Supreme Court Rule 50**, a distinct numbering from RPC 1.15.
13. **Sources** — https://www.nhbar.org/iolta-information-for-attorneys/ ; NH RPC 1.15 and Sup. Ct. Rule 50 (verify at https://www.courts.nh.gov/rules — note: courts.nh.gov returned 403 to automated fetch).

## New York

1. **Governing rule citation** — **NY Rules of Professional Conduct (22 NYCRR Part 1200) Rule 1.15 (Preserving Identity of Funds and Property of Others; Fiduciary Responsibility; Commingling and Misappropriation; Required Bookkeeping Records)**. IOLA program under **Judiciary Law §497** and **21 NYCRR Part 7000**. **[KNOWLEDGE — nycourts PDF returned 403; verify]**
2. **Commingling prohibition** — NY Rule 1.15(a) flatly prohibits commingling and misappropriation; the rule's title itself names commingling. **[KNOWLEDGE]**
3. **IOLTA program** — Program is **"IOLA" (Interest on Lawyer Account)**, administered by the **IOLA Fund of the State of New York**. **Participation in IOLA itself is effectively opt-in/discretionary** for whether nominal funds go in an IOLA account, but maintaining proper trust accounts is mandatory; interest on qualifying accounts is remitted to the IOLA Fund. **[VERIFIED program name/administrator via iola.org]**; opt-out nuance **[KNOWLEDGE — verify Judiciary Law §497]**.
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges?** — Yes; Rule 1.15 permits funds reasonably sufficient to maintain the account / cover charges. **[KNOWLEDGE]**
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** — Unearned advances generally in trust; NY recognizes that a **"true/general retainer"** (paid solely to secure availability) is earned when paid. NY does **not** permit "nonrefundable" retainer labels that defeat the client's right to a refund of unearned fees (Matter of Cooperman). **[KNOWLEDGE — verify]**
6. **Records retention** — **7 years** after the events recorded (Rule 1.15(d)). **[KNOWLEDGE — high confidence; verify text]**
7. **Required records** — Rule 1.15(d) lists: contemporaneous trust ledger book/records with running client balances, records of all deposits/withdrawals, copies of retainer/closing statements, bank statements, cancelled checks, etc. **[KNOWLEDGE]**
8. **Three-way reconciliation** — NY Rule 1.15 requires maintaining records but does **not** in terms mandate a formal monthly three-way reconciliation the way NC/NJ do; reconciliation is best practice and effectively expected. **[LOW-CONFIDENCE — verify whether a periodic reconciliation is required]**
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — **Yes — the "Dishonored Check Reporting Rule," 22 NYCRR Part 1300**, requires banks holding attorney special/trust accounts to report dishonored instruments to the Lawyers' Fund for Client Protection. **[KNOWLEDGE — high confidence]**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments** — Permitted; processing fees must not reduce client principal; chargeback risk must be managed. **[KNOWLEDGE]**
11. **Separate interest-bearing thresholds** — Funds that are not nominal/short-term must be placed in an interest-bearing account for the client's benefit rather than IOLA. **[KNOWLEDGE]**
12. **Notable quirks** — NY's IOLTA equivalent is uniquely named **"IOLA"**; overdraft reporting is **Part 1300 (Dishonored Check Reporting)**; the client-protection fund is the **Lawyers' Fund for Client Protection**.
13. **Sources** — https://www.iola.org/ (IOLA Fund); NY RPC Rule 1.15 (22 NYCRR 1200) — verify at nycourts.gov (PDF returned 403); 22 NYCRR Part 1300; Judiciary Law §497.

## North Carolina

1. **Governing rule citation** — **NC RPC Rule 1.15 (Safekeeping Property)** with subparts **1.15-1 (Definitions)**, **1.15-2 (General Rules)**, **1.15-3 (Records and Accountings)**. **[VERIFIED — ncbar.gov]**
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Entrusted property must be segregated and properly labeled; lawyer bears the burden of proving the capacity in which funds are held if rules not followed (Rule 1.15 cmt. [8]-[11]). **[VERIFIED]**
3. **IOLTA program** — **Mandatory IOLTA.** Administered by the **NC State Bar / NC IOLTA** program; interest remitted to NC IOLTA for legal aid. **[VERIFIED — ncbar.gov; mandatory framework]**
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges?** — Yes; lawyer's own funds may be deposited only to cover bank/service charges, in the amount necessary. **[VERIFIED — Rule 1.15-2]**
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** — Rule 1.15 cmt. [12]: a **true retainer** (reserving the lawyer's exclusive availability, nonrefundable on discharge) is **immediately the lawyer's** and is NOT placed in trust; a deposit "advance payment of a fee to be billed" is a **security deposit that must be placed in trust** and withdrawn as earned/billed. **[VERIFIED]**
6. **Records retention** — **6 years** after the representation/fiduciary relationship ends (Rule 1.15-3(h)). **[VERIFIED]**
7. **Required records** — Source/date receipts (deposit slips, wire/ACH confirmations), images of all cancelled checks/items, transfer authorizations, all depository statements/dishonor notices, general ledger, individual client subsidiary ledgers, plus an administrative ledger for the account (Rule 1.15-3(b),(c)). **[VERIFIED]**
8. **Three-way reconciliation** — **Yes.** **Monthly** bank-to-ledger reconciliation **and at least quarterly** a formal three-way reconciliation report verifying that (A) general-ledger balance, (B) sum of all client subsidiary ledgers + admin ledger, and (C) adjusted bank balance are **identical**; lawyer must sign, date, and retain reconciliations for 6 years. Also monthly review of statements/cancelled checks and quarterly random-transaction review (Rule 1.15-3(d),(e)). **[VERIFIED]**
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — **Yes.** NC requires trust/fiduciary accounts at banks that agree to report overdrafts and dishonored instruments to the State Bar. **[VERIFIED framework — Rule 1.15-2]**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments** — Permitted; Rule 1.15 contemplates electronic transfers; processing/merchant fees may not be charged against client funds. **[VERIFIED that e-transfers are recognized; fee-handling KNOWLEDGE]**
11. **Separate interest-bearing thresholds** — Funds that can earn net income for the client beyond costs must go in a client-specific interest-bearing account; otherwise general trust/IOLTA. **[KNOWLEDGE — consistent with Rule 1.15-2]**
12. **Notable quirks** — NC is unusually prescriptive: **mandatory quarterly three-way reconciliation with signed reports**, **quarterly random transaction review**, separate **administrative ledger**, and detailed image/check requirements. One of the strictest recordkeeping regimes in the country.
13. **Sources** — https://www.ncbar.gov/for-lawyers/ethics/rules-of-professional-conduct/rule-115-safekeeping-property/ ; .../rule-115-2-general-rules/ ; .../rule-115-3-records-and-accountings/

## Ohio

1. **Governing rule citation** — **Ohio RPC 1.15 (Safekeeping Funds and Property)**; IOLTA statutory basis in **R.C. §§ 3953.231, 4705.09, 4705.10, 120.52** and rules of the **Ohio Access to Justice Foundation**; reporting under **Gov. Bar R. VI, Section 1(F)**. **[VERIFIED — supremecourt.ohio.gov]**
2. **Commingling prohibition** — Funds held separate in an interest-bearing account at an Ohio-authorized institution; account designated "client trust account," "IOLTA account," or fiduciary title (Rule 1.15(a)). Commingling impermissible except permitted bank-charge funds (cmt. [2]). **[VERIFIED]**
3. **IOLTA program** — **Mandatory.** Administered by the **Ohio Access to Justice Foundation** (formerly Ohio Legal Assistance Foundation). Nominal/short-term client funds that cannot earn net income for the client go in the IOLTA account; lawyer must notify the Foundation of the account (Rule 1.15(h)). **[VERIFIED]**
4. **Firm funds in trust for bank charges?** — Yes, **only** to pay/obtain a waiver of bank service charges, in the amount necessary (Rule 1.15(b)). Comment [2] lists deductible charges (per-item, monthly maintenance) and non-deductible ones (check printing, NSF, stop-payment, teller/ATM, wire fees, brokerage/credit-card charges). **[VERIFIED]**
5. **Advance/flat fee treatment** — Rule 1.15(c): legal fees/expenses paid in advance must be deposited in the client trust account and withdrawn only as earned/incurred. (Ohio does not endorse generic "earned on receipt" for advance fees; see also Rule 1.5 / Prof.Cond.R. on flat fees.) **[VERIFIED on 1.15(c); flat-fee nuance KNOWLEDGE]**
6. **Records retention** — **7 years** after termination of the representation or appropriate disbursement, whichever comes first (Rule 1.15(a)). **[VERIFIED]**
7. **Required records** — Copy of each fee agreement; per-client record (name; date/amount/source of receipts; date/amount/payee/purpose of disbursements; current balance); per-account record (name; each credit/debit with date/amount/client; balance); all bank statements, deposit slips, cancelled checks; **monthly reconciliation** (Rule 1.15(a)(1)-(5)). Records may be electronic. **[VERIFIED]**
8. **Three-way reconciliation** — Rule 1.15(a)(5) requires a **monthly reconciliation** of the (a)(2) client records, (a)(3) account records, and (a)(4) bank records — i.e., an effective monthly three-way reconciliation. **[VERIFIED — monthly]**
9. **Trust overdraft notification** — Ohio requires IOLTA/trust accounts at **eligible depository institutions** that report overdrafts; tied to R.C. and Foundation rules. **[VERIFIED that eligible-institution regime exists; precise overdraft-reporting mechanics KNOWLEDGE]**
10. **Electronic/credit-card payments** — Permitted; per cmt. [2], **brokerage and credit-card charges may NOT be deducted from account proceeds** — the lawyer must bear/segregate those costs so client funds are not invaded. **[VERIFIED]**
11. **Separate interest-bearing thresholds** — Cmt. [3A]: funds go in IOLTA unless they can earn net income for the client; the lawyer weighs amount, expected duration, interest rates, account/admin costs, and institutional capability (Rule 1.15 cmt. [3A]). **[VERIFIED]**
12. **Notable quirks** — IOLTA run by the **Ohio Access to Justice Foundation**; explicit comment listing exactly which bank fees are/aren't deductible from trust; explicit annual IOLTA reporting under **Gov. Bar R. VI §1(F)**.
13. **Sources** — https://www.supremecourt.ohio.gov/docs/LegalResources/Rules/ProfConduct/profConductRules.pdf (Rule 1.15 at p. 84-87); Ohio Access to Justice Foundation (ohioaccesstojustice.org / IOLTA).

## Cross-cutting notes for the engineering team

- **Numbering varies by state and software must not assume "Rule 1.15"**: OR uses hyphenated **1.15-1/1.15-2**; NM uses **16-115**; SC splits into **Rule 407/412/417 SCACR**; SD embeds rules in **SDCL 16-18 Appendix**; NY uses **22 NYCRR Part 1200**; TN under **Sup. Ct. R. 8**; NH IOLTA under **Sup. Ct. R. 50**; NJ recordkeeping under **Court Rule 1:21-6**.
- **Overdraft/dishonored-instrument reporting programs** are near-universal but cite-differently: PA = **Pa.R.D.E. 221**; NY = **22 NYCRR Part 1300 (Dishonored Check Reporting)**. Treat as a required field per state.
- **Retention period is NOT uniform**: OH = **7 yrs**; NJ = **7 yrs**; NY = **7 yrs**; NC = **6 yrs**; OR = **5 yrs**; PA = **5 yrs (or later of termination/disposition)**. Others unverified.
- **Reconciliation**: NC is the strictest (monthly + **quarterly signed three-way** + quarterly random-transaction review); OH and PA require **monthly** reconciliation that ties bank = ledger = client-balance total; NY does not clearly mandate a formal periodic three-way reconciliation in-rule.
