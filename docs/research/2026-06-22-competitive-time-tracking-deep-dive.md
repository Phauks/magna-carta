# Competitive time-tracking deep dive — how Clio, Smokeball & peers capture and surface time

**Status:** Research · **Date:** 2026-06-22 · **For:** slice 3·1 (Time tracking + WIP + rate engine)
**Scope:** Time-tracking MECHANICS and UX SURFACING across nine legal practice-management products (Clio Manage, Smokeball, MyCase, PracticePanther, CARET Legal, TimeSolv, LEAP, Actionstep, Rocket Matter), plus two lower-confidence secondary AI-capture references (Litify Billables AI, Actionstep Trace).
**Method & confidence:** web-sourced (vendor help centers/pricing + independent legal-tech press), adversarially fact-checked (16 load-bearing claims: 11 confirmed / 1 refuted / 3 overstated) and completeness-critiqued. Product features change fast — cells are marked where unverified.
**Extends:** [`competitive-analysis.md`](competitive-analysis.md) (the feature-presence matrix) — this goes deeper on mechanics + UX surfacing.

## Scope, sourcing, and how this extends the matrix

The feature-presence matrix in this directory answers _whether_ each platform tracks time, captures it passively, supports LEDES, and so on. This deep-dive answers the questions the matrix cannot: **how the mechanism actually works, and how each product surfaces time to the daily-driver user.** The owner asked specifically for the **"how they surface it to users"** dimension, so that gets its own section and is threaded throughout. The deferred passive-capture decision gets the deepest treatment, because the point of deferring is to defer _knowingly_ — we need to understand precisely what we are choosing not to build and what a future build would cost.

**Confidence tiers — read these before trusting any cell.**

- **Nine hands-on product profiles** (Clio Manage, Smokeball, MyCase, PracticePanther, CARET Legal, TimeSolv, LEAP, Actionstep, Rocket Matter): each profiled directly from vendor help centers + independent press. These are the load-bearing sources.
- **Two lower-confidence SECONDARY references** (Litify Billables AI, May 19 2026; Actionstep Trace, GA targeted April 2026): these are **not** among the nine profiles. They are external/new launches cited only as _reference designs_ for the deferred passive-capture seam. Their agent form-factor, consent model, and review-UI details are **launch marketing, unverified** — every time they appear below as a "pattern to copy" they carry that caveat. Do not treat "firm-approved language" or "draft→approve queue" claims about Trace/Litify as confirmed mechanism; treat them as marketed intent.
- **Certainty discipline applied throughout:** where a vendor's behavior could not be confirmed, the cell reads **"not documented / unverified"** (absence-not-found), which is distinct from a confirmed **"No."** Only one confirmed hard "No" exists in the rate dimension (LEAP's documented backfill failure) and it is flagged so it stands out.

## Table-stakes vs differentiator

**Table stakes** (present almost everywhere; absence reads as broken):

- A manual time-entry form with matter, duration, rate, billable flag, and free backdating.
- At least one stopwatch timer reachable app-wide.
- Firm-configurable activity/UTBMS codes feeding LEDES (1998B at minimum; 1998BI/XML for international/insurer work).
- Per-line billable/non-billable.
- Lock-on-invoice (time editable only until billed — Rocket Matter, LEAP color-coding, CARET, and Magna Carta's Draft→Billed all converge here).
- A reusable rate-card/rate-plan concept with a resolution precedence (depth varies wildly).
- **Bulk-batch entry as a distinct surface.** This is its own table-stake, separate from any weekly grid: MyCase ships a 50-row Bulk mode (5 rows at a time), PracticePanther a 10-row "Multiple Time Entries" form, Rocket Matter "bulk add time sheets." A daily-driver who has to log a backlog expects to key many rows fast without opening one modal per entry. **3·1 design consequence:** the weekly matters×days grid must either *be* the bulk path or sit beside a dedicated batch surface — and either way grid edits, timer commits, and quick-add lines must land in **one WIP store** so the three input modes stay consistent.

**Genuine differentiators** (only some ship them, and they map directly onto 3·1):

1. **Enforced single-active server-side timer.** Only Clio is genuinely single-active (pause-resumable), and even Clio's web tab-close survival is **unknown/unverified** — keep it explicitly unknown, do not assert Clio is weaker than us on persistence. The pervasive failure mode is the opposite: multi-timer products (CARET ten timers, MyCase, TimeSolv, Actionstep) invite forgotten parked timers and over-counting; TimeSolv's multi-timer model literally freezes the screen and shows the wrong active timer until refresh (Capterra Nov-2024; GuidingDecisions hands-on). MyCase timers are **device-siloed and do not sync** — "Timers are unique to the device" (MyCase App Overview help, verified verbatim). Both PracticePanther and Rocket Matter dress a single-active reality in "multiple timers" marketing (park many, run one). Magna Carta's server-side, cross-device, offline-then-sync **single** timer is the cleanest model in the field.

2. **Effective-dated rate resolution.** No competitor confirmed it; LEAP is the one confirmed counter-example (no backfill). The rest copy the current rate onto the entry but document no dated rate-card history. This is a correctness differentiator, not a convenience one.

3. **Weekly matters×days grid.** Confirmed absent across the field. Everyone offers a list (Clio Activities table, LEAP daily list, Smokeball Time & Expenses), a day-modal (Clio Timekeeper), a bulk row-form (MyCase 50, PracticePanther 10), or a calendar rollup (Rocket Matter month, TimeSolv, Actionstep daily/monthly) — none ships the spreadsheet grid attorneys know from legacy timekeeping.

4. **Per-client/matter rounding override.** Only Smokeball and CARET document a per-matter override; PracticePanther rounding is per-**user** only; Clio/MyCase/Rocket Matter are effectively firm-fixed.

5. **Configurable rounding DIRECTION.** Distinct from increment/scope: Smokeball, CARET, and Rocket Matter document **always-round-up**; PracticePanther exposes **up or down per user**. Rounding direction (up vs nearest vs down) is a policy lever almost no one surfaces as configurable — 3·1 should treat it as a first-class policy, not just the increment value.

6. **Consent-gated AI UTBMS-code suggestion.** Still whitespace everywhere (see AI section).

## How they SURFACE time to users (UX patterns) — the owner's explicit ask

**Where the timer lives.** Two patterns dominate. (a) **Global chrome control:** Clio's header play/pause + clock-to-Timekeeper modal, PracticePanther's persistent right sidebar, Actionstep's bottom Time Panel, Rocket Matter's any-screen stopwatch icon, CARET's collapsible dashboard panel — the timer is reachable without leaving your current task. (b) **Window-bound:** LEAP's timer lives inside the Timesheet window; Smokeball's sits top-right of the desktop app. Pattern (a) is what Magna Carta's "always-visible global timer bar" should emulate; the strongest reference is **Clio's mobile surfacing** specifically — iPhone Dynamic Island live control plus an iOS lock-screen Live Activity banner with start/stop (Clio mobile help). That is the bar made ambient, and it is the single most polished timer-surfacing pattern observed; it is the polish bar for the Tauri/mobile timer.

**Timer micro-affordances worth copying.** Two concrete details the rest of the field misses:
- **Actionstep elapsed-time quick-adjust:** clicking the running elapsed value "reveals buttons for adding or subtracting preset amounts" (e.g. +6m / −6m) — a cheap fix for "I started the timer two minutes late." Trivial to build, conspicuously absent elsewhere.
- **TimeSolv Custom Timer Labels:** a timer can carry a free-text client/matter/note label even before it is bound — see the unbound-timer tension below.
- **Rocket Matter Track's Pomodoro/Focus timer** is a different timer *genre* entirely: a 25/5/15-min focus countdown that, if stopped early, "visually resets to full duration but the elapsed time is still recorded." Focus-timing (productivity) overlaps awkwardly with billing-timing (capture). Magna Carta's single-timer model *could* optionally absorb a focus mode, but only if a stopped-early focus session still commits its real elapsed time to WIP — note it as an optional, not a core, surface.

**The "Today" cockpit.** Clio is the reference for a personal daily surface: the Personal Dashboard shows an **Hourly Metrics "Today" gauge** — target billable time in grey, actual logged billable in blue, with a progress ring; non-billable is deliberately excluded from the gauge (Clio Personal Performance Targets help). TimeSolv puts a **per-user target KPI panel right on the login screen** (target vs actual by day/week/month) — time-entry _is_ the homepage. These two are the closest analogs to Magna Carta's "My Day." By contrast LEAP, Rocket Matter, and PracticePanther surface only a raw hours widget (PracticePanther's "Your Hours" shows totals, **not** a billable/non-billable split or per-matter breakdown, and carries **no targets**), and push utilization/realization into separate reports. **Smokeball and LEAP have no personal daily target dashboard at all** — they substitute passive capture plus after-the-fact variance/recap reports for a forward-looking cockpit. This is a clear opening: a forward "My Day" with running totals, targets, and missing-time is rare.

**How non-billable time is shown.** MyCase's daily summary has an explicit **"reveal hidden / non-billable activities" toggle** — non-billable work is hidden by default and surfaced on demand. For a My-Day view this is a useful surfacing decision: Clio's Today gauge *excludes* non-billable from the progress ring entirely, while MyCase keeps it one toggle away. 3·1's My Day should make the same call deliberately (billable-only progress, non-billable available behind a toggle).

**How missing time gets surfaced.** Three distinct UX models: (a) **Passive-capture-as-missing-time** — Smokeball's **Time Finder** lists un-converted tracked activity (green check = create, red X = dismiss, yellow ellipsis = open) and LEAP's **Variance Report** compares manual hours against AutoTime-captured activity to recover lost fees; both are the missing-time engine _by virtue of_ the passive layer. (b) **End-of-day recovery loop** — MyCase Smart Time Finder fires an in-app alert opening a daily summary; each item is "already tracked" or "Add new time entry," with a built-in **double-billing guard** that asks for confirmation if time already exists for that case/day. Clio's equivalent (overnight unlogged-work Action Cards) is **locked behind the paid Manage AI add-on**. (c) **Compliance/admin gap tracking** — TimeSolv's "Track Entry Gaps" is admin-oriented, not a personal real-time nudge. Critically, the products with the cleanest missing-time UX (Smokeball, LEAP, Clio's AI) all derive it from a capture layer Magna Carta is deferring — so 3·1 must deliver missing-time detection **from the manual+timer data alone** (e.g., "5.3 of 8.0 h logged today" against the target, plus a calendar overlay showing un-billed events), which the white paper already specifies.

**Auto-pause framed as an ETHICS safeguard.** PracticePanther and TimeSolv both market single-active/auto-pause not as anti-over-counting *hygiene* but as an explicit **anti-double-billing ethics/compliance feature** ("the previous timer pauses momentarily" framed as preventing simultaneous billing of two clients). This is a positioning insight, not just a mechanism one: Magna Carta's stricter **stop/commit** model is *more* defensible on ethics grounds than their pause-park model (a committed entry cannot silently keep accruing against a second client), and the first-AI-feature / time-tracking marketing should lead with that compliance framing, not just "clean timer hygiene."

**Audit/transparency surfacing of timer activity.** Clio's **Firm Feed** shows exact timer start times and a "[user] created Time Entry … ago" audit line — timer activity is made visible firm-wide, not just to the timekeeper. For a multi-tenant platform with redact-by-default error-logging discipline, this is a relevant precedent: the server-side authoritative timer should emit an audit trail (who started/stopped/committed, when) that surfaces in a firm-activity feed, consistent with the platform's observability standard — exact timer start times included.

**Lifecycle/WIP cues in the UI.** LEAP's Timesheet color-codes rows by state (green submitted, blue invoiced, white/grey incomplete, red modified-not-synced) — a useful direct precedent for surfacing Magna Carta's Draft→Billed lifecycle and the WIP-vs-posted distinction visually. Rocket Matter and others simply disable editing once invoiced. Note that LEAP and Rocket Matter expose *intermediate* states (LEAP's "submitted" is a review gate before billing) — see the lock-granularity implication below.

**Inline point-of-work prompts.** MyCase is the reference for nudging at the moment of work: "Get prompts to track time when you log a call, send an email, save a note, and upload a document," auto-creating a **draft** entry with duration prefilled. CARET adds a distinct micro-pattern for *inbound* email: a **confirm-prompt** ("Prompt for creating time entries when saving inbound emails") that pops when you file an inbound email to a matter — distinct from auto-start-on-compose, because the user explicitly opts the inbound item in. PracticePanther uses the same "Save and add time entry" idiom off any activity. This is the user-initiated calendar/activity→entry conversion Magna Carta already plans, and it is the right non-passive substitute for ambient capture.

**Voice/dictation as an input channel.** Two products offer voice narrative entry that the rest of the field ignores: **TimeSolv** mobile does voice-to-text dictation of descriptions, and **Smokeball Archie** added voice dictation in its next-gen release. Magna Carta's narrative-ergonomics plan (snippets + recent-reuse + templates) has **not** been mapped against voice as a modality. At minimum, flag voice-to-text narrative entry on mobile/Tauri as an **unaddressed input channel** worth a decision — it is cheap on-device with platform speech APIs and pairs naturally with the mobile timer.

## Passive / automatic capture — the deep dive on what we are deferring

The fact-check produced a clean **three-tier taxonomy** of passive capture. Understanding which tier each competitor occupies is what makes the deferral defensible rather than merely convenient.

**Tier 1 — Screen/app-watching desktop agents.** A native client monitors OS window/app/file focus and logs activity locally. This is the "real" passive capture and the hardest to build:
- **Smokeball AutoTime** is the market reference — "If your fingers are on a keyboard, Smokeball is capturing your time" (Lawyerist 2026). It watches Word, Outlook, Excel, Acrobat, calls (RingCentral only), tasks, and notes; tracks **idle** ("detect[s] when your mouse has gone idle") so breaks don't corrupt entries. Two hard constraints make it a cautionary tale for a web-first platform: it is **Windows-native only** — "When Smokeball runs through a browser, it cannot capture AutoTime" (Smokeball AutoTime Basics, confirmed) and Mac/Apple-Silicon firms cannot run it at all — and it is **batch, not live**: "Every night, AutoTime will take all your recorded activities... and create time entries for you," with an **AutoTime Preview** catching 0-duration / no-matter rows before the nightly run.
- **LEAP AutoTime** (launched Nov 2024) is the same model — deterministic app monitoring (despite the "AI-Driven" press headline) with an active-vs-viewing heuristic, two-level opt-in (firm-per-matter + per-staff toggle), per-activity billable toggle, and a **Variance Report** safety net. Its review gate is **weaker** than a true draft-then-approve queue: billable activity flows directly into the editable Timesheet/WIP with no mandatory approval. Notably, **LEAP's capture spans beyond the desktop**: iOS additionally captures phone calls and FaceTime audio/video, and LEAP exposes **Apple Watch and Amazon Alexa** as capture endpoints — a breadth of capture *surfaces* (wearable, voice assistant, mobile call) no other profiled product matches, and directly relevant to the Tauri-desktop-vs-mobile seam question (a future passive build is not only a Windows-desktop story).
- **Rocket Matter Track** (Apr 2024) is local-only with the strongest user-control story: you download the agent, **explicitly allow screen recording**, it captures apps/websites/files locally, and **nothing reaches the firm until you manually drag activities onto matter timers** to post. It exposes configurable **idle-time detection** and **application exclusion** (keep personal apps out), plus bulk-link/bulk-delete tooling. **Its matter-linking is rule-based drag-to-timer, NOT AI inference** — the "AI-powered" label in one review (Lawyerist) is unverified marketing; Rocket Matter's own docs describe deterministic capture + a manual human-review drag step. (Restating this because a reader skimming the Tier-1 list could otherwise read Track as an AI matter-inference reference, which it is not.)

**Tier 2 — In-product / server-side deterministic activity-matching.** No desktop agent; the platform infers candidate entries from records it _already holds_. This is the tier Magna Carta's plan largely **already covers** as user-initiated/semi-automatic capture:
- **MyCase Smart Time Finder** passively logs actions taken **inside MyCase and its Outlook add-in only** — "tracks only activities within MyCase... not external applications or operating system monitoring" (Smart Time Finder help, confirmed). It surfaces them in an end-of-day summary with the double-billing guard. Marketed as "AI-powered" but documented as deterministic matching.
- **Clio Manage AI** (formerly Duo) runs an **overnight batch** over Clio's own records (calendars, tasks, documents, notes, calls, emails) and proposes unlogged-time entries on **AI Action Cards** before a **~2:00 a.m. PT cutoff**, scoped to **billable actions from the trailing 24 hours** (Clio Manage AI Proactive Actions help, confirmed). It is inference-over-existing-records, **not** OS monitoring. Review checkpoints are built in: "review, edit, or reject suggestions... never finalizing without your consent."
- **CARET Legal** markets "Passive Timekeeping" but it is **event-triggered in-app capture only** — an auto-start email-compose timer (web client + Outlook plugin), an inbound-email confirm-prompt, phone-call-note→entry, calendar→entry, task→entry. The blog's ambient language ("drafting a motion... attending a Zoom hearing") **oversells** a mechanism that does no OS/Word/Zoom watching (confirmed marketing-vs-mechanism gap).

**Tier 3 — API-integration AI capture with a human-approval gate and no screen-watching.** The newest and most relevant reference design for a deferred-but-seamed layer — but the **lower-confidence secondary references**, so treat the mechanism claims below as launch marketing, not verified behavior:
- **Litify Billables AI** (May 19 2026; *secondary reference, mechanism per vendor/press only*) "continuously analyzes activity across the tools attorneys already use, including Microsoft 365, Zoom, Adobe," generates **client-guideline-aligned narratives**, and gates on attorney review/approve — built **"without... screenshot or screen-monitoring technology"** (LawSites May 2026). This is an explicit privacy contrast with the Tier-1 screen-recording agents. Litify is **not** one of the nine profiled products; its review UI and exact integration model are unverified.
- **Actionstep Trace** (early access Feb 2026, GA targeted April 2026, premium add-on; *secondary reference, launch marketing only*) is claimed to do "off-platform capture" across Outlook/Word/PDFs/websites, map to client/matter, and "produce time narratives for review and approval" using "firm-approved language" (LegalTech.ca Jan 2026). **Its agent form factor, consent/privacy model, and review UI are explicitly unpublished/unverified** — the "draft→approve" and "firm-approved language" descriptions are marketed intent, not confirmed mechanism. Do not lean on Trace as a *built* reference design; cite it as a *claimed* one.

**What we are choosing to defer, precisely.** The seam Magna Carta keeps open is **Tier 1 / Tier 3**: ambient capture of work done _outside_ the platform (Word, browser, PDFs, calls, Zoom) turned into draft entries. Tier 2 capabilities (calendar→entry, activity→entry, email→entry, missing-time from in-platform records) are **already in the 3·1 plan as user-initiated flows** — so we are not deferring "passive capture" wholesale, only the OS/cross-app-watching slice.

**What a future build would actually require** (the cost of the deferred seam):
- A **native client** is unavoidable for true Tier-1 capture — there is no web-only path (Smokeball's browser-can't-capture limit proves it). Magna Carta's **Tauri desktop shell** is the natural host, which is exactly why the seam can wait until the desktop app matures. (And per LEAP, the future surface is not only the desktop — wearable/mobile/call capture is part of the addressable channel.)
- An **idle/active heuristic** (mouse/keyboard idle detection) so breaks don't inflate entries — every serious agent has one (Smokeball, Rocket Matter, LEAP's active-vs-viewing).
- A **matter-attribution layer** — either rule-based (WiseTime's trick: match when a matter ID appears in a document name or email subject) or AI inference; Magna Carta's matter/client model + the AI seam covers the latter.
- A **draft→approve queue** with a **double-billing guard** (MyCase's confirm-if-time-exists) and exclusion/idle configuration — and, learning from LEAP, a **mandatory** approval gate rather than auto-into-WIP.
- A **privacy/consent model** — the conspicuous gap in the incumbents: Smokeball enablement is "a pure admin checkbox" with no documented employee-consent or retention disclosure; Actionstep Trace's consent model is unpublished. Litify's no-screen-watching, API-only posture is the (claimed) model to copy. Magna Carta's consent-gated, redact-by-default discipline is a differentiator here, not a catch-up.

The deferral is sound: every cleanest passive-capture product is **Windows-native, single-machine, batch**, none is web-first/cross-device, and the manual+timer experience the field surfaces is deep enough to stand alone. We defer the hardest, most platform-constraining slice and keep the seam (matter attribution, draft queue, idle heuristic) designed-for.

## AI time features — where the field actually is (post-fact-check)

The most important correction to the original profiles: **AI time-entry _narratives_ are now PARITY, not whitespace.** Multiple products ship them:
- **Smokeball** shipped **"Generate AutoTime descriptions using Smokeball AI"** (Support Hub, updated Sept 3 2025, **beta**) — one-click description generation trained on **your last 100 time-entry description edits**, applicable per-entry or batch. It requires AutoTime and is **Desktop-App only**. So Magna Carta is **not** first-to-market on AI time narratives. (This is the one *refuted* fact-check claim: the original profile asserted Smokeball had no AI time-narrative feature; that was wrong/outdated.)
- **Actionstep Trace** and **Litify Billables AI** generate narratives via the passive-capture path (firm-approved language; client-guideline-aligned), both behind a human-approval gate — but both are **lower-confidence secondary references** whose narrative mechanics are launch-marketing.
- **Clio Manage AI** drafts/expands entry descriptions and bill narratives from matter context (first-draft, attorney-review-required), plus natural-language logging ("log 2 hours to the Smith matter") — all behind the **paid** add-on (~$39/user/mo, Essentials+).
- **Actionstep core** (no add-on) already pre-populates the entry form from context on timer-stop ("smart AI time suggestions based on your activity").

**What remains genuine whitespace:**
1. **AI UTBMS task/activity-code suggestion** — unshipped across Clio, Smokeball, LEAP, CARET, PracticePanther, and native MyCase. Clio's docs confirm UTBMS codes are **manually** selected; Manage AI does narratives and unlogged-time, **not** code prediction (confirmed). This is the cleaner differentiation target.
2. **AI narratives delivered web-natively on a manual+timer flow** (not tied to a Windows desktop passive agent, and consent-gated). Smokeball's is desktop-only; Trace/Litify tie it to passive capture; Clio gates it behind a paid AI SKU. A web-first, consent-gated narrative+code-suggestion feature over manual/timer entries occupies a real gap.

**Confirmed AI absences (validating the seam as a differentiator):** PracticePanther has **no** native AI touching time (no "PantherBot"); CARET's only time AI is rule-based Time Entry Rules word-blocking, not generation; TimeSolv's "AI-Assisted Billing" is a thought-leadership explainer, not a shipped feature; LEAP's Matter AI (GA June 2026, with GPT-5.4 / Claude 4.6 model picker) does documents and summaries but is **not pointed at the time-entry/UTBMS surface** (phrase as "no *generative* AI pointed at the time-entry/code surface," since LEAP's deterministic "automate billing descriptions" is AutoTime auto-text, not an LLM); Rocket Matter ships only a bundled ChatGPT doc helper, not wired into time entry.

**Vendor ROI figures are marketing, not benchmarks.** Smokeball's "manual timekeepers underestimate by up to 30%" and "AutoTime users bill 10–30% more / 10–30% profitability increase" are **Smokeball's own** figures — sourced to Smokeball client research, the Support Hub, and a Smokeball-*sponsored* AWS case study (Apr 2026). No independent controlled study corroborates them; the only third-party signal is a single Lawyerist reviewer's "2–3 billable hours/week" anecdote. Cite all such numbers as **vendor marketing claims**, never as established fact.

## Distinctive billing mechanics worth absorbing

Two TimeSolv mechanisms and one CARET mechanism are distinctive enough to call out separately, because each maps onto a concrete 3·1 decision.

**TimeSolv internal-code → UTBMS mapping.** TimeSolv lets a firm define its own internal **Sub-Task and Expense codes** that *map to* a shared UTBMS code, so several internal codes aggregate against a single UTBMS code on the LEDES invoice (TimeSolv "Tip of the Week: assign a UTBMS code to sub-task/expense codes"). The mechanism: the firm tracks at its own taxonomy granularity internally, and the LEDES export collapses that to the ABA standard the insurer requires. **Implication for 3·1:** firms want their *internal* time taxonomy decoupled from the ABA/UTBMS standard — they should be able to record against rich internal codes (for their own reporting and partner habits) while the e-billing layer rolls those up to the correct UTBMS task/activity code. Our UTBMS/LEDES layer should model this as a many-internal-codes→one-UTBMS-code mapping, not force timekeepers to think in raw ABA codes.

**TimeSolv "start the timer unbound, assign client+matter later."** TimeSolv explicitly lets a user start a timer **without** a client+matter — "you don't have to associate it with a client and matter yet — the important thing is just getting the timer running" — and carry a Custom Timer Label until they assign it. This is a real capture-friction reducer (the friction of picking a matter is exactly what loses the first 30 seconds of a phone call). It **conflicts directly** with Magna Carta's stated requirement that a timer is bound to a matter. **Implication / open decision for 3·1:** do we allow an unbound "scratch" timer that captures elapsed time first and demands matter binding only at commit? The competitor evidence says capture-friction matters; our spec currently mandates binding. This is a genuine design tension to resolve in brainstorming, not assume away — a scratch timer that *cannot commit to WIP until bound* may reconcile both (capture-first, bind-before-commit).

**CARET LEDES task-code budgeting.** CARET lets a firm set a **budget per LEDES/UTBMS code within a matter** and track **billed-vs-budget** as the case progresses (CARET "How do I use LEDES Task-Code Budgeting"). The mechanism: for an insurer/corporate matter governed by outside-counsel guidelines, each UTBMS task code (e.g. L120 analysis/strategy) carries a budget, and the matter surfaces how much has been billed against each code versus its cap — a leakage/over-spend early-warning aligned to the way insurers actually scrutinize bills. It is the one *budgeting* feature in the field tied to the code taxonomy rather than to a flat matter total. **Implication for 3·1:** a near-term e-billing extension once UTBMS/LEDES is in place — per-code budget + billed-vs-budget tracking — that pairs naturally with the internal-code→UTBMS mapping above.

## Implications for Magna Carta 3·1

Mapping each finding to the white-paper 3·1 plan — where we match, lead, or lag.

**Where we LEAD (keep the bet):**
- **Single active server-side timer that stops/commits, persists across tab-close, follows you across devices, and works offline in Tauri.** No competitor has all of this. Clio is single-active but pause-resumable and **not documented as tab-close-durable (unknown)**; MyCase is the anti-pattern (device-siloed, no sync); the multi-timer crowd (CARET, TimeSolv, Actionstep) carries forgotten-timer over-counting risk, and TimeSolv's literally freezes. **Design note:** our "new STOPS prior" (commit) is stricter than Clio's "new PAUSES prior" (resume) — validate against attorney workflow; the pause-resume affordance genuinely serves the "interrupted by a call, resume the motion" pattern, so consider a *resume-last-stopped-timer* shortcut to soften the commit semantics. Lead the positioning with the **ethics framing** PracticePanther/TimeSolv use: a committed entry cannot silently double-bill a second client.
- **Effective-dated rate resolution with entry rate-pinning.** A correctness lead nobody confirmed; LEAP actively gets this wrong (no backfill). Our matter→client→timekeeper→role multi-card model with "entry keeps the rate in force on its date" is ahead of the field. Borrow CARET's explicit **per-entry rate override** as the top of the precedence (six of nine products allow it) and TimeSolv's idea of **named cards** (e.g. "insurance-capped," its "Global Rate" card). *Open:* does the model also need to handle **retroactive rate corrections** (a card amended for a past effective date) against already-drafted-but-unbilled WIP?
- **Weekly matters×days grid as a first-class entry surface** — confirmed absent everywhere; a real ergonomic lead for high-volume timekeepers. Decide whether the grid **is** the bulk-entry path (replacing the MyCase-50/PP-10 batch-form table-stake) or sits beside one — and keep grid edits, timer commits, and quick-add lines consistent in one WIP store.
- **My Day with forward-looking targets + missing-time from manual data.** Only Clio (paid-AI for missing-time) and TimeSolv (login KPI panel) come close; Smokeball/LEAP have no personal target dashboard. Deliver missing-time **without** passive capture — against the daily target plus a calendar overlay of un-billed events. Adopt Clio's billable-only progress ring and MyCase's reveal-non-billable toggle.
- **Native one-line NL quick-add** ("0.5 Smith — drafted demand letter") — no competitor has a built-in parser (Clio's is AI-gated). Cheap to build, conspicuously absent.
- **Consent-gated AI UTBMS-code suggestion** as (part of) the first AI feature — genuine whitespace; lead the AI pitch with this, not with narratives (now parity).
- **Commercial-packaging position.** Clio paywalls missing-time and NL quick-add behind the **paid Manage AI SKU**; Smokeball's AI narratives require **AutoTime + a Windows desktop agent**. Shipping **missing-time and NL quick-add in the base product** — no AI SKU, no desktop agent required — is itself a competitive position, separate from the AI-consent story. State it explicitly in positioning: the daily-driver capture features are not upsell-gated.

**Where we MATCH (table stakes — must ship, don't over-invest):**
- Manual form with duration **or** start–end, free backdating, billable flag, lock-on-invoice. We are slightly ahead by offering start–end as well as duration — most competitors are duration/units-only (MyCase, LEAP, Rocket Matter).
- **Bulk-batch entry** (MyCase 50-row, PP 10-row, Rocket Matter bulk timesheets) — must be satisfied by the weekly grid or a dedicated batch surface.
- UTBMS task/activity codes feeding LEDES 1998B/1998BI/XML; per-matter e-billing flag driving code-required validation. Add **TimeSolv's internal-code→UTBMS mapping** (internal taxonomy decoupled from ABA) and **CARET's LEDES task-code budgeting** (billed-vs-budget per code) as near-term e-billing extensions.
- Firm-default + per-client/matter rounding override (only Smokeball/CARET match) **plus a configurable rounding DIRECTION** (up/nearest/down) — distinct from increment; several competitors always round up, PP exposes up/down per user.
- Narrative ergonomics: our planned **snippet expansion + recent-reuse + per-task-code templates** matches the best of the field. Adopt **space-bar-triggered expansion** (TimeSolv), **default-only fill that never overwrites typed text** (MyCase), and **per-task-code templates that fill narrative + rate together** (Clio Activity Descriptions). Decide on **voice-to-text** narrative entry (TimeSolv/Smokeball) as an input channel.
- User-initiated calendar/activity→entry conversion (everyone has it; keep it strictly user-initiated per our non-passive stance) — including CARET's inbound-email confirm-prompt idiom and MyCase's double-billing guard.

**Where we LAG / must decide:**
- **AI time narratives are no longer first-to-market** (Smokeball beta, Actionstep Trace, Litify, Clio). Our differentiation narrows to **web-native + consent-gated + UTBMS-code suggestion + on a manual/timer flow** (vs everyone else tying it to passive desktop capture or a paid AI SKU). Reframe the first-AI-feature pitch: lead with UTBMS suggestion and consent-gating, treat narrative drafting as parity-plus.
- **Idle/abandoned-timer detection on the LIVE timer is now near-table-stake for us specifically.** Most products lack idle detection on the *manual/live* timer (Clio, MyCase confirmed absent) — but a server-side timer that survives tab-close is **more** exposed to "ran overnight by accident" than a session-bound one. This is a direct consequence of our own differentiator, not a passive-capture concern: our persistent timer needs idle/abandoned-timer detection (e.g. "this timer has run 9h with no activity — keep, trim, or discard?") as part of the *live* timer, independent of any future passive build.
- **Offline-then-sync conflict resolution is a real design cost no competitor faces.** A single authoritative server-side timer + an offline Tauri timer raises a concurrency question no profiled product hits: TimeSolv's offline model is per-device manual capture, not one authoritative timer. What happens when an offline-started timer syncs and the server already has a newer active timer (started on another device)? Define the conflict-resolution / hand-off UX now (CARET's desktop→mobile auto-stop hand-off is the nearest analog) and how it surfaces to the user without data loss.
- **Lock granularity may be too coarse.** Our two-state Draft→Billed misses the intermediate **submitted/approved** state LEAP and Rocket Matter expose (LEAP's "submitted" is a review gate before billing). Consider a Draft → **Submitted/Approved** → Billed lifecycle — the intermediate state is the natural host for the future passive **draft-approve queue**, so adding it now keeps the deferred seam cheap.
- **Passive capture deferred** — sound, but keep the **seam concrete**: matter-attribution hook (WiseTime-style ID-match or AI), idle heuristic, mandatory draft→approve queue with double-billing guard, all consent-gated, all hosted in the Tauri shell. Litify's (claimed) no-screen-watching API model is the reference; LEAP's auto-into-WIP (weak gate) is the anti-pattern to avoid. *Open:* which capture model do we commit the seam to — rule-based matter-ID matching (WiseTime), API-integration analysis (Litify), or a screen/app-watching agent (Smokeball/Rocket Matter) — since the choice constrains the attribution layer we design now.

**Open questions to resolve in brainstorming (folded from the recommendations review):**
1. Does "new timer STOPS (commits) prior" match how attorneys work, or do we need a resume-last-stopped affordance to avoid forcing a commit on every context switch?
2. Cross-device hand-off: when a second device starts a timer while one runs elsewhere, what is the conflict-resolution UX (auto-stop the remote, like CARET's hand-off)?
3. Offline reconciliation: how does the offline Tauri timer reconcile on sync if a conflicting entry was created server-side meanwhile, without data loss?
4. Is the weekly grid the **primary** entry surface or a secondary view alongside the global timer + quick-add line — and how do grid edits, timer commits, and quick-add lines stay consistent in one WIP store?
5. **Scratch/unbound timer:** do we allow a TimeSolv-style capture-first, bind-at-commit timer, against our current "bound to a matter" mandate?
6. First-AI-feature gating: consent at firm / per-user / per-entry level — and is UTBMS-code suggestion shipped before, with, or after narrative drafting (given narratives are now parity, not lead)?
7. Cold-start training signal: Smokeball trains narratives on the user's last 100 edits; with no usage history, do we cold-start from UTBMS code definitions + matter context until edit history accrues?
8. Effective-dated rates + retroactive corrections: does the model handle a card amended for a past effective date, and how does that interact with drafted-but-unbilled WIP?
9. Rounding direction policy: ship up/nearest/down as a configurable firm (and per-client/matter) policy, distinct from the increment?
10. Per-staff rounding override: PracticePanther's is per-user — is there a firm requirement for individual-timekeeper increment rules, on top of per-client/matter?
11. My Day missing-time signal: primary signal = un-billed calendar overlay, or pure daily-target shortfall (given no passive capture populates the gap)?
12. Idle/abandoned live-timer detection: what threshold and what prompt (keep/trim/discard) for the persistent server-side timer?
13. Voice-to-text narrative entry on mobile/Tauri — in or out of 3·1?
