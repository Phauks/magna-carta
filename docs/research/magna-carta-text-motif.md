# Magna Carta as a design motif: versions, sensitivities & recommendation

**Status:** Research complete (2026-06-10), verified against primary sources (legislation.gov.uk live statute text, the Magna Carta Project's Latin text, the National Archives, multiple independent translations). **Feeds:** the slice 0·7a brand + design-language workshop (the text-motif decision) and the eventual brand document. **Owner intent:** use Magna Carta text as a background/loading-screen motif (typewriter/handwritten/newspaper rendering), with an honest analysis of the 1215 charter's clauses concerning Jewish moneylenders — harm vs censorship vs version choice — and the alternative of a multi-document foundational-law quote collection.

## The versions — verified clause-level facts

- **1215 (King John, Runnymede), 63 clauses.** Clauses 10 and 11 concern debts owed to Jewish moneylenders (Latin: "Si quis mutuo ceperit aliquid a Judaeis…"). Standard translation: **10.** "If anyone who has borrowed a sum of money from Jews dies before the debt has been repaid, his heir shall pay no interest on the debt for so long as he remains under age… If such a debt falls into the hands of the Crown, it will take nothing except the principal sum specified in the bond." **11.** "If a man dies owing money to Jews, his wife may have her dower and pay nothing towards the debt from it… Debts owed to persons other than Jews are to be dealt with similarly." Their mechanism is protective (shielding heirs/widows from interest accrual), but they single out Jews by name and presuppose the crown's exploitative ownership of Jewish lenders and their bonds. Scholarly characterization is genuinely split (Hogg in *Law and History Review*: guardians, not Jews, were clause 11's target; other commentators: "anti-Jewish economic clauses"); what no one disputes is that they read very differently after the **Edict of Expulsion (18 July 1290)** — Edward I's expulsion of England's Jews, unreversed until 1656.
- **1216/1217 reissues:** Henry III's council cut the charter to 42 clauses in November 1216; **clauses 10 and 11 were dropped in 1216 and never appeared in any later reissue**. The motive was fiscal ("prejudicial to the Exchequer"), not humanitarian.
- **1225 (Henry III), 37 clauses:** the **definitive Magna Carta** used in all subsequent confirmations. No reference to Jews.
- **1297 (Edward I):** the statute-book version (a confirmation of the 1225 text). **Verified live against legislation.gov.uk: chapters I (church), IX (City of London liberties), and XXIX remain in force in England and Wales.** Chapter XXIX merges 1215 clauses 39 + 40: "NO Freeman shall be taken or imprisoned… but by lawful judgment of his Peers, or by the Law of the Land. We will sell to no man, we will not deny or defer to any man either Justice or Right."

**The load-bearing verified fact:** the 1297 statute text omits the Jewish-debt clauses because a 13th-century council dropped them 810 years ago — by the document's own historical evolution, not by any modern editorial choice. The 1225/1297 lineage is *the* Magna Carta that became and remains English law.

## How institutions handle it

- The 2015 octocentenary produced sustained mainstream commentary on clauses 10–11 (Jewish press, legal press, one pointed "the dodgy bits you won't hear about" critique) and active weaponization by white-supremacist sites — the discourse is live and findable. The British Library's *Law, Liberty, Legacy* exhibition addressed the clauses head-on in interpretive material rather than hiding them.
- Decorative reproduction practice is **scene, symbol, or celebrated excerpt — never the full 1215 text as decoration**: the US Supreme Court bronze doors depict the *sealing scene* (no text); the ABA's Runnymede memorial reads only "To commemorate Magna Carta, symbol of Freedom Under Law"; where text is quoted (courthouses, bar journals) it is overwhelmingly clauses 39/40 (1297 ch. XXIX).

## Licensing facts for reproduction

- The famous modern English translation of 1215 is **British Library Board copyright (1995)** — verify terms or avoid for commercial decorative use.
- The **1297 statute English text on legislation.gov.uk is Open Government Licence v3.0** — commercial use permitted with attribution. Clean.
- The medieval **Latin** of any version is public domain; older translations (Cheyney 1897, McKechnie 1914) are public domain.
- US materials (Constitution, Bill of Rights, Federalist, *Gideon v. Wainwright*, Blackstone) — public domain. **UDHR — caution:** UN copyright posture does not cleanly permit commercial decorative use; skip or treat as a permissions task. For Justinian use a public-domain translation (Moyle 1913), not S.P. Scott 1932 unchecked.

## Ranked recommendation

1. **Celebrated excerpts as the hero motif** — 1215 cl. 39/40 / 1297 ch. XXIX, Latin ("Nulli vendemus, nulli negabimus aut differemus rectum aut justiciam") with English translation. Exactly what the Supreme Court, the ABA, and courthouses do; the most beautiful and on-brand language in the document; public-domain Latin; zero harm exposure; reads as deliberate curation, not concealment.
2. **The 1297 statute text as long-form background texture** — the legally operative Magna Carta, OGL-licensed, omitting the Jewish-debt clauses through 810 years of documented history. Choosing it is itself a sharp brand statement for practice-management software: *the working version of the law, not the museum piece*. Keep a one-paragraph note in the brand document recording the version choice and why.
3. **Multi-document foundational-law quote collection** for rotating variety (loading screens): Magna Carta 1297 ch. XXIX + Justinian's Institutes ("Justice is the constant and perpetual wish to render every one his due," Moyle) + Blackstone + Federalist No. 78 + Bill of Rights excerpts + *Gideon v. Wainwright* ("lawyers in criminal courts are necessities, not luxuries") + the Nevada Constitution's declaration of rights (local flavor). Nearly all public domain. Pitfalls: skip the UDHR; avoid politically coded picks (Second Amendment, abortion-adjacent, anything currently culture-war-litigated); every quote needs an accurate citation — the users are lawyers and will check. Costs ongoing editorial judgment options 1–2 do not.
4. **Full 1215 text as decoration — not recommended.** As wallpaper, clauses 10–11 will eventually surface in a screenshot or zoomed loading screen, and "legal-software company named Magna Carta decorates its app with the Jewish-moneylender clauses" is an effortless story for a critic. The countermove — silently deleting clauses from something presented as "the 1215 text" — is worse: that *is* bowdlerization and is discoverable. If the 1215 charter ever appears (an "about" page), present it whole with a frank historical note, museum-style — never as wallpaper.

**Bottom line for the brand document:** option 1 as hero motif, option 2 as long-form texture, optionally option 3 for rotating quotes. The curation-vs-censorship dilemma dissolves on the facts: the 1225/1297 lineage is the Magna Carta of the law, the clauses were removed by a 13th-century council for fiscal reasons, and excerpting clauses 39/40 is the documented practice of the US Supreme Court and the American Bar Association. The only indefensible move is presenting an edited text as the unedited 1215 original.
