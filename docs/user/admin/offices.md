# Offices

An office is a place the firm practises from. It carries the address a letterhead prints, the
telephone and email a client is given, the time zone its documents' dates read in, the short code
its matter numbers can carry, who works there, and the mark its letterhead prints. A firm has one
office or several; under **Settings › Firm › Offices** an administrator keeps them.

## The register

The Offices screen is a table of every office the firm has opened, closed ones included. It shows
each office's name, its standing, its jurisdiction, its time zone, its numbering code, its street
address and telephone, and how many members work there. More columns are available from the
column header's menu: the office's email, its mailing address, whether it prints its own logo, and
which office is the firm's default. The search above the table matches the name, the code, the
jurisdiction, the email and any line of an address.

Double-click a row, or press Enter on it, to open the office's record.

## Opening an office

Press **New Office**. The record opens with its fields ready, in three tabs:

- **Office**: the name (the one thing an office cannot be without), its jurisdiction, its numbering
  code, its time zone and its email address.
- **Addresses**: the street address, and a mailing address where post goes somewhere else. The
  country comes first because it decides what the rest of the form asks for: an Irish address has an
  Eircode and no state, a German one puts the postal code before the town. Where address
  suggestions are available, the street box offers real addresses as you type, and choosing one
  fills in the rest — exactly as it does on a Directory record. Each address carries its own
  telephone, grouped the way that country writes numbers, with an extension beside it.
- **Letterhead**: the address block, the footer and the remit-to text the office's letterhead and
  bills print.

Members can be added and a logo chosen before the office is saved; both are written the moment it
is. **Save** creates the office and closes the window; the register behind it now carries it.

## Correcting an office

Open the office and press **Edit**. The same three tabs open over the values, and **Save** writes the
change. An address is corrected in its own boxes rather than deleted and retyped, and an emptied
street address is simply an office with none.

## Who works at an office

The **Members** tab lists everyone assigned to the office. Add a member from the roster at the foot
of the list; a member's first office is their primary one whatever the box says, and a later office
becomes primary only when it is marked so, which moves the mark off the office that had it. Taking a
member off their primary office hands the mark to their next one. A member can be at several offices.

## The letterhead logo

The **Letterhead Logo** tab shows the mark this office's letterhead prints. An office with no logo of
its own prints the firm's primary mark, set under Firm Profile, and the tab says so. To give the
office a mark of its own, choose a PNG or a JPEG, say how wide it prints in points, and press **Set
Logo**. Only the width is asked for: the height follows from the image's own proportions, so a
letterhead can never print a stretched logo. **Remove Logo** puts the office back on the firm's mark.

## The firm's default office

One office is the firm's default: what a matter with no office of its own numbers and prints
against. Press **Make Firm Default** on the office's record to give it that role. The default office
cannot be closed until another office has taken the role, and a closed office cannot take it.

## Closing an office

Press **Close Office** on the record and confirm. A closed office stops being offered wherever an
office is chosen; its record, the matters numbered against it and the documents produced on its
letterhead all stand, and it stays on the register with its standing shown.

## Who can change it

A member whose role allows managing the firm's offices. Everyone else reads the register as a plain
table and opens each office to read it.
