# The Firm's Costs

**Costs** in the sidebar shows what the firm laid out across every matter, most recent first —
filing fees, service, experts, postage, copying. It answers the question a matter cannot: not "what
has this file cost", which is the matter's own Costs section, but "what did the firm advance this
month, and how much of it is still waiting on a bill".

## Where Costs Live, in Two Places

- **Costs, in the sidebar.** This screen. Every cost across every matter. It is where the month
  gets read back, reconciled and checked before billing.
- **A matter's Costs section.** That matter's own record: every cost advanced for it, and where a new
  one is written down.

Both read the same costs. A cost you record on a matter appears here, because it is one record with
two doors.

## Choosing the Days

The screen opens on the whole record. To look at a span, filter the **Date** column from its own
heading: right-click the heading, choose Filter Date, and set a first day, a last day, or both. A
filter set at one end runs to the present, or back to the first cost ever recorded.

The date is **when the cost was incurred**, not when somebody typed it in.

## Billable or Absorbed

The **Billing** column starts hidden; turn it on from any column's heading and filter it to
**Billable** for what a client can be charged for, or **Absorbed** for what the firm decided to carry
itself. Real spending either way; nobody is charged for the absorbed.

The Date, Status and Billing filters are in the address, so a bookkeeper's month is a link somebody
can send, and it opens where they left it.

## Narrowing the Table

Every column carries its own filter, reached from the column's own heading — Date, Matter, Payee,
Expense Code, Status, and the rest. Filter Matter to one file, Status to approved, Payee to one
supplier, in any combination. The counts strip says how many rows are showing out of how many there
are, so you always know what a filter left out.

The Date, Status and Billing filters reach the record itself, so they hold across every page. A
Status filter holding two states at once narrows only the rows already loaded; pick one state to
have it hold across the whole record.

The search at the top reads the whole record too: type part of a description, a matter's name or
number, or a payee's name, and the rows that answer arrive from wherever they are.

## What the Figures Mean

The three figures on the bottom edge split the money the way the decisions do:

- **unbilled** — billable costs no finalized bill has taken up yet.
- **billed** — costs a finalized bill has already taken up.
- **absorbed** — costs the firm decided to carry. Never billed, and shown so the decision is visible.

Withdrawn costs stay on the record with their marker and count toward none of the three.

When a column filter is narrowing the table, the figures cover what is in view and say so. And when
the record runs past what has loaded, the line beneath the table says the figures cover only what is
loaded — so a running subtotal is never mistaken for a month's total.

## Going to the Matter

Each row names its matter, and the name is a link: it opens that matter's own Costs section. Opening
the **cost** is a different gesture — double-click the row, or press Enter on it.

## Finding the Bill a Cost Reached

Right-click a cost and choose **Open The Bill**. It takes you to that matter's Billing section with
the bill that took the cost up already open, so you can see what the client was actually told.

A cost nothing has billed yet says so and the control stays put, rather than appearing and
disappearing as costs move through their states.

The same control is on a time entry, in a matter's Time section: a billed hour reaches the bill it
was charged on the same way.

## Recording a New Cost

**New Cost** at the top of the screen records one. Because this screen is not standing on a matter,
the first field is the matter: type any part of its number or its name and pick it. Nothing is
pre-filled there — a cost filed against the wrong matter is a thing people stop trusting a ledger
over — and the form says so until you have chosen one.

**Save & New** keeps the date and starts the next one, which is how a run of vendor invoices gets
entered.

## Who Recorded It, and When

Two columns start hidden and are worth turning on when you are reconciling rather than reading:
**Entered By** and **Date Entered**. Turn them on from any column's own heading.

**Date Entered** is when the cost was written down, which is not the day the money went out — that
is **Date Incurred**. A disbursement captured three weeks after the fact is a different record from
one captured the day it was paid, and only the two dates together tell you which you are looking at.

**Entered By** is who wrote it down. It is not a permission: a cost belongs to the matter it was
advanced for, not to whoever typed it, so anybody who can record costs can work any matter's. It is
who to ask when a figure looks wrong. A cost recorded by somebody who has since left the firm says
so rather than showing you an internal identifier — the record itself is unaffected.

## What Is Not Here Yet

**Approving submitted costs from here** — reviewing a batch across matters in one pass — is not
built. Approval is on the matter's own Costs section.

**A cost's entry number** and **the files attached to it** are not on the record yet, so those two
columns say what they are waiting on rather than guessing.

**Cost review** — submitting a cost for approval, and a reviewer sending it back with a reason — is
not switched on for any firm: the setting that turns it on is not served yet, so nothing on these
screens asks you to submit anything. Where a cost does carry a reason it was sent back, the cost
shows it and the **Returned** column carries it; that half is ready ahead of the workflow.
