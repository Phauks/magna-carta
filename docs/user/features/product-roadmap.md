# What is coming — the product roadmap

_Stub — the full guide is authored when the roadmap page ships. The roadmap itself is written and kept current; there is no page to read it on yet._

## What it does

The roadmap is where you find out what is being built next, written for you rather than for the people who build it. It is the companion to the [changelog](product-changelog.md): the changelog says what has already arrived, the roadmap says what is on its way.

Everything on it sits in one of a few bands, and the band is the only claim being made:

- **Building Now** — being worked on at the moment.
- **Planned** — decided and designed, waiting its turn to be built.
- **Future** — intended, and not scheduled. Some of it is still being researched.
- **Completed** — delivered and in use. The changelog says what each one does.
- **Not on the roadmap** — something we said we would build and have decided not to. It stays listed rather than quietly disappearing.

## There are no dates, and there will not be

No entry carries a target quarter or a delivery date. A date on a roadmap is a promise made before the work is understood, and a missed one costs more than it ever bought — so instead of a date you are given a band, and the band is true on the day you read it.

**Things move backwards sometimes.** Something in Planned can go back to Future when more urgent work lands in front of it, or when the design turns out to be wrong. When that happens it moves on the roadmap too. A roadmap that only ever moves forwards is one that has stopped describing reality.

## What you will find there, and what you will not

The roadmap lists **capabilities you would ask for by name**. Internal machinery, tooling and research are deliberately left off: an entry for something you would never see or use would tell you nothing and make the page harder to read.

It is also deliberately short of detail. One line per capability is the whole entry. Where a feature exists already, the changelog has the detail; where it does not, detail would be guesswork.

## Common tasks (once the page ships)

- **See what is being worked on.** Open the roadmap and read the Building Now section.
- **Check whether something you need is coming.** Search the page for it; if it is not there, ask for it on the feedback board, where you can also see what other firms have asked for.
- **Find out whether something arrived.** If it is under Completed, the changelog says when and what it does.
