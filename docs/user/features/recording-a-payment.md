# Recording a payment

## What it does

Marks a bill as paid, in part or in full, when a client pays you directly: a check, cash, a bank
transfer, or a card you run on your own terminal. The money goes on your books in your operating
account and the bill's balance comes down by the same amount.

Online payments (a client paying by card or bank account through a payment page) are not here yet.

## Recording one

1. Open the matter's bills and open the bill that was paid.
2. In **Payments & Credits**, choose **Record Payment**.
3. Fill in:

| Field         | What to enter                                                                    |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Amount        | What was paid. It starts as what this matter still owes on the bill              |
| Date Received | The day the money arrived, which is often not today                              |
| Method        | Check, Bank Transfer, Cash, or Card                                              |
| Reference     | Optional. The check number, the transfer confirmation, or the card approval code |

4. Choose **Record Payment**.

## What to know

- **Only a finalized bill can be paid.** A draft is still being written.
- **A payment can't be more than the matter still owes on the bill.** If a client overpays, record
  what the bill is owed, then return or credit the rest.
- **The date can't be before the bill's issue date.**
- **Trust money is different.** Money a client sends for you to hold is a trust deposit, recorded on
  the client's trust ledger. To pay a bill from it, use **Apply Trust Funds** on the bill.
- **Your operating account must be set up.** If Magna Carta hasn't been told which account is your
  operating bank account, the payment is refused and nothing is recorded. An administrator sets this
  under Billing settings.

## Fixing a mistake

A recorded payment can't be edited or deleted. If you recorded it against the wrong bill or for the
wrong amount, reverse it (see [Correcting a bill](correcting-a-bill.md)) and record it again. Both
records are kept, so the history shows exactly what happened.
