# Legal Citations

Use Legal Citations to turn a link or a rough citation into a correctly formatted legal citation, in
the format your court expects, ready to copy into a brief or an email.

## What it does

Paste a link to a statute, a regulation or an opinion, or type a citation the quick way, such as
`347 us 483` or `42 usc 1983`. Magna Carta reads what it can from it and fills in the details
beneath. Each detail says where it came from:

- **from link**: read from what you pasted.
- **edited**: you changed it. Press the undo arrow beside it to go back to what was read.
- **you add**: needed for a complete citation, and not something the link carries. A pin cite (the
  page you are quoting) is usually one of these.

Anything you type always wins over what was read, and stays if you paste the link again.

The citation is shown three ways, each with **Copy**:

- **Full Citation**: the first time you cite a source.
- **Short Form**: later references to the same source.
- **Immediately Following the Same Source**: the _Id._ form.

Copy keeps the italics when you paste into a document, and gives plain text when you paste into an
email. The rule numbers under each citation are the rules of _The Indigo Book_, a free public
manual of the Uniform System of Citation, so you can check how the citation was built.

## Available Sources

Press the information icon beside the paste box to see which links can be read. Today that is
CourtListener opinions, govinfo (the U.S. Code, the Code of Federal Regulations, the Federal Register
and public laws), the Cornell Legal Information Institute, eCFR, the Nevada Revised Statutes and the
California codes. A CourtListener link gives the case name; add the volume, reporter, page, court and
year until case details are read automatically.

## Formats

Choose a **Format** at the top right:

- **Uniform System (Indigo Book)**: the standard form most courts accept.
- **Nevada State Courts**: the uniform form with Nevada's rules. Statutes read `NRS 11.190`, and an
  unpublished disposition carries its docket number, filing date and the words "unpublished
  disposition", as Nevada Rule of Appellate Procedure 36(c)(3) requires. Magna Carta warns you if
  the disposition is too old to be cited under that rule.
- **California State Courts**: the California Style Manual.

For an unpublished case, tick **Unpublished Disposition** and enter the docket number, the database
citation and the date decided.

## What it does not do

Legal Citations checks that a citation is in the right form. It does not tell you whether a case is
still good law; use your citator for that.
