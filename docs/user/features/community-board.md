# The community board

_The board is built; the pages that show it are not yet. This guide describes what it does and how
it will work when the screens ship._

## What it does

The community board is where you ask for things Magna Carta should do, vote for what other firms
have asked for, and ask how the software works today.

**Anyone can read it without signing in.** Anyone at a firm using Magna Carta can post, vote and
reply. Search engines are asked not to index it, so what you write is visible to a person who
follows a link and is not collected into search results.

There are two kinds of post, on the same board:

- A **request** says the product should do something. Other firms vote for it, and it carries a
  status you can see.
- A **question** asks how to do something today. The **answers** are what get voted on rather than
  the question, and you can mark the one that solved it.

If a question turns out to be a missing capability, we turn it into a request and the whole
conversation comes with it.

## The categories, and why there is no "general"

Every category is a part of the software — trust and reconciliation, billing and invoicing, time and
costs, matters, the Directory and conflicts, documents, templates and automation, discovery, the
custodian register, the calendar, the client portal, reports, integrations, mobile and desktop,
search, accessibility, and onboarding.

You choose one before you write, and there is deliberately no general or off-topic category. **This
board is about the software.** A question that needs a client's name, a matter number, a balance or
a party's identity belongs in support, where it stays confidential, and the composer offers a
one-click way to send it there instead.

## What other people see about you

- **Your display name.** It starts as your own name, and you can change it to a handle at any time.
  It is a setting on your account, not a choice you make per post.
- **A badge**, if you are on the Magna Carta team.
- **Nothing about your firm.** Your firm's name is never shown on the board — not by default, not as
  an option.

Vote totals are shown. **Who voted is never shown**, at any size. Where a request has enough
separate firms behind it, the board shows how many firms; below that it shows nothing, because a
small number would say too much about who they are.

Ranking counts **firms**, not people. A large firm and a small one both feel a missing feature once,
so forty people at one firm cannot out-vote forty firms with one person each. Your vote still counts
toward the total either way.

## Statuses on a request

**Future** · **Planned** · **Building Now** · **Beta** · **Completed** · **Not on the roadmap**, and
**Duplicate** when two requests turn out to be the same one — the votes move to the one that stays.

A request that is declined always carries a reason and a note saying why. Nothing sits untriaged for
more than thirty days, and a request with no activity for a year is archived — archived, never
deleted, and every word of it stays.

The public roadmap is simply the board filtered by status. **There are no target dates**, anywhere,
on purpose: a date is a promise, and a card that moves backwards is a normal thing on a roadmap.

## If something should not have been posted

**Delete it yourself, in one click.** The words are gone immediately — no queue, no waiting for
somebody to act, and nothing is kept. The same is true of a reply. Editing a post replaces it
outright; nobody can read what it used to say.

**Flag anything that looks like it carries client information.** One flag from anyone signed in is
enough to hide a post while it is reviewed, and it comes back if the review clears it. That is
deliberate: waiting for a second person to agree means waiting for the disclosure to be read twice.

**Posts are also checked automatically.** Anything shaped like a matter or docket number, a case
caption, an email address, a telephone number, or an amount sitting beside a name will hold the post
back rather than publish it. The check reads shape only — it never looks at your firm's records — so
it errs toward holding, and a post held by mistake is released on review. You will be told which
kind of thing it noticed.

**Your first post and first reply are held** until somebody has looked at them. After one approval
your account posts straight away.

## Attachments

There are none, and this one is a real trade rather than an oversight. A screenshot of your own
workspace usually carries a client name, a matter number and a balance, and an image cannot be
skimmed for those the way text can. Rich text, lists, and code or formula snippets all work. Anything
that genuinely needs a picture goes to support, where it stays confidential.
