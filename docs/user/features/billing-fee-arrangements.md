# Fee arrangements

## What it does

Records how each matter is billed, and bills it that way. A matter can be billed by the hour (the
default — nothing to set up), for a flat fee, on contingency (a share of what is recovered), or on
an evergreen retainer that is meant to stay topped up. Multi-matter bills can be presented as one
document or as a page per matter with a summary, at your firm's choice.

## Where the arrangement lives

Open the matter and press its status badge, in the top-left corner of the masthead. The status
window's **Engagement** section shows the agreement on file, how the matter is billed, the terms,
and when the version in force started; **Show the earlier versions** unfolds the history, and
**Amend** beside the arrangement records a new version. The Fees screen no longer states the
arrangement: fees are recorded against whatever the status window says.

## Setting how a matter is billed

Record the arrangement on the matter: the kind, and its terms —

- **Flat fee:** the agreed amount.
- **Contingency:** the percentage, and whether it is taken from the gross recovery or from the
  recovery after case costs are deducted. Enter the percentage exactly as agreed.
- **Retainer:** the target balance and the floor below which you want to ask the client to top up.
- **Hourly:** nothing else — recording it just puts the deal on the record.

For a fee paid in advance, record where the deal says the money sits until it is earned — the client
trust account or the firm's operating account. There is no default: rules differ by state, and it is
your firm's agreement that answers it.

**Name the signed agreement.** When you record or amend the arrangement, pick the intake agreement
or amendment the client signed from the matter's own files, so the version points at the paper the
firm can produce. It is optional: a version can be recorded before the paper is filed and the
agreement named on the next one. The status window links to the file.

**Changing the deal keeps the history.** Amending an arrangement creates a new version; the old one
stays with the dates it governed, and every bill shows which version it was computed under. You
cannot back-date an amendment behind the version currently in force.

## Billing a flat-fee matter

Keep tracking time on the matter as usual — it feeds your profitability reporting, not the bill.
When you bill, add the flat fee to the draft and pick the time entries the fee covered: the client
sees one line for the agreed amount, and the covered work is marked billed when the invoice is
finalized. To bill the fee in stages, state the instalment amount on each bill; the platform will
not stop you billing past the agreed total, but it reports it, so check the matter before a final
instalment. Trying to bill a flat-fee matter by the hour is refused and tells you why.

## Billing a contingency matter

When the matter resolves, record the recovery — the settlement, judgment, or award, with its date
and a short description. Then compose the fee onto a draft: the platform calculates it from the
recorded recoveries and your agreed percentage and election, shows you the arithmetic, and puts it
on the bill as one line you can review. Nothing is charged until you finalize.

If a recovery was entered wrong, void it with a reason and enter it again — records are corrected,
never erased. Costs you advanced on the matter can still be billed as costs, and taking the fee out
of settlement money in trust works like any earned fee: finalize the bill, then apply trust funds
against it.

## Keeping a retainer topped up

Ask for the matter's retainer position at any time: the target, the floor, and what is actually
available in trust (money under a dispute hold does not count). When the balance is below the floor,
record a replenishment request — it notes the balance you saw and the top-up that would restore the
target, so the ask is on the record. One request is open per matter at a time; resolve it when the
client's deposit arrives (deposits come in through trust as usual) or if you withdraw the ask.

## Multi-matter bills

If one bill covers several of a client's matters, your firm chooses how it reads: a page per matter
with a summary of each matter's total (the standard setting), or one combined document. Set the firm
default under billing configuration, and override it on a single bill at finalization if a client
wants it the other way. A bill keeps the shape it was issued with — changing the firm default later
never changes a bill already sent.
