# Your notifications

The bell at the top of the screen tells you what is waiting. This page is about the list behind it —
what gets into it, how to work through it, and how to decide what is worth being told about.

## The bell, and the list

The **bell** in the top bar carries a count of what you have not read yet. Click it for the newest
few unread, the most pressing first; **Open Notifications** takes you to the full list.

The count is what is genuinely waiting for _you_ — not for the firm, and not everything that has ever
happened. It only ever counts things you are entitled to see, and it is worked out fresh each time
rather than being a running total that could drift.

## What you get told about

Twelve categories, and the list says which one every item belongs to:

| Category      | What it covers                                                              |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Deadlines** | What a court or a rule requires of the firm, falling due or already overdue |
| **Calendar**  | Hearings, appointments and events as they are booked, moved and cancelled   |
| **Tasks**     | Work with your name on it, as it is assigned and passed on                  |
| **Trust**     | Movements on client money                                                   |
| **Billing**   | Invoices as they are raised, delivered, credited and voided                 |
| **Documents** | Files arriving, checking in and out, and what scanning finds in them        |
| **Custody**   | Items in safe custody due for review, or overdue back                       |
| **Email**     | Mail saved to a matter from a capture address                               |
| **Conflicts** | What a conflicts check turned up                                            |
| **Jobs**      | Long-running work you started, when it finishes                             |
| **Security**  | Events touching access and credentials                                      |
| **System**    | Matter lifecycle changes, and what the platform reports about itself        |

## Working through the list

Every item is a **pointer to a record**, not a copy of it. Double-click an item (or select it and
press Enter) to go to the thing itself — the deadline, the invoice, the document — and it is marked
read on the way.

The list is a table, like Matters and the Directory. Each row shows the notification, any plain facts
worth stating (how many days overdue, how many matches a conflicts check found), the matter's
**number** where it has one, the category, the priority, and when it happened.

- **The round check at the start of a row** is filled while the item is unread. Press it to mark the
  item read without leaving the list.
- **Priority** shows **Urgent** or **Needs Attention** where an item has one, and is blank otherwise.
- **Group By** (at the top of the list) — the list is grouped by **Day** to begin with, newest day
  first, under headings such as Today and Yesterday. Choose **Category**, **Priority**, **Matter** or
  **Read State** to group it another way, or **No Grouping** for one plain list. Each heading says
  how many items are under it.
- **Narrowing** — click or right-click a column heading to filter it: the Status heading for unread
  or read, Category for one kind of thing, Priority for the urgent ones. **Clear Filters** appears at
  the top while anything is narrowed.
- **Mark All Read** — clears what is currently in front of you. Deliberately not "everything that
  exists": anything that arrived while you were reading stays unread, because you have not seen it.
- **Dismiss** (at the end of every row) — you are finished with this, and it leaves the list.
  Separate from marking it read, because _having seen_ something and _having dealt with_ it are
  different statements. Neither one deletes anything.
- **Show Dismissed** (at the foot of the list) — brings your dismissed items back into view, below
  everything else and in a quieter style, under their own **Dismissed** heading when the list is
  grouped. **Restore** puts one back in the list, as read. It never
  shows a matter's name, a client's name, a filename or a subject line. Those live behind the link,
  where the software checks what you are entitled to see at the moment you look.

## Why the list can change without anybody editing it

What you are entitled to see is checked **when you look**, not when the notification was written. If
you come off a matter, its items leave your list and stop counting. If you go back on, they return.
Nothing is deleted and nothing is rewritten — the list is simply answering the question again.

You will never see a placeholder saying something has been hidden from you. A gap that announces
itself is still telling you something exists.

## Items that age out

Your firm keeps notifications for a set period — ninety days unless it has changed that. Older items
leave the list.

If some of them were still unread when they went, **the list says so** in its footer, with the date of the oldest.
It does not quietly show you a zero. Everything those items pointed at — the matter, the deadline,
the record — is exactly where it was; only the pointer is gone.

## Deciding what you hear about

**Settings · Notifications** has one row per category, with both channels on it. From the
Notifications page, **What You Hear About** in the menu beside the title takes you there.

- **In app** — whether it appears in this list at all.
- **Email** — whether it joins a daily summary, a weekly one, or none.

### Where your firm has a say, and where it does not

The two channels work in opposite directions, on purpose:

- **In the app, your firm can insist.** It can require a category for everybody, and then you cannot
  switch that one off. Where it has, the row says so in words rather than leaving you with a switch
  that silently refuses to move. This only works one way — your firm can make sure something reaches
  you; it cannot stop you hearing about your own work.
- **By email, you decide.** Your firm can suggest a starting cadence, and never raise it. Your
  mailbox may be personal and is past the point where the firm's controls reach, so whatever you
  choose stands — including off.

**Trust** and **Security** are always on in the app, whatever anybody sets. They are treated the way
the audit log is treated.

### What turning something off actually does

Nothing is written at all. It is not hidden somewhere for you to stumble on later.

That creates a fair question — _did nobody tell me, or did I ask not to be told?_ — so the platform
counts every one it held back, and the row shows that count beside the switch that caused it. If a
category says nothing has been stopped and you expected otherwise, that is worth reporting rather
than shrugging at.
