# Asking Magnus

Magnus is the assistant built into Magna Carta. You type a question in plain language and it answers.
There are two places to reach it, and which one you use decides where the conversation is kept.

## The Two Places

**Magnus in the sidebar** is yours. Every conversation you have had is listed there, newest first,
and a new one starts attached to no matter. Use it for anything that is not about one file. The
list sorts by date, by name, or by the matter each conversation is kept on: the control at the top
of the list, which is there even before you have asked anything.

**Magnus inside a matter** — the **Magnus** tab on the matter — keeps the conversation on that
matter's file. It lists the conversations belonging to that matter and nothing else, and anything you
start there is attached to it automatically. Use it when the question is about that case, so the
exchange is kept where the rest of the case is kept.

A conversation started inside a matter also appears in your sidebar list, with the matter named
underneath it. It is one conversation read from two directions, not two copies.

## What Magnus Can See

**What you type in that conversation, and the text of the files you attach to it.** This is the
part worth reading twice.

Magnus does not read your calendar, your directory, or the matter you are standing in. A
conversation kept on a matter is kept on that matter's record: that is a fact about where the
transcript lives. It does not hand Magnus the matter. If you ask "what is the deadline on this", it
has no way to know, and it will answer from nothing.

So if you want Magnus to work with something, put it in your message, or attach the file.

One thing more, and only if you turn it on: in a conversation kept on a matter, you can let Magnus
read your own other conversations on the same matter (see _The Pane Beside a Conversation_). It is
off in every conversation until you turn it on there, and it never reads a colleague's conversation.

## Attaching a File

Inside a conversation kept on a matter, **Attach a File** offers the matter's own files. Attaching
one does not copy it anywhere: from then on, each question you send is preceded by the readable text
of the file, under a heading naming it, and Magnus answers with that text in front of it. A file's
text is sent up to a limit; a long file is cut at the limit and the message says so, and beside each
attached file the conversation tells you what happened on your last question:

- **Sent whole** — Magnus saw the whole file.
- **Only the first part fit** — the rest was not sent; ask about the part that did, or attach less.
- **No readable text** — a scan with no words in it, an encrypted file, or a format the platform
  cannot read; Magnus saw nothing of it.

A conversation kept on no matter has no files to reach, and the button says so. Taking a file off
the conversation stops it being sent; nothing needs erasing, because nothing was copied. Keeping a
conversation on a different matter takes every attached file off it, because they belong to the
matter it was on.

## What Magnus Can Do

It answers, and when you ask for a draft it can write it as a document kept in the conversation (see
_Documents Magnus Writes_). That is all. It cannot open a matter, book time, file a document, send
anything, or change a single record. Nothing it writes goes anywhere except into the conversation you
are reading.

## Magnus Can Be Wrong

Its answers are not legal advice and are not checked by anybody. Whatever you rely on, verify. This
is the same standard you would apply to a memo from someone whose work you have not seen before.

## Before Your First Question

The first time you open Magnus you are shown a short disclosure and asked to accept it. It says what
is on this page: what leaves the platform, what Magnus can see, what it can do, that transcripts are
kept, and that it can be wrong. You accept it once. If it changes in a way that matters, you are
asked again: when Magnus gained the ability to read attached files, everybody was asked once more,
and again when it gained the pane, its documents and the switch for your other conversations.

## What Happens to What You Type

What you type is sent to an AI provider through Magna Carta's own gateway so it can be answered. The
provider does not keep it and does not train on it.

Inside the firm, **the transcript is a firm record**, like any other work you do here:

- It is kept, not forgotten when you close the tab.
- You can read your own. Somebody with the right permission — usually the firm's owner — can read
  everybody's; that is a supervision permission, not something every colleague has.
- **Clients never see it.** No conversation reaches the client portal.
- You can delete a conversation. That removes it and everything in it. It is the only way to erase
  what was typed. Editing or removing a single message (below) takes it out of the conversation but
  does not erase it.

If a matter is subject to a legal hold or a retention rule, the conversations kept on it are covered
by the same rule as the rest of that file.

## Reading a Conversation

Questions sit on the right, under the name of the person who asked them, with their initials beside
them. Magnus answers on the left, beside the gold blackletter M. A conversation shows real names
rather than "You", so it reads the same to a colleague who opens it as it does to you. Each message
shows the time it was sent, and the copy button beneath it puts that message's words on your
clipboard.

The open conversation's name sits at the top with the matter it is kept on in small print beneath
it, or "Not kept on a matter". Inside a matter's own Magnus section the matter is already named at
the top of the page, so that line is left out.

## Editing or Removing Your Own Message

Under each message you sent there are two more buttons beside the copy button. They are there only
in a conversation you started, and only when Magnus is not in the middle of an answer.

- **Edit and Ask Again** (the pencil) puts your message back in the box so you can change it. Press
  **Send Edit** and Magnus answers the new version. Your old message and everything after it leave
  the conversation, and the new message is marked **Edited**. Press **Cancel Edit** to leave it as
  it was.
- **Remove** (the bin) takes your message and Magnus's answer to it out of the conversation. A note
  shows where they were, and Magnus does not read them again when it answers your later questions.

Neither one erases anything. The words stay on the firm's record, where a colleague allowed to read
every conversation can still see them, until the whole conversation is deleted.

## Finding Words in a Conversation

To find something said earlier in the open conversation, open its **More** menu (the three dots
beside its name) and choose **Find in Conversation**. Type the words in the box that appears under
the name. Every place they appear is marked, whatever their capitals, and the box says how many
messages hold them, such as "2 of 5 messages".

Press **Enter** or the down arrow to go to the next message that matches, and **Shift+Enter** or the
up arrow to go back. Press **Escape** or the close button when you are done. The find covers the
whole conversation, including earlier messages that were not yet on screen.

## The Pane Beside a Conversation

Press the pane button beside the conversation's **More** menu to open a pane at the side. It has
three tabs:

- **Context** lists what the conversation carries: the files attached to it, with what happened to
  each on your last question, and the documents Magnus wrote in it. The paperclip attaches more
  files, and **Remove** takes one off.
- **Permissions** says what Magnus may read in this conversation. The attached files of the matter
  are always on. **Your Other Conversations on This Matter** is off until you turn it on; when on,
  each question also carries the latest messages of up to five of your most recent other
  conversations on the same matter. Turning it on or off is recorded. It is not offered in a
  conversation kept on no matter. Other sources, such as the firm's records or research services,
  appear here only once Magnus can read them.
- **Artifacts** lists the documents Magnus wrote. Choose one to open it.

Press the close button, or the pane button again, to put the pane away.

## Documents Magnus Writes

Ask Magnus for a draft, such as a letter, a clause, a table or a timeline, and it writes it as a
document rather than in the middle of its answer. The answer shows a card naming the document and its
version; press **Open** to read it in the pane.

To change a document, ask Magnus in the conversation ("make the tone firmer", "add the March
hearing"). It writes the document again as its next version, and every earlier version is kept. In
the pane you can:

- step between versions with the arrows, or pick one from **Versions**;
- turn on **Show Changes** to mark the paragraphs that differ from the version before;
- find words in the version on screen;
- **Copy** it, or **Download** it as Markdown or plain text;
- on an earlier version, press **Restore This Version** to make it the newest again. Nothing is
  overwritten: restoring adds a version, and the ones in between stay.

A document belongs to its conversation. It is not filed on the matter, and it is deleted with the
conversation. To keep it on the matter, download it and add it to the matter's files.

## While You Wait

Your question is recorded the moment you send it, and the answer arrives underneath when it is ready.
You can leave the page; the conversation is there when you come back.

Sometimes an answer does not come, and Magnus says which of these happened rather than only that
something failed:

- **The model was unavailable** — try again.
- **Magnus was switched off while your question was waiting** — the firm's setting changed between
  your sending it and its being answered.
- **The firm's daily allowance is spent** — every firm has a ceiling on how many exchanges a day; it
  resets.
- **Your message was too long** — send it in pieces.

You may also see a note on an answer saying earlier messages did not fit. Long conversations are sent
with the most recent part only, so if the dropped part mattered, say it again.

## Naming a Conversation

A conversation you have not named shows the time it started. Click its name, or choose Rename from
the conversation's More menu, type the new name, and press Enter; Escape puts the old name back, and clicking away keeps what
you typed. If the rename does not go through, the old name stays and the conversation says so.
Magnus never names one for you — it would have to read the conversation to do that, and a title
guessed from a client's confidence is not something to leave lying in a list.

## The Conversation's More Menu

The three dots beside an open conversation's name hold everything you can do to the conversation
itself:

- **Copy Link** and **Open in New Tab** give a link that opens this conversation directly. Anyone who
  follows it still needs to be allowed to read the conversation.
- **Pin** puts the conversation at the top of your list, under Pinned. **Unpin** takes it off.
- **Rename** puts the name into a field where it stands.
- **Move to a Folder** puts it in one of your folders, or back in General.
- **Save to a Different Matter** moves it, as described below.
- **Download Transcript** saves the whole conversation as a Markdown file: who asked, when, and
  every message, oldest first, including the earlier messages that are not on screen yet.
- **Delete** removes it after you confirm.

## Folders and Pinned Conversations

Your list of conversations in the Magnus workspace is in sections, and each one folds: press a
section's name to open or close it.

- **Pinned** comes first and holds what you have pinned. A pinned conversation also stays in its
  own folder, so you find it in both places.
- **Your folders** come next, closed until you open one. Make a folder with **Add Folder** at the top
  of the list. The three dots beside a folder's name rename it or remove it.
- **General** comes last and holds every conversation that is in no folder. A new conversation
  starts in General.

Removing a folder moves its conversations back to General; it deletes no conversation. Folders and
pins are yours alone: nobody else sees them, and putting a conversation in a folder does not change
who can read it. The matter it is kept on decides that, as described below.

When you search, the list shows every conversation that matches in one list, wherever you keep it.

## Keeping a Conversation on a Matter

A conversation started in the sidebar can be kept on a matter afterwards, and one kept on a matter
can be moved to another or taken off every matter: Save to a Different Matter in the conversation's
More menu. The confirmation says the one thing to know before pressing it: **where a conversation is kept
decides who can read it.** On a matter, the transcript is part of that matter's file; on none, it is
yours and the firm's supervisors' alone. Keeping it on a matter still does not let Magnus read the
matter. A closed matter cannot take a conversation; reopen it first or choose another.

## Which Model Answers

The line above the message box names the model that answers your questions. It starts as the model
your firm chose in its AI settings, marked **Firm's Model** in the list.

To use a different model for your next question, press the model control beside **Attach a File**,
which shows the model and effort in use, such as "Claude Opus 5.5, Medium". Choose a model, and then
an effort:

- **Low** and **Medium** answer sooner and cost the firm less.
- **High**, **Extra High** and **Max** let the model think longer before it answers, which helps with
  harder questions and takes longer.

The level marked **Default** is the one the model uses when you do not choose. Your choice stays in
place for the questions that follow until you change it or leave the page. You can choose only from
the models your firm could choose, and every one of them keeps nothing that is sent to it.

## If Magnus Is Not There

Magnus is **off until the firm turns it on**, and it takes two separate decisions to turn on: the
firm must allow its information to reach an AI provider at all, and it must enable Magnus
specifically. If either is closed, the page says which one, and only the firm's administrator can
change it — see [turning AI on for your firm](../admin/ai-and-the-assistant.md).

Your role also has to include using Magnus. If the page tells you your role does not grant it, ask
whoever manages roles at the firm.
