# Joining Magna Carta

Joining happens in two steps, in this order: you **make an account**, and then you say **where you work**. Registering asks nothing about a law firm, because until you have an account there is nobody for a firm to have on its books.

## What it does

**Registering creates a person.** You give your first and last name, an email address and the password you want to use, agree to the Terms of Service and the Privacy Policy, and an email is sent to you. Your account is not created when you fill the form in — it is created when you follow the link in that email, which is how the platform knows the address is really yours. The password you chose is set on it at that moment, so you can sign in straight away.

**The welcome screen decides what your account is.** The first time you sign in you land on a screen with two choices:

- **Start a new law firm.** You create the workspace and become its owner.
- **I have an invitation code.** A firm has invited you — as a member of staff, or as a client using their portal — and the code joins you to it.

Nobody can add themselves to a firm that already exists. Who belongs to a firm is that firm's decision, which is why joining one always goes through an invitation.

## Common tasks

### Create your account

1. On the sign-in screen, select **Register**.
2. Enter your first and last name, your email address, and the password you want to use (at least eight characters).
3. Tick the Terms of Service and the Privacy Policy, then select **Send Confirmation Email**.
4. Open the email that arrives. Either follow the link in it, or select **Enter the Confirmation Code** and paste the code.
5. The screen says your account is ready. Select **Sign In** and sign in with the password you chose.

You are now signed in with an account that does not belong to a firm yet, and the welcome screen is waiting.

**If the confirmation email does not arrive**, register again with the same address — a second registration replaces the first, and the newest code is the one that works. If the screen tells you the email could not be sent, nothing was lost: wait a moment and try again, and quote the ID it shows if it keeps happening.

### Start a new firm

1. Sign in. If you have not joined a firm, the welcome screen opens by itself.
2. Select **Start a new law firm**.
3. Enter what the firm is called and select **Start This Firm**.
4. Wait a moment while the workspace is built. The screen moves on by itself when it is ready.

Your firm's name does not have to be unique. Plenty of firms share a name, and yours is yours regardless of who else uses it.

You can close the page while it is building. It keeps going, and signing in again brings you back to wherever it got to.

### Redeem an invitation

You will need three things: the **code** from your invitation, the **surname** the firm has on file for you, and your **date of birth**.

There are two ways in, and both work:

**If you already have an account,** sign in, select **I have an invitation code** on the welcome screen, and enter the three details. You are taken straight into the firm — no further email.

**If you do not have an account,** you do not need to make one first. Go to the invitation screen, enter the same three details, and an email arrives with a link that sets your password. That link is deliberate: the code, surname and date of birth you type do not prove the mailbox is yours, and the emailed link does.

Either way: type the code in capitals or lowercase, with the hyphens or without — the field groups it in fours as you type, and none of that spacing matters. The code is deliberately made of characters that are hard to mistype: you will never see a capital I, a lowercase l, a one, a capital O or a zero in one.

## Tips and edge cases

**An invitation has to be addressed to the email address on your account.** If you already have an account and the firm invited a different address of yours — a work address when you registered with a personal one, say — redeeming it while signed in will not work. Redeem it from the invitation screen without signing in instead, which creates an account for the address the firm actually invited. This is a protection rather than an obstacle: it is what stops somebody who has merely seen your code from joining your firm under their own account.

**If redeeming fails, the message will not tell you which part was wrong.** You will see the same sentence whether the code is wrong, the surname does not match, the date of birth does not match, the invitation has already been used, or it was addressed to somebody else. That is on purpose, and it is protecting you rather than being unhelpful: if the form said "the code is right but the date of birth is wrong", anyone holding a stray code could confirm that a named person is a client or an employee of a named firm. Check all three against the invitation, and if it still will not go through, ask whoever invited you to send a new one.

**An invitation locks after five wrong attempts.** It unlocks by itself after a short wait. If you are locked out, the fastest route is a fresh invitation rather than waiting.

**The attempts counter on screen is counting your tries, not the invitation's.** It is telling you how many more times you may submit the form before you are asked to pause, which is not the same as how many the invitation has left — that second number is deliberately never shown to anyone, because a stranger typing a stray code into the form could read it and learn that the code belongs to a real person at a real firm. So the counter may reach zero before the invitation does, or the invitation may lock while your own counter still has room. Either way the remedy is the same: wait out the pause, or ask for a fresh invitation.

**Invitations expire.** They last two weeks unless the firm chose a shorter window. An expired invitation cannot be revived — ask for a new one.

**Your surname is matched forgivingly.** Spaces, hyphens, apostrophes and capitalisation are ignored, so `O'Brien-Walsh`, `obrien walsh` and `OBrienWalsh` all match. Accents are not ignored — type your name the way it is spelled.

**Registering tells you nothing about whether an address is already in use,** and that is deliberate. If you register with an address that already has an account, you will see the same "check your email" message as anyone else — but the email you receive will say the address is already registered and invite you to sign in instead. If you get that email and did not ask for it, someone typed your address by mistake or on purpose; you do not need to do anything.

**A confirmation code works once.** If you use it twice, the second attempt is refused. If you register twice before confirming, only the most recent code works.

**Nothing you fill in creates an account until you confirm.** The password you choose while registering is scrambled the moment you send it and is never stored or shown in readable form; nothing exists as an account until you open the link that is emailed to you, which is also how the platform knows the address is yours.

**If you started a registration and the email has not arrived,** check the spam folder first, then register again — a second registration replaces the first, and the new code is the one that works. There is no "send again" button, because registering again is what actually sends a new one.

**You can leave the welcome screen for as long as you like.** An account with no firm is a real account: it signs in, it can reset its password, and it comes back to the same two choices whenever you are ready. Nothing expires while you decide.

**The rest of the app is not there until you have a firm.** Matters, documents, time and everything else belong to a firm, so until you have joined one there is nothing to show — which is why the welcome screen is the only place you can be.
