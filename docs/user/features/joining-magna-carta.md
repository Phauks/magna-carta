# Joining Magna Carta

There are two ways to get a Magna Carta account: someone invites you into a firm, or you start a firm of your own. Which one applies to you is the first thing you are asked.

## What it does

Registering asks you to say which of three things you are:

- **I am a client at a law firm.** Your firm gives you access to their client portal. You join by redeeming an invitation from them.
- **I work at a law firm.** Someone at the firm — usually an administrator — invites you. You join by redeeming that invitation.
- **I want to start a new law firm.** You create the workspace yourself, and become its owner.

The first two lead to the same place: **Redeem an invitation**. Nobody can add themselves to a firm that already exists, because who belongs to a firm is that firm's decision.

The third sends you an email. Your workspace is not created when you fill the form in — it is created when you follow the link in that email, which is how the platform knows the address is really yours.

## Common tasks

### Redeem an invitation

You will need three things: the **code** from your invitation, the **surname** the firm has on file for you, and your **date of birth**.

1. On the sign-in screen, select **Register**.
2. Choose **I am a client at a law firm** or **I work at a law firm**. Both lead to the same place.
3. Type the code. Capital letters and lowercase both work, and you can leave the hyphens in or take them out — the field groups it in fours as you type, and none of that spacing matters.
4. Type your last name and your date of birth.
5. Select **Redeem Invitation**.

If it works, you are sent an email with a link that sets your password. Open it, choose a password, and you can sign in.

The code is deliberately made of characters that are hard to mistype: you will never see a capital I, a lowercase l, a one, a capital O or a zero in one, so there is nothing to confuse.

### Start a new firm

1. On the sign-in screen, select **Register**, then **I want to start a new law firm**.
2. Enter what the firm is called and select **Continue**.
3. Enter your name and your email address, agree to the Terms of Service and the Privacy Policy, and select **Send Confirmation Email**.
4. Open the email that arrives and use the confirmation code in it — select **Enter the Confirmation Code**, type or paste the code, and select **Confirm Email Address**.
5. Your workspace is built — this takes a moment — and you are sent a second email with a link that sets your password.

Your firm's name does not have to be unique. Plenty of firms share a name, and yours is yours regardless of who else uses it.

## Tips and edge cases

**If redeeming fails, the message will not tell you which part was wrong.** You will see the same sentence whether the code is wrong, the surname does not match, the date of birth does not match, or the invitation has already been used. That is on purpose, and it is protecting you rather than being unhelpful: if the form said "the code is right but the date of birth is wrong", anyone holding a stray code could confirm that a named person is a client or an employee of a named firm. Check all three against the invitation, and if it still will not go through, ask whoever invited you to send a new one.

**An invitation locks after five wrong attempts.** It unlocks by itself after a short wait. If you are locked out, the fastest route is a fresh invitation rather than waiting.

**Invitations expire.** They last two weeks unless the firm chose a shorter window. An expired invitation cannot be revived — ask for a new one.

**Your surname is matched forgivingly.** Spaces, hyphens, apostrophes and capitalisation are ignored, so `O'Brien-Walsh`, `obrien walsh` and `OBrienWalsh` all match. Accents are not ignored — type your name the way it is spelled.

**Registering tells you nothing about whether an address is already in use,** and that is deliberate. If you register with an address that already has an account, you will see the same "check your email" message as anyone else — but the email you receive will say the address is already registered and invite you to sign in instead. If you get that email and did not ask for it, someone typed your address by mistake or on purpose; you do not need to do anything.

**The confirmation email carries a code, not a link — for now.** Copy the code out of the email and paste it into the confirmation screen. A clickable link is coming; when it arrives, the same screen will accept it, and the code will still work for anyone whose mail client will not open links.

**A confirmation code works once.** If you use it twice, the second attempt is refused. If you register twice before confirming, only the most recent code works.

**Nothing you fill in creates an account until you confirm.** No password is asked for while you register: you set it afterwards, from the link that is emailed to you, which is also how the platform knows the address is yours.

**If you started a registration and the email has not arrived,** check the spam folder first, then register again — a second registration replaces the first, and the new code is the one that works. There is no "send again" button, because registering again is what actually sends a new one.
