# Tasks

A task is one piece of work with somebody's name on it. Find them two ways: on a matter under
**Tasks**, which shows that matter's alone, and under **Tasks** in the sidebar, which gathers every
one you can reach.

## What a Task Is, and What It Is Not

A task is the firm's own work — "call Mrs Herrera about the offer", "get the opposition draft to
Sarah by Thursday". It is not a deadline. A deadline is what a court or a rule requires of the firm,
and those live on the matter's **Calendar**, where missing one is a serious matter. Keeping the two
apart is deliberate: if the deadline list filled up with reminders, people would stop reading it.

A task is also not a calendar entry. Nothing about it is scheduled with anybody else, and moving its
due date is just you changing your mind rather than a hearing being reset.

## The Tasks List

Every task sits on a row. From left to right the row shows its **status**, its name, who it is
assigned to, its **Start** and **Due** dates and its **Priority**. You change those last three
straight from the row: press a date to pick a new one from a calendar (or **Clear** it), and press
the priority to choose another.

A due date that has passed on a task still owed is shown in red. Nothing else is said about it:
the red date is the whole message.

The eye button beside a task's name shows its detail and checklist without opening it.

The list is grouped by **List** to begin with. **Group By** groups it by status, by when it is due,
by who it is assigned to, by matter, or not at all. **View** chooses:

- **Layout**: **One Line** per task, or **Two Lines**, where the second line shows the start of the
  task's detail.
- **Row Size**: Small, Medium or Large.
- **Columns**: which columns to show. The status and the task's name always show.

## Lists and Statuses

A firm keeps its tasks on one or more **lists** (for example "Litigation", "Intake" and
"Administration"). Every task is on exactly one list. One list is the firm's default, where a new
task lands unless you choose another.

Each list has its own **statuses**, each with a name and a colour, such as To Do, In Progress and
On Hold. The status is the coloured pill at the left of the row. Press it to move the task to
another status. A list can say which status may follow which (its flow), so the menu offers only
the statuses this task can move to next.

**Mark Complete** sits at the foot of the same menu. Finishing a task is not a status: completing
it leaves its status where it was, and **Reopen** brings it back exactly as it stood. Completed
tasks leave the list; **Show Completed** at the foot of the list brings them back.

Anybody whose role manages task plans can open **Task Settings** (the gear beside **View**). The
**Lists** tab adds and renames lists, makes one the default, and retires a list, moving its tasks
to another list first. For each list it adds, renames, recolours and removes statuses, and sets
which statuses each one can move to. A status that tasks are still on cannot be removed. The
**Teams** tab is described below.

## The Board

**Board**, beside **List** at the top of the Tasks list, shows the same tasks as cards in columns,
one column for each status. The board shows one list at a time, because each list has its own
statuses: choose which with the list button beside **Board**.

- **Drag a card to another column** to move the task to that status. While you carry a card, the
  columns the list does not allow it to move to next fade and say **Not a next step**.
- **Drop a card on Mark Complete**, after the last column, to complete the task. It keeps its
  status, so reopening it puts it back where it was.
- **Click a card** to open the task.

A card shows the task's matter (on the firm's Tasks page), its due date (red once it has passed),
its priority when it is not Normal, how much of its checklist and its sub-tasks are done, and who it
is assigned to. Sub-tasks are not cards of their own: open the task to see them. Me Mode, search,
Show Completed and Show Archived work the same way on the board as on the list.

## Me Mode

Everyone in the firm sees every task, apart from tasks on matters they are walled off from. **Me
Mode** narrows the list to your own work:

- **Assigned To Me**: tasks assigned to you, or to a team you are on.
- **Watching**: tasks you are watching.
- **Both**: either of those.

Turn it **Off** to see everyone's tasks again.

## Opening a Task

Press a task's name to open it in its window. There is no Edit button: every field in the window is
changed where you read it, and each change is saved as you make it. Type a new title or detail and
move on, pick a date, choose a priority, tick a checklist item.

The window's action bar holds the task's **status** and **Mark Complete** (or **Reopen**). The
**...** menu beside **Print** holds **Duplicate**, **Archive** and **Delete**. Duplicate makes a
copy with the same people, dates, checklist and links, named "(Copy)".

The window has these sections:

- **People**: who the task is **Assigned To** (people, teams, or both) and its **Watchers**.
  Watchers are kept informed of the work without doing it, like being copied on a letter.
- **Location**: a room, an address, or a meeting link. When it is a web address, a **Join** button
  opens the meeting.
- **Checklist**: the steps inside the task, each ticked off on its own.
- **Sub-Tasks**: smaller tasks that belong to this one. See below.
- **Links**: web addresses the work refers to, each with an optional name.
- **Attachments**: documents from the matter's files.

## Creating a Task

Press **New Task**. The same window opens with nothing in it yet, and this time it has a **Save**
button: nothing is created until you press it. Say what to do, and add detail if the name is not
enough on its own.

Choose the **List** the task goes on and the **Status** it starts at; the default list and its first
status are already chosen. **Start** and **Due** are both optional. If you give a due date, the task
is due at the end of that day in your own time zone, so a task due Friday is not late on Friday
morning. Add a **Time** only where a specific hour matters.

**Priority** is one of four: Urgent, High, Normal, Low. Normal is the default.

Assigning a task to somebody else, or to a team, and adding watchers needs a role that includes
assigning work. Without it you can still create tasks. You cannot assign a task on a matter to
somebody who cannot open that matter.

## Who Is Told

- **When a task is assigned to you**, or to a team you are on, you get a notification. You are not
  told about work you assign to yourself.
- **When you are added as a watcher**, you are told, and from then on you hear when the task is
  marked complete or reopened, moves to another status, is assigned to different people, or has its
  due date moved. Changing a task's wording tells nobody.
- **Nobody is told about their own change.**

These arrive in your notifications under Tasks, and you can choose how they reach you in your
notification settings.

The checklist, links and sub-tasks are added once the task has been saved.

## Sub-Tasks

Open a task and press **Add Sub-Task** to break it into smaller tasks. A sub-task is on the same
list and the same matter as its parent, has its own status, and can have sub-tasks of its own.

On the list, sub-tasks sit under their parent, indented. The arrow to the left of a task's name
folds its sub-tasks away and brings them back.

## Teams

A **team** is a named group of people, such as "Trial Team". Assigning a task to a team gives the
work to whoever is on it, so somebody who joins the team picks up its tasks without anybody
reassigning them. Teams are kept under **Task Settings**, on the **Teams** tab.

## Tasks That Belong to No Matter

Not everything a firm does belongs to a client file. Renewing the malpractice policy, interviewing a
candidate, getting the research contract reviewed: create those from **Tasks** in the sidebar and
they will carry no matter. Like every other task, everyone in the firm can see them.

## Archiving and Deleting

Both take a task off the list, and they differ in one thing:

- **Archive** keeps it. Everything the task carries stays — its detail, its documents, the time
  logged against it — and **Show Archived** at the foot of the list brings it back into view. **Put
  Back** returns it to the list exactly as it stood. You can say why you archived it, but you do not have
  to.
- **Delete** removes it. The task is gone and cannot be recovered.

Archive anything you might want to look at again, which is almost everything. Delete is for a task
that should not exist — a typo, a duplicate, a line you started and abandoned before it meant
anything.

Archiving is not a status: a task is archived from wherever it stood, so a completed task you
archive stays completed, and one you had On Hold comes back On Hold.

## Waiting on Another Task

A task can say it is waiting on another one on the same matter. It shows as **Waiting on another
task** and sorts below.

**This does not stop you.** You can complete a task that is waiting, and nothing objects. Real work
has exceptions constantly, and a checklist that argues with you is a checklist you stop using. It is
there to say what the intended order is, not to enforce it.

## Documents

Open the task and go to its **Attachments** tab to point it at one of the matter's documents. The
list names what a task already carries; attaching and detaching happen in the window, with the rest
of what you can do to a task. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is copied — the document stays in the
matter's files with its versions and everything else that follows from being there, and detaching it
later leaves it exactly where it is.

You can pick documents for a task you are still writing. They are attached the moment the task
itself is saved.

Tasks that belong to no matter cannot point at documents, because every document in Magna Carta
belongs to a matter.

## Finding Things

The search box matches what a task says (its name and its detail) on the list you are looking at.
Whenever the search or Me Mode is narrowing the list, a **Show Everything** control appears beside
them; when it is not there, nothing is hidden.

By default the list shows what is still owed. Below the list, **Show Completed** and **Show
Archived** add the rest; each then reads **Hide Completed** or **Hide Archived**.

## Tasks in the Sidebar

**Tasks** in the sidebar is every task in the firm in one place: across every matter you can see,
plus the firm's own work on no matter. Turn on Me Mode for just your own. A matter's own **Tasks**
section is the same list narrowed to that one matter.

Tasks on closed matters are left out. Closing a matter changes nothing about its tasks; they simply
stop cluttering the day-to-day list. Reopen the matter and they come straight back.

## Being Told About a Task

When somebody puts a task in your name you get a notification. You will not be notified about tasks
you assign to yourself, or about a change that leaves the task with whoever already had it.

Reminders about tasks that are coming due or past due are not built yet. They will arrive with the same
change that brings deadline reminders, so that both use one idea of what "due" means.

## Matter Plans

A matter plan is your firm's own list of what a kind of matter takes — the calls, the letters, the
filings. Write one under **Settings → Matter Plans**, and it is offered every time a matter of that
kind is opened.

**A plan offers; it never imposes.** When a matter has a plan, its Tasks page says so. Press
**Review** and you see exactly what the plan would add: each step, the date it worked out, and who it
would go to. Nothing has been created at that point. Untick what this matter does not need, fix
anything that is wrong, and press Add — and only then do the tasks exist. Closing the review adds
nothing, which is all declining is.

### Writing a Plan

Give the plan a name, then add its steps. Each step has:

- **What to do**, and detail underneath if the title is not enough.
- **Due**, either "no date" or a number of days after the matter opens. **These are plain days.**
  Nothing skips weekends and nothing knows about court holidays, so a step set to 14 days can land on
  a Sunday — which is one of the reasons somebody reviews a plan before it becomes tasks.
- **A role**, not a person. Write the role the way your firm records it on a matter, such as
  `assigned_paralegal`. When the plan is offered, that role is matched against that matter's own team.
  A plan naming a person would be wrong the moment their matters moved; a plan naming a role works on
  every matter.
- **Priority**, as on any task.

Where a role matches nobody on the matter, the step arrives unassigned and says which role could not
be filled, so you can fill it in the review rather than wondering why a seat is empty.

### Changing a Plan Later

Editing a plan changes what it offers **from now on**. It does not touch tasks already on a matter —
those were reviewed and accepted as they stood, and a plan edit never reaches back into open matters
to move a date somebody is working to.

**Stop Offering** takes a plan out of circulation without deleting it, and there is no way to delete
one. That is on purpose: every task a plan produced records the plan and the step it came from, and
deleting the plan would strand that record. A plan you have stopped offering is not offered on any
new matter and changes nothing on the matters that already took it.

### What Magna Carta Does Not Give You

**No plans come with the product, and that is deliberate.** A plan is a statement about how your firm
practises in your jurisdiction. A plan written by us, with a missing step or a step in the wrong
order, would look authoritative precisely because it shipped with the software. Yours will be right;
ours could not be.

## Time On A Task

A time entry can say which task the work was for. Open the entry, pick the task in the **Task**
field, and save — the field offers that matter's open tasks and nothing else, because time on one
matter cannot be work on another matter's task. Most entries name none, and that is the ordinary
case: time is recorded against a **matter**, and naming a task is an extra you choose when you want
to know how long a particular piece of work took.

Once there is time against a task, the task says so: **Time logged** appears in its row with the
total. That total is minutes, not money — what a task was billed for is a billing question, asked on
a billing screen.

**Whose time you see depends on what you may see.** If you are allowed to see other people's time,
the total is the whole task's: everything the firm has put into it. If you are not, you see your
own and the row says **by you**, so the number is never mistaken for the whole.

Some things this deliberately does not do. **One task per entry** — if a block of time served two
tasks, record two entries, because splitting one entry between tasks would mean deciding how many of
its minutes went to each, and that is a second set of books. **Nothing starts a timer from a task
yet.** And **a task never carries an estimate that the product compares against the actual time** —
that is a report, and reports belong on the reporting screens.

A task on no matter — the firm's own work — cannot carry time, because there is no client matter for
that time to belong to.

## Connecting Tasks To Another Tool

Magna Carta does not connect to ClickUp, Asana or anything else on its own, and nothing in the
product will offer to. What it has is the groundwork an **add-on** needs, so one can be built without
changing how tasks work.

What that means in practice: a task can carry its identity in another system, and when it does, the
task's row says so and links straight to it. A task can be mirrored into more than one tool at once.
Editing on either side is possible, and the record keeps enough to stop the two copies fighting —
including who changed a task last, so an add-on can tell its own updates from a person's.

Deleting a task is handled properly rather than quietly: the record keeps a note that the task
existed and was deleted, so a connected tool learns the work is gone instead of leaving it sitting on
somebody's board forever.

**An add-on only ever sees what the person it is connected as can see.** Connecting one does not open
a side door to matters that person has no access to.

## What Is Not Built Yet

There is no recurring task and no comment thread on a task. A timer cannot yet be started from a task.

A plan cannot yet be set to apply itself without review, and its dates can only count from the day
the matter opened.
