# Tasks

A task is one piece of work with somebody's name on it. Find them two ways: on a matter under
**Tasks**, and all together under **My Work** in the sidebar.

## What a Task Is, and What It Is Not

A task is the firm's own work — "call Mrs Herrera about the offer", "get the opposition draft to
Sarah by Thursday". It is not a deadline. A deadline is what a court or a rule requires of the firm,
and those live on the matter's **Calendar**, where missing one is a serious matter. Keeping the two
apart is deliberate: if the deadline list filled up with reminders, people would stop reading it.

A task is also not a calendar entry. Nothing about it is scheduled with anybody else, and moving its
due date is just you changing your mind rather than a hearing being reset.

## Creating a Task

Press **New Task**. Say what to do, and add detail underneath if the title is not enough on its own.

**Due** and **Time** are both optional, and plenty of tasks have neither. If you give a date, the
task is due at the end of that day in your own time zone — so a task due Friday is not treated as
late on Friday morning. Add a time only where a specific hour matters.

**Priority** is one of four: Urgent, High, Normal, Low. Normal is the default and means nothing in
particular, which is why there is no separate "none".

**Assign to** puts the task in somebody's name. You can leave it as _Nobody yet_ and pick somebody
later. If you cannot see this field, your role does not include assigning work to other people; you
can still create tasks and take them yourself.

You cannot assign a task on a matter to somebody who cannot open that matter. A task nobody can look
at is a gap in their list that nothing explains, so it is refused when you try rather than appearing
and then not working.

## Tasks That Belong to No Matter

Not everything a firm does belongs to a client file. Renewing the malpractice policy, interviewing a
candidate, getting the research contract reviewed — create those from **My Work** and they will
carry no matter.

**A task with no matter is private to you and whoever you assign it to.** Nobody else in the firm
sees it, including people who can see every matter. If work should be visible to the team, put it on
the matter it belongs to.

## Finishing, Reopening, and Deciding Not To

Press **Done** when the work is finished. The task records who did it and when.

Press **Reopen** if you marked something done too early, or if a decision changed. The task comes
back to the list, and the firm's audit record keeps the fact that it was closed and reopened.

**Cancel** and **Delete** are different and both are offered.

- **Cancel** is for work the firm decided not to do. It asks why, and that answer stays on the task.
  Six months later somebody can see what was proposed and why it did not happen.
- **Delete** is for a task that should not exist — a typo, a duplicate, a line you started and
  abandoned. The task is removed and cannot be recovered.

Use Cancel where the decision matters. Deleting a piece of real work loses the record that anybody
ever considered it.

## Overdue

A task shows **Overdue** when its due date has passed and it is still open. Nothing is stored to make
that happen: it is worked out fresh whenever you look, in the task's own time zone, so it is never
based on a stale calculation. Tasks that are done or cancelled are never marked overdue.

## Waiting on Another Task

A task can say it is waiting on another one on the same matter. It shows as **Waiting on another
task** and sorts below.

**This does not stop you.** You can complete a task that is waiting, and nothing objects. Real work
has exceptions constantly, and a checklist that argues with you is a checklist you stop using. It is
there to say what the intended order is, not to enforce it.

## Documents

Press the paperclip on a task to point it at one of the matter's documents. Nothing is uploaded and
nothing is copied — the document stays in the matter's files with its versions and everything else
that follows from being there, and detaching it later leaves it exactly where it is.

Tasks that belong to no matter cannot point at documents, because every document in Magna Carta
belongs to a matter.

## Finding Things

The search box matches what a task says — its title and its detail — on the list you are looking at.

By default a list shows what is still open, which is what a task list is for. Tick **Done** or
**Cancelled** to bring the rest back. Whenever anything is narrowing the list, a **Clear** control
appears; when it is not there, nothing is hidden.

## My Work

**My Work** in the sidebar is every task you can reach, in one place: across every matter you can
see, plus your own work on no matter. Filter it by yourself to get just your list.

Tasks on closed matters are left out. Closing a matter changes nothing about its tasks — they are
not cancelled or completed — they simply stop cluttering the day-to-day list. Reopen the matter and
they come straight back.

## Being Told About a Task

When somebody puts a task in your name you get a notification. You will not be notified about tasks
you assign to yourself, or about a change that leaves the task with whoever already had it.

Reminders about tasks that are due or overdue are not built yet. They will arrive with the same
change that brings deadline reminders, so that both use one idea of what "due" means.

## Matter Plans

A matter plan is your firm's own list of what a kind of matter takes — the calls, the letters, the
filings. Write one under **Settings → Matter Plans**, and it is offered every time a matter of that
kind is opened.

**A plan offers; it never imposes.** When a matter has a plan, its Tasks page says so. Press
**Review** and you see exactly what the plan would add: each step, the date it worked out, and who it
would go to. Nothing has been created at that point. Untick what this matter does not need, fix
anything that is wrong, and press Add — and only then do the tasks exist. Closing the review adds
nothing, which is all declining is.

### Writing a Plan

Give the plan a name, then add its steps. Each step has:

- **What to do**, and detail underneath if the title is not enough.
- **Due**, either "no date" or a number of days after the matter opens. **These are plain days.**
  Nothing skips weekends and nothing knows about court holidays, so a step set to 14 days can land on
  a Sunday — which is one of the reasons somebody reviews a plan before it becomes tasks.
- **A role**, not a person. Write the role the way your firm records it on a matter, such as
  `assigned_paralegal`. When the plan is offered, that role is matched against that matter's own team.
  A plan naming a person would be wrong the moment their matters moved; a plan naming a role works on
  every matter.
- **Priority**, as on any task.

Where a role matches nobody on the matter, the step arrives unassigned and says which role could not
be filled, so you can fill it in the review rather than wondering why a seat is empty.

### Changing a Plan Later

Editing a plan changes what it offers **from now on**. It does not touch tasks already on a matter —
those were reviewed and accepted as they stood, and a plan edit never reaches back into open matters
to move a date somebody is working to.

**Stop Offering** takes a plan out of circulation without deleting it, and there is no way to delete
one. That is on purpose: every task a plan produced records the plan and the step it came from, and
deleting the plan would strand that record. A plan you have stopped offering is not offered on any
new matter and changes nothing on the matters that already took it.

### What Magna Carta Does Not Give You

**No plans come with the product, and that is deliberate.** A plan is a statement about how your firm
practises in your jurisdiction. A plan written by us, with a missing step or a step in the wrong
order, would look authoritative precisely because it shipped with the software. Yours will be right;
ours could not be.

## Time On A Task

A time entry can say which task the work was for. Open the entry, pick the task in the **Task**
field, and save — the field offers that matter's open tasks and nothing else, because time on one
matter cannot be work on another matter's task. Most entries name none, and that is the ordinary
case: time is recorded against a **matter**, and naming a task is an extra you choose when you want
to know how long a particular piece of work took.

Once there is time against a task, the task says so: **Time logged** appears in its row with the
total. That total is minutes, not money — what a task was billed for is a billing question, asked on
a billing screen.

**Whose time you see depends on what you may see.** If you are allowed to see other people's time,
the total is the whole task's: everything the firm has put into it. If you are not, you see your
own and the row says **by you**, so the number is never mistaken for the whole.

Some things this deliberately does not do. **One task per entry** — if a block of time served two
tasks, record two entries, because splitting one entry between tasks would mean deciding how many of
its minutes went to each, and that is a second set of books. **Nothing starts a timer from a task
yet.** And **a task never carries an estimate that the product compares against the actual time** —
that is a report, and reports belong on the reporting screens.

A task on no matter — the firm's own work — cannot carry time, because there is no client matter for
that time to belong to.

## Connecting Tasks To Another Tool

Magna Carta does not connect to ClickUp, Asana or anything else on its own, and nothing in the
product will offer to. What it has is the groundwork an **add-on** needs, so one can be built without
changing how tasks work.

What that means in practice: a task can carry its identity in another system, and when it does, the
task's row says so and links straight to it. A task can be mirrored into more than one tool at once.
Editing on either side is possible, and the record keeps enough to stop the two copies fighting —
including who changed a task last, so an add-on can tell its own updates from a person's.

Deleting a task is handled properly rather than quietly: the record keeps a note that the task
existed and was deleted, so a connected tool learns the work is gone instead of leaving it sitting on
somebody's board forever.

**An add-on only ever sees what the person it is connected as can see.** Connecting one does not open
a side door to matters that person has no access to.

## What Is Not Built Yet

There is no board view, no recurring task, and no comment thread on a task. Sub-checklists inside a
task are not built either; use a second task and the waiting-on link. A timer cannot yet be started
from a task.

A plan cannot yet be set to apply itself without review, and its dates can only count from the day
the matter opened.
