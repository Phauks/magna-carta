# Tools

Tools is the firm's workbench: the utilities that are not about one matter — looking up who
registered a domain, opening a suspicious link at arm's length, reading a file that belongs nowhere
yet, measuring this connection. Open it from the sidebar.

## Finding a tool

The Tools page lists everything the firm has, grouped by subject: property, business entities,
people and licences, mailing and shipping, printing, web and domains, documents and files, and
diagnostics. Each entry says in one line what the tool does.

**The search is the magnifying glass at the top right of the page.** Click it and the field opens;
type and the page narrows as you type, showing only what matches and only the headings that still
have something under them. The count inside the field says how much of the whole you are looking at
— "11 of 20". Search by anything on the entry: a tool's name, the line describing it, or the
heading it sits under. Some tools also answer to words they do not display, so "bandwidth" reaches
the network speed test and "registrant" reaches the WHOIS lookup.

Clear the search with the cross at the end of the field, or by pressing Escape. Pressing Escape on
an already-empty field closes it again. The field stays open, with a coloured edge, for as long as
something is typed in it, so a shortened list always shows its reason.

This is the search for the page you are on. The one at the top of the window is different: it
searches the whole application and takes you places.

## The two views

The two buttons beside the search choose how the page reads:

- **Tiles** — a card for each tool, with the descriptive line at full length. Good for seeing what
  the firm has.
- **List** — one dense line per tool. Good when you know what you are after.

Your choice is remembered in this browser, and it is remembered separately for Tools and for
[Reports](reports.md), which reads the same way.

## Opening a tool, and what Soon means

A tool that is built opens when you click it. A tool marked **Soon** is listed rather than hidden,
so you can see what is coming; it does not open yet. Nothing on the page pretends to work.
