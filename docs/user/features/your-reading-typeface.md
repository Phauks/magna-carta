# Your reading typeface

## What it does

You choose the typeface the interface's text is set in. The choice is **yours alone**: it changes
how Magna Carta reads for you, on this browser, and never what anyone else at your firm sees. It is
an accessibility choice, so your firm cannot lock you out of it.

Five faces are offered:

| Choice                    | Who it suits                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Atkinson Hyperlegible** | Everyone, by default. Drawn for low vision: the letters people confuse in names, figures and account numbers (I, l and 1; O and 0) differ. |
| **Lexend**                | People who read for long stretches. Wider, more evenly spaced letters made to be easier on reading stamina.                                |
| **OpenDyslexic**          | Dyslexic readers. Weighted letter bottoms keep letters from appearing to flip or swim.                                                     |
| **Literata**              | People who read more easily in a serif. Drawn for long reading on screens.                                                                 |
| **System**                | People who prefer their computer's own interface font. Nothing extra is downloaded.                                                        |

## Choosing a typeface

1. Open **Settings**, then **Text** (under Appearance).
2. Pick a face from **Font Family**. Each choice in the list is shown in its own face, so you can
   see it before you pick it.

The interface changes at once, with no reload, and every other Magna Carta tab you have open in the
same browser follows. The next time you open Magna Carta it starts in your face rather than flashing
the default first.

## Tips and edge cases

- **Page titles and the firm's mark keep their own faces.** The large serif page titles and the
  Magna Carta mark are lettering rather than reading text, so they stay as they are. Everything you
  read and type — tables, forms, menus, messages — follows your choice.
- **Figures still line up.** Amounts and dates sit in columns whichever face you pick.
- **Undoing a change.** The history button at the top of the Text page lists your changes; Restore
  puts a previous face back.
- **Another computer starts from the default.** The choice is kept by your browser, so set it once
  on each computer or browser you use.
- **Text size.** **Text Size**, on the same page, sets interface text anywhere from 50% to 200% of
  your browser's normal size (100% is normal), without changing the spacing around it. Type a
  number into the field to jump to it. It applies at once, like
  the face, and stacks with your browser's own text-size setting.
