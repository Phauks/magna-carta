# The Firm's Bills

**Billing** in the sidebar shows every bill the firm has raised, for every client, with what is still
owed and how late it is. It answers the question a matter cannot: not "what has this file been
charged", which is the matter's own Billing section, but "what has the firm billed, and what is it
owed".

## Where Bills Live, in Two Places

- **Billing, in the sidebar.** This screen. Every bill for every client, newest first, with the
  receivables total over it. It is where bills are found and read.
- **A matter's Billing section.** That matter's own account: the bills that charge it, each showing
  that matter's share rather than the whole document's total, and every act you can take against a
  bill — finalizing a draft, applying trust money, writing off a balance, recording a correction.

Both read the same bills. A bill you finalize on a matter appears here the moment it is raised,
because it is one document with two doors.

## What Is Owed, Over the Table

The strip above the table is the firm's receivable, split by how far past due each bill is: not yet
due, then the bands of lateness, each with what is owed in it and how many bills that is.

It is worked out fresh every time you open the screen, over **every** finalized bill — including the
ones further down than the page has loaded. That is why it sits above the table rather than in the
totals underneath: the column totals cover what is loaded and grow as you load more, and this figure
does not. A bill settled this morning is never counted as late.

## Choosing Which Bills

The control at the top names which bills you are looking at:

- **All bills** — the whole register, which is where it opens.
- **Drafts** — bills still being written, which no client has been given.
- **Finalized** — the bills that have been issued.
- **Voided drafts** — drafts that were abandoned. They stay on the register and count toward nothing.

The choice is in the address, so a partner looking at the firm's open drafts can send that exact view
by sending the link.

## Narrowing the Table

Every column carries its own filter, reached from the column's own heading — Client, Status,
Delivered, and the rest. That is the search on this screen: filter Client to one client, Status to
unpaid, in any combination. The counts strip says how many rows are showing out of how many there
are, so you always know what a filter left out.

## What the Columns Say

Across the firm, a bill's figures are the **document's own**: what it totals and what it still owes,
across every matter it charges. A bill belongs to a client and may charge several of that client's
matters, so there is no single matter's share to show here — that column is on the matter's own
register, where a matter is standing behind it.

**Due** turns red with the day count once a bill is past due and unpaid. That is worked out from
today's date each time you look, never stored, so it is right the moment a payment lands.

**Delivered** says whether there is any record of the bill having reached the client. A dash means
the record has not been read yet — it is read when you open the bill.

## Opening a Bill

Double-click a row, or press Enter on it, to open the bill. You see what it charges, what has been
applied to it, what is still owed, and its lines.

The address changes when you do, so **the open bill is a link**. Sending that link sends the
document, not a register and an instruction to go looking for it.

## Acting on a Bill

Applying a client's trust funds, writing off a balance and recording a credit note are a **matter's**
acts: they move one matter's money, against one matter's account. On this screen they are shown and
refused, and the refusal says where they live — open the bill from the matter it charges.

The bill itself offers the way in. Open it and it lists the matters it charges, each by name, and
each one takes you to that matter's Billing section with the same bill open. A bill that charges
three of a client's matters offers all three, because which one you want is a choice only you can
make.

## What Is Not Here Yet

**Raising a bill** starts on the matter it charges. There is no "new bill" here, and the reason is
not simply that a bill needs a matter: a bill is not a form you fill in. A draft is opened and then
built up by claiming that matter's unbilled time, fees and costs onto it, and that work is on the
matter's own Billing section. A New Bill here would open an empty draft on a screen that could not
then fill it.

**Batch and recurring bills** — running a month's billing for many matters at once — are designed and
not built.

**Printing the client's bill** is not offered anywhere yet: the invoice document itself has no
renderer. What you can print is the record's own sheet — what the platform holds about the bill.
