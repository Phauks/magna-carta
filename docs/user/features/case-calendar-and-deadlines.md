# Case calendar and deadlines

_The firm calendar, the matter's Calendar section, the room grid and the Home card are live. A matter's whole history is its [Activity section](matter-activity.md)._

## What it does

Two things that sound like one and are not.

**A record of what has happened on a case**, and what is coming. The matter's timeline pulls together
everything the firm has already recorded about that case — documents filed, email captured, documents
assembled, originals moved in and out of the safe, client money moved through trust, time and costs
booked, conflicts checks run, the matter opened and closed — and puts it in one order, alongside the
hearings, filings, and deadlines you record here. Nothing is copied to build that list: each entry is
read from the record it belongs to, so the timeline can never quietly disagree with the register,
the ledger, or the file it came from.

**A place to record the litigation itself**, which the platform has no other way to know about.
Hearings, filings, service, orders, depositions, mediations, trials, client meetings, site visits —
and separately, the dates you must act by.

## The calendar screens

**Calendar**, in the sidebar, is the firm's calendar. It opens on the whole firm's month: every
setting and deadline on every matter you can see, and the firm's own events beside them.

- **Your calendars, down the left.** Your firm does not have one calendar; it has one for the
  firm's matters and deadlines, one for the firm's own events, one for each colleague, one for each
  conference room, and a few that are dates rather than people. They are listed in folders — your
  attorneys, your paralegals, your receptionists, each under the role your firm gave them, then
  your rooms, then your dates. Tick one to add it to the grid; tick a folder to add everyone in it,
  and tick it again to take them all off. A folder with only some of its calendars on shows a dash
  rather than a tick, and pressing it turns the rest on.

  Three are on when you first arrive, under **The Firm**: **Case Settings** (every matter's
  hearings, depositions and conferences), **Deadlines**, and **Firm Events**. Each has its own
  colour and its own tick, so you can read only the deadlines, or everything but them. Everything
  else is yours to add. What you have open is **yours**: it follows you between screens
  and visits on this browser, and changes nothing for anybody else.

  **Fold a folder you are not using.** Press its name and it closes up, so a firm with twenty
  conference rooms does not scroll past all twenty to reach what is under them. Folding takes
  nothing away: the tick beside the folder's name still turns every calendar in it on or off while
  it is closed, and a closed folder shows how many of its calendars are switched on so nothing
  disappears quietly. What you have folded is remembered on this browser, like the rest of the list.

- **A small month sits under the list, and it stays there.** It shows where you are and takes you
  somewhere else in one press, and it does not scroll away when you scroll the calendars above it.
  Its arrows look at the months either side without moving the calendar; press the month's name to
  pick any month from a grid of twelve, with arrows for the year. Pressing a day moves the calendar
  to that day **without changing your view** — press a Thursday
  while you are reading a week and you get that Thursday's week, not a day view of it. Its week
  begins on the day your firm chose, so its columns and the grid's are the same seven days.

- **A colour per calendar.** Press the dot beside a calendar's name and pick a colour. It paints a
  stripe down the edge of every entry from that calendar, and heads that calendar's column on the
  day view, so one person's day is told from another's at a glance. Calendars you have not coloured
  are given colours to start with, so eight of them turned on are still eight you can tell apart.
- **Custody Dates**, under **Dates**, adds the safe custody register's dates to the calendar: when
  an original checked out is due back, and when a packet or an item is due for review. Each shows
  its custody number and opens the register.
- **File Destruction Dates**, under **Dates** beside it, shows the day each matter's paper file may
  be destroyed after, as your firm recorded it on the matter's status window. Each entry shows the
  matter's number and opens the matter. It is a reminder only: nothing is destroyed when the day
  arrives.
- **Whose calendar you are reading is the list on the left.** To read your own day, turn your own
  calendar on and the others off; to read a colleague's, turn theirs on. A colleague's calendar
  shows the settings they are a participant at or the responsible attorney for, over the matters
  you can see, and every firm event whose audience is everyone.
- **What sort of thing is the list on the left too.** Case settings, deadlines, firm events and the
  register's dates are each their own calendar, so turning one kind off is unticking it.
- **The date is the heading over the calendar.** The month you are reading (or the week, or the
  day) is written large at the top left, with the arrows just before it. Press the heading to jump
  to any month from a grid of twelve, with arrows for the year.
- **Hide settled.** On the agenda, open **View** and tick it to leave out what is no longer coming:
  cancelled settings and satisfied, vacated or superseded deadlines. The agenda says how many it
  left out. The month and the week always show them, struck through.
- **Month, week, day, agenda.** Four views of the same calendar, chosen from the **View** menu at
  the top right, with **Today** beside it. The screen's name runs across
  the top of the whole page, above both the list of calendars and the calendar, with the search and
  **New** on its line. The month grid shows a few
  entries per day and says how many more there are; press that and the agenda opens on that day.
  The **week** and the **day** are the two with hours: the time runs down the side, and an entry is
  drawn from the hour it starts at to the hour it ends, so a two-hour deposition is twice the block
  a one-hour conference is and a free afternoon shows as a gap. **An event that runs for several days
  shows on every one of them**: the first day from its start time to midnight, a day in the middle
  filled, and the last day from midnight to when it ends — which is what "in court until Wednesday
  afternoon" looks like on a calendar. **All-day entries sit in a band above the hours** rather than
  at midnight, because a filing has no hour and parking it at the top of the column implied one. The week's columns are its seven
  days; the day's are the calendars you have turned on, so a Tuesday your firm spends in four
  places reads at a glance and a setting two people are at appears in both their columns. Anything
  with no time — a filing, a deadline, anything running several days — sits in the band above the
  hours. The agenda is a fortnight as a list. The view and the date live in the address.
- **Side by side, or overlapping.** On the week and the day, two things at the same hour can either
  divide their column between them, so neither hides the other, or lie over one another so each
  keeps more of its width. Choose it from the **View** menu on those two views; the choice is yours
  and follows you on this browser. Something too short to hold its own name is still drawn tall
  enough to read, so a fifteen-minute call is never a sliver.
- **Double-click an empty day, or an empty hour on the day view, to start recording an event on
  it.** The form opens with that date, and that time, already filled in. It is a shortcut and not
  the only way: **New** at the top right does the same thing and reaches the keyboard. The week begins on the day your firm chose, Sunday unless
  an administrator changed it under Settings › Calendar; it is the firm's setting rather than a
  personal one, so everybody in the firm reads the same week
  ([the administrator's guide](../admin/calendar-week-start.md)).
- **What an entry says on the firm calendar.** The kind and the matter, never the title: "Hearing,
  2026-0042". A calendar that spans every case does not broadcast what each case is about. Press
  the entry and it opens with its title, its people, its place and the zone its time is read in.
  Deadlines are the exception: they show their titles, because a deadline is read per matter you
  can see, and they say when they are past due.
- **Cancelled settings stay on the calendar, struck through.** A setting that was called off and one
  that never existed are different facts.
- **New** at the top right records an event, a deadline on a matter, or a firm event.
- **The month shows only its own weeks.** Four, five or six rows, as the month needs, sharing the
  height of the screen, so the calendar does not scroll when there is nothing more to see.
- **An empty month says nothing.** A month with nothing on it is a month with nothing on it; the
  agenda, which would otherwise be a blank space, says so in words.

**A matter's Calendar section** keeps its two tables (Deadlines, then Events) as the **List** view and
adds **Month** and **Agenda** beside them, which are the firm calendar narrowed to this one matter
and showing titles. **Record a deadline** and **Record an event** are on the section's own row.

**Two cards read the same calendar.** **Today** on Home is what is on your own calendar today and
tomorrow, six entries deep, with a line to the rest. **Coming up** on a matter's Overview is that
matter's next thirty days, six entries deep, with a line to its Calendar section. Neither is a
second calendar: press an entry on either and it opens the same window.

**Opening an entry.** Every entry opens in the same window: the record on the left, its people, its
links and its history on the right, and the verbs it allows along the bottom. Edit changes the
record in place. A scheduled setting can be **cancelled** with a reason or **continued**; an open
deadline can be **satisfied**, **vacated** with a reason, or **superseded** by a new one. A verb the
record's state or your permissions refuse stays visible and says why.

**Linked settings.** Open an event and press **Link Settings** — **Link Events** on a firm event —
to link it to another setting on the same matter (or, for a firm event, to another firm event within
six months), with a note on why they belong together. The Links tab lists them by title and opens
either; **Remove** takes a link off without touching either setting. A continuance is never linked by
hand: the record makes that link itself.

**Who is at an event.** When recording, pick a person under **Staff** (your firm's roster),
**Directory** (a client, opposing counsel, anyone your firm keeps a record of), or **Other** — type
a name once for somebody who is neither, such as a judge or a court reporter. Each can carry a
role, and the people you have added are listed under the heading they came from, so you can tell a
colleague from a contact from a name typed once. **You can change it afterwards**: open the event,
press Edit, and the Participants tab becomes the same picker. What you change there is saved with
the rest of the record, when you press Save.

## Recording an event

An event is anything that happened or will happen. There is **one form** for all of them, in four
tabs — **Details**, **When**, **Where** and **Who** — and the first line of Details asks who the
event belongs to: **a matter**, **the firm**, or **a staff member**. The field beside it is the
rest of that answer, and it stays in the same place whichever you choose.

- Choose **a matter** and the matter picker is beside it. What you record is a setting on that
  case — a hearing, a deposition, a closing. Where asks for the court reference.
- Choose **the firm** and what you record is a firm event: a meeting, a training, an office closure,
  a reservation. Beside it you say **who sees it** — everyone at the firm, or just the people you
  list on Who — and Where asks **which office** it is at, which is what tells the form which
  conference rooms are nearby. There is no court reference, because a firm meeting has no court.
- Choose **a staff member** and pick the colleague: what you record lands on that person's own
  calendar. It is a firm event with them on it, so it takes the office and the audience of one; add
  anyone else who is there on Who.

On a matter's own calendar there is nothing to choose — the matter is the one you are standing in.

Give the event a title, a date, and the time zone it happens in. Everything else is optional.

- **A time is optional, and All day says so.** Open **Starts** and choose **All day** at the top of
  the list of times, and the event is an all-day one, which is what a filing is: the times are
  cleared and **Ends** asks for a day alone. Choose a time instead and the event sits at that hour.
  A setting that runs across days, such as a three-day trial, is drawn in the all-day band at the
  top of the week and the day whatever hours it carries, spanning its days, with its hours written
  on the entry.
- **The time zone is the one the event happens in** — for a hearing, the court's. A Nevada firm
  appearing in a California court records a Los Angeles hearing, because that is where nine in the
  morning is nine in the morning. Nothing about your firm, the matter, or your office can work that
  out on its own, so you are asked.
- **The end starts out the same as the start.** Pick the start date and the end date takes the same
  day; pick a start time and the end is set an hour later. Change either to make a longer event, such
  as a three-day trial.
- **Starts and Ends are one field each**, holding the day and the time together. Type into it, or
  press the arrow at its end to open the month beside a list of times. The list steps in fifteen
  minutes, but any minute can be typed (a hearing at 9:40 shows up in the list in its place), and the
  Ends list says how long the event would run beside each time. **Time zone** is asked once beneath them, because one
  setting is read in one zone. Every zone reads the same way — "(UTC-08:00) Pacific Standard Time ·
  Los Angeles": how far it is from UTC on that day, its name, and its city — and the list runs west
  to east. Type a code, a city or a name to find it: PST, pacific and los angeles all land on the
  same row, and CST is three different rows with their cities to tell them apart.
- **Location** offers the places your firm already has and the places it deals with: your offices,
  each with its street address, then your conference rooms under the office each is in, then — once
  you have typed two letters — the organizations in your directory, so a courthouse or opposing
  counsel's firm is one pick. Type anything else and it is kept — an address, a department number,
  a place nobody has a record of — because most hearings are not at one of your offices.
- **Choosing one of your conference rooms books it, and changing it moves the booking.** The room is held for the event's own window
  and the event appears on that room's calendar, so "held here" and "this room is taken" are one
  act rather than two you have to remember. If the room cannot be held — somebody else has it, or
  the booking is refused — the form says so and names the room; the event is still recorded, and
  you can book the room from its Rooms tab. Typing a place your firm has no record of books
  nothing, which is what a courthouse should do.

  **Editing the location afterwards moves the booking too.** Move a deposition from the Boardroom
  to Conference Room B and the Boardroom is released and Conference Room B held; change the
  location to a courthouse and the room is released; change one courthouse for another and nothing
  moves. A car or a second room you booked by hand is never touched, because the location field
  never named it. If the new room cannot be held, your change to the event is still saved and the
  window says the room was not held.

- **A conference link** is its own field, for a setting held on a call: paste the Zoom, Teams or
  other meeting link. It is separate from Location on purpose, because a deposition can be in a
  conference room and on a call at the same time.
- **Meeting** schedules one of your firm's own meetings on the event: **Video meeting**, **Audio
  call**, or **None**. Choosing one creates the room as the event is saved, so everybody on the
  event sees **Join** straight away rather than waiting for somebody to press Start — and each
  colleague on it gets a notification with a link to the event. A client or another party from your
  directory is not sent a way in yet, and the form says so under the choice.

  You do not need a conference link as well: the meeting happens here. Use the link field for a
  meeting somebody else is hosting.

  **Moving the event keeps the meeting** — a meeting has no time of its own, it happens when its
  event does. **Cancelling the event closes a room nobody has joined.** If people are actually in
  the meeting, cancelling is refused until somebody ends it: a change to the calendar will not close
  a room you are sitting in. Editing an event can add a meeting where there was none; it never takes
  one away.

  The **Meetings** page has a **New Meeting** button that opens this same form, already set to a
  video meeting. See [Meetings](meetings.md).

- **Who is already busy** is shown beside each colleague you add on the Who tab: what is on their
  calendar at the time you have chosen, or nothing when they are free. It never stops you booking —
  two things at once is often exactly right — it puts the answer where you are asking the question.
- **That day's calendar stands beside the form** while you fill it in, so you can see who is already
  busy before you choose a time. It appears once you have chosen a date, and its **Calendars** menu
  turns colleagues and rooms on and off — each in its own column — so you can check one attorney's
  afternoon or one room's day without leaving the form. What you turn on there is what the calendar
  screen's own rail shows afterwards; they are one setting.
- **Repeats** turns one record into a series. Choose how often — every day, week, month or year —
  and how many of them to skip between: every week, every second week, every third. A weekly rule
  falls on the day the event starts unless you pick the days yourself, and can fall on several
  (Monday, Wednesday and Friday). A monthly rule asks which day it means: the same date every month,
  or the same weekday in the same position — the 15th, or the third Tuesday. Then say where it
  stops: never, after a number of times, or on a date. Each is its own line under **Ends**, with
  the number or the date beside it, and the line under the controls names the day the last one
  falls on — "Last one Thursday, March 4, 2027" — because twelve times is a number whose
  consequence is a date you cannot see from the number alone.

  Under the controls the form reads the rule back to you in words — "Repeats every second Tuesday,
  12 times" — and the record shows the same sentence. Check it before you save: a repeat is the one
  answer on this form whose mistake is invisible until somebody misses a setting.

  It is **one record**, not one per date. A weekly status conference is a single event with a rule
  on it, so correcting the series is one correction rather than fifty-two. A month that has no such
  date — the 31st in February — is skipped rather than moved to the 28th, because a firm that said
  the 31st did not mean the end of the month.

  An event imported from another calendar can carry a repeat rule more elaborate than this form
  offers. Where it does, the form says the rule was recorded elsewhere rather than showing you a
  simplified version that saving would make true; turning Repeats on replaces it with yours.

**Every day of a series is on the calendar.** A repeating event shows on each day it falls on,
marked with a small repeat sign, and opening any of them opens that day: the window shows the date
you pressed, not the day the series began.

**Changing or cancelling one day of a series asks how far the change reaches.** When you save a
change to a repeating event, or cancel one, the window asks:

- **This event only** — just the day you opened. The other days are untouched, and that day is from
  then on its own event, which you can open, change again or cancel like any other.
- **This and all later events** — the day you opened and every one after it. Earlier days stay as
  they were, so a status conference that moved from Tuesdays to Wednesdays in March still shows the
  Tuesdays it really was on before that. Chosen from the first day of the series, this is the same
  as all events.
- **All events in the series** — every day, including ones already past. Moving this Tuesday to
  Wednesday and choosing this moves every Tuesday to a Wednesday.

A change to how it repeats — the cadence, the days, where it stops — reaches more than one day, so
**This event only** is not offered for it.

- **The court's own case number** goes on the event as the court writes it.
- **Who is there** can be a member of your firm, a contact from your directory, or somebody who is
  neither — a judge, a court reporter, an interpreter, a witness. You are not made to create a
  directory contact for a judge you will never write to.

**When the form will not save, it says which answers it still needs.** The reason appears beside the
button, as it always has, and the field it is about is marked with the same sentence underneath it —
so you are told where to go rather than only what is wrong. Nothing is marked while you are still
filling the form in: the marks appear the moment you try to save and cannot, and they clear when the
record goes in.

**Correcting a record and rescheduling reality are different acts, and there is a verb for each.**
Typed the 13th when the hearing was always on the 14th? Edit the event — the record misdescribed
reality, and fixing it is one change with the audit trail behind it. But when the court vacates a
setting and makes a new one, **continue** the event instead: both settings happened in the world,
and the record keeps both — see [Continuing a hearing](continuing-a-hearing.md). A hearing that
comes off entirely is **cancelled**, not deleted, with an optional note saying why — that it was
vacated is itself a fact about the case.

## Recording a deadline

A deadline is a different kind of record from an event, because two things are true of it that are
not true of a hearing: it can be **satisfied**, and it **came from somewhere**.

Give it a matter, a title, a date, and a time zone. A deadline always belongs to a matter, so the
matter list on this form has no **No matter** choice; a date the whole firm keeps is a firm event.
Add a time if you know it — many filing deadlines are five in the afternoon, and a deadline whose hour
nobody wrote down is due at the end of its day, not at midnight. The time field takes only a time of
day, and one left half-typed is pointed out beneath the field rather than recorded as no time. Write down
**the authority** it comes from in your own words: the rule, the statute, the order. That note is
what you or a colleague reads six months later when deciding whether the date is right.

If another event started the clock — the date of death, the date of service, the entry of an order —
link it. The link records what the deadline hangs off. It does not calculate anything, and moving
that event does not move the deadline.

### What the platform will not do, and why

**The platform does not calculate deadlines.** It does no business-day counting, keeps no
court-holiday calendar, adds no days for the method of service, and never recalculates one date
because another moved. This is a decision, not a missing feature.

Missed deadlines are the single largest source of malpractice claims, and that fact points the
opposite way from where people expect. A deadline the software worked out and got wrong is worse than
one it never offered, because it will be trusted **precisely because** it was computed — and the
person who would have looked the rule up will not. Court rules are jurisdiction-specific, change, and
interact; a rule set that is ninety per cent right is not a help.

What the platform does instead is make every deadline **visible**: on the matter, on the firm-wide
list, and on the list of what is coming — with who recorded it and on what stated authority.

If a verified, jurisdiction-checked rule is ever encoded, it will **propose** a date and a person
will confirm it. The confirmation is what creates the record. It will never create one by itself, and
a rule that changes later will never move a date you already accepted — it will raise it for review.

### Satisfying, vacating, superseding

- **Satisfy** it when the firm has done the thing. Record the filing that discharged it if there is
  one; that is what turns "we did it" into "here is the thing we did".
- **Vacate** it when it is no longer in force — and say why. The reason is required, because "why is
  this deadline gone" is the question somebody asks much later and the only bad answer is silence.
- **Supersede** it when a new deadline replaces it. Both stay on the record and the old one names the
  new.

A settled deadline is never edited or re-decided. Record a new one that supersedes it — and if
somebody settles a deadline while you have it open for editing, your edit is refused rather than
quietly rewriting a deadline the firm has already dealt with.

## Overdue

A deadline that is still open and whose date and time have passed is shown as **overdue**, worked out
in that deadline's own time zone every time you look. Filtering a list to the overdue ones asks the
same question of every row, so a deadline due today in a Pacific court is not on that list at nine in
the morning — it is due at the end of its own day, in its own zone, not at midnight somewhere else. A
deadline with no time recorded is due at the end of the day it names.

Nothing anywhere records that your firm missed something. There is no flag, no nightly job, and no
status that changes on its own. The platform reports what it can see and does not draw a conclusion —
the deadline may have been extended by a stipulation nobody has typed in yet, met by a filing recorded
somewhere else, or vacated by an order that arrived this morning.

## Event types

Events are classified from a starting list — hearing, trial, filing, service, order entered, judgment
entered, deposition, mediation, arbitration, settlement conference, status conference, discovery
cut-off, appeal filed, notice received, plus client meeting, site visit, and call.

That list is a starting vocabulary and not a claim about how any court works. Add your own types for
whatever your practice sees, and retire the ones you never use — anything already classified under a
retired type keeps its meaning. The shipped types can be retired or regrouped but not renamed, so
"Hearing" means the same thing everywhere.

**Type is a field you type into.** Once a firm has added its own, the list runs long, so start
typing and it narrows to what matches. The list is alphabetical whatever order the types were added
in.

## Tips and edge cases

- **The firm-wide calendar asks for a date range, and it is capped at a year.** Ask for more and you
  are told so rather than quietly given less — a short answer that looks like a complete one is worse
  than an error.
- **Time and costs are on the timeline, and there are a lot of them.** They are the densest records a
  firm keeps. Filter them out when you want the shape of the case rather than every six minutes of it.
- **A timeline entry is a pointer, not a copy.** It says what happened and when, and links to the
  record itself — email subject lines, document names, narratives, and people's names stay behind that
  link and out of any list that spans several matters.
- **Reading the calendar and setting dates on it are separate permissions.** Everyone can see what is
  coming; asserting when a hearing is, is its own capability. A record control you cannot use stays
  on the screen, disabled, and says which permission it needs.
- **A month never asks for more than six weeks**, so the firm calendar never meets the one-year cap
  on its own; the cap still applies to anything reading the calendar through the API.
- **A very busy window says so.** The calendar walks each read to its end, up to a ceiling; past
  it, the screen says it is showing the first entries rather than pretending the month is complete.
