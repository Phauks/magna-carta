# The Firm's Fees

**Fees** in the sidebar shows the flat charges the firm made for its work across every matter,
most recent first. It answers the question a matter cannot: not "what has this file been charged",
which is the matter's own Fees section, but "what did the firm charge for work done this month, and
how much of it is still waiting on a bill".

## What a Fee Is

A **fee** is a flat charge for professional services, recorded by hand — a settlement drafting fee, a
fixed amount for a document, anything agreed as a number rather than metered.

**A fee is never worked out from your time.** If a matter is billed hourly, its hours still bill as
hours; a fee recorded here is a separate charge beside them. If a matter is on a flat-fee agreement,
that agreement's amount is the agreement's; a fee recorded here is something else the firm decided to
charge. The amount on a fee is what somebody decided, and nothing multiplies anything.

## Where Fees Live, in Two Places

- **Fees, in the sidebar.** This screen. Every fee across every matter. It is where the month gets
  read back and checked before billing.
- **A matter's Fees section.** That matter's own record: every fee charged on it, and where a new one
  is written down.

Both read the same fees. A fee you record on a matter appears here, because it is one record with two
doors.

## Choosing the Days

The screen opens on the whole record. To look at a span, filter the **Date** column from its own
heading: right-click the heading, choose Filter Date, and set a first day, a last day, or both. A
filter set at one end runs to the present, or back to the first fee ever recorded.

The date is **the day the work was done**, not the day somebody typed the charge in.

## Billable or Absorbed

The **Billable** column starts hidden; turn it on from any column's heading and filter it to
**Billable** for what a client can be charged for, or **Absorbed** for what the firm decided not to
charge for. Real work either way; nobody is billed for the absorbed.

The Date, Status and Billable filters are in the address, so a bookkeeper's month is a link
somebody can send, and it opens where they left it.

## Narrowing the Table

Every column carries its own filter, reached from the column's own heading — Date, Matter,
Timekeeper, Status, Billable, and the rest. Filter Matter to one file, Status to approved, Timekeeper
to one person, in any combination. The counts strip says how many rows are showing out of how many
there are, so you always know what a filter left out.

The Date, Status and Billable filters reach the record itself, so they hold across every page. A
Status filter holding two states at once narrows only the rows already loaded; pick one state to
have it hold across the whole record.

The search at the top reads the whole record too: type part of a description, a matter's name or
number, or a timekeeper's name, and the rows that answer arrive from wherever they are.

## What the Figures Mean

The two figures on the bottom edge split the money the way the process does:

- **unbilled** — billable fees no finalized bill has taken up yet.
- **billed** — fees a finalized bill has already taken up.

Both count billable fees only, and neither counts a withdrawn one: a fee the firm decided to absorb
is never asked for, and a withdrawn charge was never owed. Withdrawn fees stay on the record with
their marker and count toward neither.

When a column filter is narrowing the table, the figures cover what is in view and say so. And when
the record runs past what has loaded, the line beneath the table says the figures cover only what is
loaded — so a running subtotal is never mistaken for a month's total.

## Going to the Matter

Each row names its matter, and the name is a link: it opens that matter's own Fees section. Opening
the **fee** is a different gesture — double-click the row, or press Enter on it.

## Finding the Bill a Fee Reached

Right-click a fee and choose **Open The Bill**. It takes you to that matter's Billing section with
the bill that took the fee up already open, so you can see what the client was actually told.

A fee nothing has billed yet says so and the control stays put, rather than appearing and
disappearing as fees move through their states. The same control is on a cost and on a time entry.

## Recording a New Fee

**New Fee** at the top of the screen records one. Because this screen is not standing on a matter,
the first field is the matter: type any part of its number or its name and pick it. Nothing is
pre-filled there — a charge landing on the wrong client's bill is a thing people stop trusting a
ledger over — and the form says so until you have chosen one.

A fee names four things: the matter, the date the work was done, what the charge is for as the client
will read it, and what it comes to. It saves as a draft.

## Taking a Fee Off the Ledger

Two different acts, and the menu offers both.

- **Delete Draft** removes a fee nobody has been shown. It does not come back, and there is nothing
  to preserve: it was a typing mistake.
- **Void** withdraws a fee that has gone further than that. The fee freezes, stops counting toward
  every figure, and stays on the record struck through — because it is something you may be asked to
  account for.

Choosing the wrong one is refused and says why.

## Who Recorded It, and When

Two columns start hidden and are worth turning on when you are reconciling rather than reading:
**Entered By** and **Date Entered**. Turn them on from any column's own heading.

**Date Entered** is when the fee was written down, which is not the day the work was done — that is
**Date**. A charge captured three weeks after the fact is a different record from one captured as it
happened, and only the two dates together tell you which you are looking at.

**Entered By** is who wrote it down. It is not a permission: a fee belongs to the matter it charges,
not to whoever typed it, so anybody who can record fees can work any matter's. It is who to ask when
a figure looks wrong. A fee recorded by somebody who has since left the firm says so rather than
showing you an internal identifier — the record itself is unaffected.

A third hidden column, **Returned**, carries the reason a reviewer sent a draft back. It is empty on
nearly every row, which is why it starts hidden, and it is the only thing that tells a returned draft
from one nobody has submitted.

## What Is Not Here Yet

**A fee's entry number** and **the files attached to it** are not on the record yet, so those two
columns say what they are waiting on rather than guessing.

**A fee's history** — what happened to it after it was recorded — has no record to read yet.

**The internal memo** is on the record and is not editable here: showing you what it already holds
comes first, so that editing it cannot quietly overwrite a note you never saw.

**Fee review** — submitting a fee for approval, and a reviewer sending it back with a reason — is not
switched on for any firm: the setting that turns it on is not served yet, so nothing on these screens
asks you to submit anything. Where a fee does carry a reason it was sent back, the fee shows it.

**Moving a fee to another ledger**, and **refiling a draft onto another matter**, are designed and not
built. Each control says what it is waiting on.
