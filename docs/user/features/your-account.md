# Your account

Your name, your photo, your email address and your password — the things that are yours rather than your firm's. **Account Settings**, reached from your initials at the top right.

Account Settings opens over the app with its own stripped header: the Magna Carta mark and a **Close** button, nothing else. Close (the X, top right) returns you to where you came from.

## What it does

Three pages, listed down the left:

- **Profile** — your name, your photo, and the email address your account uses.
- **Security** — your password, and two-factor sign-in when it arrives.
- **Active Sessions** — every device signed in as you. Its own guide: [Signed-in devices](signed-in-devices.md).

## Common tasks

**Set your name**

Profile holds your name in five parts — prefix, first, middle, last, suffix — and shows you the one line they read as, under **You will appear as**. That line is what letterhead, a signature block and every generated document print, and it is what your colleagues are listed under.

The parts are collected separately because a single line cannot be sorted by surname or used to address a letter, and cannot be split back into parts afterwards without guessing. You do not type the line itself; it is composed from what you enter.

**Prefix** and **Suffix** each hold as many as you carry: "III, Esq.", "M.D., Ph.D.", "Hon., Dr." Pick from the list and each one is added to what is already there; pick one you already hold and it comes back off. You can also just type the whole line, commas and all — the list is a shortcut, never a limit, so a title the list has never heard of is still yours to enter.

Press **Save** when the line reads right. **Discard** puts back what was there.

If your account was created with a single name and you have never set the parts, the form offers a reading of that name — first word given, last word family — and says so. Nothing is stored until you press Save, so check it or correct it first.

**Add or change your photo**

On Profile, under **Photo**, choose a PNG or a JPEG up to 2 MB. It is set as soon as you choose it — there is no separate save — and **Remove** takes it off again. Until you set one, your initials stand in.

Your photo is your firm's: it is stored with your firm's files, and it is what colleagues see beside your work here.

**Change your password**

On **Security**, next to **Password**, select **Change**. You will be asked for the password you have now and the one you want, at least 8 characters. There is no emailed link and no waiting.

**Sign out my other devices** is ticked by default, and on the day it matters it is the point of the whole thing: if you think somebody else has your password, changing it and ending their session have to happen together, or they can simply change it back. You stay signed in where you are.

If you have never set a password — your account was activated from an emailed link — use **Forgot Password** on the sign-in screen first, and this will change it afterwards.

**See which email address your account uses**

Profile, under **Identity**. It is the address you sign in with and the one notices are sent to.

## Tips and edge cases

- **Your email address cannot be changed here, and that is deliberate.** It is not just a contact detail: it is how you sign in, and it is what any invitation or registration still waiting for you is addressed to. Changing it safely means proving you hold the new mailbox and deciding what happens to anything addressed to the old one, which is a piece of work rather than a setting. If yours is genuinely wrong, ask your firm's administrator.
- **Your photo and your name are not the same kind of thing.** Your name follows your account; your photo belongs to the firm you are a member of.
- **A wrong current password is told to you as such.** If the change is refused because the current password was wrong, the message says so rather than leaving you retyping a new password that was never the problem.
- **Two-factor sign-in is marked _Soon_** on Security and does nothing yet. It is the only thing on this surface that is still a placeholder.
- **A photo that will not upload** is usually the wrong format or too large — PNG and JPEG only, 2 MB at most. Both are refused before the upload starts, so you are told straight away.
