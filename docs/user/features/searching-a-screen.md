# Searching The Screen You Are On

Every screen under **Workspace** has a search in the top right of its title row. It rests as a small
magnifying glass and costs the screen no space until you press it; press it and a field opens beside
it, ready to type in.

This is the search for **what is on the screen you are already looking at** — the matters in your
docket, the people in your directory, the checks in your conflicts log. It is a different thing from
the search box in the top bar, which takes you to other parts of the application.

## Using It

Press the glass and type. What happens next depends on the screen, and you do not have to think about
which: screens that already hold everything they are showing you narrow as you type, and screens that
have to ask the server wait until you stop typing, so one question goes out rather than one per
keystroke.

While a search is live the field stays open with a faint line down its edge, so a shortened list
always carries the reason it is short. Press the **✕** at the end of the field to clear it, or press
**Escape**. Escape a second time closes the field and puts you back on the glass.

Clicking anywhere else on the page closes an empty field. A field with something in it stays open —
that is deliberate, so a narrowed list never looks like the whole list.

On a screen that also has column filters, the search and the filters narrow together, and the
**Clear** control beside them puts everything back.

## What Each Screen's Search Looks At

Mostly this is what you would expect, and you can stop reading here. Three are worth knowing.

**Magnus** searches what you actually **said** in a conversation, not just its name. Nothing gives a
conversation a name unless you do, so searching the words you typed is usually the only way back to
one. It searches your own conversations.

**Conflicts Check** searches the matter a check was run against — by number or by name — and the
**names the check surfaced**. So "has this firm ever run a check that turned up this person" is one
search, rather than opening every check in the log. Whether a check was automatic, who asked for it
and how strict it was are narrowed from the column headings instead, which match those exactly.

**The Calendar searches the dates you are currently looking at, and nothing else.** If you are on
September, it searches September. It tells you so under the calendar — how many entries in that month
or week match, and that other dates were not searched — so a search that finds nothing means "nothing
here", not "nothing anywhere". Move to another month and search again, or use the agenda view to
cover a longer stretch at once.

## Tips

- A search is not case-sensitive, and matches part of a word: `hend` finds Henderson.
- Punctuation you type is matched as punctuation. `50%` looks for `50%`, and does not behave like a
  wildcard.
- Searching a person's name finds them whether or not you get the apostrophe right: `O'Brien` and
  `OBrien` reach the same records.
- On **Notifications**, search the words you see on the cards — "overdue", "invoice", "trust" — or a
  matter number. Your unread count is never changed by a search; it always counts everything waiting
  for you.
- **Home** has no search of its own. It is a summary of your other screens, so searching from there
  is the top bar's job, and that goes anywhere in the application.
