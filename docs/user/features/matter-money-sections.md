# A matter's money sections

_Time, Fees, Costs, Billing and Trust are in daily use._

## What it does

Everything a matter has moved or owes lives under **Money** in the row of sections beneath the
matter's title: **Time**, **Fees**, **Costs**, **Billing** and **Trust**. The five screens are laid
out the same way, so what you learn on one is true on all of them.

- The **row at the top** names the section you are on and lists the other four. Press one to step
  across; nothing else on the screen changes shape.
- The **table** starts directly under that row and fills the rest of the screen. Sort by clicking a
  column heading, and right-click a heading to filter, hide or pin the column.
- The **counts strip** on the bottom edge says how many records there are, how many are selected,
  and the section's totals. The control at its far end opens the details panel beside the table.

## Moving between the money sections

Use the row at the top. The section you are on is shown in the stronger weight. The **Money** menu in
the row of sections beneath the matter's title reaches the same five places.

## Leaving the matter for the firm's whole record

Four of the five money sections have a firm-wide twin, and the end of that same row is the way to
it: **All time**, **All fees**, **All costs**, **All billing**, **All trust**, past a short rule so
it never reads as a sixth section. It takes you out of the matter to the same money at the firm's
scale — every matter's hours for a week, every matter's spending for a month, every client's bills,
the firm's trust accounts — which is the question a matter's own section cannot answer.

## Clearing filters

When a column filter is narrowing the table, **Clear Filters** appears on the top row, and the counts
strip says how many records are showing out of how many there are. Press either to see everything
again. When nothing is filtered, the control is absent: that is how you know nothing is hidden.

## Comfortable or compact rows

The rows control on the top row switches the table between comfortable and compact rows. The choice
is remembered on this device and applies to all five money sections.

## The framed table

The table runs to the edges of the screen, as Files does. If you prefer it framed inside the page,
press the frame control on the top row; press it again to run it back to the edges. Like the row
density, the choice is remembered on this device and applies to all five money sections at once.

## Recording something

The **New** control at the top of the matter records into the section you are on: New Time, New
Fee, New Cost, New Bill, New Deposit. Where a section cannot record yet, the control says what it is
waiting for.

## Fees

A **fee** is a flat charge for professional services: a settlement drafting fee, a fixed amount for a
document, anything you agreed as a number rather than metered. **New Fee** records one with its date,
who it is for, what it covers and what it comes to. It saves as a draft, and a draft bill takes it up
the same way it takes up a cost.

**A fee is never worked out from your time.** The amount is what you decided to charge. On a matter
billed hourly your hours still bill as hours, and a fee recorded here is a separate charge beside
them.

The section shows every fee on the matter in every status, with two figures on the counts strip:
what is still **unbilled**, and what has been **billed**. Both count billable fees only and neither
counts a withdrawn one — a fee you decided to absorb is never asked for, and a withdrawn charge was
never owed. A billed fee opens the bill it was charged on, the same way a billed cost does.

### Deleting a draft is not voiding a fee

These are two different acts and the menu offers both.

- **Delete Draft** removes a fee nobody has been shown. It does not come back, and there is nothing
  to preserve: it was a typing mistake.
- **Void** withdraws a fee that has gone further than that. The fee freezes, stops counting toward
  every figure, and stays on the record struck through — because it is something you may be asked to
  account for.

Choosing the wrong one is refused and says why.

## Which bills a matter's Billing section shows

The bills that charge **this matter**, drafts included — not every bill the client has ever been
given. A bill belongs to a client and may charge several of that client's matters, so the money
column reads **This Matter**: what this file was charged on that bill, which is smaller than the
document's total whenever the bill covers more than one of the client's matters. The document's own
total is a column you can turn on from the same heading menu as any other, and it is named
**Total (all matters)** so the two can never be confused.

A draft has no stored total yet — one is drawn when the bill is finalized — so a draft's figure is
the sum of the lines it is carrying and moves as work is claimed onto it. The cell says so when you
hover it.

## Opening a bill from somewhere else

A bill opened here puts itself in the address, so you can send somebody the bill rather than the
register. That is also how a billed record reaches its bill: right-click a billed cost or a billed
time entry and choose **Open The Bill**, and it lands here with that bill already open.

The control is on every cost and every time entry, not only the billed ones — one that came and went
with a row's state would be a control you could never learn the place of. A record nothing has billed
says so.

## Trust

The Trust section shows the matter's trust ledger: every deposit into it, every payment out of it,
and every transfer between this client's matters, with who recorded and who authorized each one.
The counts strip carries the balance, the amount under hold, and what is available.

A matter's ledger lives in one of the firm's trust accounts. With one open account the section
reads it; with several, the account control above the ledger picks the one to read, and the choice
is remembered on this device. A matter that has never received money into that account has no
ledger there yet, and the section says so: the ledger is created by the first deposit.

### Recording money in

**New Deposit** at the top of the matter records money received into this matter's ledger: the
amount, the day it reached the account, how it arrived, who it came from, and why. It lands in
full; a bank or processor fee is never taken out of it.

### Paying money out

**Pay Out** on the top row proposes a payment out of the ledger to a payee. What happens next is
your firm's policy ([trust money-out approvals](../admin/trust-disbursement-approvals.md)): if you
hold the authority to release client money and the amount needs no second person, the payment is
made there and then; otherwise nothing moves, the proposal waits, and the section names who can
approve it. A line above the ledger says how many payments are waiting; press it and the details
panel opens on them, each with its amount, payee and purpose. The person who raised one can
**Withdraw** it; anybody else who may decide it can **Approve** or **Reject** it, and a rejection
asks for a reason the proposer can answer. A payment can never take
more than what is available, which is the balance less anything under hold.

### Moving money between the client's matters

**Transfer** moves money from this matter's ledger to another matter of the same client, inside the
same trust account. No money leaves the bank, so nothing appears on a statement; the transfer shows
on both matters' ledgers. A matter belonging to a different client is refused.

Nothing on the ledger is edited or deleted. A movement that reached the ledger is a fact the firm
answers for, and a mistake is put right by a further movement.
