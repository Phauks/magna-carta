# Signing in

Signing in is how you get into Magna Carta. It asks for your email address, then your password, and — if your account uses an authenticator app — a six-digit code.

## What it does

The sign-in screen takes you through up to three short steps, one at a time:

1. **Your email address.** The address your firm set your account up with.
2. **Your password.** The screen shows the address you entered above the password box, so a typo is easy to spot before you try it.
3. **Your authentication code**, if your account has an authenticator app set up. This is the six-digit code your authenticator shows, which changes every 30 seconds.

Two tick boxes decide how much you will be asked next time:

- **Keep me signed in on this device** (on the password step, ticked by default) keeps you signed in on this computer between visits instead of ending your session when you close the browser. Leave it unticked on a shared or public computer.
- **Trust this device for 30 days** (on the code step) skips the authenticator code on this computer for the next 30 days. Your password is still required every time. Only tick it on a device that is yours alone.

**There is no sign-up.** Accounts are created for you by your firm's administrator — there is no way to register yourself, and no link to try.

## Common tasks

**Sign in**

1. Enter your email address and select **Continue**.
2. Enter your password and select **Sign in**.
3. If you are asked for a code, open your authenticator app, type the six digits it is showing, and select **Verify**.

You can paste a code instead of typing it — paste into the first box and all six fill at once. On a phone, the code your authenticator or messages app offers will fill the whole field in one tap.

**Correct an email address you mistyped**

On the password step, select **Use a different email**. You will be taken back to the first step with the address ready to edit.

**Show your password as you type it**

Select the eye button inside the password box. Select it again to hide the password. Use this if a long or complex password is not being accepted and you want to check what you have typed.

**Sign in when you cannot reach your authenticator**

Select **Use a recovery code** on the code step. Recovery codes are the one-time codes you were given when you set up your authenticator; each one works once. If you have neither your authenticator nor a recovery code, contact your firm's administrator.

**Reset a forgotten password**

Select **Forgot password?** on the password step, or ask your firm's administrator to send you a reset link.

## Tips and edge cases

- **"That email and password combination doesn't match our records."** This one message covers every reason a sign-in was refused — a wrong password, a mistyped address, or an account that is not active yet. It is deliberately not more specific: a message that said "no such account" would let anyone test which addresses belong to your firm. Check both the address and the password, and contact your administrator if it keeps happening.
- **"That code didn't verify."** Authenticator codes expire every 30 seconds. Wait for your app to show a fresh code and enter that one. If codes are consistently rejected, your device's clock may have drifted — check that its time is set automatically.
- **"Too many attempts."** Repeated tries are slowed down deliberately. Wait a minute and try again.
- **The web address never changes** as you move between the steps. That is on purpose: it keeps the fact that your account uses an authenticator out of your browser history and off the screen.
- **Refreshing the page starts over** from the email step. Nothing you typed is kept, which is what you want on a shared screen.
- **After you sign in** you land on your home page — or, if you followed a link into Magna Carta before signing in, on the page you were trying to reach.

## Related features

- Your account's password and authenticator settings live under **Account → Security**.
- Administrators invite new users and set their access under **Settings → Users**.
