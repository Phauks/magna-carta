# The Directory

The Directory is the firm's one book of the people and organizations it deals with: clients,
opposing parties, counsel, courts, experts, vendors. Every matter's client, every party on a matter,
every payee on a cost and every subject of a conflicts check is a record here, so a person recorded
once serves every file they appear on.

## Finding a record

Open **Directory** from the sidebar. The list shows everyone the firm holds, people and organizations
together, in name order, with the count beside it. Each row carries the record's kind, the email
address and telephone number a message would go to, the city it is in, and when it was added.

Every column's own heading is where you narrow and sort: Kind holds people, organizations or both;
City and Region pick out who is local; any column sorts. The heading menu also offers columns the
list does not show — first, middle and last name, an organization's legal name, the country, and when
the record last changed — so the book can be sorted by surname or scanned by country on the day you
need that, and stays a list of names on the day you do not. When something is narrowing the list,
**Clear Filters** appears at the top of the screen to put it back.

The search finds a record by any name it has ever held: a maiden name, a former trading name or a
typo still finds the right record, and the results say which name matched.

A field with something wrong in it turns red rather than printing a message under itself, so the
form never shifts while you type; hover over the field to read what is wrong.

Press **New** to record a person or an organization. A person is recorded whole, on one form that
scrolls: their names, the ways to reach them, an address, an About section for their pronouns and
dates, sealed identifiers and the organizations they belong to, one under another. The parts most people leave empty (an address, an identifier, an organization) start as an **Add** button and open their fields when pressed. Press **Tab**
to move through every field in order. Each section is a card with its name and how many entries it
holds at the top; press that header to fold the card away, and press it again to open it. The list
down the left side jumps to a section (opening it if it was folded), shows how many entries each one
holds, and highlights the section you are in. On the right, as you type
the name, the Directory shows records that may already be the same person, with a way to open each
beside your draft, so the same client does not end up in the book twice under two spellings. Below
that, the record is read back as it will read, with the email address and the address themselves,
so you can check it before you save. Everything you entered is recorded together when you press
Save, or not at all.

## A record

A record opens in a window over whatever you were doing, and it looks the way it did when it was
recorded: one page that scrolls, with every section of the record one under another in cards that fold
from their headers, and the list down the left side jumping between them. A person's pronouns and dates of birth and death are
under their name at the top. A long list, such as the matters a record is on, shows its first few
lines and a **Show All** button. Close the window and you are back exactly where you
were, with nothing to retrace. It is the same window from the Directory's list, from a party on a
matter, from the client on a matter's overview and from a conflicts check, so a record has one shape
everywhere. Opened from a matter, a column on the right lists the roles this record holds on that
matter.

A record has an address you can paste into a message. Following it opens the Directory with that
record's window already open. The **More** button (the three dots at the top right of the window)
holds what you can do with the record as a whole:

- **Copy Link** copies the record's address, ready to paste.
- **Email Link** starts a message in your mail program with the record's name and address in it.
- **Download** saves the record as a file, in one of three formats:
  - **PDF**, a printout of the record: its names, dates, every way to reach the person (with the
    ones they asked the firm not to use marked), addresses, relationships and matters. Sealed
    identifiers are never on it. Making a PDF needs the permission to export reports.
  - **Plain Text**, the same printout as a `.txt` file that pastes into an email.
  - **Contact Card (.vcf)**, a file that phones, Outlook and other address books open. It carries
    the name, a person's date of birth, and the email addresses, numbers and postal addresses the
    person allows the firm to use. A contact point marked as former, or one the person asked the
    firm not to use, is left off, and so are identifiers, matters and relationships, because a
    contact card is meant to be handed on. If the record has a picture, the card carries it too.

A record can have a picture: a person's photograph or an organization's logo. It sits in the circle
beside the name at the top of the window, and the record's initials stand in until there is one.

- **Add a picture**: press the circle (it shows a small camera while it is empty), choose a PNG or
  JPEG, and drag the picture, or use the arrow keys, until the face sits inside the ring. **Zoom In**
  and **Zoom Out** enlarge it. Only the square you see is saved, so the rest of the photograph never
  reaches the firm's storage.
- **Change, view or remove it**: press the picture. **Change Photo** opens the same cropping window,
  **View Full Size** opens the picture in a new tab, and **Remove Photo** takes it off the record.
- When you record a new person, **Add Photo** above the name fields does the same before the person
  is saved. If the person is saved but the picture is not, a message says so, and you can add it
  from their record.

Adding, changing and removing a picture needs the permission to edit the Directory. Somebody who
may only read it sees the picture and can open it full size.

A record answers the questions the firm keeps an answer to:

- **Names**: every name the record has held, with the current one marked. Recording a marriage or a
  rename keeps the old name findable. A person's name is a first name, a middle name and a last name,
  with a prefix and any number of suffixes chosen from a list you can add to — somebody who is both
  Jr. and Esq. is recorded as both. Only the first name is required: a person who has one name is
  recorded under it, with a note on screen saying that is what the firm will file and search them
  under. When you record a new person, **Add Other Name** opens a second full set of name fields
  (prefix, first, middle and last name, suffixes) for a maiden name or an alias, with its kind beside
  it; add as many as you need, and one left empty is ignored.
- **Contact**: one line per way to reach them. Choose the kind first — Email, Telephone or Fax — and
  the fields follow: an email address is checked as you type; a number takes its country from the
  dial code and is formatted as you type it, so a Chicago number reads as (312) 555-0148 and a London
  one as +44 20 7946 0000. Each line has a **Type** (work, home, mobile and so on), and shows whether the person asked the firm not
  to use it, and which one they prefer. A number that turned out to be disconnected, or mail that
  came back, is recorded as failed rather than deleted, so the next person does not ring it again.
  A value entered wrongly is corrected in place: press the pencil on its line and it comes back into
  the fields it was entered in, so a mistyped digit does not cost you the whole line and everything
  recorded against it.
- **Address**: the postal addresses the firm holds, entered in the shape the country actually uses.
  Press **Add Address** to open the form. The country is the last field and starts as the United
  States; choosing another re-arranges the boxes above it — an Irish address asks for a county
  and an Eircode, a German one puts the postal code before the town, a Japanese one starts with the
  postal code and reads down to the street, and none of them is asked for a state that does not
  exist. Nothing is required: an address the firm holds half of is still worth recording.
  In the United States, typing the ZIP code first fills in the city and the state, leaving anything
  you have already typed in those boxes as it is.
  Where address suggestions are available, the street box offers real addresses as you type, and
  choosing one fills in the town, the state or province and the postal code while keeping any
  apartment or suite you had already typed. A building with several units is offered once, with how
  many addresses it holds, and choosing it lists the units. Addresses in the state you have already
  chosen — or, before you choose one, the state your office is in — are offered first; the rest of
  the country is still offered. Depending on how your platform is set up, the suggestions come either
  from Google Maps, which then shows its name under the list, or, in the United States, from public
  address records published by states, counties and cities, where a very new street, or the unit
  number in some buildings, may not be there yet. The offers are only a shortcut: what you type is what is kept,
  whether or not you choose one. If a postal code does not look like one for
  the chosen country, its box turns red (hover over it to read why) and the address saves anyway,
  and the state or province box offers the country's list without refusing a name that is not on
  it. Each address has a **Type**, such as business or residential. Addresses are corrected in
  place, the same way.
- **About**, on a person: the pronouns they stated, and their dates of birth and death.
- **Identifiers**: sealed values such as a social security number, a driver's licence, a bar
  number or an account. The type decides what is asked for: nine digits for a social security
  number, the issuing state and the number for a licence, and for an account who it is held with,
  the number, and the username and secret that go with it. The list names what is held and what it
  is of, and shows bullets for the value; revealing one needs a separate permission and is written
  to the firm's audit log.
- **Duplicates**: records the Directory suspects are the same, with a way to fold one into the other.
  Folding keeps everything from both records and never deletes either. Opening a suspected
  duplicate, or a related record, shows it in the same window in place of the one you were reading.
- **Relationships**: how this record connects to others. On an organization, **People** lists its
  officers, directors, partners, employees, members, shareholders and points of contact; on a person,
  **Organizations** lists the organizations they belong to. Recording one is choosing the other
  record from the Directory and the kind of relationship, with an optional role and note.
- **Matters**: every matter this record is a party to, and in what role.
- **Categories** (organizations only): the firm's own labels, such as court, expert firm or vendor.
  Assign one from the list, or type a new one and it is created and assigned in one step.

## Who can change the Directory

Reading needs only access to the firm. Adding a record, changing names, contact points,
relationships and categories needs the Directory edit permission. Revealing a sealed identifier needs
its own permission and is always logged.
