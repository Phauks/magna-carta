# Calls and texts

_You can record calls and texts that happened, read them across the firm or on one matter, file one
onto a matter, and take one back off. The firm has no phone number connected yet, so nothing is sent
or received from Magna Carta — every row is written down by whoever made or took the call. Call
recording and transcripts are not built._

## What it does

Keeps the firm's record of what was said on the phone and by text message. A case file remembers every
document and every email by itself; the phone call where the client actually decided something lives
only in somebody's memory of it. **Messaging** is where that stops being true.

Two ideas are worth knowing before you use it.

**The log is the point, not the sending.** Right now you write down what happened after it happens.
When the firm's number is connected, the carrier will write these same rows by itself — the same
screen, the same columns, the same matters. Nothing you record now is throwaway.

**A conversation does not need a numbered matter.** A first phone call from somebody who is not a
client yet is the most important call the firm takes, and there is no file to put it on. So a call or
text can sit on an **intake** or a **consultation** — neither of which has a matter number — or on no
matter at all, waiting in a queue for somebody to say where it belongs.

Email is not here. Mail lives in your own mailbox and is filed onto a matter from there; see
[`filing-email-to-a-matter.md`](filing-email-to-a-matter.md).

## Recording a call

1. Open **Messaging** in the sidebar and press **New Call**, at the top of the screen beside the
   search. Inside a matter, the same button is at the top of the matter screen and fills the matter
   in for you.
2. Choose the **kind** — a call, a text, or a picture message — and **which way** it went.
3. Type the **number** at the other end. Type it the way you dialled it; the firm's own format is
   whatever you use.
4. Say **when** it happened. This defaults to now, which is right for a call you have just put down,
   and you can change it: a call on Tuesday written up on Thursday belongs on Tuesday, and every list
   puts it there.
5. For a call, give the **length in minutes**, or **what was said**, or both. A call with neither is
   refused — if you have nothing to record, there is nothing to save. A call with only a length is
   fine: that is a missed call, and it is a real record.
6. For a text, type **the message**. You can add your own note about it as well.
7. Choose a **matter** if you know it. Every matter the firm has is offered, including intakes and
   consultations. Leave it empty and the row waits in the queue.

## The queue: calls and texts nobody has filed

A row with no matter sits under **Waiting to be filed**. This is where a text from a number nobody
recognises lands, and where a call goes when you record it before you know which case it is about.

Click any row in the queue to file it. You will be asked for the matter, and again, an intake or a
consultation counts.

Whether you see the queue at all depends on your role. Your firm decides who works it, the same way
it decides who opens the post: by default the owner, administrators and paralegals see it, and other
seats do not. What is in the queue has not been sorted yet, so it can concern any case in the firm,
including ones you would not otherwise see.

## Reading the log

**Across the firm**, Messaging shows every call and text on every matter you can see, plus the queue
if your role includes it. Search matches what was said **and the number** — most people look for a
text by the number they dialled, so that works.

**On one matter**, the Messaging section shows only that matter's calls and texts, and drops the
matter column, since every row is on the same one. The matter's own search finds them too, and each
one appears on the matter's activity in date order.

## Taking something off the wrong matter

Filed a call onto the wrong case? Take it off. The row goes **back into the queue**, not into the
bin: what was said still happened, and it has to land somewhere you will see it again. Then file it
where it belongs.

## What is not built yet

- **Sending and receiving.** Nothing leaves or arrives through Magna Carta. Connecting the firm's
  number needs a carrier account and a registration that takes weeks, and that is a separate step.
- **Call recording and transcripts.** Recording a call is not simply a feature to switch on: about a
  dozen US states require every person on the call to consent, and Nevada is not one of them. The
  machinery that knows which rule applies where is still being designed, and recording will not ship
  before it.
- **Linking a number to a person in your directory.** For now a call names the number. When the
  carrier connects, an incoming number will be matched to your directory automatically.
- **Text messages to and from the client portal.** That is a different feature and lives elsewhere.
