# Continuing a hearing

_Continuing is a verb in the window a setting opens in, on the firm calendar and on the matter's
Calendar section._

## What it does

When a court vacates a setting and makes a new one, two things happened in the world — a scheduling
order was entered, a setting will not occur, a new date now stands — and the record should show
both. **Continuing** an event does exactly that:

- **The old setting stays, untouched.** Its date, its time, who was appearing — everything is kept
  exactly as it was. It is marked cancelled (true: that setting will not occur) with your reason:
  "Continued on defendant's motion."
- **A new setting is recorded** with the date and time the court gave, the same title and type, and
  the participants carried over — the same people are expected to appear, and you edit the list if
  not.
- **The two are linked**, so from either setting you can see the whole history: continued from,
  continued to, all the way down the chain.

A hearing continued three times reads as four settings in order — dates, statuses, and reasons —
which is the procedural history you actually need: what was vacated when, and why, and what stands
now.

## Continuing is not correcting

If somebody typed the 13th when the hearing was always on the 14th, do not continue — **edit** the
event. The record misdescribed reality and the honest fix is a correction, kept in the audit trail.
Continuing is for when reality itself moved: the court took one setting off and made another. The
platform offers both verbs because only you know which one happened.

On the calendar, the vacated setting shows as cancelled — struck through, filtered out of "what is
coming" — while the case's history shows every setting in order. Nothing is hidden and nothing is
rewritten.

## What continuing does not do

**It never moves a deadline. Not one, not ever.**

When you continue a setting, the response lists the open deadlines that were linked to it — the
opposition due before the vacated hearing, the exhibits exchange keyed to it — so you can look at
each one while the order is in your hand. That list is information, not action. Whether a continued
hearing moves a stipulated briefing schedule is a question of the order, the rules, and sometimes a
call to chambers — a legal judgement the platform is not entitled to make, and a wrongly-moved date
would be trusted precisely because the software moved it. You decide what each deadline does now,
and you record it.

Two other things it does for you, honestly:

- **Rooms come free.** Any room or vehicle booked for the vacated setting is released the moment
  the continuance is recorded — a booking only holds a resource while its event is live. The
  response lists what was released, so you can re-book for the new date if the room is yours to
  book.
- **The chain stays a chain.** A setting can be continued once. If a colleague continued it while
  you were typing, yours is refused and you continue the latest setting instead — so two versions
  of the same hearing can never fork.

## Tips and edge cases

- **Only case events continue.** A moved staff meeting or firm training is just edited — nobody
  litigates the all-hands.
- **A cancelled setting cannot be continued.** Continue the live setting at the end of the chain.
- **The zone can move too.** If the court transferred the matter, give the new setting its new
  court's time zone.
- **Related events can be linked without any of this machinery** — a prep session to its
  deposition, say — with a plain link and a note. Those links are annotations: add and remove them
  freely. A continuance link is history, and cannot be removed.
