# Now — the build register

Every capability in the program, with its real status, and the arithmetic over the whole set. This
is the complete register rather than a digest: all 826 nodes of the feature ledger in
[`roadmap.d/`](../roadmap.d/) are listed below, at the status the ledger actually holds, with
nothing collapsed and nothing curated away.

**Assembled, never hand-edited.** Run `vp run register-build` after changing a ledger node.

**Where we are, and where we have been.** The ledger records only each capability's current status,
but it is version controlled, so its counts at every past commit are read back in
[Where we have been](#where-we-have-been) below rather than estimated — and over the same quantity
the standing above counts: **units of work**, with roll-up parents excluded at every past commit
exactly as they are excluded here. Two limits hold: a snapshot
shows when a node's **status** changed and never when the work happened, and a capability added
to the ledger late arrives as a jump in the total rather than as work done.
[`CHANGELOG.md`](../CHANGELOG.md) is still the dated record of what shipped and when.
Slice-by-slice build stages live in [`STATUS.md`](../STATUS.md), the narrative of what each stream
is doing lives in [`ROADMAP.md`](../ROADMAP.md), and what each of the nine statuses means lives in
[`roadmap.d/README.md`](../roadmap.d/README.md).

### What a roll-up is not

**143 of the 826 nodes are parents, and 111 of those parents are `partial`.** A parent's status is a
summary of its children, so a parent goes `partial` the moment its children stop agreeing — which
means counting parents beside leaves does not add work, it adds the same work a second time under a
status nobody chose. The distortion is not spread evenly either: it lands almost entirely on
`partial`, the one status the reader is most likely to mistake for progress. So every share above is
taken over units of work. The parents are still in the register and still carry their status; they
are not counted as one more thing built.

### What `partial` hides

**Shipped alone is 317 of 683 (46%). Shipped plus partial is 442 (65%).**

The 125 units between those two numbers are work that is engineered and paid for and is not
finished. Reading the larger number as how much of the product exists overstates it; reading the
smaller one understates what has been built. Both are true and neither is the answer on its own.
Which of the two a reader wants is usually really a question about reach — whether anybody can get
to the thing — and that is [Built, and can anybody reach it](#built-and-can-anybody-reach-it) below,
which reads a fact the status field does not hold.

### Built, and can anybody reach it

**442 units of work have something built behind them** — `shipped`, `partial` or `in-build`. Whether
a person can reach any of them is a different fact from how complete it is, and `status` has never
held it: a backend nobody can open and a screen somebody uses every day are both `shipped`. The
ledger records it separately, in `surface:`.

| Reach | Units | Share of what is built |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| A person can open it today (`screen`) | 104 | 24% |
| Built — the screen is the only thing missing (`awaiting-screen`) | 157 | 35% |
| No user surface by nature (`internal`) | 181 | 41% |
| Not yet classified | 0 | 0% |
| **Everything with something built** | **442** | **100%** |

`awaiting-screen` is the program's interface debt stated directly rather than inferred from
`partial`: capabilities that work, that are paid for, and that nobody can get to. `internal` is not
debt — a port, a table or a fitness function is finished without a screen and will never have one.

### What has moved

Every figure in this section counts **units of work**, the same quantity [Where the program
stands](#where-the-program-stands) counts, so a level here is comparable to a level there. Roll-up
parents are excluded at every past commit the same way they are excluded now — the reconstruction
reads the hierarchy off the ledger as it stood, not off today's. The whole-node totals are on the
record too: 780 nodes on 2026-09-02, 799 on 2026-09-07.

**+17 shipped since the ledger began** — 276 on 2026-09-02, 293 on 2026-09-07, across 5 days. The
ledger itself grew by +17 units over the same period (642 to 659), so the share shipped went from
43% to 44% against a denominator that was moving too.

That is **23.8 capabilities shipped a week** across the whole series.

**No completion date is derived from any of this, and none can be.** The ledger puts no size or
effort on a node, so a date extrapolated from these counts would treat a jurisdiction research
corpus and a second authentication factor as the same amount of work. A rate is a rate.

## Waiting on you

**29 answers would let work start.** Nothing is built behind these yet, and nothing can be until they land.

- **Client-consent model** (`ai.consent`) — **Not built.** Whether client consent for AI is taken once in the engagement letter or per matter. No AI feature reaches a client's data until this is settled.
- **Feedback board** (`community.board`) — **Not built.** Two calls, both on the open pull request. Whether to ratify a named exception to the firm-scoped data rule, since a shared board is cross-firm by definition; and how a signed-in member's session reaches the community host, which is a different origin from the application.
- **A supplied asset-schedule template** (`matter-assets.schedule-template`) — **Not built.** Two questions. Whether shipped starter content rides the court-form adoption path designed in docs/specs/2026-08-14-nevada-court-form-library-design.md once its N1-N5 flags clear, or wants a distribution path that does not wait on them — an asset schedule is not a court form and carries none of that design's copyright or attorney-review weight. And what an estate asset schedule should actually say: heading, grouping, columns, whether unvalued assets are listed or counted, and what the document calls itself. Every firm provisioned after it ships inherits the answer, and neither the design nor ADR-0255's A-flags specify any of it.
- **Semantic search** (`discovery.search.semantic`) — **Not built.** Whether the semantic half is built for review sets at all, and if so on what trigger. It is DEFERRED rather than undecided-by-neglect — the cost side is now priced (a queried Vectorize index bills the resident corpus once a month at twenty times the storage rate, so a 250,000-document review set is roughly 490 times the keyword half's marginal cost, plus a one-time embedding charge of $261 to $522 per review set), and the recall side has never been measured on legal prose with relevance judgements, which is the only thing that would justify it. The experiment that settles it is specified in full in docs/research/2026-08-14-semantic-half-convergence-and-cost.md and is blocked on acquiring a judged corpus, not on building anything. Until it is answered the per-review-set flag stays a column nothing reads: turning it on would embed a corpus on a guess, and the embedding model pin is unchangeable once one vector exists.
- **Numbering schemes** (`exhibits.numbering`) — **Not built.** Which letter form follows Z — doubled (AA, BB, CC) or bijective (AA, AB, AC). The surveyed trial-practice guidance says double letters and that is the recommendation, but a label goes into filed documents and is frozen onto the member row at issue, so changing the form after the first set has gone out is a migration over records nobody may rewrite. Beside it and unsettled: which starter presets ship, if any — the two candidates read from primary text are numbers-for-plaintiff with letters-for-defendant, and plaintiff-from-1 with defendant-from-500, and both are current practice in the same courthouse, so shipping both means shipping two conflicting readings of one court. Exhibits design flags E1 and E4.
- **In-text exhibit references** (`exhibits.references`) — **Not built.** Whether the platform offers to detect the plain-text "Exhibit C" references already sitting in a firm's existing documents and propose bindings for them. It is genuinely useful on a firm's back catalogue and it is a scan over privileged prose, so the recommendation is yes, proposal-only, never applied without a person accepting it one at a time, and only inside a document a member already has open. A no means the feature works only for references authored through the picker, and a firm's existing prose stays unbound forever. Exhibits design flag E8.
- **Issued-set freeze** (`exhibits.issue`) — **Not built.** Two questions about how strict an issued set is, and both decide a build. Whether issuing is truly irreversible, with a superseding set as the only remedy — the recommendation — or whether an issued-in-error reversal exists, which is a second way for a frozen label to become unfrozen and therefore a second way the whole invariant can fail. And whether a member may correct an issued member's recorded title or underlying file at all: recommended yes, narrowly, audited and never renumbering, because the alternative leaves a firm unable to fix its own record of what it filed. Exhibits design flags E2 and E6.
- **Compiled exhibit package** (`exhibits.package`) — **Not built.** Whether a compiled package paginates itself by default, and in which corner. The Nevada rule read for this design requires exhibits over ten pages to be numbered in the lower right — which is exactly where a page produced in discovery already carries its Bates stamp — and no reliable detector for an existing stamp exists, so a false negative overprints a number that was already served and a false positive drops pagination a rule requires. Defaulting on makes the common filing correct and risks overprinting; defaulting off makes every package a member has to remember to configure. Exhibits design flag E5.
- **Chart of accounts** (`financial.ledger.operating-gl.chart-of-accounts`) — **Not built.** Does a starter chart of accounts ship? A seeded chart makes a firm usable on day one and also makes the platform's opinion most firms' chart by inertia; the alternative is that a firm cannot bill until its accountant authors one. The recommendation is yes for the accounts and no for the mapping — a small chart of system accounts plus common law-firm expense accounts, marked as a starting point, with no role mapping the firm did not confirm, because the mapping is where the money goes. General ledger design, flag G1.
- **Fiscal-period close** (`financial.ledger.operating-gl.period-close`) — **Not built.** Five flags, and the first decides whether this sub-slice exists — period close is not in the slice map's scope, and against that, statements nobody can rely on are not worth assembling. Then: who may close, and does closing take a second person the way a trust act does. What an operator may do to a closed period — reopen with a reason and an append-only event, post into it under a permission, or never. Whether the hard lock ever gets a route or stays a column nothing sets until a jurisdiction requires it. And whether the lock is checked inside the posting primitive itself, which is the one with reach: after it, a trust movement or an invoice finalization backdated into a closed period refuses, which is a behaviour change in shipped subsystems and is also the only version of a lock a caller cannot decline to honour. General ledger design, flags G3, G4, G5, G6 and G12.
- **Accounts payable** (`financial.ledger.operating-gl.accounts-payable`) — **Not built.** May accounts payable pay from anything but the resolved operating account — a firm with two operating banks has a legitimate need the single role cannot express, and the recommendation is a second role rather than a caller-supplied parameter. At what status a bill-originated cost enters the cost store, where draft respects the billability review and a firm whose lawyers never review costs would leave advanced costs unbilled forever. And whether vendor credits are in this pass or their own design, given they are the mirror of billing's credit note and need the same care about attribution. General ledger design, flags G7, G8 and G9.
- **Financial statements** (`financial.ledger.operating-gl.financial-statements`) — **Not built.** Are trust accounts on the firm's balance sheet? Correct accounting includes them — the firm holds an asset and an equal liability — and some accountants present a firm balance sheet without them; the recommendation is to include them in labelled sections that must net to zero, and to refuse the statement when they do not, because omitting them hides the one relationship an examiner checks. Also whether a derived cash-basis view is in scope here at all, which it is recommended not to be: the book is accrual and cannot be otherwise, and a cash view is not a tax basis and must never be presented as one. And who is seeded the capability to read statements, where a bookkeeper reads them and posts nothing. General ledger design, flags G16, G2 and G14.
- **Public intake forms** (`intake.public-forms`) — **Not built.** Two things stand in front of an anonymous form. Whether it accepts attachments — the value is real (the contract, the letter they were served) and so is the surface, since it is an unidentified stranger writing into the untrusted-ingest and malware-scan pipeline at the firm's quarantine and storage cost. And whether Turnstile is switched on first: the verifier is built but dormant, and every unprotected pass is counted as unenforced rather than blocked, so a public form shipped ahead of the activation would ship in front of a bot control that is not actually running. Client intake design flags J3 and J11.
- **Firm-wide note search** (`notes.search.firm-wide`) — **Not built.** Neither existing index can take notes as it stands, and which one grows to fit is an owner call. The natural-language feed reserves work-product as a class its writer must never produce, and a note is exactly that; the keyword index takes a file or a review document fed from R2 objects, and a note is a database row. The third option is a note-only index. The snippet question is already answered (no snippet, notes design flag W6).
- **Payments port** (`payments.port`) — **Not built.** Can both deposit destinations live inside one connection's credential payload, or must the registry's one-connection-per-category resolution widen? Payments design flag K9.
- **Manually recorded receipt** (`payments.manual-receipt`) — **Not built.** Does this ship as the first payments slice, ahead of any processor work? Payments design flag K1.
- **ACH rail (default)** (`payments.ach`) — **Not built.** Who presses the button on the first electronic payment, given the client portal is not started. Staff-operated only, where the firm opens the hosted modal on the client's behalf in the office or over the phone; a one-time pay link, on the precedent of the one-time link that already proves a mailbox; or wait for the portal and ship no electronic rail until then. This decides whether the first payments slice has any client-facing surface at all. Payments design flag K7.
- **Card rail (fallback)** (`payments.card`) — **Not built.** Is the trust rail in scope at all before a returned-deposit movement kind exists, given a chargeback against trust is unrecordable today? Payments design flags K2 and K3.
- **Operating-only surcharge** (`payments.surcharge`) — **Not built.** Confirm surcharge stays out of the first slice — it needs a new typed jurisdiction requirement in the restriction bucket, 56 jurisdictions of primary-source research with attorney review, and card-network registration. Payments design flag K6.
- **Public changelog/roadmap/status pages** (`product-updates.public-pages`) — **Not built.** Whether the public changelog and roadmap get a page on the marketing site, and whether that page is indexed by search engines. The public changelog is rendered to docs/public-changelog.md; the public roadmap's renderer is written and linted but deliberately writes nothing to disk, because there are no users yet and a document nobody reads is a document nobody keeps honest.
- **Reaping an abandoned tenant** (`self-serve.reaping`) — **Not built.** The clock. Notices, dormancy, suspension, an export window and only then a proposal to destroy — the design proposes a ladder measured in months from last human activity, with a much shorter one for a tenant that was provisioned and never used, and settles none of the numbers. What turns on them is how long the platform carries a stranger's abandoned workspace and how much warning a lawyer who went quiet actually receives; the design deliberately recovers the recurring cost early in the ladder so the platform is never under cost pressure to shorten the destruction end of it. Self-serve provisioning design flag F2.
- **Quota ceilings** (`self-serve.entitlements.quotas`) — **Not built.** The free tier's actual numbers: the storage ceiling, the member ceiling and the matter ceiling. This is the flag the founding-a-firm screen is waiting on — it already tells a visitor their firm starts on the free tier while nothing in the tree defines what that tier is, and the registration screen, the upgrade panel, the refusal body and the enforcement are all meant to read the same catalog so a limit cannot be right in one place and stale in another. Two recommendations on shape rather than value: the member ceiling is the single most effective anti-abuse limit and should be small, and the storage ceiling should be small enough that an abandoned free tenant's bytes are never individually worth worrying about. Self-serve provisioning design flag F1.
- **Free evaluation tier** (`self-serve.free-tier`) — **Not built.** Whether trust accounting unlocks on verification or for any paying firm. The design argues verification: a card proves somebody will pay and does not prove a real practice is holding real client funds, and withholding trust from a free tenant is also what makes the rule against reaping a firm holding client money absolute rather than a judgement call. Unlocking it for any paying firm is a real revenue edge and it means an unverified stranger with a card keeps a ledger the platform's own jurisdiction rules have not certified. Self-serve provisioning design flag F3.
- **Firm verification** (`self-serve.verification`) — **Not built.** What verification is at launch — a human reviewing each firm, or an attestation the firm makes that a person spot-checks and an operator can revoke. Review is stronger and becomes a bottleneck at exactly the volume self-serve exists to reach; attestation plus spot-check is the recommendation, and it decides whether this ships as a review queue or as a form. Either way there is no national registry to call and per-state scraping is a maintenance liability, so the first version records a claimed bar number and jurisdiction and control of a matching domain, and must never be worded anywhere as though the platform checked something it did not. Self-serve provisioning design flag F4.
- **Pasted-text translation** (`translation.pasted-text`) — **Not built.** Whether a pasted run is recorded or ephemeral. Recommended recorded: a firm has to be able to answer what it sent to which engine, about which client and when, and a retention story with a hole in it labelled the paste box is not one — the platform already answered the same question the same way for what a member types into its assistant. Ephemeral is the intuitive answer and it means privileged text passes through a place nothing accounts for. Recording it costs a run row and an object per paste. Translation design flag T4.
- **Document translation** (`translation.documents`) — **Not built.** Whether a translated document is a new file beside its source or a new version of it. Recommended a derived sibling linked to the source version: a version chain means successive states of one document, and a Spanish original and its English rendering are two documents that both stay current, so folding them into one chain means a member downloading the latest version of a Spanish contract silently gets English. Choosing the version chain instead removes the link table and breaks the "show me the original" read. Translation design flag T5.
- **Side-by-side and target-only renditions** (`translation.renditions`) — **Not built.** Whether the statement that this is machine output appears as a visible header on every generated rendition, or only in the closing provenance appendix. Recommended the header, removable only by a human certification path if one is ever built: an appendix is the part that gets detached first, and the artifact that survives detachment is a fluent translation with nothing on it saying no person has read it. The appendix-only answer produces a cleaner document a firm can hand over, and a cleaner document is exactly the one that gets mistaken for a certified one. Translation design flag T8.
- **Translation provenance record** (`translation.provenance-record`) — **Not built.** Whether this artifact is offered at all. It is one word away from a certificate of translation accuracy, which is a named person's signed attestation that no machine can hold either half of — and a document reciting an engine and a date under a heading like that will be filed by somebody as though it were one. Recommended yes, in the constrained form: refusing to build it does not remove the risk, it moves it into a document somebody writes in a word processor with no constrained vocabulary, no fitness function and no statement of what it is not. Translation design flag T2.
- **Translation engines and consent gates** (`translation.engines`) — **Not built.** Which engine is the default and whether third-party translation vendors are adopted. Recommended: the on-platform model as the default, behind the feature gate alone rather than the third-party-processing gate, because it sends content to no third party and requiring that gate would deny the on-platform route to exactly the most conservative firms; the frontier route behind all three gates; and the dedicated translation vendors refused for now, since they buy quality the frontier route already delivers while adding a fourth data processor to the privilege chain and a credential lifecycle nobody has built. A yes to a vendor means building the credentialed adapter that is currently a deferred seam. Translation design flag T1.

The design wants a rule to inherit an authorizing act a person actually performed, and a directly-authored rule has none — its audit terminates in a configuration screen. And whether a matter may refuse to open over undisposed tier-1 hits, or only advise loudly — a hard block is the platform making an ethics judgement, and no block means a firm can open a file over an unanswered exact-identifier match. Conflicts deepening design, flags F2 and F11. - **Matters** (`matters`) — **A group of capabilities.** The platform carries no note, comment or timeline entity on any record, and one design now asks whether to build one. Is the note primitive built at all, and as a platform slice belonging to every matter and every Directory entity rather than to whichever subsystem needs it first? If no, intake's prospect timeline stays derived-only and cannot hold "she is thinking about it", the matter detail keeps promising a Notes section it will not have, tasks land on an activity stream nobody owns, and every subsystem that needs somewhere to write a sentence grows its own note column. If yes, it comes before tasks and before intake. Notes and activity design flag W1, raised by the client intake design as J6.

M1, M2 and M4 are settled.
- **Retention and destruction dates** (`custodian.retention-destruction`) — **Built; no screen yet.** Does the platform ever ship a retention default? The recommendation is never — a firm sets the retention date per packet or item, and a jurisdiction catalogue supplies suggestions only once it is built and verified. The node says owner-gated in prose and has said so since the design merged; the reason it is not merely unbuilt is that shipping a default here is the platform deciding when a firm's irreplaceable original may be destroyed, which is unrecoverable in the one direction that matters. Custodian design, flag C7.
- **Search** (`discovery.search`) — **A group of capabilities.** Six settings, all reasoned rather than measured, because nothing in the repository can price them until the search container runs against a real corpus. The container's instance size is a guess that mirrors the malware scanner, whose working set is one file where search's is however many splits a firm keeps open — and whether a split cache earns its authorization complexity is the same unmeasured question. Then the four from compaction: the merge thresholds, how long a superseded index object is kept before retirement, whether the search worker gains a compaction endpoint, and ratification of the changed query input shape.
- **Review workflow** (`discovery.review`) — **A group of capabilities.** Three, and the first two gate the next sub-slice outright. P1 — does privilege coding require its own permission tier, separate from ordinary coding? P3 — what is the default when a privilege designation splits a document family, produce the rest or withhold the family? Both decide what reaches opposing counsel and where waiver risk sits, and 5·2·2 privilege review cannot start until they are answered. (P2 and P4 are production-side and gate 5·2·3, not this slice.) Third, and separate: the per-review-set access list can be emptied and there is NO WAY BACK. The grant route is itself behind the wall (apps/api-worker/src/routes/discovery-coding.ts checks memberHasReviewSetAccess before grantReviewSetAccess) and the list is seeded once at creation with the creating member alone, so if the last listed member leaves the firm or is revoked the review set is permanently unreachable — nobody can read it, nobody can close it, nobody can grant access back, and there is no Owner bypass and no operator override. On a corpus under a protective order that is material a firm may be obliged to produce and cannot open. It is deliberately an owner question rather than a fix, because every escape hatch weakens the exact property the design chose — that no role sees through the wall. The narrowest option is probably refusing to revoke the LAST member on a list, which keeps the wall intact and only forbids emptying it; it is not built.
- **Dedicated PDF form filling** (`document-automation.pdf-form-filling`) — **Built; no screen yet.** Does a filled form file flattened, interactive, or is it a per-template setting? A flattened form is what a signature wants and cannot be corrected; an interactive one is what a firm wants when the recipient still has to complete a box. Today it is interactive, which is the non-destructive answer to sit behind the question — changing it is one constant, or one column read. Document automation tail remainder plan, flag B5.
- **Filing-grade generation** (`document-automation.filing-grade-generation`) — **Built; no screen yet.** The curated Nevada starter library is designed and its plan does not start until five calls land. Whether the platform authors its own templates from the text a statute prescribes, or redistributes the courts' published documents — authoring narrows the copyright question to prescribed text and produces templates the engine can actually render, redistribution takes on somebody else's asserted rights in every file. What the starter set contains, which is the deliverable rather than a detail. Whether one named Nevada attorney's review is enough, or each firm must acknowledge an entry before first use. Whether fillable-PDF templates get built at all, without which no family-law form — the forms most Nevada firms actually file — can ever be in the library. And whether to write to the Supreme Court of Nevada and the Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada asking permission, since both assert copyright expressly and until a written permission exists no court-published form is shippable. Nevada court form library design, flags N1 through N5.
- **Template storage reclamation** (`document-automation.template-storage-reclamation`) — **Built; no screen by nature.** Should the sweep be armed to delete, and at the cost of what change? Deleting the complement of the live set is only as safe as that set is complete, and completeness is currently unverifiable: a template key carries no firm segment and every firm resolves to the same bound bucket, so the retention set has to be the whole fleet's — and a per-firm database addressed under a name it does not answer to does not throw, it self-migrates and returns zero rows, which reads identically to a firm that holds no templates. The residency seam makes that reachable rather than theoretical. Arming it safely wants each object stamped with the firm that wrote it, so an orphan is decided against one firm's rows and the fleet-wide union disappears along with its blast radius — a schema and write-path change, not a line to flip.
- **Offline checkout / check-in** (`documents.editing.checkout`) — **Built; no screen yet.** Should documents:forceReleaseLock also reach Attorney? It is seeded to Owner/Admin alone, so in a firm whose administrator is away nobody can free a document another member has exclusively locked until the idle-checkout sweep releases it overnight. Widening is one additive migration; narrowing later is a conversation with every firm that had got used to the wider default.
- **PDF document scrub** (`documents.pdf.scrub`) — **Built; no screen yet.** Whether the scrub should also strip a PDF's executable content, which today it leaves entirely intact — the document-level JavaScript name tree, the action that fires when the file is opened, the catalog actions that fire on save, print and close, the additional-actions on pages and on form fields, and the action attached to any annotation. A document produced to an opposing party can therefore still carry code, from the step whose whole purpose is that it does not. This is not a hypothetical path: prepareFormTemplateVersion in packages/document-assembly/src/pdf-form.ts runs the scrub on every PDF form template a firm registers, so whichever way this is answered changes something already running. Detection is not the missing half: inspect_pdf in containers/scan already recognises JavaScript, auto-actions, launch actions and embedded files, with a byte-scan fallback for a compressed catalog, and records the finding as active-content on the verdict. Two things are done with it — nothing, and nothing. It blocks no upload unless the firm's policy flags the file for another reason, and scanAndGateStatements in apps/api-worker/src/routes/templates.ts activates a template version even on an unscanned verdict, discarding verdict.activeContent entirely. So the third option below is closer to wiring up an answer the platform already computes than to building one. Stripping is not free, and the cost falls on fillable forms a FIRM uploaded — a court form still uses field actions for validation and formatting, so a scrub that removes them hands back a form that looks right and computes nothing. Three ways to answer. Strip always, and accept that. Strip with an exception a firm marks at upload for forms it needs to keep working, which puts the choice on the person who knows what the form is for and has to be recorded so a recipient cannot forge it. Or surface the active content without removing it, and let whoever is producing the document decide per file — which means carrying a verdict flag that is already computed and today thrown away, and closing the unscanned hole so the flag cannot be silently absent. Note that an exception keyed on "templates the platform authored" would exempt nothing today: every fillable template in the tree is a firm upload, and whether the platform ever authors its own is itself unanswered on document-automation.filing-grade-generation. The page-split path in crates/pdf-core/src/generate.rs removes some of the same entries, and does so for safety — but a different safety, that no byte of an excluded page survives into the output: it drops the catalog OpenAction, AA and Names because they reference pages across the whole document, and strips an action from an annotation on a kept page only when that action points at an excluded one, with a fail-closed gate behind both. An action pointing inside the kept range survives. A split is not a scrub, and neither path settles what the other should do.

A yes makes it a capability with its own model rather than three columns bolted to an exhibit set. Exhibits design flag E7.

And whether a template's anchor vocabulary may grow beyond "matter opened": it stays at that one value, because every other candidate anchor — service date, filing date, a trigger event — is the front half of a docketing rule, and one anchor is the whole reason the offset arithmetic is defensible while the calendar refuses to compute deadlines. Tasks and matter plans design flags W11 and W12.
- **Human-attested certification** (`translation.certification`) — **Parked.** Whether the platform offers human-attested certification at all. It is the difference between a reading aid and something a firm can file — under the governing federal standard a competent person may run an engine, review the output line by line and then sign, at which point the certificate is true — and it is also the part with the most ways to go wrong, so the recommendation is yes but only once the artifacts it would certify exist. A no means the product ships permanently as an explicitly non-certifying reading aid, which changes nothing else in this area. Translation design flag T3.

## The register

Every node in the ledger, by area, in the order the area file writes them.
Indentation follows the dotted id, so a child sits under its parent and a parent's status is a
roll-up of what is beneath it. Nothing is collapsed, nothing is summarised, and nothing is left out:
to change a line here, edit the node in its area file and re-run the build.

### Translation Services

[`roadmap.d/translation.md`](../roadmap.d/translation.md) — 6 units of work, 0% shipped. 0 done, 0 part-built, 5 ahead, 1 parked.

- `translation` · `designed` · **Translation services** — Machine translation of pasted text and of matter documents, aligned side-by-side and target-only renditions, and a provenance record of how each translation was produced. _Note: Spoken interpretation is a person and not a feature; translation memory, glossaries and term bases, product localization, and any automated judgement of translation quality are all outside this capability._
  - `translation.pasted-text` · `designed` · **Pasted-text translation** — A member pastes a passage, names a language pair, and reads the result aligned beside the original on the matter-free tool surface.
  - `translation.documents` · `designed` · **Document translation** — Translating an uploaded or already-filed document end to end — conversion where needed, per-page native or recognized text extraction, segmentation into aligned units, and where the translation lands in the matter.
  - `translation.renditions` · `designed` · **Side-by-side and target-only renditions** — The generated artifacts — an editable side-by-side document with the source and target in adjacent columns, its printable, and the translation on its own for handing to a client.
  - `translation.provenance-record` · `designed` · **Translation provenance record** — A stored, immutable record of how one translation was produced — source bytes and their hash, the extraction route per page, any conversion, the engine and its pinned snapshot, the languages, who ran it and when, and whether any human edited the output. _Note: It is never called an audit report — audit already names this platform's access and event auditing — and it carries no signature block, no seal, no ruled line and no confidence score, because a document that invites a signature is a certificate with the signature not yet applied._
  - `translation.engines` · `designed` · **Translation engines and consent gates** — Which engine may see a privileged document under which consent — the on-platform engine behind the feature gate alone, and a frontier model through the shipped inference chokepoint behind all three gates. _Note: The engine actually used is recorded per run rather than as a policy statement, so a firm asked later what saw a client's document has an answer for each translation._
  - `translation.certification` · `deferred` · **Human-attested certification** — A named person reviews the aligned translation, edits it, and signs a certificate of accuracy declaring their own competence in the language pair and the completeness of the rendering. _Note: The platform supplies the form and records who signed it and when; it never asserts that a signer is competent, because it has no way to know and that declaration is the signer's to make._
