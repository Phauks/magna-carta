# Meetings — in-app video conferencing, design

**Status:** accepted for slice 1 (members-only meetings from a calendar event), 2026-09-18.
**White paper:** _Communications layer_ and _Calendar and rules-based docketing_ —
[`docs/Magna-Carta-White-Paper.md`](../Magna-Carta-White-Paper.md).
**Ledger:** [`roadmap.d/meetings.md`](../../roadmap.d/meetings.md) — the roadmap for this component, kept
current as each slice lands.
**Owner ruling (2026-09-18):** meetings are built into the product rather than delegated to a
conferencing vendor, so a firm runs one fewer subscription; a Zoom/Teams adapter may exist later as a
port, never as the default.

## 1. What this is

A member opens a calendar event and starts a meeting in it. Other members on the event join from the
same place. Everyone sees and hears each other, anyone can share a screen, and when the host ends it
the record shows who was in the room and when. That is the whole of slice 1.

The media never touches a Worker. The browser's own WebRTC stack talks to the **Cloudflare Realtime
SFU** (selective forwarding unit), which routes audio, video and screen-share tracks between
participants. The Worker's part is _signaling_: a Durable Object per meeting brokers each browser's
session with the SFU over a WebSocket, keeps the roster, and writes attendance through the firm-scoped
data-access layer. No container, no FFmpeg, no C/C++ dependency anywhere in the path — the browser
decodes, the SFU forwards, and the memory-safety rule is satisfied without an exception.

## 2. What it is not (yet)

Each of these is a node in the ledger, sequenced after slice 1 and not designed here:

- **External guests.** A client or opposing counsel needs a way in and the portal grant model is not
  started. Slice 2 mints a signed one-time join link on the precedent of the tokenized e-signature
  link; until then a participant is a firm member with a session.
- **Recording.** Server-side media processing, privileged recordings into R2 through the untrusted-file
  pipeline, and a consent gate on the jurisdiction profile — none of which is basic.
- **Dial-in by phone.** There is no telephony port to bridge to.
- **Large rooms.** The SFU scales; the tile layout, bandwidth adaptation and active-speaker detection
  for more than a handful of tiles are their own slice. Slice 1 caps a room at
  `MAX_MEETING_PARTICIPANTS = 12`.
- **A conferencing port for Zoom or Teams.** A `ConferencingPort` under `packages/integrations` whose
  adapter puts a vendor's link on the event, for firms that keep a vendor. Later, and optional.

## 3. Where a meeting hangs

A meeting belongs to exactly one calendar event, matter or firm. It carries no title, time or
participant list of its own: those are the event's, read through a join. One meeting per event
(unique index), created on demand by the first member who presses Start, so an event with no meeting
row simply has not been started.

Authorization is the event's. A matter event is reachable when the member may see the matter
(`visibleMatterPredicate` from `@magna-carta/calendar/visibility`); a firm event is firm-visible. On
top of that, two permission keys in `packages/access-control/src/registry.ts`, both binding
`resource: "firm"` because a firm event has no matter:

- `meetings:join` — join a meeting on an event the member may see.
- `meetings:host` — start a meeting on such an event, and end one.

## 4. Schema (per-firm lineage, additive)

Two tables in `packages/database/src/per-firm-schema.ts`, one hand-written migration, snapshot
refreshed in the same commit, worker bundle regenerated by `pnpm run migration-bundles`.

```
meeting
  id                  text pk            -- meeting_ handle
  event_kind          text not null      -- 'matter' | 'firm'  (CHECK)
  matter_event_id     text null  -> matter_event.id
  firm_event_id       text null  -> firm_event.id
  status              text not null      -- 'scheduled' | 'live' | 'ended'  (CHECK)
  created_by_user_id  text not null -> member.user_id
  started_at          text null          -- instant, first join
  ended_at            text null          -- instant
  ended_by_user_id    text null  -> member.user_id
  created_at          text not null
  updated_at          text not null
  CHECK exactly one of matter_event_id / firm_event_id, matching event_kind
  UNIQUE (matter_event_id) WHERE not null; UNIQUE (firm_event_id) WHERE not null

meeting_attendance
  id                  text pk            -- meetingAttendance_ handle
  meeting_id          text not null -> meeting.id
  user_id             text not null -> member.user_id
  joined_at           text not null      -- instant
  left_at             text null          -- instant
  created_at, updated_at
  INDEX (meeting_id, user_id); INDEX (meeting_id, left_at)
```

Identifiers: `meeting` and `meetingAttendance` in `packages/identifiers` (types + registry).
PII registry: `meeting.created_by_user_id`, `meeting.ended_by_user_id`, `meeting_attendance.user_id`
are `retain-under-matter` — who was in the room is part of what the record says happened.
Audit rows (`domainAuditStatement`) on create, start, join, leave, end: identifiers and instants only.
The meeting stores nothing anyone said.

## 5. `@magna-carta/meetings` (packages/meetings)

The domain package, on the `@magna-carta/communications` shape: validates in memory, composes
statements into `composeEffectBatch`, holds no binding, reads no clock, calls nothing external.

- `src/types.ts` — `MEETING_EVENT_KINDS`, `MEETING_STATUSES`, `MAX_MEETING_PARTICIPANTS`, the row
  types, and the `MeetingSummary` a route returns (meeting + event title/starts/ends + live count).
- `src/validation.ts` — refusal codes: `meeting-unknown-event`, `meeting-already-ended`,
  `meeting-not-live`, `meeting-room-full`, `meeting-not-joined`.
- `src/meeting-statements.ts` — SQL; the read of an event through the visibility predicate is the one
  place the walled-matter question is answered. An event that does not exist and one the reader may
  not see raise the same `meeting-unknown-event`.
- `src/meeting-service.ts` — `createMeeting`, `joinMeeting` (opens an attendance row; flips
  `scheduled → live` and sets `started_at` on the first join; refuses at the cap), `leaveMeeting`
  (closes the caller's open attendance), `endMeeting` (host; closes every open attendance at the same
  instant). Each returns an `EffectWrite` with its audit row.

## 6. Signaling: the `MeetingRoom` Durable Object

`apps/api-worker/src/meetings/meeting-room.ts`, keyed `${firmId}:${meetingId}`, addressed by the
SESSION firm id at the route. Hibernating WebSockets on the `MagnusConversation` pattern: everything
a frame needs rides on the per-socket attachment `{ protocolVersion, firmId, meetingId, userId,
attendanceId, displayName }`, all server-derived at upgrade.

Unlike Magnus, the room holds a small transient state in `ctx.storage`: the roster —
`participant:<attendanceId> → { userId, displayName, sfuSessionId, tracks: [{trackName, kind}] }`.
It is signaling state, not content, and it is deleted on leave, on end, and when the last socket
closes. The SFU app secret lives only here and in the SFU client; a browser never sees it.

### Frames (JSON, `v: 1`)

Client → room:

| type          | fields                                                                     | room does                                                                                           |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `publish`     | `sessionDescription {type:"offer", sdp}`, `tracks[{trackName, mid, kind}]` | creates the SFU session on first publish, `tracks/new` with `location:"local"`; replies `published` |
| `subscribe`   | `tracks[{sessionId, trackName}]`                                           | `tracks/new` with `location:"remote"`; replies `subscribed` carrying the SFU's offer                |
| `renegotiate` | `sessionDescription {type:"answer", sdp}`                                  | `PUT renegotiate`; replies `renegotiated`                                                           |
| `close`       | `mids[]`                                                                   | `tracks/close`; replies `closed` (with the SFU's offer when it requires renegotiation)              |
| `leave`       | —                                                                          | removes the participant, broadcasts `roster`, closes the socket 1000                                |
| `ping`        | —                                                                          | `pong` (the byte-exact pair from `progress/job-progress-protocol.ts`)                               |

Room → client:

| type           | fields                                                                                        |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `welcome`      | `participantId`, `roster[]`                                                                   |
| `published`    | `sessionDescription {type:"answer", sdp}`, `tracks[{trackName, mid}]`                         |
| `subscribed`   | `sessionDescription {type:"offer", sdp}`, `tracks[{sessionId, trackName, mid}]`               |
| `renegotiated` | —                                                                                             |
| `closed`       | `sessionDescription?`                                                                         |
| `roster`       | `participants[{participantId, userId, displayName, sfuSessionId, tracks[{trackName, kind}]}]` |
| `ended`        | `endedAt`                                                                                     |
| `refused`      | `reason` (a closed vocabulary), then the socket closes 4000                                   |

### The SFU client

`apps/api-worker/src/meetings/sfu-client.ts` wraps exactly the Cloudflare Realtime SFU HTTPS API,
base `https://rtc.live.cloudflare.com/v1/apps/{appId}`, bearer `REALTIME_SFU_APP_SECRET`:

- `POST /sessions/new` → `{ sessionId }`
- `POST /sessions/{id}/tracks/new` body `{ sessionDescription?, tracks[{location, trackName?, mid?, sessionId?}] }`
  → `{ requiresImmediateRenegotiation, sessionDescription?, tracks[{location, mid, trackName, sessionId, errorCode?}] }`
- `PUT /sessions/{id}/renegotiate` body `{ sessionDescription }` → `{ sessionDescription? }`
- `PUT /sessions/{id}/tracks/close` body `{ tracks[{mid}], sessionDescription?, force? }`
- `GET /sessions/{id}` → `{ tracks[{location, mid, trackName, sessionId?, status}] }`

Every upstream body is parsed defensively; an upstream failure is reported to the client as
`refused` with reason `sfu-unavailable` and never echoes the upstream body, which may name the app id.

Bindings, beside the existing Realtime TURN pair in `apps/api-worker/src/app.ts`:
`REALTIME_SFU_APP_ID?`, `REALTIME_SFU_APP_SECRET?`. Absent, the create and join routes answer 503
"Meetings are not configured" and the calendar shows no Start button (the tools-turn-credentials
precedent). The TURN pair, when present, mints ICE servers at join; when absent the client uses
`stun:stun.cloudflare.com:3478` only.

## 7. Routes (OpenAPI, chokepoint, `apps/api-worker/src/routes/meetings.ts`)

| method | path                                        | permission      | does                                                                               |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| POST   | `/api/v1/meetings`                          | `meetings:host` | body `{ eventKind, eventId }`; creates or returns the event's meeting (idempotent) |
| GET    | `/api/v1/meetings/{meetingId}`              | `meetings:join` | `MeetingSummary`                                                                   |
| GET    | `/api/v1/events/{eventId}/meeting`          | `meetings:join` | the matter event's meeting, or 404                                                 |
| GET    | `/api/v1/firm-events/{firmEventId}/meeting` | `meetings:join` | the firm event's meeting, or 404                                                   |
| POST   | `/api/v1/meetings/{meetingId}/join`         | `meetings:join` | opens attendance; returns `{ participantId, displayName, iceServers[] }`           |
| POST   | `/api/v1/meetings/{meetingId}/leave`        | `meetings:join` | closes the caller's attendance                                                     |
| POST   | `/api/v1/meetings/{meetingId}/end`          | `meetings:host` | ends it; the room broadcasts `ended` and closes every socket                       |
| GET    | `/api/v1/meetings/{meetingId}/attendance`   | `meetings:join` | who was in the room and when                                                       |
| GET    | `/api/v1/meetings/{meetingId}/socket`       | `meetings:join` | WebSocket upgrade to the room; 426 otherwise; requires an open attendance row      |

`MeetingSummary`: `{ id, eventKind, eventId, matterId | null, title, startsOn, startsAtTime | null,
endsAtTime | null, timeZone, status, startedAt | null, endedAt | null, liveCount, configured }`.

Problem types: `meeting-not-found` (404, and the same for an event the member may not see),
`meeting-ended` (409), `meeting-room-full` (409), `meetings-not-configured` (503),
`websocket-upgrade-required` (426).

### Amendments taken during the build

- **`GET /api/v1/meetings`** (`meetings:join`, cursor-paged) — the list the Meetings screen paints,
  in one of three scopes: `live`, `upcoming` and `recent`. `upcoming` requires `today`, the caller's
  own civil date, and refuses 422 without it: a day is a day in a place and the server has no zone to
  derive one in. Items are `MeetingSummary` with `configured`, exactly as the single read.
- **The attendance read is cursor-paged** like every other list. The room's ceiling bounds who is
  present at one instant and bounds nothing else — a meeting run over a bad line accumulates stays.
- **A subscription is checked against the room's own roster.** The whole fleet's meetings share one
  Realtime application, so the room is the only thing that knows which meeting a session belongs to; a
  pair that is not on this roster is `refused` with `unknown-track` and never forwarded. A `publish`
  whose track name is not prefixed with the publisher's own participant handle is `foreign-track-name`,
  and a participant is capped at eight tracks (`track-limit-reached`). All three leave the socket open.
- **`subscribed` carries `requiresImmediateRenegotiation`** (boolean, additive), so a null
  `sessionDescription` is distinguishable from an answer the browser has to act on.
- **`refused` may carry `errorId`** (optional, additive) — present only where the room actually
  recorded a failure through the standard pipeline, which today is `sfu-unavailable`.
- **One live socket per stay, newest wins.** An upgrade for a stay that already has one retires the
  older socket (`refused` with a new reason `superseded`, then a 4000 close), resets the participant
  to no session and no tracks, broadcasts `roster`, and closes the old session's tracks at the
  routing service. This is forced by WebRTC rather than chosen: a reconnecting browser brings a new
  peer connection — new ICE credentials, new DTLS fingerprint — so it cannot resume the session its
  predecessor opened, and two tabs cannot share one session at all. Carrying the session forward left
  the returning member publishing where nobody was subscribed while everybody else held a
  subscription to a session that would never send again — a black tile that never resolves.
- **A dropped socket arms a grace sweep, timed per person**: the instant is recorded on the
  participant (`socketLostAt`) and the alarm closes only those whose own sixty seconds have run out,
  then re-arms for the soonest still running. One deadline for the room was a grace period only for
  whoever armed it — the second person to drop was swept while twenty seconds into theirs. The sweep
  closes the stay through the firm-scoped data-access layer (`meetings.meeting.left`,
  `reason: socket-lost`), drops the roster entry and closes the participant's tracks at the routing
  service. It never ends the meeting.
- **The upgrade is same-origin.** An `Origin` header that is present and is not this application's is
  refused 403: a browser opens a WebSocket cross-origin with the member's cookie where it would refuse
  to let that page read any other call here.
- **The medium is on the record and on the wire** (§7b): `POST /api/v1/meetings` takes an optional
  `medium` (`video` | `audio`, default `video`) read only when the request opens the room, and
  `MeetingSummary` carries it on every read including the list. The socket upgrade passes it inward
  off the meeting row, and a `publish` naming anything but an audio track in a call is `refused` with
  a new reason `track-kind-refused`, socket kept open and nothing asked of the routing service.

## 7b. Audio calls (owner direction, 2026-09-18)

An audio call is a meeting whose medium is sound alone, and it is the same record, the same room
and the same screen rather than a second subsystem. The owner's direction: the realtime kit is
built so that a call with no video is a first-class thing a member starts, not a video meeting
somebody forgot to turn a camera on in.

- **The record.** `meeting.medium` — `'video' | 'audio'` (CHECK), `NOT NULL DEFAULT 'video'`,
  added by an additive migration; chosen at start (`POST /api/v1/meetings` body gains
  `medium`, default `video`) and never changed afterwards, because the people already in the room
  agreed to one thing. `MeetingSummary` carries it.
- **The room enforces it.** In an audio call a `publish` naming a `video` or `screen` track is
  refused with `track-kind-refused` (socket kept open), so a modified client cannot turn a call
  into a video meeting for the people in it. The roster therefore never lists a picture, and no
  browser is asked to pull one.
- **The browser.** `join` takes the medium; in an audio call the client captures the microphone
  only, the controls are microphone and leave (End for a host), and each participant is a tile
  with their initials and name rather than a picture. Screen share is a video track and is not
  offered.
- **The calendar and the Meetings page.** The entry window offers **Start meeting** and **Start
  call** side by side (one row of two controls, the join key still gates both); Join reads
  **Join call** or **Join meeting** from the medium. The Meetings page rows carry a glyph for the
  medium (Lucide `phone` for a call, `video` for a meeting).
- **The ledger.** `meetings.audio-calls` in `roadmap.d/meetings.md`.

## 8. The screen (`apps/web`)

- `src/lib/meetings/meeting-client.svelte.ts` — the whole WebRTC state machine as a Svelte 5 runes
  class: `join()`, `publish(tracks)`, `subscribeTo(participant)`, `shareScreen()`, `stopSharing()`,
  `toggleMicrophone()`, `toggleCamera()`, `leave()`, exposing `participants`, `localStream`,
  `remoteStreams`, `state` (`idle | joining | live | ended | failed`). One `RTCPeerConnection`, tracks

- `src/routes/(app)/meetings/[id]/+page.svelte` (+ `+page.ts` loading the summary through the
  generated client) — a tile grid (one tile per remote stream, the local preview pinned), a control
  bar (microphone, camera, share screen, leave; End meeting for a member holding `meetings:host`), a
  roster rail. Design-system components only; Lucide icons; no raw CSS.
- Calendar: an event's detail surface gains **Start meeting** (creates and navigates) or **Join

No `Permissions-Policy` change is needed in slice 1: with no header the default allowlist for
camera, microphone and display capture is `self`, which is the app origin. When the security-header
baseline lands it must keep those three on the app origin and deny them on the cookieless files
origin.

## 11. Large rooms — slice 3 (owner direction, 2026-09-20)

The owner's target is the capacity Zoom sells on its Business tier: **300 participants** in one room.
`MAX_MEETING_PARTICIPANTS` moves from 12 to 300, and everything below is what has to change for a
room of that size to work rather than merely admit people. The SFU forwards whatever it is asked
for; the problem is entirely on the two ends — what each browser asks for, and how the room tells
300 browsers what changed.

### The four rules

1. **A browser never pulls every track.** It pulls video for the tiles it is showing and audio for
   the people who are talking, and nothing else. What it shows is decided by a pure policy function
   (`apps/web/src/lib/meetings/subscription-policy.ts`) over the roster, the presence facts, the
   pins and the gallery page: at most `MAX_VIDEO_SUBSCRIPTIONS = 16` camera tracks, at most one
   screen share, at most `MAX_AUDIO_SUBSCRIPTIONS = 24` audio tracks, ordered pinned first, then the
   active screen share, then the most recent speakers, then roster order. Everybody else is a name
   tile. The policy is a pure function so the 300-participant case is a unit test, not a rehearsal.
2. **Cameras publish simulcast.** `sendEncodings` with rids `f` (full), `h` (half), `q` (quarter);
   a subscriber names `simulcast.preferredRid` on `subscribe` and changes it with a new `layer`
   frame (`tracks/update` upstream), choosing `f` for up to four visible tiles, `h` for up to nine,
   `q` beyond. Screen share publishes one layer.
3. **The roster is sent once, then only what changed.** `welcome` still carries the full roster.
   Afterwards the room sends `roster-delta` frames — `{ joined: [...], left: [participantId...],
   updated: [...] }` — never the full list, because a full roster to 300 sockets on every one of 300
   joins is ninety thousand frames of thirty kilobytes. A client that suspects a gap sends `sync`
   and receives one full `roster` on its own socket. The `roster` frame stays in the vocabulary for
   that reply and for rooms below `ROSTER_DELTA_THRESHOLD = 16` participants, where the full frame is
   smaller than the bookkeeping.
4. **Who is talking is a fact the room relays, not one every browser measures for itself.** A
   participant's own browser measures its microphone (an `AudioContext` analyser on the local
   track, a 300 ms hysteresis) and sends `presence` frames `{ speaking, muted }` on transitions
   only; the room stores the two booleans on the participant record, throttles a participant's
   presence broadcasts to at most one per second, and sends `presence` deltas
   `{ participantId, speaking, muted, at }`. Speakers are what the policy function ranks by.

### Room and protocol

- `MAX_MEETING_PARTICIPANTS = 300` in `packages/meetings/src/types.ts`; the guarded insert holds.
- Client frames: `presence`, `layer { sessionId, trackName, mid, preferredRid }`, `sync`;
  `subscribe` tracks gain optional `preferredRid`.
- Room frames: `roster-delta`, `presence`; `subscribed` unchanged.
- `StoredParticipant` gains `speaking`, `muted`, `presenceAt`; the participant record stays small.
- Every broadcast to a room above the threshold is a delta. `end` still sends one `ended` frame.
- A room of 300 must not read 300 storage keys on every frame: `storedParticipants()` is read once
  per frame at most, and the roster check on `subscribe` uses a `Map` built from that one read.

### The screen

- **Gallery** pages of up to 16 tiles with page controls, the active speaker outlined, a **Speaker
  view** that shows the current speaker large with a strip of the last few, and **Pin** (local)
  on any tile. A participant with no pulled video is a name tile with initials, and the tile says
  "Not shown" rather than pretending to load.
- The roster rail shows the count, a search box above 24 people, who is speaking, who is muted.
- The control bar is unchanged.

### Testing

- `subscription-policy.test.ts`: the 300-participant case, pins win, screen share wins, speakers
  displace roster order, the audio cap holds, the layer choice per tile count, audio calls yield an
  audio-only policy.
- Room: roster deltas above the threshold and full rosters below it, `sync` answers one socket,
  presence throttling, `layer` reaches `tracks/update`, a 300-participant roster costs one storage
  list per frame (asserted through the fake storage's call count if the harness allows, else through
  a counter on the class under test).
- Load profile: a 300-participant roster fixture the policy function is measured against.

## 12. What Zoom sells that this does not have — measured 2026-09-20

The owner asked for the enterprise feature set to be measured against Zoom's and the gaps put on
the roadmap. Zoom Workplace's published plans (zoom.us/pricing, read 2026-09-20): 100 participants on
Basic and Pro, 300 on Business, 500 or more on Enterprise with large-meeting licences to 1,000; and
across the plans breakout rooms, waiting room, host controls, co-hosts, pin and spotlight, reactions
and raise hand, polling, in-meeting chat, whiteboard, local and cloud recording, recording
transcripts, captions and translated captions, AI meeting summaries and in-meeting questions,
virtual backgrounds and filters, noise suppression, screen sharing with annotation and remote
control, scheduling and booking pages, webinars and events, live streaming, phone, rooms and
hardware, end-to-end encryption, single sign-on, managed domains, an admin portal with dashboards,
archival and data-loss-prevention APIs, and information barriers. Each gap is a node in
[`roadmap.d/meetings.md`](../../roadmap.d/meetings.md); the ones that are not gaps are the calendar
(built), attendance on the record (built), phone (the communications layer's own port), single
sign-on and managed domains (the platform's auth), and information barriers (the conflicts
subsystem's walls, which every meeting already honours through the event).

Two rulings taken on 2026-09-20 after the measurement: **in-meeting chat is kept on the record**,
so a chat is the meeting's first stored content and carries a filed email's redaction, retention
and personal-data treatment; and **a firm runs several meetings at once** — one meeting per event
already allows it, and it means every per-meeting surface that follows (the waiting room first) is
one per meeting, never one per firm.

Two more on the same day: **scheduling is a high priority** — booking pages move directly behind
host controls and the waiting room; and **recording's consent mechanism is the announcement and the
indicator** — "Recording in progress" said to everyone when it starts and to every late arrival,
audibly and on screen, with a recording mark on every screen for as long as it runs, the way every
conferencing product does it. Recording and transcription therefore no longer wait on the
jurisdiction-profile machinery.
