This file keeps its name and path so its history stays put, and the mockup keeps its `tk`-prefixed ids and `timer/timer-manager.html` path so the concurrent unification stream's extraction target does not move (recorded call, §5.8.1).

**Mockup:** the Time Sheet mockup (scratchpad `timer/timer-manager.html`), built to the same token vocabulary as The Timesheet mockup.
**Rounds:** round one proposed a chip-anchored popover; the owner struck it (round two, verbatim): _"opening the time sheet should be a full modal, it is that important. this small fitting is not enough for the magnitude of this task. reorganize and try again. this really needs to be a table, so we can use table functionality. table is much better for mass data review and data entry."_ Round three then reframed the surface fundamentally (verbatim, **the governing ruling**): _"it should be more or less a spreadsheet!"_ and _"this is the time sheet. It should have that functionality."_ — plus the itemized gaps: _"no way to edit the time quantity… no way to change elapsed time" · "no way to type to change matter number" · "no settable date field" · "no way to set fee rate" · "no calculation for total value" · "no one cares when time started" · "time is automatically logged. As soon as it is entered, it goes in. It does not need approval to get saved." · "no way to attribute who did something" · "no undo stack for deleting data" · "what is open my time sheet? this is the time sheet."_

The manager is therefore **not a timer-segment viewer with a logging handoff — it IS a rapid time-entry spreadsheet that timers feed into.** §5 specifies that surface; the single-active auto-pause model (§4) and the chip (§6) survive all rounds. Round two's stop-&-log handoff, bulk-select bar, Started column, and read-only logged tail are **superseded** where §5 says so.

**Round four** (owner review of the round-three build) issued nine corrections plus two addenda, all folded into §5 (see §5.6 for the list and the recorded reasoning): the rate becomes a card **dropdown** with a Custom override; **Recorded by becomes changeable** (the settled delegation model, rate re-resolving); the **Paused and Draft chips are struck**; a **private Internal Memo column** (Soon); rows **open into the full entry dialog stacked above** the sheet; **two dates** (work + recorded); **search**; the **description gets its own row line**; a **full undo/redo history**; the instruction text and the "Earlier" band are removed; "what was done" is corrected to "Description".

**Round five** issued fourteen further corrections, folded into §5.7: **save without anything** (an empty entry persists, marked incomplete — _"otherwise we risk data loss"_); **the approval vibe goes** (a governing stance, §5.7.2); an **immutable time code** with a provisional-at-birth model and its own column; the **matter number and title combined** into one column matching on either; durations parsing **both** `1:30` and `1.5`; **one date format** everywhere; a **left status stripe**; search as an **expand-from-button**; a **date-scope control**; **Entered by as a proper column**; **wrapping descriptions**; a **full-screen toggle**; **file associations** on the entry; and **native spell-check** as the shipping v1.

**Round six** issued nine corrections, folded into §5.8: the surface is **renamed The Time Sheet**; **every editable cell's input fills its cell** (the defect: long matter titles were clipped by the field, not by the column); **Hours becomes typable** and reconciles with Duration both ways; **row actions render at rest**, never hover-revealed; **every date preset's span matches its name** (the defect: "This week" showed three days); **the scope widens itself** rather than hide a row the person just entered; a **New entry button** at the top opens the dedicated single-entry screen; the **standing blank row is replaced by an add-a-row button** at the end of the sheet; and the footer's saved-the-moment-it-exists line is **struck**.

**Round seven** issued six corrections plus a **binding cross-surface consistency ruling**, folded into §5.9: the **internal memo becomes the description's twin on demand** — a third row line, revealed by a button at the end of the description, wired for real rather than Soon-badged; the **undo/redo history moves off the toolbar into dropdowns** on the undo and redo controls; a defect where **"Entered by" rendered empty**; the **custom range applies as it is picked**, with no Apply button; the record window gains **Save & Close and Save & New**; the **row actions pin** so horizontal scrolling never puts them out of reach; and — the ruling — the Time Sheet's record window **must BE the matter Time tab's record modal**, lifted from the shared template rather than authored twice.

## 1. The surface's job

The top-right Time Keeper chip today is the handle for **one** running timer. The owner's commission: clicking it should open a **timer manager** — every timer the person has run for a period (today, by default), across different matters: see them, switch between them, resume, stop, and log each one.

The real day is interrupted. An attorney drafting on _Pemberton_ takes a call on _Whitfield_, answers an email on _Marsh_, and returns to the draft. Each interruption is billable time on a different matter, and the tool must make switching cost one click — because the alternative is what actually happens today: the timer keeps running on the wrong matter, or gets discarded, and the interrupting call is reconstructed from memory at week's end, badly. The manager's job is **capture continuity across interruptions**, and its measure of success is that no timed minute is silently lost or silently misattributed.

This grows up the header surface of the personal-timesheet design's three-place model without changing the model's boundaries:

| Place                 | Job                                                                                         |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Header** (this)     | Capture anywhere — now: the running timer AND the day's other timer runs, switch/resume/log |
| **`/time`** timesheet | Review and edit a period — all entries, correction, submission                              |
| **Matter Time tab**   | The matter's record                                                                         |

The timesheet design recorded "the chip never grows review affordances (no entry lists in the popover)." This design **revises that line deliberately**: the manager lists **timer runs** — capture-in-progress objects that exist nowhere else — plus a read-only tail of today's already-logged runs for continuity. The owner's round-two ruling then settled the magnitude: this is a **full modal with a real table**, sized for mass review and mass entry of the day's runs. The boundary that survives is the _object_, not the size: the manager works runs; a logged entry's row links out to `/time`, and entry editing, period review, and submission stay the timesheet's.

## 2. Current state (as built)

**The server timer** (`apps/api-worker/src/routes/timekeeping/timer.ts`, `src/timer/live-timer-durable-object.ts`):

- One Durable Object per `firmId:userId`; the DO stores **exactly one run** — a single JSON blob `{ liveRunId, startedAt, pausedIntervals, matterId?, narrative?, utbmsTaskCodeId?, lastActivityAt, status }`.
- Operations: `start` (mints a `live_run_id`), `pause`, `resume`, `bind` (matter required only at commit), `read` (client computes elapsed from `startedAt` — the DO never ticks), `stop-commit` (two-phase, idempotent on the run id; commits a **draft** `time_entry` with `source: "timer"`), `discard`. An abandoned-timer alarm flags a run left ≥ 10 h; resolution is keep / trim / discard.
- **A replacing `start` silently overwrites the stored run.** The route's own description says "stopping any timer already running," but nothing is committed and nothing is kept — `start` writes a fresh state blob over the old one. Starting a timer on a second matter today **destroys the first run's elapsed time**. This is the data-loss hazard the manager exists to close, and it means the manager is not UI over the existing API: the run store must become plural (§6).
- `stop-commit` refuses an unbound timer (422) and a sub-minute run that rounds to zero; the committed entry's narrative may be empty (`state.narrative ?? ""`), unlike manual capture, which requires Info.

**The chip** (`apps/web/src/lib/components/app-shell.svelte`): ships as resting anatomy — hairline chip, status dot geometry, tabular `0:00`, aria-disabled, with its light-up contract documented in place ("live elapsed while a timer runs; click opens the timer panel"). This design fills that contract with the manager as the panel.

**Listings:** `GET /api/v1/time-entries` answers one timekeeper × one day, or one matter. There is no read of timer **runs** (only the single `timer/read` state), no `source` filter, and no matter titles on the listing — all gaps §6 registers.

## 3. Competitive principles (verified 2026-08-26)

Four packages read — two legal (Clio, PracticePanther), two dedicated time trackers (Toggl Track, Harvest). Principles, not copies.

**Clio** (the owner's named reference):

1. **One timer runs at a time; starting another pauses the previous, and the previous stays resumable from where it left off.** Clio is explicit that concurrent running timers are impossible — the pause-and-stack model is the honest alternative.
2. **The pause moment is the logging moment.** Pausing surfaces the save modal — the tool asks for the entry while the work is fresh. (Adopted in softened form: **stop** offers the log dialog immediately; pause stays deliberately cheap and quiet, because a pause mid-switch must not interrupt the switch.)
3. **A "Timekeeper" surface beside the timer control lists the day's entries in one place** — restart, edit, create, arrows through previous days. Adopted: the day-scoped list of runs beside the timer control. Rejected (again — the timesheet design rejected it first): day-paging arrows and in-panel editing. Clio crams period review into header chrome because it has no equivalent of `/time`; we have one, one link away.
4. **The timer is startable from anywhere and survives navigation** — already true here by construction (the DO is server-side; the chip is fixed chrome).

**PracticePanther** (legal — the field's closest thing to a shipped timer manager):

5. **Held timers stack in one always-reachable place** (a right-side timer stack on every page); **starting a new timer auto-pauses the running one**, and the new timer stacks on top — newest-first ordering, switch-by-click. This is the held-run model working in a legal package today, and the strongest external validation of §4's recommendation.
6. **Binding can wait until save**: the timer runs first, and Save opens the entry screen where contact/matter are chosen — matching this platform's scratch-then-bind-at-commit design exactly. A timer's note becomes the entry's description, as a run's narrative does here.

**Harvest** (dedicated tracker):

7. **Restarting an existing entry's timer accumulates into that same day-entry** — one entry per project per day, however many sittings. This is §4's "resume accumulates into the same segment" argument, shipped: fragmenting an interrupted day into confetti entries is the failure both models avoid.
8. **Idle time gets an explicit resolution, not a silent policy.** The desktop apps detect keyboard/mouse idle and offer keep / remove the idle minutes / move them to a new entry. The timer-over-lunch is the inverse honesty leak of the unlogged run — time that _didn't_ happen, captured as if it did. The platform's abandoned alarm (≥ 10 h) catches the overnight case only; input-level idle is a client signal the slice 3·1 design already assigns to the UI. Noted as an owner question (M9) — v1 defers the detection but the run model must not preclude a trim-idle resolution (the DO's `lastActivityAt` + trim already fits).

**Toggl Track** (dedicated tracker):

9. **There is no pause at all — "continue" starts a new segment of the same work** (same metadata, new entry), keyboard-first (N starts, C continues the last). The radical version of commit-on-switch: every run _is already_ an entry from the moment it starts, so an unlogged run is structurally impossible. Not adopted (a legal entry needs a matter, an Info, and a rate — birth-as-entry would litter the WIP store with unbillable stubs), but the principle survives translated: **a run must never be silently lost** — held is the platform's equivalent of Toggl's already-saved.
10. **The day's list and its running total stay visible beside the timer** — timing and "where am I today" are one surface, not two. Adopted: the sheet shows the whole day's rows beside the live one (§5.1), with the day's totals always in the footer (§5.5).

Sources: [Clio Help — Time Entries](https://help.clio.com/hc/en-us/articles/9289741706779-Time-Entries) · [Clio — Is it Possible to Have Multiple Timers Running at Once?](https://support.clio.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000159554-Is-it-Possible-to-Have-Multiple-Timers-Running-at-Once-) · [Clio — Using Timers](https://support.clio.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003090754-Using-Timers-in-Clio) · [PracticePanther — How do I keep track of multiple time entries?](https://support.practicepanther.com/en/articles/1362748-how-do-i-keep-track-of-multiple-time-entries) · [Harvest — Tracking time: Day view](https://support.getharvest.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048181892-Tracking-time-Day-view) · [Harvest — How does the idle timer work?](https://support.getharvest.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048685231-How-does-the-idle-timer-work-for-the-Mac-and-Windows-apps) · [Toggl Track — Creating a Time Entry](https://support.toggl.com/en/articles/1957597-creating-a-time-entry) · [Toggl Track — The Timer Page](https://support.toggl.com/en/articles/2015973-the-timer-page).

## 4. The model: one live run, many held runs

### Recommendation

**Single-active stays; the manager adds a held-run set.** At most one run is `running` at any moment. Starting or resuming a run **auto-pauses** the current one — never discards it, never commits it. A day is therefore a set of runs across matters, exactly one live, the rest held (`paused` or `stopped`), each keeping its own accumulated elapsed time, matter binding, and narrative. Switching between matters is one click on the target run.

### True concurrent timers — rejected

- **Arithmetic honesty.** One person's concurrent timers sum to more than wall-clock time; the platform would be manufacturing billable hours. Every hour of the day attributed to at most one matter is the property that makes the day's total mean something — and it is the property a fee-audit reads for.
- **The platform's timer is single-active by design** — the DO serializes exactly this invariant, the slice 3·1 design records it as a decision, and offline reconciliation (drafts, never a second live timer) is built on it.
- Clio, the owner's own reference, refuses concurrency for the same reason and ships the pause-stack instead.

### Switch-commits-the-previous (each switch mints a draft entry) — rejected as the default

Committing on every switch fragments an interrupted day into confetti: six returns to the draft become six one-line entries on _Pemberton_ needing manual merge, and each commit demands Info at the worst moment — mid-interruption. Holding the run and **resuming accumulates into the same segment**, producing one entry per matter per sitting, logged when the person is ready — the accumulate-don't-fragment behavior Harvest ships as restart, and the auto-pause-on-new-timer stack PracticePanther ships on every page (§3). Toggl's no-pause model (every run is born an entry) is the one competitor that structurally cannot leak an unlogged run, but only because it demands nothing at birth; a legal entry's matter/Info/rate obligations make held-then-logged the honest equivalent. (Under round three's auto-save this argument strengthens: a complete row is already a draft when the switch happens, and resuming still accumulates into that same row rather than fragmenting it.)

### What a "segment" is

One **run** = one manager row = one prospective time entry. Pause/resume cycles within a run accumulate into it (the DO already stores `pausedIntervals`). Stop ends the run's clock; **log** commits it as one draft `time_entry` (`source: "timer"`) through the existing two-phase idempotent path. A day's history across matters = the held/live runs plus the runs already logged today.

## 5. The time-sheet spreadsheet (round three — the governing ruling)

Clicking the chip opens a **near-full-viewport modal** (capped per the settled dialog rules — only the table region scrolls, the modal's chrome is never pushed off; supersedes round two's mid-size modal). It carries no instruction text — the sheet teaches by being a sheet. Header: title, a **quiet period selector** (‹ Today · Wed, Aug 26 › — the reduced remnant of any `/time` link, per the owner: _"this is the time sheet"_), undo, filter-clear, the columns menu, close. Footer: the day's honest totals (§5.5) and one quiet line of truth: "Rows save as drafts the moment they are complete — the review chain lives on the matter's Time tab."

### 5.1 Rows are entries; a timer is one way a duration cell gets filled

The runs-are-rows unification: **a row IS a time entry** (existing draft, or one being typed), and a timer is simply a live feed into one row's Duration cell. The running row ticks in place (accent, tabular); pausing freezes it; starting the timer on another row auto-pauses the current one (§4, unchanged). Rows carry the play/pause control, open-the-full-entry, and delete in a trailing Actions cell, **all rendered at rest** (§5.8.4) and **disabled-says-why** on locked rows. There is no separate start-a-timer affordance and no Started column (owner: _"no one cares when time started"_).

### 5.2 Every cell edits in place, spreadsheet-style

All cells are inputs at rest (quiet until hover/focus), Enter commits, Escape abandons:

- **Work date** — settable, defaults today (the day the work is FOR; `work_date`). Beside it, **Date entered** — automatic, never edited, available as a column (off by default, the settled two-dates ruling TL8).
- **Matter** — a **typable combobox**: type a number or any part of the title, pick from the filtered list; an exact or unique match commits on Enter/blur. Setting the matter resolves the rate.
- **Description** — the entry's billed text, and it **gets its own line**: each row renders on two lines — the data columns on the first, the description spanning the full width beneath (it is long and important). Both lines are one row to hover, selection, and the keyboard (one `tbody` per entry); heights stay consistent. Inline editing works on the second line exactly as in any cell.
- **Duration** — typable as decimal hours (`1.2`), `h:mm` (`1:12`), `h:mm:ss`, or minutes (`45m`); the ticking cell renders `h:mm:ss` so its display re-parses to exactly itself. **Editing a ticking cell pauses the timer first** — a cell cannot honestly hold a live feed and a hand edit at once.
- **Hours** — the duration rounded to the firm increment (tooltip states the rule). _Computed-only is superseded in round six (§5.8.3): the cell is typable, and typing it drives the duration back._
- **Rate** — a **dropdown, not a bare number** (owner, round four): a typable combobox over the **named rate cards** with each card's resolved figure ("$340.00 · Standard card — timekeeper rate"), plus a **Custom…** entry that takes a typed hourly rate as the audited per-entry override — the settled TD3 shape, provenance always named.
- **Total** — computed, and the **arithmetic is visible** on the cell: "0:42 → 0.7 h × $340.00 = $238.00".
- **Recorded by** — **changeable** (owner, round four; the delegation model is settled — TD9): a typable person combobox over the firm's timekeepers. Setting another person makes them the timekeeper, records the enterer automatically, keeps **both** people on the entry ("Marcus Reyes · entered by Rosalind Osei" on the description line), and **re-resolves the rate** through the rate chain for the new timekeeper (a custom override survives untouched). Initials avatar + always-visible name (TL6).
- **Internal Memo** — the settled private memo (TD10): never leaves the firm, never on a bill. ~~A column in the columns menu, **Soon-badged and disabled** against its API gap.~~ **Superseded in round seven (§5.9.1):** it is not a column at all but a third line on the row, revealed on demand — and it is wired for real, its API gap promoted to a delta to build (§9.10).
- **Status** — only two things ever render here (owner, round four: _"there is no difference between paused and draft, and we don't want draft"_): the **Running** chip on the one live row, and genuine **chain advances** (Submitted / Approved / Billed) with their locks. **The Paused and Draft chips are struck** — reasoning recorded: under auto-save everything visible is saved or shows its save state, so a Draft chip carries no information the save indicator doesn't; and a paused row's truth is simply that its duration is not ticking — a chip would dress a non-state. A **submitted/approved row locks**: cells read-only saying why, actions disabled with the same truth (TS3's chain, unchanged).

### 5.3 The fresh row, and auto-save

~~**The last row is always a fresh empty input row** — date defaulted to the day in view, everything else blank. Typing in it materializes the next entry, and another fresh row appears beneath: heads-down entry never reaches for a button.~~ **Superseded in round six (§5.8.8):** no blank row stands at rest; the sheet ends in an add-a-row button that creates the row and focuses it.

**Auto-save is the model** (owner ruling: _"time is automatically logged. As soon as it is entered, it goes in. It does not need approval to get saved."_): the moment a row is **complete** — matter + description + a positive duration — it persists as a **draft** entry. Saving is not submission; the review gate, when on, still runs on the matter tab. **Each row shows its database state** in a leading indicator: a quiet check (**saved**, tooltip naming the matter), a spinner (**saving…**), or a warning mark (**not saved yet — needs a matter / a description / a duration**, named exactly). A lock glyph marks saved-and-submitted. Nothing is ever silently unsaved: the chip's badge counts unsaved rows, and the footer names them.

### 5.4 History, deletion, and carried-over rows

- **One full undo/redo history** (owner, round four: _"no undo stack for deleting data"_ — widened to every change): each cell edit, delete, timekeeper change, rate change, dialog save, and new-entry creation enters a single **undo/redo stack**. Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) redoes; the toolbar's undo/redo controls **say what they will do** ("Undo edit of duration on 2026-0147"), the footer echoes the top of the stack, and the deletion toast carries an Undo action. This supersedes round two's confirm-first discard and round three's delete-only stack. History is client-session state (§9.7); undo of a saved change writes back through the same auto-save path.
- **Carried-over rows — the "Earlier" band is removed** (owner, round four item 10; choice recorded): with the two-date columns, search, and filters in place, a carried-over unsaved row is simply **a row** — its real work date is visible in the Work date column, the save-state mark names exactly what is missing, and it sorts and filters like everything else. The date column now says what the band was trying to say; a separate warning band restated it louder and interrupted the sheet. The rows still surface on every later day until completed or deleted, and completing one saves it **on its own date**.

### 5.5 Search, full table machinery, and the day's totals

- **Search** (owner, round four): a search field in the modal's header filters rows by description, matter number or title, and person (timekeeper or enterer). It **composes with the column filters** (a row must match both), takes the active-filter accent treatment when in play, clears from its own control, and shares the filtered-empty pattern — the empty table names search and filters together and offers one clear.
- The sheet keeps the one-table system in full: sortable columns, **in-header right-aligned set filters** with per-value counts and the always-present filter-clear, **drag-to-reorder**, **drag-to-resize** with double-click fit, and a **columns menu** (hide/show any non-anchored column — Date entered and Internal Memo live there, off by default).
- The footer states the day in view honestly: **"Worked 4:47 · billed 4.8 h · value $1,496.00 · 3 rows not saved yet"** — server-computed when the API lands; the unsaved count is the nag.

### 5.6 The row's third altitude: the stacked entry dialog (its shape settled in round seven — §5.9.7)

The three-altitude ruling reaches the sheet (owner, round four): **cell → row → full record**. Double-clicking a row's non-interactive area, or its explicit open control, raises the **full entry dialog stacked above the Time Keeper modal** — viewport-capped per the dialog rules, properly stacked (the sheet stays visible and live beneath), carrying every field at full size: matter, work date, timekeeper (with the delegated-entry hint), category, activity code, the description, the Internal Memo (Soon), the arithmetic cluster, and both dates ("Work date Aug 26 · recorded Aug 26 — automatic"). Save & Close writes back through the same auto-save path and returns to the sheet with the row updated; a locked row opens read-only, its footer stating the chain reason. Closing never destroys sheet state.

The mockup structures all of this as an extractable module (`tk`-prefixed ids, `window.tkMoveColumn` surface) for the concurrent unification stream merging the time mockups; the phrase "what was done" is corrected to **"Description"** everywhere (round four, item 11).

### 5.7 Round five — save-with-nothing, the time code, and twelve more

1. **Save without anything** (owner: _"otherwise we risk data loss"_): an entry persists **the moment it exists** — no matter, no description, no duration required. Round four's "not saved yet — needs a matter" refusal becomes an **incomplete state on a saved row**: the save indicator's warning mark names what is missing ("Saved — incomplete: needs a matter. It stays here until completed."), the warning stripe carries it at a glance, the chip badge and footer count incomplete rows, and incomplete rows from earlier days surface in every later scope until completed or deleted. This **supersedes M11** — the owner decided it the other way, and the D4 concern survives as the incomplete nag rather than a save-blocker.
2. **The approval vibe goes** (owner, verbatim, recorded as a **governing stance**): _"we don't need a submitted for review kind of vibe. I think we have overdone the checks and balances on submitting things for approval."_ In the Time Keeper: no submission affordances, no Submitted fixtures or chips, no review language anywhere — entries are just entries. A row that has genuinely advanced in the chain elsewhere still renders its lock honestly, but this surface never solicits submission. Platform reading: the review gate stays firm-configurable, **default OFF**, and surfaces stop foregrounding it — a direction for every time surface, not just this one. (The Status column is gone with the chips — the running row reads through its accent stripe, ticking duration, and the top-bar chip; recorded call.)
3. **The immutable time code, and its column.** Every entry carries a code minted at creation and never changed. The permanent code is the settled TL7 entry number — **matter number . four-digit sequence** (`2026-0147.0042`). A **matterless entry gets a provisional code at birth** (`TMP-0114`, a firm-scoped temporary sequence) so save-with-nothing still yields an addressable record; the **permanent code mints the moment the matter is set**, the provisional is retired but recorded on the entry ("was TMP-0114"), and **both are immutable once minted** — a later matter edit never re-mints. The column renders mono, provisional visibly provisional (dashed underline, warning tone, stated title).
4. **Matter and title are one column**: the cell presents `2026-0147 · Pemberton v. Hale Construction Co.`, and the combobox matches **either** the number or any part of the name.
5. **Duration accepts both forms**: `1:30` and `1.5` parse to the same ninety minutes (plus `h:mm:ss` and `45m`); the committed display normalizes to one form.
6. **One date format everywhere**: Work date, Date entered, and the dialog's dates line all render `MM/DD/YYYY`, matching the native date inputs.
7. **The left status stripe**: a 3-px edge on every row, spanning both lines and the full wrapped height — success = saved-and-complete, warning = incomplete, accent = running, info = chain-locked; semantic tokens throughout.
8. **Search is a button** (the file manager's S2 expand-from-icon pattern): collapsed to its icon, expanding on choice with the placeholder just "Search", collapsing again when left empty; active search keeps it open with the accent treatment; composes with the column filters unchanged.
9. **The date scope** sits at the top: Today (default) / Yesterday / This week / a custom range, scoping the rows and the footer totals (the caption names the scope); incomplete rows from before the scope always surface — they are the reason save-with-nothing exists. _Round six corrects the presets' spans (§5.8.5) and makes the scope widen itself for a row just entered (§5.8.6)._
10. **Entered by is a proper column** — off by default (recorded call), but it **shows itself automatically whenever a visible row is delegated**, so dual attribution is never hidden; the round-four inline row annotation is gone. Recorded by stays the primary person column.
11. **Long descriptions wrap** — the description line grows to as many lines as it needs (a self-sizing field; no horizontal overflow ever), and the stripe and row model span the wrapped height. Recorded call: **no clamp** — billed text is bounded in practice, and truncating it hides exactly what the owner asked to see.
12. **Full screen**: a maximize/restore toggle beside close — _"so it isn't so much a modal as it is the full screen"_ — flipping between the capped modal and a full-viewport takeover; the table takes the height; the state is remembered for the session; Escape still closes.
13. **Files on an entry** (_"so that you can specifically state what it was you were working on"_): a Files column (off by default) rendering compact open-the-document chips, and a typable picker in the entry dialog over the **matter's** documents. More than one file per entry is allowed (recorded call — the association is many-to-many by nature). A matterless entry cannot pick files yet, and the field says why. API gap named in §9.8.
14. **Spell-check**: the shipping v1 is **native browser spellcheck turned on** for the description and memo fields — real squiggles, zero backend, nothing mocked. Anything richer (a firm dictionary, a legal-terms lexicon, suggestions in our own UI) is a **Soon seam** whose dependency is a platform spell-check service that does not exist yet; no UI pretends otherwise.

### 5.8 Round six — the Time Sheet: the name, two defects, and the deliberate row

Nine corrections. Two are defects with a shared shape — a fact stated in one place and computed in another, free to drift — and both are fixed by removing the second copy rather than by correcting it.

1. **The surface is THE TIME SHEET.** The name is carried everywhere the surface speaks: the masthead, the document title, the accessible names, the chip's tooltips ("Click to open the Time Sheet"), and the mockup's own chrome. "Time Keeper" appears nowhere in the build. **Recorded call:** two names deliberately do _not_ change — this spec's filename, so its history stays put, and the mockup's `tk`-prefixed ids and `timer/timer-manager.html` path, which §10 hands the unification stream as an extraction target. Renaming an extraction surface mid-stream costs a merge and buys nothing the masthead does not already say. The word _timekeeper_ meaning **the person whose work an entry is** is untouched — it is domain vocabulary (TD9), not the surface's name.

2. **Every editable cell's input fills its cell.** _The defect:_ a long matter title was cut off. _Root cause:_ each cell's input carried its own hardcoded pixel width — the matter field 236 px, duration 80, rate 92, timekeeper 118, memo 110 — while the table laid out automatically. A resizable column and a fixed-width field are two different widths: the **field** was doing the clipping, and widening the column could not fix it because the input never grew. _The fix removes the second width:_ the table lays out **fixed** with a measured width per column, `.cellin` is `width: 100%`, and no input carries a width of its own — so the column's width **is** the input's width, and dragging the column's grip drives the field (verified: a 200 px column renders a 230 px-cell field, a 560 px column a 544 px one). Every editable cell was audited at 1600×900 and 1152×648 with **all** columns shown — 64 fields, none clipped. The audit found a second instance the eye had missed: a **native date input has its own intrinsic width** (142 px with this surface's padding) that no CSS width can shrink below, so the Work date column is now sized from that measurement rather than from the text; at the old width the day was silently cut at narrow viewports. A third, vertical: the self-sizing description was short by exactly its own 2 px border, clipping the descenders of its last line, because the field is `border-box` and the border was never added back.

3. **The Hours column is typable, and reconciles with Duration BOTH WAYS.** Both forms parse — `1.5` and `1:30` are the same ninety minutes (round five's parser, item 5, now serving two cells). Duration → Hours is unchanged (the duration rounded to the firm increment). Hours → Duration **snaps the typed figure to the increment and sets the duration to its exact equivalent**, so the two cells can never show a quantity and a duration that disagree. Editing either one **pauses a running timer first** (§5.2's rule, now binding on both), and the two cells plus the Total re-read one quantity after every change — never the cell being typed in. **Recorded call:** typed hours snap rather than being carried at arbitrary precision — a billed quantity that is not on the firm's increment is not a quantity the firm can bill, and the alternative (1.53 h displaying as 1.5 over a 1:31:48 duration) is the same two-copies drift as the defects above.

4. **Row actions render AT REST.** Per-row controls are never hover-revealed — the file manager's settled I4 ("rows do not reflow on hover; the icon buttons are always present"). Verified by measurement, not by eye: with the row un-hovered all three controls are present and visible, and the row group's height and the action cluster's width are **identical** hovered and not, at both viewports.

5. **Every preset's span matches its name.** _The defect:_ "This week" showed three days. _Root cause:_ a preset existed as three unconnected literals — the menu's label text, the handler's `from`/`to`, and the caption — so the label said `This week · Aug 24–26` while the handler scoped Monday **through today**. Neither was wrong on its own; they were simply free to disagree, and did. _The fix removes the copies:_ one `scopePreset(kind)` computes a preset's name and its span **together**, and the menu, the handler, the caption and the scope control all read that one value — a preset can no longer say one thing and scope another. **This week** is now the **firm's week from its week-start setting** (Monday here, a firm setting the preset reads), spanning the whole week: Aug 24–30, seven days. Every preset verified against its name: Today = 1 day, Yesterday = 1 day, This week = 7. **Recorded call:** no "Last week" preset was added — the ruling was to make the presets honest, not to grow the set; a custom range already covers it.

6. **The range widens itself; it never hides a row the person just made.** If a newly created or edited row's work date falls outside the scope in view, **the scope widens to include it** — on re-dating a row, on saving the entry dialog, and on creating a row dated outside the range. The scope control shows the widened span and the footer caption says why ("Aug 26–Sep 4 — widened to include the row you just re-dated on Sep 4"), with a toast at the moment it happens. Choosing a preset or a custom range deliberately replaces the scope and clears the note. The reasoning: a row that saves and then vanishes is the data-loss shape §5.7.1 exists to close, wearing different clothes — the sheet must not answer an entry by hiding it.

7. **A New entry button at the top opens the DEDICATED single-entry screen.** A labelled primary control in the modal's header. It creates the row exactly as the grid does — a real entry from that instant, provisional code minted, persisted — and then raises the **stacked full-entry dialog** (§5.6) on it instead of putting the cursor in the grid: the path for when the whole form is wanted rather than heads-down grid entry. Closing the dialog without saving leaves the row in the sheet, incomplete and named as such; that is save-with-nothing (§5.7.1) working, not a leak.

8. **The always-empty bottom row is replaced by an add-a-row button.** No blank row stands at rest. The sheet **ends in a control** — one click creates the row and focuses its Matter cell, and the row is a real entry from that instant. Save-with-nothing is unchanged; what changed is that a row is now created **deliberately** rather than standing empty in wait. The materialize-on-first-keystroke path goes with it, and with it a whole class of state: there is no longer such a thing as a visible row that is not yet a record, so the save indicator, the status stripe, the time code, the actions cell and the incomplete count now mean the same thing on every row in the sheet. (The empty sheet is therefore just the button, which is the honest empty state.)

9. **The footer line is struck**: "Everything here is saved the moment it exists — incomplete rows stay until completed." Every row carries its save state and its incompleteness at a glance, and the footer already counts the incomplete ones; the sentence only restated them louder. The same species as X3's lecture sweep.

**Carried in round six, not an owner correction — recorded for review.** The entry dialog's four dropdowns (matter, staff, category, activity code) were native `<select>`s. TC1/X4 are binding and unambiguous — _every_ dropdown on these surfaces is a typable combobox — and round six's item 7 promotes that dialog from a drill-down to a **primary creation path**, so the gap stopped being cosmetic. They are now typable comboboxes on the settled shape: typing filters, the chevron shows everything, Enter commits the highlighted choice, Escape restores the committed value (and closes only the list, leaving the dialog alone). Fixing them surfaced a defect the grid shared: focusing a **filled** combobox and typing **appended** to its committed label, so the filter matched nothing and the field committed garbage. Focus now selects the committed text on every combobox and quantity cell, grid and dialog alike — a spreadsheet's type-over, and the only behavior under which a combobox's own filter can work.

### 5.9 Round seven — the memo line, the record window, and two defects

Six corrections and one binding ruling. The ruling (item 7) is the largest: it settles that this surface's record window is not its own design.

1. **The internal memo gets the description's treatment, on demand.** The memo is a long line too, so it gets the same full-width wrapping treatment — **a third line on the row, underneath the description**. It is **not a column**: most rows never carry one, and a column mostly empty is a column paying rent. A small button at the **far right of the description line** adds or reveals it; a row with a memo **shows** it, a row without shows only the button, and an opened-but-unwritten memo collapses back to the button rather than leaving a blank line behind. **It is wired for real, not Soon-badged** — written on the row or in the record window, kept on the record, carried in the event history, and searchable (this surface is the firm's own; a memo nobody can find is a memo written twice). Its meaning is intact and _demonstrated_, not asserted: the memo renders only inside nodes marked `data-internal-only`, and the record's **print sheet — the client-facing rendering — does not carry it**, which the verification asserts directly against a real print preview. The Soon badge is gone because the badge was honest only while the field was inert; the API delta it waited on is now recorded as a delta to build (§9.10).
2. **The undo/redo stack moves into dropdowns.** The bottom-right line naming what the undo would be is **struck** — a toolbar is not where a stack belongs, and one line could only ever name the top of it. Undo and redo become **split controls**: the button does one step, its caret opens the stack. Each entry in the list says what that step **was** ("edit of hours on 2026-0153.0019"), and choosing the Nth walks all N steps at once, so several changes can be seen and jumped back through rather than clicked away one at a time. Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y still take one step.
3. **Defect — "Entered by" rendered empty.** _Root cause:_ `via` was modelled as **"the delegate, when it differs"** — set only when someone entered for someone else, `null` otherwise. But TD9's settled model is that the record keeps **BOTH people, always**: the timekeeper whose work it is, and the enterer who typed it. Under the old model a column titled "Entered by" was blank on seven of eight fixture rows and on every entry a person records for themselves — the column was structurally empty for the common case, which is exactly what the owner saw. _The fix is to the model, not the cell:_ `via` is now **the enterer, always populated** (the actor at creation, re-recorded on every edit that changes the timekeeper), so the column carries a name on every row. **Delegation becomes a comparison** — `via !== who` — which is what marks the enterer's avatar, what the tooltip distinguishes ("delegated entry; both people stay on the entry" versus "who is also the timekeeper"), and what still auto-shows the column (round five item 10, unchanged). The record window states both people in its Staff hint and on its record line, and the print sheet carries both.
4. **The custom range applies as it is picked.** The Apply button is gone: choosing a start and an end re-scopes the sheet immediately, and the caption and the scope control follow. The menu **stays open** while the range is adjusted, so the presets above it remain the visible way back — that, plus a preset always returning to a named scope, is the escape hatch. A range that reads backwards is refused **in place**, in the menu's own note, rather than by a toast that closes what the person is using.
5. **Save & Close and Save & New.** The record window's single primary is replaced by the settled pair (TD5b). **Save & New** keeps the window open on a fresh entry, **carrying the context forward** — work date, matter, staff, category, activity code, and the resolved rate — and clearing what belonged to the entry just saved: description, memo, duration, attachments. Focus lands in Description. Cancel closes without saving.
6. **The row actions pin.** Round six's own note recorded that the sheet scrolls ~190 px sideways at 1152 once every column is shown; a control you must scroll to reach is a control you do not have. The actions cluster is now **sticky to the row's trailing edge** (the settled column-pinning machinery), opaque, carrying a hairline so it reads as pinned, with the header's own cell pinned too or the column would slide out from under its rows. Verified by scrolling the table fully both ways at 1152: the cluster stays wholly inside the viewport at either end and everywhere between.
7. **BINDING, cross-surface — the record window is ONE component.** The owner, verbatim: _"the time entry screen we have here really should match the other time history screen with history and attachments and the like."_ The Time Sheet's stacked full-entry dialog **is** the matter Time tab's record modal, lifted from the shared WIP template rather than authored a second time — the two-pane shell, its tab set, and its header actions, with the class names kept verbatim so the app has one component to build:
   - **LEFT — the record:** the changeable facts, dense and top-aligned. Description first (it wraps and grows), then work date / matter / staff, then category / activity / time worked, then the one-line arithmetic strip (billed × rate = amount) and its rounding note, then the internal memo.
   - **RIGHT — context in tabs:** **History** in its own scrollable tab (the event rail: what happened, an absolute stamp, who did it, any recorded detail), **Attachments** (as many files as the work produced, each removable, from **two sources** — the matter's own documents first, upload second — detaching never deleting), and **Billing** (the disposition, the arithmetic, and the honest "not on a bill yet"; no chain and no review language, per §5.7.2).
   - **HEADER:** edit, print, full screen, close — in that order, as the shell's own controls so no record window can be missing one. Edit renders disabled with its reason here, because a Time Sheet entry is always already editable; that is the shared template's own behavior for a workable draft, not a local variation.
   - **An entry with attachments opens on them**, so the file is in front of the reader rather than one click away.
   - **The one genuine difference is a field, not a window.** The Time Sheet is cross-matter, so its record carries a **Matter** field the matter tab's copy does not. That difference lives in the record's field list, where differences belong.

## 7. Hygiene under auto-save

Auto-save (round three) dissolves most of the old stopped-unlogged leak: a bound, described run **is already a draft** the moment it exists. What remains, and how the sheet keeps it honest:

- The leak shrinks to **incomplete rows** — measured time missing a matter or a description. They render with the warning save-state mark naming exactly what is missing, surface under "Earlier — not saved yet" on every later day (§5.4), count into the chip's badge (§6), and appear in the footer's "N rows not saved yet." They cannot age out.
- **No fabrication to force a save**: the sheet never invents a description or picks a matter to make a row saveable (the timesheet design's D4 argument, restated for auto-save — see M11 for the owner's call on Info-less drafts).
- **Deletion is honest and reversible** — one click, named in the toast, on the undo stack (§5.4). Honest deletion beats a silent leak; undo beats a scolding confirm.
- The **inverse leak** — a timer left ticking over lunch — remains the idle-detection question (M9, Harvest's keep/trim pattern): deferred, but the duration cell is where its resolution will render, beside the abandoned-run resolution that already exists.

## 9. API deltas (revised for the auto-save model; supersedes the round-two list)

In dependency order:

1. **Auto-save = draft-create-on-entry, then PATCH-per-cell-commit.** A row that becomes complete issues a normal `time-entries` create (`source: "timer"` when a timer fed it, `"quick_add"`/`"manual"` otherwise), idempotent on a client-minted row id so a retry never doubles an entry; each later cell commit is a draft `PATCH` (the route exists — draft-only, owner-only), debounced per row. **Per-row save-state** is client truth derived from the request lifecycle — `unsaved` (incomplete, or not yet acknowledged), `saving` (request in flight), `saved` (acknowledged) — no new server state; the server's only new obligation is that create and PATCH stay cheap enough to fire per cell commit (they are — single-row writes through the chokepoint).
2. **Runs become rows: the DO slims down.** The DO no longer needs a plural run store carrying matter/narrative — those live on the draft entry row. It holds **tick state only**: `{ liveRunId, entryId?, startedAt, pausedIntervals, lastActivityAt }` for the one live feed, attached to a row by entry id. Start-on-row auto-pauses the current feed (single-active, §4 unchanged — this still replaces today's silent-discard `start`); pausing writes the accumulated seconds onto the row (a duration PATCH keyed by `liveRunId`, riding the existing two-phase idempotency), after which the paused row needs nothing from the DO. Round two's plural-run migration therefore **shrinks to a single live feed with an entry-id pointer** — materially less DO work.
3. **The sheet's read** — the timekeeper-day listing (exists) **plus matter number + title denormalized** and the entry's `source`; the personal-timesheet design's range-listing delta covers it. `timer/read` stays the chip's cheap poll and gains the **unsaved-row count** (a cheap incomplete-drafts read, or client-computed).
4. **Rate override per entry** — already in the capture/PATCH contract (`overrideRateE4`); the sheet's Rate cell uses it; who may override remains the standing TP4 question.
5. **Delete + undo** — delete is the draft delete/void the API already allows; **undo is client-side re-create through the same idempotent create** (a restored row is a new row with the same facts). No server undo machinery.
6. **Submitted/approved locks** — already enforced server-side; the sheet renders the 409 `frozen-entry` truth _before_ the request, per the settled chain rules.
7. **Round-four notes.** (a) **Internal Memo** stays a registered API gap (the time-rulings register already carries it): the column ships Soon-badged and disabled until the entry contract serves and accepts the memo — no new delta, the existing one now has a second consumer. (b) The **undo/redo history is client-session state**, never a server feature: undo/redo of saved changes replays through the same idempotent create/PATCH path (an undone delete is a re-create; an undone edit is a PATCH back), so the server sees only ordinary writes and the audit trail records each replay honestly as a change. (c) The **stacked entry dialog** adds no API: it reads and writes the same row through the same contracts, and its stacking is a client-only layering contract (sheet modal below, entry dialog above, both viewport-capped; closing the dialog must never disturb sheet state). (d) **Changing the timekeeper** rides the delegated-entry contract the rulings settled (TD9): capture/PATCH accepts a foreign `timekeeperUserId` under the delegation permission, the actor is recorded as enterer, and the rate re-resolves server-side for the new timekeeper — the sheet's client-side re-resolve is a preview of that authoritative result.

8. **Round-five deltas — the first real schema/contract changes this spec asks for:**
   - **Save-with-nothing** requires the capture contract (and `time_entry`) to accept an **entry with no matter, no narrative, and zero duration**: `matter_id` becomes **nullable** (superseding the "binding required at commit" rule for sheet-born entries — the scratch state moves from the DO into the store, which is exactly the owner's data-loss point), `narrative` drops its `min(1)`, and a zero `duration_minutes` is legal pre-completion. Billing safety is structural: billing reads select by matter + status, and an incomplete row has no matter to bill against; a completeness predicate (`matter_id IS NOT NULL AND narrative != '' AND duration_minutes > 0`) gates every downstream consumer.
   - **The time code**: the permanent code is TL7's matter-scoped entry number, minted server-side (via `@magna-carta/sequence-numbering`) at first matter-bind; a **provisional firm-scoped code** (`TMP-nnnn` sequence) is minted at create for matterless entries and recorded permanently beside its replacement (`provisional_code` column). Both immutable at the service layer.
   - **File associations**: net-new — nothing links a `time_entry` to a file today. Shape: an association table **`time_entry_file` (`time_entry_id`, `file_id`, `added_at`, `added_by`)** — many-to-many, auditable, deletable without touching the entry row — not a file-id list column. Served on the sheet listing as `{fileId, name}` pairs (denormalized name, no N+1); constrained to files on the entry's matter.
   - **Spell-check**: no API. Native browser spellcheck is v1; a platform spell-check service (firm dictionary, legal lexicon) is a named Soon seam.

9. **Round six — three notes, no new contract.** Items 1–5 and 7–9 are client-side: a name, a layout rule, a parse-and-reconcile between two cells of one quantity, a resting control, a date computation, a second entry point onto the same row, and a struck sentence. The two that touch the notes above:
   - **When the create fires (revises (1)).** Round five moved the create to the row's birth; round six moves **birth itself** to a deliberate act — the add-a-row button or New entry — instead of the first keystroke in a standing blank row. The contract is unchanged (an idempotent create on a client-minted row id, matterless and narrative-less), but the client now issues exactly one create per row the person asked for, and never one speculatively on a keystroke. The provisional `TMP-nnnn` code is minted at that same instant, so a row cannot exist unaddressed even for a moment.
   - **The listing's range must be re-askable mid-session (touches (3)).** The scope-widening rule (§5.8.6) means the range the sheet reads is **not fixed at open**: entering or re-dating a row outside the current range widens it and the sheet re-reads. The personal-timesheet design's range-listing delta already serves this; the note is that the widened range is a normal re-request, not a special case, and that the footer totals recompute against the widened range (the server-computed totals of §5.5 take the same range parameter).
   - **The firm's week-start is a read the preset needs.** (round six) "This week" spans the firm's own week (§5.8.5), so the sheet reads the firm's week-start setting — a firm-settings value, not a new time contract. It is a settings read the client already has cause to make; recorded here so the preset is not built against a hardcoded Monday.

10. **Round seven — the memo becomes a delta to build, and the record window names three reads.** Items 2, 4, 5 and 6 are client-side (a dropdown, a live-applied range, a second save verb, a sticky cell). The rest:
    - **The internal memo, promoted from gap to delta (§5.9.1).** It was a registered API gap the column wore a Soon badge against; the owner has ruled it real on this surface and on the unified artifact at once, so it is now a delta to build, in the same shape on both: **`time_entry.internal_memo`, nullable text**, served on the listing and settable on `PATCH`. Nothing else changes — it is one nullable column and one field on an existing contract. Two consumers, one shape, so the surfaces cannot drift. **Confidentiality is a contract obligation, not only a UI one:** the memo must never be selected into any bill-facing or print-facing read — invoice line composition, the LEDES export, the client portal, and the printed entry sheet — and the natural enforcement is that those reads name their columns rather than selecting the row whole. This supersedes §9.7(a).
    - **Dual attribution is now load-bearing on the listing (§5.9.3).** The enterer is rendered on **every** row, not only delegated ones, so `enteredByUserId` (with its denormalized display name, per TK1's rule that a name is history) becomes a listing field the sheet always reads rather than one it needs only sometimes. This raises the priority of the standing dual-attribution gap; it does not change its shape.
    - **The record window's History tab needs the per-entry audit read.** The rulings register already carries it as a gap (no endpoint serves an entry's events with actor and instant); the shared record modal is now a second concrete consumer, and the shape it wants is exactly what the register describes — `{what, at, who, detail?}`, oldest first. Until it exists the tab renders the client's own session events honestly rather than inventing a server history.
    - **Attachments need an upload path, not only an association.** Round five's `time_entry_file` association table covers "associate the matter's existing document". The shared Attachments tab offers **two sources**, so the second one — upload a file from this computer, which lands in the matter's files **and** attaches — needs the documents-upload path to accept a matter target and return the new file's id for association. One extra call in the client's hands, no new table.

Nothing else touches the WIP store's invariants, rounding arithmetic, rate resolution, or the entry lifecycle — beyond (2), (8) and (10) the sheet is a client over exactly the machinery that exists.

## 10. Relationship to the personal-timesheet design

The 2026-08-25 personal-timesheet design planned `/time` as a page: a period list first, a capture grid later, with the header chip as a small capture-anywhere surface. Round three collapses that geometry for the personal scope: **this modal IS the capture grid that spec's phase plan wanted** — reachable from anywhere, spreadsheet-fast, auto-saving into the same WIP store — and it absorbs the timesheet identity ("this is the time sheet"). Reconciliation:

- **Its API deltas survive intact** (range listing with matter titles; period totals): this sheet consumes them for the day view and the period selector.
- **Its list-view review role** (multi-week correction, submission under the review gate, the `time:viewFirm` switcher) is _not_ rebuilt in the modal — the sheet is one person × one day in view. Whether that review surface still ships as a `/time` page, or as a period-scoped view of this same modal, is now an owner decision (M10).
- **Its week grid (days × matters cells)** remains the distinct bulk-catch-up idea; the sheet's fresh-row entry covers the daily case, and the grid's fate rides M10.
- The three-place model's surviving boundary: the **matter Time tab** is still the matter's record and the review chain's home, and this sheet never grows approval machinery.

The concurrent unification stream merging the time mockups should treat this modal as the capture surface of record; the mockup is structured for extraction (`tk`-prefixed ids, a `tkMoveColumn` module surface).

**The record window is shared with that stream, by ruling (§5.9.7).** The Time Sheet's copy is lifted from the unified artifact's `recordModal` — the same two-pane shell, tab set, header actions and class names — so the port builds **one** component, mounted twice with different field lists, not two windows that happen to resemble each other. Any behavior gap between the Time Sheet's record window and the matter Time tab's is a defect by definition, exactly as TB2 makes a gap between two tables a defect. The Time Sheet's own difference (it is cross-matter, so its record carries a Matter field) is a parameter of the field list, not a fork of the component.

## 12. What this design does NOT do

- No approval machinery in the sheet — saving is not submission; the review chain lives on the matter's Time tab (locked rows render its truth).
- No change to entry-lifecycle invariants, rounding arithmetic, or rate resolution — auto-save writes through the existing capture/PATCH contracts.
- No idle-detection UI beyond surfacing the DO's existing abandoned flag and its keep/trim/discard resolution.
- No multi-week review or firm-wide (`time:viewFirm`) views in the modal — one person, one day in view; the broader review surface is M10.
- No desktop divergence — the sheet is the same surface on Tauri (feature parity; offline capture still reconciles as drafts).
