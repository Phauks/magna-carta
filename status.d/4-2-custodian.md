Slice 4·2·1 (the register) built: eleven per-firm tables, trigger-enforced append-only movement ledger, mandatory Directory ownership, allocation-ledger custody numbers, holds, and scan binding via a system holding matter. Slice 4·2·2 built the review-and-reporting half: review dates with an anniversary-anchored roll forward and a `reviewed` ledger entry, one query-shaped reminder emission point covering both reviews and overdue check-outs, three register reports (holdings per owner, occupancy per location, holds in force), and printable Code 128 label sheets — twenty-five routes in total. Slice 4·2·3 built gated release: a `released` movement must name who collected the property and carry the authority it leaves on, how the collector was identified, and the member who made that check, frozen on the append-only ledger row and enforced by three `BEFORE INSERT` triggers rather than by a CHECK, so releases recorded before the gate existed stay exactly as they were recorded. Two-signatory release is a per-firm policy, off by default, and the two signatories must be two people. The authority vocabulary is deliberately not invented — the basis is free text a firm writes. Receipts and portal visibility, the other half of 4·2·3, are not built: the document-assembly path is matter-routed and matter-permissioned, and no portal grant model exists. Slice 4·2·4's stocktake half is built: a firm can open a count over the whole register or one branch of the container tree, record observations in batches (a packet seen at a location, a packet looked for and not found, an object on the shelf with no register row), and close it producing a reconciliation of missing, misplaced, unexpected, and unregistered against what the register expected. **A stocktake observes and reports only** — nothing on the path writes the movement ledger, a packet, or an item; corrective movements are named as proposals a person records through the one movement write path, a missing original proposes nothing at all, and a source-scan fitness function holds that boundary. Sighting rows are append-only at the database with the movement ledger's three triggers plus a fourth refusing an observation against a closed count; closing freezes the findings so a reported number cannot drift, and an unfinished count is abandoned rather than closed. Seven routes over the existing two custody permissions, two per-firm tables, no new movement kind. The destruction workflow is built, now that the owner has ratified the shape flags. Raising a request, approving it, and recording that it was carried out are three calls made by at least two people: **the requester and the approver differ by construction**, enforced by database triggers as well as by the service — including the case a column check cannot see, where a request names no requester and any approver would otherwise satisfy it. A decided request is terminal and is re-raised rather than re-decided. Execution appends its `destroyed` entry through the one movement write path, so a destruction carries a person's name on the same append-only ledger as everything else, and the request row names the ledger entry it produced. **Two refusals carry no override anywhere:** a hold in force stops both the request and the execution (asked twice, because a hold placed between them is exactly what a hold is for), and an item recorded as an irreplaceable original is never destructible — that refusal now binds the ordinary movement path and the database itself, not only the workflow, so it holds for a write path nobody has written. No retention period, default, derived date, or sweep exists anywhere on the path. Five columns and nine triggers on the existing table; no new permission.

The will-deposit duty catalog is built with **Nevada as the first encoded jurisdiction**, following the trust-rules posture: an unencoded jurisdiction is REFUSED rather than answered, "not recorded" stays a different refusal from "not encoded", and there is no baseline. Nevada carries the NRS 136.050(1) 30-day delivery duty quoted verbatim with its citation, the clock starting at knowledge of the death, both delivery targets, the subsection 3 civil liability, and the public-record consequence of delivering to the clerk; the personal representative's own duty and the NRS 136.060 court-ordered production sit beside it, the latter carrying no interval because the court names the time. The verified absence of any lifetime court deposit is encoded as an explicit negative. County lodging (EDCR 4.40, Second Judicial District Rule 57.7) is keyed BELOW the state, and Nevada's other nine districts refuse on their own terms. The catalog carries what the statute says and never an interpretation: the research's open questions — constructive knowledge, "reasonable cause" case law — stay open, and a due date is computed only from a date a person recorded. **No packet column records a death**, so that computation ships as a seam. Deliberately not built and still owner decisions: the NRS 133 qualified-custodian role, the clerk-versus-personal-representative guidance surface, and the succession/transfer manifest.

The remaining ratified flags are wired: the location depth cap of eight is proven at eight and at nine on both write paths (C4), the ownership label on an owner link is validated against the firm's own catalog when it has configured one and against nothing when it has not (C5), and the matter close path gained an advisory-only notice — closing a matter surfaces the custody packets linked to it, reports a count and nothing identifying, and never blocks (C6), which is deliberately a separate mechanism from the close-precondition hook rather than a precondition with a softer flag.
