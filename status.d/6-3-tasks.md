`@magna-carta/tasks`: one `matter_task` row per task, edited in place, carrying what to do, whose it is, when it is due, whether it is still owed, the one task it waits on, and the matter's files it points at. `matter_task_document` links those files many-to-many on the `matter_note_document` model. `tasks:read`, `tasks:write` and `tasks:assign` seeded to the seats that do firm work — Billing included, because chasing a retainer replenishment is a billing clerk's own work, and a list that cannot hold it sends them to a spreadsheet.

**`matter_id` is nullable**, which is the opposite of the call `firm_event` made, and the divergence is the slice's central decision. The mechanical half of the calendar's argument does not apply to a column nullable from its first migration; the design half turns on where the records are read, and tasks have one surface either way — "what is on my plate" spans a member's client work and their firm work in one list, so two tables would mean unioning them back together on the read every member makes most often. The does-not-apply branch the calendar warned about is real and is answered by confinement: `taskVisibilityFragment` is the entire population of "may this member see this task", and every read composes it. A task on a matter is firm-visible within it; a task on none is private to its assignee and whoever created it.

**Nothing derives a date and there is no overdue column.** A task stores the deadline's own civil-date shape and resolves it through `packages/calendar/src/time-zone.ts`; overdue is computed where the reader is. The calendar's date-arithmetic source scan runs over this package, and a schema scan asserts no overdue column exists on either table — a task template with a "+14 days" offset is the docketing engine this platform refuses to build wearing a different hat.

**Archiving and deleting are both real, and cancelling is retired.** An archive keeps the task and everything it carries; a delete is for a task that should not exist. Cancellation said what archiving says and required a reason before it would say it, which is why no firm used it — the owner's ruling of 2026-09-14, on which the reason became optional. A task also moves along four rungs (To Do, In Progress, On Hold, Done), and archiving is orthogonal to them: a task is archived from wherever it stood and comes back there. `state` narrowed to whether the work is finished and the rungs arrived as an added `progress` column, because widening a CHECK on a table three others reference is the rebuild a durable object refuses at the commit (ADR-a-vocabulary-on-a-parent-table-grows-by-adding-a-column).

**Notifications:** four topics for two acts, split by whether the task is on a matter — the `calendar.firmEvent.*` split, forced by the two registry fitness rules about matter anchoring. The assignee needed a new audience: the design routed assignment to `designated`, which is the firm's trust-compliance designation and resolves to nobody when unconfigured, so as designed every task assignment would have delivered to no one, silently. `named-recipient` reads the member from the payload. `tasks` joined the category vocabulary and `matterTask` the subject kinds, two CHECKs and therefore two table rebuilds. Due-soon and overdue are declared and wired to nothing, beside the calendar's deadline points and for their reasons.

**Surfaces:** the matter's Tasks section and the firm-wide Tasks list at `/tasks` are one component reading two routes. Open tasks register a close **advisory** and never a precondition; closing a matter changes no task, and they drop out of the default list through a filter so reopening brings them back with no backfill.

**The matter plan is an offer, not a write.** A `matter_plan_template` names a matter type and holds ordered `matter_plan_task_template` steps; each step carries a title, a priority, an offset in days from the matter's anchor, a role to assign to, and at most one predecessor. Asking for a proposal is a **GET** that writes nothing — it reads the matter's anchor, adds the offsets, resolves each role against `matter_assignment`, and hands back tasks a person edits, drops and confirms. Acceptance is the POST, and it writes only the subset kept, in one batch, with every identifier minted first so a step whose predecessor was dropped simply waits on nothing. Provenance lands on `matter_task` as `plan_template_id` and `plan_task_template_id`, and **nothing reconciles afterwards**: editing a template never touches a task already accepted, because the tasks are the firm's account of the work and the template is only where they came from. `tasks:managePlans` seats authoring with Owner, Admin and Attorney; authoring lives under Settings, and the offer appears on the matter's Tasks section rather than in the new-matter flow, so a plan is chosen when the work is being planned rather than while the file is being opened.

**One named date-arithmetic exception.** `addCalendarDays` in `packages/tasks/src/validation.ts` is the only place in the package that adds days to a date, and it exists so the calendar's source scan can see it in exactly one spot. It is plain calendar addition — no holidays, no court rules, no weekend rolling — which is precisely why the result is a **proposal** a person reviews: a plan can and will put a task on Christmas Day, and the person accepting is the one who moves it. The anchor vocabulary stays at one value, `matter opened`, because every other candidate is the front half of a docketing rule this platform does not build.

**Where a task meets time.** One nullable `task_id` on `time_entry`, answering flag **T2** the way the scope study recommended: ONE task per entry, never a set. An entry split across two tasks needs an allocation of its minutes, and an allocation is a second ledger with its own rounding and its own disputes; the honest encoding is two entries. A join table stays an additive migration over this column if many-to-many is ever wanted, and the reverse is not, so the reversible direction is the one taken. `assertTaskOnMatter` is the whole enforcement of the rule SQLite cannot hold — the task must be on the entry's own matter — and it refuses three shapes under one code on the `file-not-on-matter` model: no such task, a task on another matter, and a task on NO matter, which is firm work with nothing to bill it to. A null `matter_id` there is a refusal, never a wildcard, so the firm's own work is timeless by construction.

The read is **minutes and never money** — what a task cost has a different audience and a different permission — and it is **batched by construction**, riding the task list's own `pageFrom` beside the attachments read rather than a route of its own. A standalone per-task endpoint was built first and removed rather than left uncalled. Whose time it counts is the reader's `time:viewFirm` answer arriving as data, and the scope travels beside the totals because the same two numbers mean two different things.

**The load profile earned its keep on the first run.** The task volume lays down the deep list, the firm's own matterless work and the linked time, and measures the four-term keyset cursor, both halves of the visibility fragment, the LIKE search under its narrowing, and the grouped time read. Page 20 costs less than page 1 (0.48 ms against 0.71 ms) and the time read is **one statement for a page of fifty**, asserted in the smoke test because no EXPLAIN can see that failure. It also found a real scan: `tasks.my-work-page-1` cannot seek, because the unnarrowed firm-wide read names no matter and no assignee and its visibility fragment is an OR spanning the presence of `matter_id` itself. Recorded by name with the number the trade was made against — 2.1 ms against 1.1 ms narrowed — and not fixed here, because a measurement change measures. Two pre-existing firm-window scans, `matterNote` and `matterTask`, were sitting unallowlisted and are now named with the same reason as the other eight.

**The seam an addon writes through, and no adapter.** The owner intends to mirror tasks to and from an outside tool, so four primitives ship now — each because it cannot be added afterwards. `matter_task_external_link` records that a task IS some object over there, with `system` as open text on the `directory_edge.edge_type` precedent so a second system is an addon rather than a migration. Its two uniqueness rules are **indexes rather than service checks**: the failure they prevent — two tasks fighting over one mirror — is produced by exactly the race a pre-check loses. `matter_task_deletion` is written in the delete's own batch, because a row that is gone cannot be enumerated afterwards and a mirror that never learns keeps the work alive somewhere the firm stopped looking; it carries identifiers and nothing else. `matter_task.updated_by` is echo suppression, stamped by every write path including the cascade that clears a successor's blocker, and required rather than optional in the signatures so the compiler named all six call sites.

**The feed carries two positions, and that is the part worth knowing.** One shared cursor looks sufficient and drops rows silently: its id half comes from whichever stream produced the last row, and a `matterTaskDeletion_` handle used as the tie-break against `matter_task.id` excludes every live task sorting below it at that instant — permanently, at a page boundary, under a clock collision nobody can reproduce on demand. Each stream carries its own `(instant, id)`. A test walks five tasks written at one instant a page at a time and asserts nothing is skipped or repeated.

**Visibility is never relaxed for a sync**: the feed composes the same predicate every other task read does, and the external-id lookup returns the same null for "no such correspondence" and "you cannot see the task it names". What the platform deliberately does NOT decide — status mapping, conflict resolution, sync scheduling, field mapping — belongs to the adapter on `@magna-carta/integrations`. The contract an adapter author is held to is in `packages/tasks/CLAUDE.md`.

Not built: any adapter, and that is the design rather than a gap. Also unbuilt: a plan's event and document steps, starting a timer from a task (which waits on the live-timer run store becoming plural — a replacing `start` silently destroys the first run's elapsed time), the board and its firm-defined statuses, recurrence, watchers and comments. The platform ships no curated plans of its own.

**Lists, statuses, teams and a task's own parts (2026-11-09).** The owner approved the task screen redesign and it is built: a firm keeps several lists, each with named, coloured statuses and a flow between them; teams can be assigned; a task gains a start date, a location, several assignees, watchers, a checklist, links and sub-tasks up to five deep. Completion is its own act and leaves the status where it stood, and a task on no matter is now visible to every member, with Me Mode narrowing the list to the reader's own work. The list screen is a `DataTable.Grid` with the status pill, inline dates and priority, sub-task rows and Group By; the task window edits in place. Spec: `docs/specs/2026-11-09-task-lists-and-task-screen-design.md`.

**Adding a task from the list, and a repeat in its own window (2026-11-14).** Every band of the task list ends in an add line (`DataTable.Grid`'s new `bandFoot`): pressed, it opens in the table's own columns, Enter adds the task, and `presetsForBand` in `task-columns.ts` starts it in that band's list, status, person, matter or day. A task's repeat is now set and changed only in `task-repeat-dialog.svelte`; the task window shows the rule as a sentence with Change Repeat. Owner picks B and Repeat 1 from `/mnt/project-files/task-quick-add/`.
