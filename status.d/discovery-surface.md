Slice 5·1 was built end to end and completely invisible — the lanes, the processing queue, deduplication, container families and both record artifacts, with no screen anywhere. This is the surface over it, and the design that answers the owner's framing question first: **discovery is not a super-powered search engine.** Five built things say so — the split catalog is keyed by corpus rather than by matter so a matter search is structurally unable to name an opposing party's production, the per-review-set access list is fail-closed and its reader is not the matter's reader, the corpus has forward-only states where a search has none, its outputs are documents a court reads, and the reviewer's act is coding a queue. Search is one criteria provider among several.

**Three addresses, one per reader.** The matter's Discovery section lists that matter's corpora and starts one; `/discovery` lists every review set the member is on the access list for, across matters; and the workspace at `/discovery/review-sets/<id>` leaves the matter chrome entirely. That last is structural rather than a layout preference: the matter header mounts a consolidated search on every section by construction, and that search must never reach a review set, so a workspace inside the matter layout would put a search field on screen aimed at a different corpus than the documents underneath it. The matter's one create control has the same problem, and the reader may hold no matter access at all — which is also why the workspace names the matter as a fact on its meta line and never as a link.

**The expandability answer is the repository's own, applied four times, each registry arriving with the screen that renders it.** `criteria.ts` declares every way the corpus can be narrowed — five served, four (keyword, date range, coding state, near-duplicate clusters) rendered visible and disabled with what each waits on and which read it will compose into. `deliverables.ts` declares every record the corpus produces for somebody outside the firm — two written, three declared. `review-panels.ts` declares the reviewer's working edge — three reading panels built (the document's facts and digests, its family, and the other copies of it the corpus holds), five to come, none of which changes the reviewer's shell. `renditions.ts` decides how a document is shown, and its ordering is the reviewer's own order of questions: a document that could not be read leads with its named reason even when text was salvaged, because the reason is what a person has to act on. Under all of it, one property does the load-bearing work: **a selection is a criteria set and never a list of identifiers**, so the day keyword narrowing lights up, bulk coding over search results composes rather than being built.

**The reviewer is the second presentation of one read, not a second screen.** The criteria set rides the address, so opening a document from a narrowed list walks exactly that narrowing in exactly that order, and going back returns to it still narrowed. It is also the one place in the product with three regions, and it earns them by not being a list: the Custodian register lost a rail on 2026-09-06 because a rail that NARROWS is a filter wearing the costume of navigation, and a queue is not that — it is the position marker in a walk, and the walk is the task. Brackets move along it, and only when nothing is being typed into.

**Four backend additions, each necessary for a surface above it to be honest.** A firm-wide `readVisibleReviewSets` — the matter-scoped builder without its matter clause, registered in the visibility fitness pass in the same commit — because the wall is the access list and a register scoped by matter access would hide from a contract reviewer exactly the work they were given. And `GET /api/v1/loads/{loadId}/artifacts/{artifact}`, because the manifest and the exception report were reachable only as R2 keys on a row: the two records that are the entire point of the ingest lane could not be handed to anybody. It is audited as an export, access-scoped through the same list, and offers each record in the printable form first. Then the reviewer's two: one document by identifier with its family placing, its digests and every named reason it could not be read — carrying no R2 key, because an object key is an internal address a client can only misuse — and its extracted text, **served through the discovery routes and gated by the review set's access list rather than by the file permissions the native bytes sit behind.** That gating is the decision, not an accident of routing: a reviewer brought in for one production is on the access list and may hold no file permission at all, and the extracted text is exactly what they need to code a document. The native file stays behind its own gate, reachable for the readers who have it and never the only way in.

**What is deliberately not here.** No coding, no search, no production, no filter rail, and no Records strip entry — a load's two artifacts belong to the load that wrote them, so a third strip entry would today be a screen that is always empty; the strip grows when the privilege log does. The native document is named in the reviewer and unavailable with what it waits on, alongside page images and the recognized-text overlay, so the shape of a finished pane is visible before the pane is full.

**The access-list dead end changed shape twice, and both corrections matter.** The feature ledger and the 5·2 design both record the last-member revoke refusal as an open owner question with nothing built; it is built. The residual hole was on a different verb — the guard counted ROWS while a deactivated member keeps their row and is absent to every read, so a list reading "two members" could hold one person who left the firm last year. The count now joins `member` on `status = 'active'`, so the guard means what its own message says, and `readReviewSetsHeldOnlyBy` answers the question the deactivation path has to ask: every review set where a member is the only active person on the list. **That path does not exist** — nothing in the platform sets a member to deactivated — so the read has no caller and is named at the seam instead, beside the `status` field in the members route, where whoever builds it will be standing. Writing a precondition registry with one entry and no route would have been the scaffolding this stream refuses elsewhere. The surface still does not offer the access list.
