**Designed — the operating books as a classification laid over the one ledger, not a second ledger.** Four additive columns on `ledger_account` (a code, a statement section, a system-account marker, a description), the `type` CHECK untouched and normal balance still derived in its one home, one posting primitive, and a source scan asserting the new package composes no entry, line, or balance write of its own. A firm's operating books and its trust books are provably the same rows, so a bar-audit pack and a balance sheet cannot disagree about what happened. **Supersedes the design and plan on the dormant `slice-3-5-operating-general-ledger` branch**, whose two load-bearing choices were right in July and are wrong now: it assumed this slice would land before billing and invented a `semantic_key` resolver, and billing landed first with `firm_account_role`.

**Trust and operating stay separate structurally, and the new risk is the general journal.** A person typing debits and credits is a third door onto a primitive that is account-agnostic by design, so a journal entry naming a matter leaf and an income account would be balanced, leaf-only, single-currency, correctly dated, and would post client money into fee income. The journal is therefore made incapable of naming a trust account rather than taught the trust rules: every line resolves through **trust's own** `loadTrustAccountRoles` and the whole entry refuses by name unless every line is outside trust — the inverse of a rule trust owns, computed from trust's resolver, so a new tier of trust account is refused the day it exists with no edit here. A fitness query re-proves it over stored rows and catches what no pre-check can: an ordinary account journalled against and later registered as a trust bank leaf. The same refusal binds the vendor-bill and bill-payment composers. Nothing is a flag on `assertTrustComposition` and nothing widens the bank-charge carve-out. A chart **may and must** classify a trust account for statement presentation (a trust bank is an asset, the client-trust liability an equal one, and omitting them hides the relationship an examiner checks) and **may not** map one to an account role, re-section one, or re-type one — with no `is_trust` flag anywhere, because registration is already stored in three places and a fourth could go stale in the one direction that matters.

**Pin 4 resolves in the direction the build order took.** `firm_account_role` is the resolver; a `semantic_key` column is refused because two independently editable maps from meaning to account both look correct in their own surface and one of them is where the money went. The map moves into a pure leaf both consumers can depend on (the `billing-core` precedent, no-behaviour-change gate), exactly two roles are added — `accounts_payable` and `owners_equity`, with deliberately no default expense account — and three configuration guards land, the load-bearing one being that a role's account `type` must match its declared normal balance, since mapping `fee_income` to an asset account inverts every future invoice posting while the entry still balances and the batch still commits.

**Two schema corrections to the prior plan.** Widening `cost_entry.source` is unbuildable (SQLite cannot widen a CHECK without a destructive rebuild of a table holding data), so the bill-to-cost link lives on `vendor_bill_line.disbursement_entry_id` and provenance is derived from it, leaving `cost_entry` untouched. And `vendor_bill.status` becomes a derived state — an append-only payment table plus a bounds-checked `paid_minor` delta, the `invoice_matter_balance` arrangement — so two payments racing for one remainder abort rather than overpay.

**Period close is proposed as a scope addition, with the race closed rather than accepted.** A monotonic lock date in its own table, checked inside `postEntry` because a lock only one caller honours is not a lock and because period membership is a property of the ledger's own `effective_date` column. A reversal always passes, dated into the open period, or fixing a closed month would require reopening it. The close-versus-posting race uses billing's refuse-on-frozen-work pattern: the batch aims a value the lock column's date CHECK forbids at the row only when the lock has moved since the read, so a stale read aborts its own whole batch. Retained earnings is computed, never posted.

**Four statement primitives that refuse rather than degrade**, bitemporal in the shape 3·6 shipped, with the temporal contract moved down into a shared leaf so one validation of a triple exists. A trial balance that does not foot, a balance sheet that does not balance, trust sections that do not net to zero, and any posting account no section would place each refuse by name. Re-sectioning is bounded so it can only move an account within one statement. Nothing is materialised, and the `ledger_line.effective_date` denormalization is argued from this slice's own measurement or not at all. **QuickBooks is a sync structurally**: the `accounting` category declares export operations only with a fitness assertion rejecting any import-shaped operation name, there is no column anywhere that could hold a remote figure, an export refusal is a dispatch outcome, and a divergence caused by editing the remote is stated as undetectable rather than simulated — because detecting it needs the read-back that would make the remote authoritative.

**Five sub-slices, each shippable alone; seven ADR fragments; sixteen questions flagged.** 3·5·1 (the chart) is first because it also closes a live gap — billing refuses to finalize an invoice whose roles are unmapped and there is no surface to map them. **Three things are scoped out rather than half-built**: cash does not arrive until payments' first slice lands, so the operating bank balance moves only through a typed journal entry and the books are correct but sparse; there is no operating bank reconciliation until an importer and the payments clearing account exist; and per-office or per-matter income statements are not designed, because the dimension columns land here and the paths producing income belong to slices this one does not own.
