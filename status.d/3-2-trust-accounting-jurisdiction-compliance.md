**Sub-slices 1 and 2 built.** Sub-slice 1 — trust schema, the three-level client/matter tree, the zero floor written in the creation batch, the composition guard owning no-commingling and fees-never-touch-trust, holds and available balance, the two-linked-entry transfer, and the fitness suite. Fifteen owner flags stay open; the code refuses rather than defaults wherever one would have to be guessed.

**Sub-slice 2** — deposits, disbursements, and matter-to-matter transfers, each behind a required-idempotency route; the append-only `trust_movement` client-ledger record carrying source or payee, purpose, method-as-a-value, reference, and the authorizing member, with its shape enforced by a database CHECK; available balance as the disbursement ceiling; a bank-free reallocation between two matters of one client; and a movement refusal on an account whose office recorded no jurisdiction. The jurisdiction corpus remains open.

**The matter-close gate** is the matter shell's close hook filled for the first time: a matter whose client trust ledger still holds money, or carries an active disputed-funds hold, is refused with the balance, the account holding it, and the movements that clear it. Rules are handed to the shell by the composition root rather than imported by it, and a rule may also guard the close batch itself, so a deposit landing between the check and the commit aborts the close instead of slipping under it. A matter with no trust ledger closes freely.

**Two-person disbursement authorization** (owner-directed) closes the last open piece of sub-slice 2: a disbursement is a pending request until a second member approves it, and the movement posts only on that approval, with the approver as `authorized_by` and the submitter as `recorded_by`. The approver-differs rule is a database trigger, not route discipline; the available-balance ceiling is re-read at approval time through the same composer an ungated payment used, so a request overtaken by other activity refuses instead of posting; rejection and withdrawal are recorded, append-only, alongside the request. A firm with a single member holding `trust:manage` may still pay, with that basis stated on the record, and the database withdraws the exception the moment a second such member exists. Three recommended defaults are flagged for owner review: every disbursement is gated rather than only those over a threshold, a pending request never expires, and no new permission was minted.

**The Nevada jurisdiction corpus** fills the jurisdiction seam for the first time, and it still refuses. Nevada's rules are read out of the state's own published text — retention, the IOLTA regime, overdraft notification, unclaimed funds, annual compliance, and advance-fee treatment — with every value carrying the citation and the verbatim sentence it came from, because a value the reviewing attorney cannot check against the rule is a value they cannot review. Encoded is not verified: a profile with no recorded attorney review refuses every requirement with its own code, distinct from the never-researched refusal and from the no-office-state one, and the review binds to the profile revision it was performed against so an amendment re-closes the gate rather than inheriting an attestation about superseded text. Nevada ships NOT VERIFIED, and the read that surfaces the profile with its citations never throws — a firm shown nothing has no reason to get anything reviewed. Every other jurisdiction stays not-encoded with no baseline behind it. Three facts this repository had already written down turned out to be wrong against the current rules, all traceable to the State Bar's own 2019 handbook: retention is five years and not seven, the account-mechanics rule is SCR 78 and not the repealed SCR 78.5, and Nevada mandates no reconciliation at any cadence — the approved schema design's golden test for Nevada would have locked the last two in. The bank-charge carve-out is encoded as a cited rule and deliberately not wired: Nevada permits it, states no cap, and exempts pooled accounts from the charges it would pay, so the permission sits beside a composition guard that still refuses firm money in trust. Flags T7 and T8 shipped as owner-ratified; the attorney review itself and three research caveats are listed for the owner.

**The authorization policy** (owner-directed) turns the always-two-person rule into the firm's own posture: off, threshold (a second person for amounts strictly above a stated amount), or always, plus an independent option requiring whoever lets money go to hold the new `trust:authorize` capability — authority encoded as a capability predicate, seeded to Owner/Admin and Attorney, never a role name. The shipped default is the strict posture as an absent row, so nothing relaxes on migrate; a change is a new append-only version requiring `trust:authorize`, audited from-and-to. Every request stamps the version current at submission and is decided under that stamp — an in-flight request keeps its policy in both directions — and the earned-fee application follows the same policy through the same queue: an application is a request of kind `earned_fee_application` whose invoice lines billing keeps beside it, approved through billing, posting the transfer, the invoice applications, and the decision as one batch. Basis exactness (`second_person` / `sole_trust_manager` / `policy_exempt`), the authority requirement, the stamp itself, and the transfer's recorded actors are all database triggers. Flagged for the owner: whether relaxing the posture should itself take a second person, and whether matter-to-matter reallocations should fall under the policy.

**Review-gate hardening and the catalog re-verification** land the corpus follow-ups before the port is bound or any attorney review is recorded. A review now binds to a content-derived revision — the authored date plus a fingerprint of everything the reviewer attests to — so an amendment that forgets the revision bump closes the gate rather than silently inheriting the attestation. The two newer refusals (encoded-but-unreviewed, and a reviewed jurisdiction's unresearched field) map to the jurisdiction-unavailable problem type with their siblings instead of the generic catch-all; a source-scan fitness guard trips any future surface that computes on an unreviewed value through the display seam; and the silence-resolved singletons are frozen against caller mutation. The narrative 56-jurisdiction catalog was swept against the primary-source records: the Nevada entry's repealed SCR 78.5 citations are corrected, its unclaimed-funds facts aligned with NRPC 1.15(f) and the NRS 120A.500(6)(f) exemption, and the 55 sibling entries — which no primary source in the repository can check — are banner-marked NOT VERIFIED, research leads rather than findings.

**Three-way reconciliation** closes sub-slice 4. A bank statement is a recorded period with its lines — entered by a member, or imported from a file through a preview that reports what it found and stores nothing until a separate confirmation quotes that report's fingerprint back. Statement lines are tied to recorded ledger entries by a person: the platform ranks bounded candidates and shows the reasons, and nothing anywhere records a match on its own. The proof ties three totals as of the period end — the statement's closing balance adjusted for outstanding items, the trust bank account's book balance, and the sum of the client ledgers — both ledger legs computed from dated rows rather than a stored balance, so a period reproduces. Unmatched lines and unmatched recorded items are named outcomes rather than errors, and a proof that does not tie out records which leg disagrees and by how much rather than being forced green. Closing freezes the proof on an append-only row the database refuses to reopen; an unbalanced period closes recording exactly that, and a correction is a new reconciliation superseding the old. The load profile gained the trust volume — the sub-ledger tree, movements, holds, two years of monthly statements each carrying an unrecorded service charge, and the reconciliations over them — with the three-way computation and the client-ledger reads measured against index-walk plans. Flags T9, T10, and T11 shipped as the analysis's recommended defaults and are listed for owner review.

**Authority-based authorization** (owner-directed) reshapes the posture the policy expresses: authority is a property of the person, not a count of people. A member holding `trust:authorize` releases money out of a client ledger — or takes earned fees against a finalized bill — alone and in ONE atomic act (the request, the movement, and the decision recording the footing ride one batch), and a member without it raises the same payment and is told, before composing anything, who can settle it. The shipped posture is now that rule as DATA: a per-firm migration seeds version 1 (`off` plus the authority requirement) conditionally on the table being empty, so the absent-row reading stays strict for the requests it describes, no shipped trigger is touched, and a firm that already chose a posture keeps it. A firm wanting a ceiling still writes a `threshold` or `always` version; above the ceiling the authority requirement binds whichever of the two decides, which is peer approval with no new setting and no new mirror. The one addition is `resolveTrustAuthorizationDisposition`, a read in front of the guarantees that both money-out paths choose solo-versus-route from — it calls the existing predicate rather than restating it, asks the authority question first and always of the ACTOR, and fails closed on an unrecognized kind. The lone-holder count-plus-membership pair is extracted into one helper all three TypeScript callers share. Flagged for the owner: the exception still counts `trust:manage` rather than `trust:authorize` (the design's open question 1, left open because the change would relax a firm with several managers and one authority holder), and the seed relaxes any firm that exists today with no policy row from always-two-person to authority-solo. UI deferred.

**The bank-charge carve-out** (owner flag T3, owner-directed "wire it narrowly") gives a trust bank account somewhere to put the one item on a real bank statement that no member authorized. The firm's own funds go in for the single purpose the conduct rules permit, and the narrowness is structural in five independent places rather than stated as policy: the record lives in its own table with **no column for a client, a matter, a node, or a payee**, so a row of it cannot describe a movement of anybody else's money; the entry goes through a second composition door admitting exactly the firm-funds balance paired with its own account's bank leaf, while the door client money goes through still refuses a firm-funds line outright and was not relaxed to make room; the cushion carries the same `account_balance` zero floor a matter ledger carries, which is how "a bank charge is never borne by client funds" becomes a database guarantee rather than a comparison somebody could forget; a distinct `trust:firmFunds` capability seeded to Owner/Admin alone gates opening and funding it, narrower than the authority to release a client's money; and opening it is refused outright on a pooled interest-bearing account, which the researched rules exempt from the charges the cushion would pay. The three-way proof gained a fourth figure: the cushion is its own leg, never folded into the client total (which previously summed every node and would have counted firm money as client money) and never left out of the comparison (which would have reported a `book-to-client` disagreement on every account that enabled it, poisoning the one signal that means something bypassed the composition guard). Three fitness queries re-prove all of it over stored rows. Three questions are flagged for the owner, all places the rule text is silent: whether the cushion carries a maximum, whether surplus firm funds may be withdrawn, and whether the pooled-account refusal is too strict.

**Unclaimed client funds** (owner flag T16) ship as a read and an evidence trail, and deliberately not as an escheat feature: the primary-source research found designated client trust accounts exempt from Nevada's unclaimed-property presumption outright, with the one onward destination the rules name being permissive rather than compelled, so there is no deadline to count down to and no screen says otherwise. Dormancy is a comparison made at read time against a window **the reader states** — no stored flag, no sweep, no default period — mirroring the calendar's overdue posture and for the same reason: a firm may have been in contact with the client this morning in a way the platform never saw. The read reports the earliest receipt still on the ledger separately from the last activity, because the rules' clocks run from receipt of the property. Each locate attempt is recorded when it is made, with the address or number actually used, in an append-only file that carries one open review per client ledger. **Every money movement takes a person, and the gate is the absence of the inputs**: closing a review as a distribution names a disbursement that already posted through the ordinary authorized path, and the closing composer receives no amount, payee, date, or method, so there is nothing it could assemble into a ledger entry — it writes one row and moves nothing. The remittance packet, the jurisdiction type-fit repair, the separate non-trust holder regime, and the routes and interface are written into the slice's spec rather than half-built.

**The first production round of the interface** puts trust in front of a member for the first time, in two places. The matter's Trust section, built whole a fortnight earlier against the movement record and standing empty for want of an account, now reads the firm's trust accounts, chooses one (the only open account, else the one remembered on the device, else the first open one alphabetically), reads the matter's ledger against it, and treats the read's 404 as the fact it is — no ledger until the first deposit — rather than as a failure. Its three writes are dialogs: a deposit, a payment out proposed under the firm's posture (released at once, or pending with the eligible approvers named on the screen), and a transfer to another of the client's matters; pending requests on the matter show in the notices band with withdraw for the proposer and approve or reject for anybody else. The sidebar's Trust row lights over a firm screen: the accounts register as the list, the selected account's record beside it — the approval queue with the same verbs, the statements and reconciliations as recorded with each closed proof's figures, the bank-charge cushion or the fact that none is open, and the firm's authorization posture with its change behind `trust:authorize`. One route was added because nothing had ever opened a trust account over HTTP: `POST /api/v1/trust/accounts`, `trust:manage`, idempotency required, deriving the jurisdiction from the office and refusing to take one. Named on the screen rather than half-built: the reconciliation workbench, statement entry and import, the cushion's forms, the unclaimed-funds file, and the matter behind a queued request, which the queue serves only as a ledger node.

**The second production round** closes every seam the first named as Soon. The reconciliation workbench: a statement is recorded by hand with its lines, or imported from a file through a preview that stores nothing until a confirmation quotes its fingerprint back; a reconciliation opens on a statement and takes its own screen under Trust, where the three-way proof stands first, the unmatched statement lines sit beside the outstanding recorded items with the candidate pairings and their reasons marked, a person records each match and may take it back, and closing freezes the proof balanced or not — a closed reconciliation shows what the firm attested to beside what is true now, never one as the other. The bank-charge cushion's three acts become forms behind `trust:firmFunds`. The unclaimed-funds file becomes a section of the account's record: dormant balances read against a day the reader states with no default, the review opened on the observation as served, each contact attempt recorded as made, and the round closed against a disbursement that already posted. On the backend, an account's position is one read computed from dated rows through the same leg computation the proof uses, a reconciliation's matches are readable with their withdrawals, and each queued request carries the matter it would pay from by number and title.
