`@magna-carta/costs` + `@magna-carta/billing-core` (shared WIP lifecycle); first per-firm queue consumer; recoverable-charge seam. cost→A/R + LEDES → 3·4.

**Costs becomes a place you can go, which took a listing shape it did not have.** `GET /api/v1/costs` answered one matter entire and returned an empty list for anything else, so Costs was absent from the sidebar's Money group entirely while the matter's own Money group carried it — the two disagreed about what Money holds, for a structural reason rather than an oversight. The listing now also takes a `from`/`to` span of incurred dates across every matter, narrowable to one matter, one status and to billable or not, cursor-paged over the same statement the per-matter shape walks so a cost reads back identically whichever found it. The unnarrowed range had no index whose leading column was the date, so `cost_entry` gained one, exactly as `time_entry` did for its own span.

`/costs` is that period: what the firm advanced, what is still waiting on a bill, and what it decided to absorb. Its columns are the matter ledger's own declaration with the Matter column brought forward, so a cost carries the same words on both screens.

**The cost record answers with what the table already held.** `cost_entry.created_at`, `.created_by` and `.kick_back_reason` were in the baseline schema and were never projected, so Date Entered, Entered By and the reason a cost was sent back said "not served" on every cost surface about facts the firm's own database was holding. The kick-back reason was the costly one: the transition returns a cost to **draft** and writes the reviewer's reason on the row, and the next submission clears it, so a non-null value means exactly "this draft came back and has not been sent again" — the only thing telling a returned draft from one nobody submitted. Unserved, the person who had to fix the cost was told nothing. It now leads the record window's Details pane, sits above the chain on the details panel, and has a hidden Returned column; it is read as a DRAFT fact so no later transition that forgets to clear it can print it over an approved cost, and it shows whether or not the firm runs review, because a reason already on a record is a fact rather than an invitation to a workflow. Both reach the wire now, the recorder's name resolved by a `member` outer join in the same statement the vendor's and the matter's already were — outer, and `created_by` deliberately not a foreign key, because who wrote a disbursement down has to survive that person leaving the firm. Entered By is not a permission fact: this slice's design rules that a cost belongs to the matter and not to its recorder, so there is no per-user self-scoping on costs at all, and the two surfaces' duplicated `canWorkDraft` — whose comment promised a recorder gate that ruling refuses — is now one function that states the real rule. The detail read stopped being a third shape at the same time: it was a hand-written column list resolving none of the three names, so opening a cost answered with less than the row it was opened from.

And a billed record now names the bill that took it up. `cost_entry.invoice_id` existed and never reached the wire, so a billed cost could not answer what the client was actually told; the row menu's **Open The Bill** lands on the matter's Billing section with that bill open. The verb lives in the shared ledger catalogue rather than on this menu, because time asks the same question of the same kind of row — `time_entry.invoice_id` had been on the entry contract since it was written and nothing on the time surfaces read it, so that gap was a verb rather than a read. A ledger declares `opensTheBill` when it has somewhere to send a reader.

**And Fees becomes the third work-in-progress store, which reverses a standing ruling.** The 2026-08-26 fee-surface design ruled that Fees is NOT a work-in-progress surface: a matter's fee value was the time ledger's or the arrangement's, so the screen paired the fee AGREEMENT with the invoice LINES a bill had composed from it. The owner moved the arrangement into the matter's status window on 2026-09-03 — it is a fact of the engagement rather than of the ledger — which removed that ruling's own justification, because the pairing WAS the argument. What was left was the pure invoice view the design had itself rejected: a ledger of lines can show nothing that is still unbilled, which is the whole of what a work-in-progress ledger is for. The reasoning also had a hole the pairing hid — a flat sum agreed to settle a file on an hourly matter is neither the time ledger's product nor the arrangement's amount, so it could only be typed straight onto a draft bill, invisible to every unbilled read and gone if the draft was abandoned.

`fee_entry` carries the same anatomy `time_entry` and `cost_entry` do, deliberately, and rides the same chain through `@magna-carta/billing-core` rather than a third copy of it. A draft invoice claims it through the same `invoice_wip_claim`, whose `source_kind` CHECK is widened by a **hand-written** rebuild: the two triggers freezing a finalized invoice's claims are not in the Drizzle schema, so a generated rebuild would have dropped them silently and unfrozen every finalized bill's claims in the fleet.

**That rebuild then had to answer the durable-object finding**, which landed on main while this was being built and collapsed the per-firm lineage with it: a rebuild DROPS the table it replaces, a durable object enforces foreign keys at the statement AND at the commit, and `PRAGMA foreign_keys=OFF` is a no-op inside the one execution context a migration runs in — so a rebuild of a table other tables point at cannot complete against a firm that holds data, and completes only against the empty databases the suite used to build. This one ships on the guard's own criterion rather than by exemption: `invoice_wip_claim` is a **leaf**, nothing in the schema references it, the claim points OUT at the invoice and the line it was composed into, and `parentRebuildViolations` skips a rebuild whose table has no children. The tag joins `REVIEWED_REBUILDS` and NOT the grandfathered parent list, which is documented as debt that must never grow. And the proof is in a real durable object rather than in `node:sqlite` — that distinction IS the lesson of the outage, because the row-preservation harness cannot reproduce commit-time enforcement, so a leaf rebuild passing there says nothing about the runtime it has to survive. The migration's header, which claimed the bracketing pragmas protected the swap, is corrected: they are kept as ordinary practice and are doing no work. The kinds became a union (`WORK_IN_PROGRESS_KINDS`) so the draft composer's two-way ternaries and its two hand-written mirror-clearing statements derive from it rather than enumerating it.

`GET/POST /api/v1/fees` serves both listing shapes on billing's own permissions — reading a fee is reading unbilled work in progress, which `billing:read` already names — and the matter's Fees section reads the per-matter shape whole, in every status, paged. **Nothing computes a fee from time entries**, and the non-hourly-arrangement guard stays scoped to `time_entry` so a recorded fee on a flat-fee matter is a separate charge rather than a second billing of the same agreement.

What the second ledger shares with the first was hoisted rather than copied, which is the point of doing it at the second surface: `$lib/records/work-in-progress-record.ts` is the one home for the readings every ledger makes of a record, and the void and kick-back confirmations moved beside it. Fees is the first ledger with a DELETE route, so **delete-draft** joins them and `deletesDraft` sits beside `opensTheBill` as a declaration on the shared row menu — deleting is not voiding, and the refusal on the wrong one is where the difference gets taught. The nullable-amount branch the built surface carried came out: the table's CHECK makes it unreachable and the cross-ledger move that would produce it does not exist, so "Needs an amount" was a state no reader could ever meet.

`/fees` is the period across every matter, and it takes the row Fees never had — absent from the sidebar's Money group for exactly the reason Costs was, structurally rather than by oversight, because nothing served the record. Its columns are the matter ledger's own declaration with Matter brought forward (`firmFeeColumns`), so a fee carries the same words on both screens; its period arithmetic is the Time Sheet's, reused a third time rather than written again, because three money screens computing "last month" separately is three chances to disagree about where a month starts. The record window gained the matter picker the cost window has, on the same reasoning: a fee pre-filled with whichever matter came first would be a wrong answer arriving faster than no answer. Four of the five money sections now have a firm-wide twin, and the toolbar's way out derives from the sidebar's own registry, so **All fees** appeared with no second declaration.
